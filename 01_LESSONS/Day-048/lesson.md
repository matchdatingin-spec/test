Spelling recall. Most verbs add s: reads, drives, cooks. Verbs ending in sounds like ch, sh, s, x, o often add es: watches, washes, passes, fixes, goes. Consonant plus y changes to ies: study, studies; carry, carries. Have becomes has.

Meaning remains routine, habit, fact. My father drives to work. Leena teaches science. The shop opens at nine. This machine makes a strange sound. Missing s gives “My father drive,” which listener understands but notices.

Negative and question danger. He works. He doesn't work. Does he work? Once does or doesn't is present, main verb returns to base. Not “He doesn't works.” Not “Does he works?” Positive sentence owns the s: He works. In question or negative, does owns that agreement signal.
