Current process or situation section:

Opinion and improvement:

In my view, the library should remain open until seven on two evenings each week. The first reason is access. Students with late classes and adults with full-time jobs would have a realistic time to visit. The second reason is that the existing building and books would be used by more people without requiring a completely new service. I understand that longer hours would increase staffing and electricity costs. For that reason, I would not begin with every evening. A two-evening trial for three months would be more practical. If very few people used the extra hours, the schedule could be reviewed. If demand were strong, the service could continue.

Conclusion:

Overall, the library is modest, but it has real value because it gives people a shared place to read and study. My own experience showed me that access matters as much as the number of books. A limited evening schedule would make the library more useful while keeping the change manageable. That is why I would support a short trial.

One-minute mental plan example anchors: first computer course. Small crowded lab. Instructor calm and practical. First-day login problem. Learned record checking. Class process. Improvement: more practice time. Concern: cost and schedule. Conclusion.

Guided opening:

I would like to describe a short computer course I attended last year. The course improved my confidence with spreadsheets, but it also showed me how practical training could be organised more effectively.

Person and place description:

The instructor was a patient, direct person who demonstrated each task slowly and then asked us to try it ourselves. The training room was narrow, with two rows of computers and a whiteboard at the front. It was often crowded, and the sound of several keyboards made it difficult to hear questions from the back. Still, the atmosphere was friendly because learners helped one another.

Incident or challenge:

On the first day, I could not open my practice file because I had saved it in the wrong folder. I thought the file had been lost, and I became nervous when the rest of the group moved to the next task. The instructor asked me to explain exactly what I had done. Then she showed me how to search by file name and check the save location. We found the file within a few minutes. After that incident, I began checking the folder before saving each exercise. By the end of the course, I was helping another learner find her files.

Process and skill:

Each lesson followed a simple process. A task was first demonstrated on the main screen. Then practice files were shared with us, and we completed the same task individually. Our work was checked before the next activity began. This method was useful because we saw the action, tried it, and received correction immediately. It also developed one of my strengths: careful step-by-step problem solving.

Improvement argument:

However, I think future groups need more independent practice time. Many learners could follow the demonstration but became unsure when they worked alone. An extra twenty minutes at the end would allow them to repeat the main task without watching the instructor. It would also help the instructor identify who genuinely needed support. I understand that a longer session may be difficult for people who travel home by bus. A better option may be to reduce the number of new tasks in each class and protect the final twenty minutes for practice. If the course tried that schedule for one group, feedback could be collected before changing the whole programme.

Conclusion:

Overall, the course was valuable because it combined clear demonstrations with immediate practice. The file problem was small, but solving it gave me confidence and changed how I organise my work. With slightly more independent practice, the course could help future learners become more confident as well.
