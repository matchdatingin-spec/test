Now ago. Ago counts backward from now. It comes after the amount of time: two minutes ago, three days ago, a week ago, six months ago, many years ago. Malayalam influence കൊണ്ട് “before two days” വരാം. Natural English for counting back from now is two days ago.

I saw him two days ago. She left ten minutes ago. We bought this table a year ago. The event happened three weeks ago.

Ago phrase usually goes after the amount, not before. “Ago two days” wrong. “Two days before” needs another reference point and is not today’s simple now-backward expression. Use two days ago.

Ago works with a measured distance from now. Last names the previous calendar unit. Compare: I met her last week. That means during the previous week. I met her a week ago. That means approximately seven days before now. Similar, but not always identical.

ഇനി for. For gives duration, the length of an activity or state. For ten minutes. For two hours. For three days. For a long time. Completed past examples: I waited for twenty minutes. We stayed there for two nights. She worked on the task for three hours. The meeting lasted for an hour.

Question meaning: How long? How long did you wait? I waited for twenty minutes. How long did they stay? They stayed for two nights.

Do not use in for duration in these sentences. “I waited in two hours” wrong for length. I waited for two hours.

Now since. Since points to a starting point, not a length. Since Monday. Since 2020. Since six o'clock. Since childhood. Think arrow beginning at that point and continuing to a chosen reference point. For answers “how long?” with duration. Since answers “from when?” with starting point.

For three years tells length. Since 2021 tells start. For two hours tells length. Since six o'clock tells start. For a week tells length. Since Monday tells start.

Important course boundary: English often uses since inside a tense pattern that we have not opened yet.
