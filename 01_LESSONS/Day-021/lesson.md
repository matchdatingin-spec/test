Finished? Conceptual recap. Complete English thought needs a subject. Identity, description, and location use learned be forms. Routine action follows subject–verb–rest order. I, you, we, they use base-form action verbs; he, she, it and singular equivalents use third-person agreement in positive statements. Frequency words normally come before main actions and after be. Do and does form yes-or-no action questions; don't and doesn't form Present Simple action negatives. After does or doesn't, the main verb returns to base form. Have and has connect possession, family, features, feelings, and common conditions; there is and there are introduce existence. Articles, plurals, possessives, and place words make details accurate.
