# FORENSIC AUDIT — 90-Day English Course for Malayalam Speakers

**Artefact audited:** `90-day-english-syllabus-malayalam.md` (12 weeks + Days 85–90)
**Question being answered:** Is this syllabus safe to freeze as the master source of truth for generating 90 independent lessons?
**Audit stance:** The syllabus is audited exactly as written. No days have been moved, added, or rewritten in this document.

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE VERDICT

**Overall Score: 54 / 100**

**Master Syllabus Status: REQUIRES STRUCTURAL CORRECTIONS**

The syllabus passes the test it is easiest to pass and fails several of the tests that actually matter.

What is genuinely sound: the phase architecture, the topic inventory, the checkpoint cadence, the decision to start pronunciation on Day 6 rather than Week 9, the deliberate splitting of place prepositions (Day 12) from time prepositions (Day 41), and the restraint shown in omitting Type 3 conditionals, future perfect, past perfect continuous, and inversion. Day-to-day continuity is mostly clean. A learner following this course would not be confused about where they are.

What fails is structural, and three findings are severe enough on their own to block freezing:

1. **The course is grammar-heavy in exactly the way the design brief forbade.** Roughly 61% of days are grammar-owning, 3% are listening-owning, and 2% are vocabulary-owning. Worse, Days 70–77 — the window where the brief explicitly demanded increasing fluency emphasis — is the single most grammar-dense stretch in all 90 days (conditionals ×2, reported speech, passive, phrasal verbs, gerunds/infinitives). The syllabus states Phase 5 begins at Day 78; in practice the grammar syllabus runs to Day 76.

2. **There is a hard prerequisite violation at Day 46.** Present Perfect requires past participles (V3). Day 32 taught irregular *past simple* forms (V2) only. No day in the syllabus owns the third verb form. Day 46 and Day 74 (passive) both depend on knowledge the course never delivers.

3. **Three of the course's own named Malayalam-speaker problems have no owning day.** Hesitation / thinking-time and Malayalam-influenced English expressions are described as core targets in the Week 8 and Week 12 problem statements, but no Day 1–84 topic teaches them. Articles get exactly one day (Day 10) and are never reinforced despite being flagged as a top-tier difficulty. A problem named in a week blurb but absent from the day list will not be taught, because the day list is what generates lessons.

The topic inventory does not need to be rebuilt. Approximately 12–14 targeted edits, most of which are day-swaps and reclaiming slots from thin days, would move this to READY. A full redesign is not warranted and would destroy work that is already correct.

## 2. TOP 10 PROBLEMS

### 1. 🔴 Grammar block placed exactly where fluency was required

**Location:** Days 70–77 (Week 11), and whole-course balance
**Problem:** Week 11 packs conditionals (2 days), reported speech, passive voice, phrasal verbs, and gerunds/infinitives into 6 teaching days. Across the whole course, ~55 of 90 days are grammar-owning; listening owns 3; vocabulary owns 2. The brief required that from Day 70 grammar increasingly serve communication rather than remain the objective. Days 71–76 are the opposite.
**Why it matters:** This is the point where learners either break through to fluency or plateau. Loading the heaviest abstract grammar here produces learners who know conditionals and still cannot speak. It also creates a motivation cliff 20 days before graduation, in a YouTube context where drop-off is the main failure mode.
**Recommended correction:** Move phrasal verbs (Day 75) and gerunds/infinitives (Day 76) earlier — gerunds belong in Week 6, phrasal verbs in Week 8. Keep conditionals, reported speech, and passive in Week 11 but repurpose the vacated slots as applied-communication days. Cap Week 11 at four new grammar systems.

### 2. 🔴 Past participles (V3) are never taught but are required twice

**Location:** Day 46 (present perfect), Day 74 (passive) — prerequisite absent
**Problem:** Day 32 is "Past Simple — the most useful irregular verbs," which owns V2. Nothing owns V3. Day 46 asks the learner to produce "have + past participle" using forms they have never been shown. Day 74 compounds this.
**Why it matters:** This is the only true critical dependency violation in the course, and it sits under two major topics. A lesson generator writing Day 46 will either silently teach V3 inside Day 46 (making it a double-load day and creating an ownership conflict with Day 32) or assume knowledge the learner lacks.
**Recommended correction:** Give V3 an explicit owning day before Day 46. The cheapest fix: Day 34 ("Past Time Expressions") is a thin day whose content folds naturally into Day 33; repurpose Day 34 as "The Three Verb Forms — V1 / V2 / V3."

### 3. 🔴 Listening owns 3 days out of 90

**Location:** Days 27, 62, 88 — gaps of 34 and 25 days
**Problem:** The Day-90 outcome includes "understand common spoken English at an appropriate learner level." The curriculum allocates three lessons to it, with the first meaningful conversational listening arriving at Day 62 — six days *after* the learner is asked to hold a three-minute conversation on Day 56.
**Why it matters:** Listening is the bottleneck skill for Malayalam-speaking learners and the one most damaged by neglect, because it cannot be compensated for by preparation. A conversation checkpoint that precedes connected-speech training tests only the production half of the skill.
**Recommended correction:** Target six listening-owning days. Add one in Week 5 (past-tense listening), one in Week 8 before Day 56, and one in Week 10. Retain Day 88 as the culminating challenge rather than the second real exposure.

### 4. 🔴 Articles taught once, never recycled

**Location:** Day 10, with no reinforcement anywhere in Days 11–90
**Problem:** Malayalam has no article system, which the syllabus itself identifies as a top-frequency error source. The response is a single Day 10 lesson delivered before the learner has any verb system to practise articles inside. Articles then never reappear as an owned topic.
**Why it matters:** Article accuracy is acquired through repeated corrective exposure across many contexts, not through one explanation. One-shot treatment of a no-L1-equivalent system guarantees fossilisation. The same criticism applies to prepositions (Days 12 and 41, then silence).
**Recommended correction:** Add one "small words" correction-and-recycling day around Day 42–45 and a second around Day 77, each revisiting articles and prepositions inside sentences the learner can now already build. This is spiral learning, not repetition.

### 5. 🔴 Named Malayalam problems with no owning day

**Location:** Week 8 problem statement (hesitation, translation-driven pauses, "What is your good name?" / "do the needful" register); Week 12 problem statement (Manglish formal English)
**Problem:** The draft that preceded the final syllabus contained a "fillers and thinking time" day; it was displaced when the opinion lesson was added, and the content was never re-homed. Manglish expression correction is likewise described but unowned. Both surface only at Day 87 and Day 86 respectively, as part of generic consolidation.
**Why it matters:** Hesitation management is the highest-leverage single intervention for a learner who has grammar but freezes. Because lessons will be generated from the day list, an unowned problem is an untaught problem regardless of how prominently it appears in a week description.
**Recommended correction:** Create explicit owning days: "Thinking Time — what to say instead of going silent" in Week 8, and "Manglish → Natural English" in Week 8 or early Week 12.

### 6. 🔴 Reported speech compressed into one day

**Location:** Day 73
**Problem:** Reported speech comprises tense backshift, pronoun shift, time/place-word shift, reported questions (with the do-support removal that Malayalam speakers get wrong), and reported commands. That is five sub-systems, one of which reverses a rule taught on Day 17.
**Why it matters:** Single-day treatment of a five-part system produces recognition without production. It is also the day most likely to force a lesson generator to invent scope, since "telling what someone said" gives no boundary on which sub-systems are in or out.
**Recommended correction:** Split into reported statements and reported questions/commands across two days, funded by merging Day 22 and Day 23.

### 7. 🟠 Pronunciation dead zone and a stress-order inversion

**Location:** Days 32–56 (25 consecutive days with no pronunciation ownership); Day 20 vs Day 60; nothing after Day 63
**Problem:** Three separate defects. (a) After Day 31's `-ed` sounds, pronunciation vanishes for 25 days and returns as the Week 9 block — the exact "isolated pronunciation block" the brief warned against. (b) Day 20 teaches sentence rhythm and stress; Day 60 teaches word stress *and* sentence stress. Word stress is the prerequisite for sentence stress, so the course delivers them inverted and then overlaps them. (c) Days 64–90 contain no pronunciation ownership at all, though Day 90 claims a pronunciation outcome.
**Why it matters:** Pronunciation gains decay without maintenance, and the fluency phase is where they are most likely to be abandoned under cognitive load. The Day 20 / Day 60 overlap is also a lesson-generation collision.
**Recommended correction:** Assign word stress to Day 20 and sentence stress/rhythm to Day 60, resolving both the inversion and the overlap. Add one pronunciation-in-real-speech day in Week 12.

### 8. 🟠 SVO taught on Day 1, first usable on Day 15

**Location:** Day 1 → Days 3–14 → Day 15
**Problem:** Day 1 establishes Subject–Verb–Object as the central mind-shift. Days 3–5 then teach the be-verb, which is not SVO but Subject–be–Complement. Days 8–14 are noun-phrase grammar. The learner cannot produce a single action-verb SVO sentence until Day 15 — 14 days after being told SVO is the core insight.
**Why it matters:** The signature Malayalam transfer error ("I tea drank") is a *verb-position* error. Diagnosing it on Day 1 and then withholding practice for two weeks is the reverse of what the transfer-error framing demands. It also weakens Week 1's YouTube promise: the learner is shown the key to English and then handed a different lock.
**Recommended correction:** Introduce two or three high-frequency action verbs in the Week 1–2 range so SVO is practised as a pattern from Day 1, or move a simple action-verb sentence day into Week 2. Day 15 then becomes systematisation rather than first use.

### 9. 🟠 Gerunds/infinitives and frequency adverbs are both far later than first need

**Location:** Day 76 (gerunds/infinitives), Day 68 (adverbs of frequency)
**Problem:** "I want to go," "I like reading," "I enjoy watching" are needed from Week 6 onward and are unavoidable in Days 50–56 conversation work, but the pattern is not owned until Day 76. Adverbs of frequency (always/usually/often/never) are the natural partner of the habitual present taught in Week 3, yet they sit at Day 68 inside a complex-sentence week where they do not thematically belong.
**Why it matters:** When a required pattern has no owning day before it is needed, learners improvise from Malayalam and fossilise "I am interested to learn," "I enjoy to read." Late ownership also means the Day 76 lesson must un-teach habits the course allowed to form.
**Recommended correction:** Move basic verb + to / verb + -ing patterns to Week 6. Move frequency adverbs to Week 3 alongside present simple.

### 10. 🟠 Day 45 "Tense Map" precedes the tenses it maps

**Location:** Day 45, positioned before Day 46 (present perfect)
**Problem:** Day 45 is billed as the milestone where learners learn to choose the right tense while speaking. At that point present perfect has not been taught. The map is missing the tense that causes the most selection errors, and Day 47 then re-does selection work for the pair Day 45 could not cover.
**Why it matters:** A selection framework taught against an incomplete inventory must be re-taught, and Days 45 and 47 now overlap without a clear boundary — a production-readiness problem as much as a sequencing one.
**Recommended correction:** Swap Day 45 and Day 49 so the tense map lands after the full tense inventory and functions as the week's genuine consolidation.

### Notes on the lowest scores

**Listening (3/10)** — three owning days cannot deliver a listening outcome. This is the weakest dimension in the course.

**Vocabulary Support (4/10)** — only Day 50 (chunks) and Day 75 (phrasal verbs) own vocabulary; Day 13 owns adjectives. Nothing owns everyday topical vocabulary (health, money, transport, food, time), sequencing language for narration, opinion/preference language, or workplace lexis. Day 80 and Day 82 are situational, not lexical. The course expects a 5-minute unprepared talk on Day 84 from a learner whose lexical development was left implicit.

**Course Balance (4/10)** — nine of twelve weeks are grammar-dominant. Weeks 1–7 and 10–11 contain nine consecutive-ish grammar weeks broken only by Weeks 8 and 9.

**Translation-Dependency Reduction (4/10)** — the mechanism inventory across Days 1–84 is: Day 1 (word-order contrast), Day 45 (tense selection), Day 50 (chunks). Three touchpoints in 84 days, then Day 85 is asked to deliver the outcome. Classification: **mostly postponed to the end.**

**Production Readiness (5/10)** — see §12 and the boundary collisions in H12. The generic review days (30, 89) and the pairs 20/60, 27/62, 45/47, 89/90 do not carry enough scope definition for independent lesson generation.

### Audit 6 — Pronunciation strand map

The strand also fails to separate the ladder cleanly: word stress and sentence stress are collapsed into Day 60 while sentence rhythm already occurred at Day 20, and intonation gets a single day with no later application.

### Audit 7 — Listening strand map

Day 27 (contractions) → Day 62 (connected speech) → Day 88 (natural speed). Three days, two long voids, and the critical ordering fault that Day 56's conversation precedes Day 62. ### Audit 14 — YouTube learner experience

**Strong engagement points:** Day 7 (visible win in one week), Day 21 (a full minute of speech), Day 50 (immediately usable phrases — likely the highest-retention video in the course), Day 56 (identity shift to "I can converse"), Days 78–84 (clear real-world payoff), Day 90 (graduation).

**Risk periods where the course will feel like "another grammar class":**
- Days 8–14 — six consecutive noun-phrase grammar lessons with no speaking payoff until Day 14.
- Days 36–41 — six consecutive modal/preposition lessons; the least communicative week in Phase 2.
- Days 64–77 — the 14-day grammar corridor, and the most dangerous drop-off window because learners have just tasted conversation in Weeks 8–9.

**Structural motivation problem:** the sequence Weeks 8–9 (speaking, sounding natural) → Weeks 10–11 (grammar) → Week 12 (communication) makes the course feel like it regresses. Learners who have experienced conversation rarely return willingly to abstract grammar.

## 14. MINIMUM REVISION PLAN

Deliberately minimal. No phase boundary moves, no theme rewrites, no new weeks. Every addition is funded by a slot harvested in §8, so the course stays at 90 days.

### MUST CHANGE (resolves all 🔴)

1. **Own the third verb form.** Fold Day 34's time expressions into Day 33; repurpose Day 34 as V1/V2/V3. Unblocks Days 46 and 74.
2. **Move the Tense Map from Day 45 to Day 49**, swapping with the checkpoint, so the map follows the full tense inventory and stops colliding with Day 47.
3. **Split reported speech across two days** (statements; questions and commands), funded by merging Days 22 and 23.
4. **De-load Days 70–77.** Move phrasal verbs to Week 8 and gerunds/infinitives to Week 6. Convert the two freed Week 11 slots into applied-communication days so Phase 5 genuinely begins around Day 70, not Day 78.
5. **Give hesitation management and Manglish-expression correction their own days in Week 8**, funded by the Day 30 deletion and the Day 59 merge. This also converts Day 87 into real consolidation.
6. **Add two article-and-preposition recycling days**, one around Day 42–45 and one around Day 77, each set inside sentences the learner can already build.
7. **Raise listening to six owning days.** Add one in Week 5, one in Week 8 *before* Day 56, and one in Week 10. Keep Day 88 as the culminating challenge.

### SHOULD CHANGE (resolves most 🟠)

8. **Swap the stress ladder:** word stress to Day 20, sentence stress and rhythm to Day 60.
9. **Add countable/uncountable nouns and quantifiers in Week 2**, funded by merging Days 8 and 9.
10. **Add past perfect in Week 10**, before Day 78, and fold "used to" into the storytelling preparation.
11. **Move frequency adverbs to Week 3** and relocate comparatives out of Week 10 to the Week 2–3 or Week 5 region.
12. **Split Day 12** into existential there is/are (with the ഉണ്ട് "have vs there is" contrast) and place prepositions.
13. **Add question tags and indirect questions in Week 8** — the highest-visibility Malayalam-English question markers.
14. **Delete the Day 30 milestone** and restore the unbroken was/were → past simple sequence.
15. **Add one pronunciation-in-real-speech day in Week 12** so the Day-90 pronunciation outcome has a terminal touchpoint.
16. **Add a prepared 2–3 minute monologue milestone around Day 70** to bridge Day 56 to Day 84.
17. **Resolve the four boundary collisions** (20/60, 27/62, 11/19, 89/90) with explicit scope notes in the master syllabus, since these notes are what a lesson generator will read.

### OPTIONAL

18. Open Day 71 with the zero conditional.
19. Split Week 10's theme in two ("Joining Ideas" / "Describing and Comparing") so the week titles match their content.
20. Add verb-dependent prepositions (listen to, depend on, married to) in Week 6 or Week 10.
21. Add preferences (prefer / would rather) to Week 8.
22. Add scope notes to Days 30, 50, 53, 82, 86, 89 specifying exactly which structures each covers.

**Projected result after MUST + SHOULD:** roughly 78–82 / 100, and status **READY**. MUST alone lifts it to approximately 68–72 and status **READY WITH MINOR CORRECTIONS**.

## 15. RECOMMENDATION

**Do not freeze this syllabus yet.**

The architecture is sound and worth preserving: the phase logic, the checkpoint rhythm, the early pronunciation start, the split preposition treatment, and the restraint on advanced grammar are all correct decisions that a redesign would put at risk.

But three defects make it unsafe as a generation source. The Day 46 participle gap will silently corrupt two later lessons. The three-day listening allocation cannot produce the comprehension outcome the course promises. And the 40% of days with unresolved scope will cause 90 independently generated lessons to overlap and contradict each other.

Apply the seven MUST changes, then re-audit Audits 3, 4, 7, 15, and 20 only. The remaining audits will not be materially affected.
