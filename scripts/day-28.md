## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(High energy. Fast.)*

You're in a meeting. Your manager makes a good point. You want to support him. And you say:

> ❌ *"Yes sir, I am agree with you."*

*(Freeze frame.)* Everyone heard it. Nobody corrected you. And you've been saying it for **ten years**.

The correct sentence is beautifully simple:

> ✅ **"I agree with you."**

Just three words. No *am*. No *are*. No *is*.

*(Point at camera.)*

Why do we add that *am*? Because **agree** *feels* like a feeling, and feelings feel like "I am happy, I am sad, I am agree."

*(Shake head.)* But **agree is a VERB** — an action, like *run* or *eat*. And you don't say ❌ *"I am run"*, do you? 😄

*(Energetic.)*

Today: **25 mistakes** in one class. Each one with its logic. Save this video. Rewatch it. Some of these will shock you. Let's go.

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow down. There's a pattern behind almost all 25.)*

Most of these mistakes come from **three root causes**. Understand the roots, and you fix dozens of sentences at once.

### 🌱 Root 1: Malayalam has no separate "be" verb — so we add it or drop it randomly

In Malayalam, **ആണ്** merges into the sentence, or vanishes:
- ഞാൻ സന്തോഷവാനാണ് → I am happy ✅ *(needs am)*
- ഞാൻ സമ്മതിക്കുന്നു → I agree ✅ *(needs NO am)*

*(The problem.)* Your brain can't tell which English words are **verbs** *(need no "am")* and which are **adjectives** *(need "am")*.

*(The fix — one question.)*
> **Is this word an ACTION or a DESCRIPTION?**
> Action → *I agree, I understand, I know* ✅ **no "am"**
> Description → *I am happy, I am tired, I am ready* ✅ **needs "am"**

*(The list to memorise.)* These are **verbs**, so **NEVER** add am/is/are:

**agree, understand, know, remember, forget, believe, need, want, like, love, hate, prefer, think, feel, hope, mean, belong, own**

- ✅ *I agree.* ✅ *I understand.* ✅ *I know.* ✅ *I remember.* ✅ *I need it.*
- ❌ *I am agree.* ❌ *I am understand.* ❌ *I am know.* ❌ *I am need.*

### 🌱 Root 2: Malayalam case endings become extra English prepositions

**-നെക്കുറിച്ച്, -ലേക്ക്, -ഓട്** → we add *about, to, with* where English wants nothing.

- ❌ *discuss about* → ✅ **discuss**
- ❌ *reach to* → ✅ **reach**

### 🌱 Root 3: Malayalam word order (S-O-V) leaks into English (S-V-O)

- ❌ *Where you are going?* → ✅ **Where are you going?**
- ❌ *I English speak* → ✅ **I speak English.**

*(Point at camera.)* Three roots. Twenty-five mistakes. Let's go through them.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ നിങ്ങളോട് യോജിക്കുന്നു. / എനിക്ക് സമ്മതമാണ്.

*(Notice the second version!)* **സമ്മതമാണ്** — that **ആണ്** is "am/is." So your brain says: "there's an ആണ് here, so I need *am*!" ❌

*(Explain.)* But **സമ്മതമാണ്** is a *noun + is* construction in Malayalam. English uses a **verb** instead. Different grammar, same meaning.

Build it:

1. **Is "agree" a verb or an adjective?** → **VERB** ✅ → no *am*!
2. **Who** → I
3. **Verb** → agree
4. **With whom** → with you

> ✅ **"I agree with you."**

*(Note the preposition!)* **agree WITH a person.** ✅

*(The full preposition family — very useful.)*
| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| agree **with** + person | *I agree with you.* ✅ |
| agree **to** + a plan/proposal | *He agreed to the plan.* ✅ |
| agree **on** + a decision | *We agreed on a date.* ✅ |
| agree **that** + sentence | *I agree that it's difficult.* ✅ |

*(Upgrade — sound professional!)* Instead of always "I agree," try:
- ✅ *I completely agree.*
- ✅ *That's a good point.*
- ✅ *I couldn't agree more.* ← **strong** agreement! ⭐
- ✅ *Exactly!*
- ✅ *You're absolutely right.*

*(And to disagree politely — Day 14!)*
- ✅ *I see your point, but...*
- ✅ *I'm not sure I agree.*
- ✅ *I have a slightly different view.*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** നമുക്ക് ഈ വിഷയത്തെക്കുറിച്ച് ചർച്ച ചെയ്യാം.

*(Find the trap.)* **വിഷയത്തെക്കുറിച്ച്** — that **-കുറിച്ച്** means "about." So your brain adds *about*:

❌ *"Let's discuss about this topic."*

*(Explain the English logic.)* The verb **discuss** already **contains** the meaning "talk about." So *"discuss about"* = *"talk about about."* Repeating yourself! 🔁

Build it:

1. **Let's** → Let's
2. **Verb** → discuss *(no "about"!)*
3. **What** → this topic

> ✅ **"Let's discuss this topic."**

*(Compare — this makes it click.)*
- ✅ *Let's **discuss** this topic.* ✅ *(no preposition)*
- ✅ *Let's **talk about** this topic.* ✅ *(talk needs "about")*

*(Rule.)* **Some verbs swallow the preposition. Some need one.** ✅

### 🚫 The "no preposition" verbs — memorise these six

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| discuss **about** it | **discuss it** |
| reach **to** the station | **reach the station** |
| enter **into** the room | **enter the room** |
| marry **with** her | **marry her** |
| contact **to** him | **contact him** |
| answer **to** the question | **answer the question** |

### ➕ The "needs a preposition" verbs — six more

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| listen the song | **listen to** the song |
| wait me | **wait for** me |
| depend the weather | **depend on** the weather |
| laughing him | **laughing at** him |
| explain me | **explain to** me |
| reply me | **reply to** me |

*(Emphasise "explain to me.")* ❌ *"Please explain me this"* → ✅ **"Please explain this to me."** ✅ Very common error!

*(Stop at #23 — explain it.)* Why **"one of my friends"** with an -s?

Because *"one of..."* means **you're picking one out of a group**. And a group must be **plural**! 👥
- ✅ *one of my **friends*** ✅
- ✅ *one of the **students*** ✅
- ✅ *one of my **best friends*** ✅

*(And the verb stays singular!)*
- ✅ *One of my friends **is** a doctor.* ✅ *(one → is)*

*(Stop at #12 and #13.)* The **he/she/it → S** rule:
- ✅ *He **doesn't** like it.* ✅ *(not "don't")*
- ✅ *She **has** a bike.* ✅ *(not "have")*
- ✅ *It **works** fine.* ✅ *(not "work")*

*(Memory.)* **He, she, it — add the S!** But careful: when *does* or *doesn't* appears, the main verb loses the S:
- ✅ *He **likes** it.* ✅
- ✅ *He **doesn't like** it.* ✅ *(not "doesn't likes")*

*(Connect to Day 03.)* The boss rule again! **The helper takes the S, the worker stays plain.** 🔗

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### The deepest trap: adjective vs verb confusion

*(This is #1 on the list, and it deserves the spotlight.)*

Let's build a clean two-column list you can memorise. This single table fixes dozens of sentences:

| ✅ NEEDS "am/is/are" *(descriptions)* | ✅ NO "am/is/are" *(verbs)* |
|---|---|
| I **am** happy | I agree |
| I **am** tired | I understand |
| I **am** ready | I know |
| I **am** sure | I remember |
| I **am** busy | I forget |
| I **am** afraid | I need it |
| I **am** interested | I want it |
| I **am** worried | I like it |
| I **am** hungry | I think so |
| I **am** sorry | I hope so |

*(The test.)* Ask: **"Is this word describing me, or is it something I DO?"**
- Describing → **am** ✅
- Doing → **no am** ✅

*(Practise — say these ten with me!)*
1. I agree. ✅
2. I am ready. ✅
3. I understand. ✅
4. I am busy. ✅
5. I know. ✅
6. I am tired. ✅
7. I need help. ✅
8. I am interested. ✅
9. I remember. ✅
10. I am sorry. ✅

*(Now the tricky pairs — these confuse everyone!)*

| ❌ | ✅ | Note |
|---|---|---|
| I am agree | **I agree** | verb |
| I interested | **I am interested** | adjective! needs *am* |
| I am know | **I know** | verb |
| I ready | **I am ready** | adjective |
| I am understand | **I understand** | verb |
| I sure | **I am sure** | adjective |

*(Point out.)* See? **Both directions of error happen!** Sometimes we add *am* where it's not needed, and sometimes we drop it where it IS needed. Both come from the same Malayalam root: **ആണ് is invisible.**

### The second deep trap: Indian English phrases nobody outside India uses

*(Fun but important — these appear in the 25.)*

| ⚠️ Indian English | ✅ International English |
|---|---|
| do the needful | **please take care of it** |
| revert back to me | **get back to me** |
| out of station | **out of town** |
| pass out *(from college)* | **graduate** |
| prepone | **move earlier** / **reschedule earlier** |
| cousin brother/sister | **cousin** |
| real brother | **brother** |
| co-brother | **my wife's sister's husband** 😄 |
| batchmate | **classmate** |
| convent-educated | *(just don't)* |
| tiffin | **snack** / **lunch** |
| hotel *(for a restaurant)* | **restaurant** |
| mention not | **you're welcome** |
| what is your good name | **may I know your name?** |

*(Important note — be fair here.)* These phrases are **completely fine** when you're speaking with other Indians! Nothing wrong with them culturally.

*(But.)* If you're in an **international interview**, an **IELTS speaking test**, or talking to a **foreign client** — they will not understand. So learn both versions and **switch based on your audience.** ✅

*(That's real fluency.)* Knowing **who you're talking to**. 🎯
