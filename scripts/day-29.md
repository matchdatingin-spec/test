## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Explain what an idiom actually is.)*

### What is an idiom?

An **idiom** is a phrase where the **words don't mean what they say**.

- **തല തിന്നുക** — nobody is actually eating a head 🧠
- **കണ്ണിൽ എണ്ണയിടുക** — nobody has oil in their eyes 👁️

The **meaning** is agreed by the **culture**, not by the words. That's why you can't translate them.

### The 3-step rule for handling idioms

*(This is the actual skill. Teach the process, not just the list.)*

When you want to say a Malayalam idiom in English, do **NOT** translate the words. Instead:

**Step 1: Find the FEELING.**
തല തിന്നുക → the feeling is *"you are irritating me by talking too much."* 😤

**Step 2: Say the FEELING in simple English.**
→ *"Stop annoying me."* ✅ *"You're irritating me."* ✅

**Step 3: (Optional) Learn the English idiom for the same feeling.**
→ *"Stop bugging me."* ✅ *"Don't drive me crazy."* ✅ *"You're driving me nuts."* ✅

*(Emphasise Step 2!)* **Step 2 is enough.** You don't need fancy idioms to be fluent. **Simple, clear English is fluent English.** ✅

*(Slogan — say it three times.)*
> **Don't translate the words. Translate the FEELING.**

*(Reassure them.)* And listen — even if you never learn a single English idiom, your English can be excellent. Idioms are the **decoration**, not the **building**. 🏠

But **understanding** them matters, because native speakers use them constantly. So today you get both: what to say, and what to understand.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എന്റെ തല തിന്നരുത്!

**Step 1 — What's the feeling?** 🤔
Someone is talking too much / bothering me / not leaving me alone. I'm irritated. 😤

**Step 2 — Say it in simple English:**
- ✅ *"Stop bothering me."*
- ✅ *"Please leave me alone."*
- ✅ *"You're annoying me."*

**Step 3 — The natural English idioms:**
- ✅ *"Stop bugging me."* 🐛 ← very natural!
- ✅ *"You're driving me crazy."* / *"...driving me nuts."*
- ✅ *"Give me a break!"* ← "stop it, I need peace"
- ✅ *"Get off my back."* ← stop pressuring me

*(Pick one and drill it.)* **"Stop bugging me."** Say it three times. It's the closest natural match. ✅

*(Softer, polite versions — for work!)*
- ✅ *"Could you give me a few minutes?"*
- ✅ *"I need some space to focus."*
- ✅ *"Can we talk about this later?"*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ കണ്ണിൽ എണ്ണയിട്ട് കാത്തിരിക്കുന്നു.

**Step 1 — What's the feeling?** 🤔
I have been waiting **very intensely**, without sleeping, watching carefully, for a long time. ⏳

**Step 2 — Simple English:**
- ✅ *"I've been waiting eagerly."*
- ✅ *"I've been waiting a long time."*
- ✅ *"I've been waiting all night."*

*(Note the grammar!)* **"I've been waiting"** — remember Day 27? Started in the past, still going ✋. Perfect use! ✅

**Step 3 — Natural English idioms:**
- ✅ *"I've been waiting with bated breath."* ← formal/literary 📖
- ✅ *"I've been on the edge of my seat."* ← excited waiting 🪑
- ✅ *"I've been counting the minutes."* ⏰
- ✅ *"I couldn't wait!"* ← simple and perfect ✅

*(Best practical choice.)* **"I've been eagerly waiting for this."** ✅ Clear, natural, professional. Use it in emails! 📧

*(Stop at #16 — fun moment!)* **മുതലക്കണ്ണീർ = crocodile tears** — identical in both languages! 🐊 Because both came from the same old story about crocodiles crying while eating. Languages share more than you think! 😊

*(Stop at #7 and #10.)* Also matches! **"Wash my hands of it"** and **"give it a try"** exist in English with the same meaning. ✅

*(Stop at #18 and #19 — these are the practical ones!)*

**#18 — "ഞാൻ ഇപ്പോൾ വരാം"**
❌ *"I will come now."* → sounds like you're arriving, not leaving! 😄
✅ **"I'll be right back."** ⭐ Learn this exactly. It's the phrase for stepping away briefly.

**#19 — "ചോറ് കഴിച്ചോ?"**
❌ *"Did you eat rice?"* → a strange question about a specific grain 🍚
✅ **"Have you eaten?"** ⭐ or **"Did you have lunch?"** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* These two — #18 and #19 — you will use **every single day**. Practise them now. 🗣️

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: Body-part idioms

*(A pattern discovery!)* Notice something about the list — Malayalam idioms **love body parts**: തല, കണ്ണ്, ചെവി, വയറ്, കൈ, കാല്, ചങ്ക്, മുഖം.

Malayalam builds emotion through the **body**. It's a beautifully physical language. 💪

English builds emotion through **objects and actions** instead: *break the ice, spill the beans, hit the road, kick the bucket.*

*(Practical advice.)* So whenever your Malayalam idiom has a **body part** in it — **stop.** Do not translate. Find the feeling instead. ✋

*(Fun exception.)* Some English idioms DO use body parts, and they're worth learning:
- ✅ *give me a hand* = help me 🤝
- ✅ *keep an eye on it* = watch it 👁️
- ✅ *my heart sank* = I felt sad 💔
- ✅ *cost an arm and a leg* = very expensive 💰
- ✅ *pull someone's leg* = joke with them 🦵
- ✅ *face the music* = accept the consequences 🎵

*(Say each one with me!)*

### Trap 2: The "coming/going" confusion

*(A structural leak, not just idioms.)*

In Malayalam, **feelings and events COME to you**. They arrive like visitors:

| Malayalam | ⚠️ Literal | ✅ English |
|---|---|---|
| ഉറക്കം വരുന്നു | Sleep is coming | **I'm sleepy.** |
| ദേഷ്യം വരുന്നു | Anger is coming | **I'm getting angry.** |
| ചിരി വരുന്നു | Laughter is coming | **I can't stop laughing.** |
| കരച്ചിൽ വരുന്നു | Crying is coming | **I feel like crying.** |
| മഴ വരുന്നു | Rain is coming | **It's going to rain.** ✅ |
| പനി വന്നു | Fever came | **I got a fever.** / **I came down with a fever.** |
| ഓർമ്മ വരുന്നില്ല | Memory is not coming | **I can't remember.** |
| ഉത്തരം വരുന്നില്ല | Answer is not coming | **I don't know the answer.** |

*(The core insight — say it strongly.)*

> **Malayalam: the feeling comes TO you. English: YOU BECOME the feeling.**

*(Connect to Day 01.)* This is the same logic from our very first class! 🔗 One rule, dozens of sentences fixed. That's how you learn efficiently.

### Trap 3: "-ഇല്ല" phrases

Malayalam expresses "cannot / don't have" through negative endings, and we translate them oddly:

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| സമയമില്ല | Time is not there | **I don't have time.** |
| പറ്റില്ല | Not possible | **I can't.** / **It won't work.** |
| അറിയില്ല | Not knowing | **I don't know.** |
| മനസ്സിലായില്ല | Didn't come to mind | **I didn't understand.** |
| ഇഷ്ടമല്ല | Not liking | **I don't like it.** |
| വിശ്വാസമില്ല | Belief is not there | **I don't believe it.** / **I don't trust him.** |

*(Rule.)* **English says "I don't ___" — with a subject and a verb.** Never a floating negative. ✅

*(Drill — say these five with me!)*
1. I don't have time. ✅
2. I don't know. ✅
3. I didn't understand. ✅
4. I can't. ✅
5. I don't think so. ✅
