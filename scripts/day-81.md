# Day 81 — Strengths, Weaknesses, and Salary Expectations: Smart Interview Answers

**Target length:** 16–18 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** The three questions where Malayali candidates lose the most — including the one that costs you actual money.

**📌 Format note:** Fourth video of the **career block**. Section 3 takes the three questions in depth, with the salary conversation getting the most time. Section 4 addresses the cultural discomfort around discussing money.

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Direct, slightly serious — money is the real subject here.)*

The interviewer asks:

> *"What are your salary expectations?"* 💰

And you say:

> ❌ *"Sir, as per company norms."*

*(Pause. Look at camera.)*

*(Explain quietly.)* Do you know what just happened? ✅

You handed them the **entire** decision. ✅ And "company norms" means whatever number they were **already** planning to offer — which is usually the **lowest** they can justify. ⚠️

*(Emphasise.)* That one sentence has probably cost Malayali professionals **crores** collectively. 💸

*(Lean in.)*

*(Explain.)* And it's not a language problem. You **know** how to say a number. ✅ The problem is that **asking for money feels wrong** — culturally, personally, deeply. ✅

*(Confident, warm.)*

Today: strengths, weaknesses, and — most importantly — **salary**. With the exact sentences, and permission to use them. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 5:00)

*(Set up all three, with the money framing done properly.)*

### 🎯 Why these three questions are different

*(Explain.)*

*(Explain.)* Most interview questions ask about your **past** — what you did, what you handled. ✅ Those are **factual**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* These three ask you to **judge yourself**: ✅

| Question | What it demands |
|---|---|
| **Strength** 💪 | claim you're good at something ✅ |
| **Weakness** 🤔 | admit a flaw without damaging yourself ✅ |
| **Salary** 💰 | put a **price** on your own work ✅ |

*(Firmly.)* And all three are uncomfortable for exactly the same reason — **വിനയം**. ✅ 🔗 *(Days 78, 79)*

*(The reframe that matters.)*

> ### 👉 These aren't questions about your **character**. They're questions about **information**. ✅

*(Explain.)* The interviewer needs data to make a decision. ✅ Withholding it doesn't make you humble — it makes you **hard to evaluate**. ⭐

### 💰 The salary principle — the most important idea today

*(Spend real time here.)*

*(Explain.)* Understand what a salary actually is: ✅

> ### 👉 A salary is not a **reward** for being good. It's the **price** of solving a problem. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* The company has a problem — accounts aren't reconciled, tickets aren't closed, code isn't written. ✅ They've already **budgeted** money to solve it. ✅

*(Point out.)* So the question isn't *"am I worth this much?"* 🤔 It's *"what does this problem cost to solve in the market?"* ✅

*(Firmly.)* That's a **market question**, not a **moral** one. ⭐

*(The three numbers you need before any interview.)* 📊

| Number | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Current** 💼 | your existing CTC — exact ✅ |
| **Target** 🎯 | what you want — based on market research ⭐ |
| **Walk-away** 🚪 | the number below which you'd say no ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Know all three before you enter the room.** ✅ Deciding live, under pressure, always costs you money. ⚠️

*(How to find the market rate.)* 🔍
- ✅ Job portals — filter by role, experience, city ✅
- ✅ Ask **two** people in similar roles *(not at your own company)* ⭐
- ✅ Recruiters — they'll usually tell you the band ✅
- ✅ Glassdoor / AmbitionBox for company-specific data ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **A salary is a price, not a compliment.** 💰
> **ശമ്പളം ഒരു വിലയാണ് — പ്രശംസ അല്ല.** ✅

## 🔍 3. THE THREE QUESTIONS (5:00 – 14:30)

---

## 💪 QUESTION 1 — "What is your greatest strength?"

### What they're testing
Can you do the job — and do you **know** what you're good at? ✅

### The shape
> ⭐ **One** strength + **evidence with a number** + **relevance to this job** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **One.** ✅ Not a list. ✅ A list of five strengths proves nothing about any of them. ✅

### Model answers

**Accounts:**
> ✅ *"I'd say accuracy under time pressure. Month-end for me is a two-day window across about 200 accounts, and in two years I haven't had a reconciliation query come back from audit. Colleagues send me their statements to check before they submit. I noticed this role has a monthly reporting deadline, so that habit would transfer directly."* ⭐

**IT Support:**
> ✅ *"Probably pattern-spotting. Instead of just closing tickets, I look for the ones that keep coming back. I found a recurring VPN issue that was generating around 30 tickets a month, traced the cause, and got it down to about three. My manager now asks me to look at repeat categories."* ⭐

**Fresher:**
> ✅ *"I'd say I'm genuinely good at teaching myself things. For my final-year project I needed React and I'd never used it — I gave myself three weeks, followed a course, and built a working app. I know I'd need training here, but I don't think I'd need it twice."* ⭐

### The strength menu 📋

*(Practical — pick one that fits your real work.)*

| Strength | Best for |
|---|---|
| **accuracy / attention to detail** | accounts, data, QA ✅ |
| **staying calm under pressure** | support, sales, healthcare ✅ |
| **learning fast** ⭐ | freshers, career changers ✅ |
| **spotting patterns / root causes** | technical, analytical ⭐ |
| **explaining things clearly** | training, client-facing ✅ |
| **following through** | project work, coordination ⭐ |
| **being reliable** 🔗 | any role ✅ |

*(⚠️ Avoid the empty four.)* ❌ *hard-working · sincere · punctual · honest* ✅ — every candidate says all four, so they carry **zero** information. ✅

---

## 🤔 QUESTION 2 — "What is your greatest weakness?"

### What they're testing
Self-awareness. ✅ Can you look at yourself honestly **and act on it**? ✅

*(Explain.)* They are **not** hunting for a reason to reject you. ✅ They're checking whether you're someone who can **be given feedback**. ⭐

### The shape
> ⭐ **Real** weakness + **what you're doing** about it + **evidence of progress** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* All three parts. ✅ The third part is what most people miss. ✅

### Model answers

**Delegation:**
> ✅ *"Delegation, honestly. I tend to do things myself because it feels faster — which was fine alone, but became a problem when my team grew to four. What I've been doing is handing over one recurring task a month and letting people learn it, even when it slows things down initially. Two of my juniors now handle the weekly reports independently, which they couldn't six months ago."* ⭐

**Speaking up in meetings:**
> ✅ *"I used to stay quiet in larger meetings — I'd have a point but wait for the right moment and then it had passed. What I started doing is writing my point down as soon as I think of it, and making myself say it within the next two minutes. It's not fully natural yet, but I contributed in every review meeting last quarter, which I definitely wasn't doing before."* ⭐

**Saying yes to too much:**
> ✅ *"I've had a habit of accepting every request because saying no felt uncomfortable — and then quality suffered on my main work. I've started asking about the deadline before agreeing, and going back with a realistic timeline instead of just saying yes. It's made me more reliable, even if it means I take on slightly less."* ⭐

### 🟢 The safe zone vs 🔴 the danger zone

| ✅ Safe *(skills and habits)* | ❌ Dangerous *(character and attitude)* |
|---|---|
| delegation ✅ | I get angry ⚠️ |
| public speaking ✅ | I'm lazy ⚠️ |
| saying no ✅ | I don't like being told what to do ⚠️ |
| over-checking work ✅ | I have no weaknesses ⚠️ |
| asking for help too late ✅ | I'm a perfectionist ⚠️ *(overused!)* |
| impatience with slow processes ✅ | I get bored easily ⚠️ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Pick something about how you work, never about who you are.** ✅

*(The one-line test.)* Would a manager think *"that's fixable"*? ✅ Then it's safe. ✅ Would they think *"that's a problem to manage"*? ⚠️ Then don't. ✅

## 💰 QUESTION 3 — "What are your salary expectations?"

*(The most valuable section of the video. Take the time.)*

### What they're testing
Do you know your **market value** and can you have a **professional** conversation about money? ✅

### The shape
> ⭐ **Current figure** + **a range with reasoning** + **openness to discussion** ✅

### ✅ The core model answer

> ✅ *"My current CTC is ₹6.5 lakhs. Based on the responsibilities in this role — particularly the reporting piece, which is beyond what I handle now — I'd be looking at somewhere between ₹8 and ₹9 lakhs. That said, I'm open to discussing it based on the overall package."* ⭐

*(Break it down.)*
| Piece | Why it works |
|---|---|
| *My current CTC is ₹6.5L* | factual, confident, no hedging ✅ |
| *Based on the responsibilities* | you're pricing **the role**, not yourself ⭐ |
| *between ₹8 and ₹9L* | a **range** leaves room to negotiate ✅ |
| *open to discussing* | collaborative, not rigid ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice — **"based on the responsibilities in this role."** ✅ That phrase moves the conversation from *"what do I deserve"* to *"what does this job cost."* ⭐

### 📋 The four common salary situations

#### Situation 1: They ask before you know the role well ⚠️

> ✅ *"I'd rather understand the role a bit better first, if that's okay — could you tell me the band you have in mind for this position?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Turning it back once is completely acceptable** ✅ and often reveals their budget. ✅

*(But.)* If they insist, **give a number**. ✅ Don't dodge twice — that looks evasive. ⚠️

#### Situation 2: You're a fresher with no current salary 🎓

> ✅ *"As this would be my first role, I've looked at what similar positions in Kochi are offering, and it seems to be around ₹3 to ₹4 lakhs. I'd be comfortable in that range, and honestly the learning matters more to me at this stage than the exact figure."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Still give a range.** ✅ ❌ *"Anything you give, sir"* ✅ costs freshers real money. ⚠️

#### Situation 3: They offer less than you asked 😐

*(This is where most people accept immediately. Don't.)*

> ✅ *"Thank you — I'm genuinely interested in the role. The figure is a bit below what I was expecting. Is there any flexibility, or could we look at the variable component?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice — **no anger, no drama.** ✅ Just a calm question. ✅

*(The three levers if base salary is fixed.)* 💡
- ✅ *"Could we revisit it at a six-month review?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Is there a joining bonus possible?"* ✅
- ✅ *"What about the variable or performance component?"* ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Asking once is normal and expected.** ✅ No professional recruiter is offended by a polite question about money. ⭐

#### Situation 4: They ask for your current salary slip 📄

> ✅ *"Sure, I can share that. I'd just mention that my current figure reflects a role with narrower scope than this one, so I've based my expectation on the market rate for this level of responsibility."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ This prevents them from simply applying a **10% hike** to a low base. ✅

### 🎯 The one sentence to memorise

*(Give them the essential line.)*

> ### ⭐ **"Based on the responsibilities in this role, I'd be looking at ₹X to ₹Y."**

*(Firmly.)* Say it five times, with **your** real numbers. ✅ 🗣️

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (14:30 – 17:00)

### Trap 1: "As per company norms" 💸

*(The single costliest sentence — address it directly.)*

*(Explain warmly.)* Why do we say it? Three reasons, all genuine: ✅
1. Asking for money feels **greedy** 😔
2. We worry a high number will **lose** us the job ⚠️
3. Nobody ever **taught** us the sentences ✅

*(Address each.)*

**On greed:** ✅ Stating a market rate is **information**, not greed. ✅ The recruiter states a number too — nobody calls **them** greedy. ⭐

**On losing the job:** ✅ A **reasonable** number never loses you an offer. ✅ If your figure is too high, they'll **counter** — they won't silently reject you. ✅ *(And if a company rejects you for politely naming a market rate, that tells you something useful about them.)* ⚠️

**On not knowing the words:** ✅ You have them now. ⭐

*(The firm instruction.)*

> ### 👉 Never say *"as per company norms."* ✅ Always give a **range**. ⭐

### Trap 2: Accepting the first offer immediately ⚠️

*(A specific and expensive habit.)*

*(Explain.)* When the offer comes, the instinct is relief — *"they said yes!"* — and we accept in the same breath. ✅

*(Firmly.)* **Don't answer immediately.** ✅

*(The professional response.)*
> ✅ *"Thank you, that's great news. Could I have a day to look at the full package and come back to you?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Completely standard.** ✅ Every recruiter expects it. ✅ It costs you nothing and it gives you space to negotiate. ⭐

*(And what to check in that day.)* 💰
- Base vs variable split ✅
- PF, gratuity, insurance ✅
- Notice period and bond *(if any)* ⚠️
- Actual **in-hand** monthly figure ⭐

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **CTC is not take-home.** ✅ Always ask for the **in-hand** number. ✅

### Trap 3: Apologising while negotiating 🙏

*(A very Malayali pattern.)*

| ❌ Apologetic | ✅ Professional |
|---|---|
| *Sorry sir, actually if possible, maybe slightly more...* | **Is there any flexibility on the figure?** ⭐ |
| *I know I'm asking too much but...* | **Based on my experience, I was expecting closer to ₹X.** ⭐ |
| *Whatever you feel is fine, sir* | **I'd be comfortable at ₹X.** ✅ |
| *I don't want to trouble you* | **Could we look at the variable component?** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **One short, calm question.** ✅ No apology. ✅ Apologising signals that you think your request is unreasonable — which invites them to agree. ⚠️

*(Practise.)* **"Is there any flexibility on that?"** ✅ Five times. Neutral tone. 🗣️

### Trap 4: Not knowing the market rate 📊

*(A preparation gap.)*

*(Explain.)* Many candidates genuinely don't know what their work is worth — because salaries aren't discussed openly in Kerala. ✅ It can feel improper to ask. ✅

*(The practical fix.)*

> ### 👉 Before **any** interview, find **three** data points for the role. ✅

*(How to ask a peer without awkwardness.)* ⭐
> ✅ *"I'm exploring a move and I'm trying to work out the right range. Would you mind telling me roughly what band someone at my level gets in your company?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Ask about the **band**, not their personal salary. ✅ Much easier for them to answer. ✅

### Trap 5: The strength/weakness mismatch ⚠️

*(A subtle interview mistake.)*

*(Explain.)* Don't let your weakness **contradict** the job's core requirement: ✅

| Job | ❌ Bad weakness choice |
|---|---|
| Client-facing role | *"I'm not comfortable talking to people"* ⚠️ |
| Deadline-heavy role | *"I struggle with time management"* ⚠️ |
| Detail work *(accounts)* | *"I miss small details"* ⚠️ |
| Team lead role | *"I don't like managing people"* ⚠️ |

*(The rule.)* ⭐ **Pick a weakness that is NOT the main skill of the job.** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Read the job description, identify the **core** requirement, and choose a weakness from a **different** area. ✅

### 💙 And a word about money and worth

*(Close the section sincerely — this matters.)*

*(Slowly, warmly.)* I want to say something about why this is hard. ✅

*(Explain.)* Many of us were raised to believe that **good work speaks for itself**, and that asking for money is undignified. ✅ There's real dignity in that value. ✅

*(Firmly.)* But in a professional market, it doesn't work that way. ✅ **Salaries are not awarded for merit — they're negotiated.** ✅ And the person who names a number gets more than the person who waits to be recognised. ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* Two people, same work, same skill. ✅ One says *"as per company norms."* One says *"₹8 to ₹9 lakhs based on the responsibilities."* ✅

*(Slowly.)* Over a **career**, that difference compounds into a house. 🏠

*(Warm, firm.)* So please — know your number. Say your number. Calmly, once, without apology. ✅ That's not greed. That's **basic professional practice**. ⭐
