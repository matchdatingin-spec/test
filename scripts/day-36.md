# Day 36 — Speak for 2 Minutes on Any Topic with This Easy Formula

**Target length:** 14–16 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** A 5-block formula — **P-E-R-F-O** — that fills two minutes on any topic, even one you've never thought about.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Relatable, slightly painful.)*

Someone says: *"Tell me about your hometown."*

And you say:

> *"My hometown is Thrissur. It is a beautiful place. There is a temple. People are good."*

*(Pause. Look at watch.)* **Eleven seconds.** 😅

And then — silence. Your brain is completely empty. You **know** English. You have plenty to say about Thrissur in Malayalam. But in English? Four sentences and the tank is dry. ⛽

*(Point at camera.)*

Here's what's actually happening. Your problem is **not** vocabulary. Your problem is **not** grammar.

Your problem is: **you don't know what to say NEXT.** 🤷

*(Lean in, confident.)*

Fluent speakers aren't inventing content on the spot. They're following **an invisible structure**. And once you know that structure, two minutes becomes easy — even about a topic you've never thought about before.

Today I give you that structure. It has five blocks. Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:30)

*(Slow, clear. This formula is the whole video.)*

### 🔑 THE FORMULA: P–E–R–F–O

*(Write it big. Explain each letter.)*

> **P** = **Point** — what it is *(1 sentence)*
> **E** = **Example** — a specific detail *(2–3 sentences)*
> **R** = **Reason** — why it matters to you *(2 sentences)*
> **F** = **Feeling** — your emotion about it *(1–2 sentences)*
> **O** = **Opinion / Outlook** — general view or future *(1–2 sentences)*

*(Do the maths with them.)* Five blocks × about 20 seconds each = **100 seconds.** Add a little pausing and you're at **two minutes**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* You don't need more English. You need **five blocks instead of one**. 🧱

### Why this works — the real insight

*(This is the deepest point of the video.)*

Most learners answer with only **P**. They state the fact and stop:

> *"My hometown is Thrissur."* → **P only.** Done in 3 seconds. ❌

*(Point out.)* But **P** is the least interesting block! Nobody wants the fact — they want the **story**.

*(Explain.)* **E, R, F** are where the content lives. Example, Reason, Feeling. Those three blocks are made of things you **already know** — your own life, your own opinions. No new English needed. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Don't say more words. Say more BLOCKS.**
> **കൂടുതൽ വാക്കുകൾ വേണ്ട — കൂടുതൽ ഭാഗങ്ങൾ വേണം.**

### The magic connectors for each block

*(Very practical — give them the exact phrases.)*

Each block has a **starter phrase**. Learn these five, and the blocks come automatically:

| Block | Starter phrase |
|---|---|
| **P** | *"Let me tell you about..."* / *"So, ..."* |
| **E** | *"For example..."* / *"To give you an idea..."* ⭐ |
| **R** | *"The reason is..."* / *"That's mainly because..."* ⭐ |
| **F** | *"Honestly, I feel..."* / *"What I love about it is..."* ⭐ |
| **O** | *"Overall..."* / *"So in general, I'd say..."* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* **These five phrases are your engine.** When your mind goes blank, say the next starter phrase out loud — and your brain will **automatically** fill the rest. That's how the brain works. Start the sentence, and it finishes it. 🧠

*(Try it.)* Say *"The reason is..."* out loud right now. Feel how your mind immediately starts producing a reason? ✅ **That's the trick.**

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:30 – 11:00)

### Example 1 — "Tell me about your hometown" *(built block by block)*

*(Build it slowly on screen, labelling each block.)*

---

**🅿️ P — Point** *(what it is, one sentence)*

> ✅ *"Let me tell you about my hometown. I'm from Thrissur, which is a city in central Kerala."*

*(Note.)* **"which is..."** ⭐ — such a useful little addition! It turns a bare fact into information. *(We'll master this on Day 58.)*

---

**🅴 E — Example** *(a specific detail — this is where most people stop too early!)*

> ✅ *"To give you an idea, Thrissur is famous for a festival called Thrissur Pooram. Every year, thousands of people come to see the elephants and the drums. The whole city stays awake all night."*

*(Point out the technique.)* Notice — I gave a **specific name** *(Thrissur Pooram)* and a **specific image** *(elephants, drums, awake all night)*. ✅

*(Rule.)* **Specific beats general, always.**
- ❌ *"It is a beautiful place."* ← says nothing
- ✅ *"There's a huge temple right in the centre of the city."* ← says something ✅

---

**🆁 R — Reason** *(why it matters to you)*

> ✅ *"The reason I like it is that it's not too big and not too small. I can get anywhere in fifteen minutes, but I still have everything I need."*

*(Note.)* **"The reason I like it is that..."** ⭐ Memorise this exact frame. It works for any topic. ✅

---

**🅵 F — Feeling** *(your emotion)*

> ✅ *"Honestly, whenever I come back from travelling, I feel relaxed the moment I reach Thrissur. It just feels like home."*

*(Emphasise.)* Feelings make people **listen**. Facts make people wait. In an interview or IELTS test, the **F block** is what makes you memorable. ⭐

---

**🅾️ O — Opinion / Outlook** *(general view or future)*

> ✅ *"Overall, I'd say it's one of the best cities in Kerala to live in. And I'd definitely like to settle there after a few years."*

---

**Now the whole thing together:**

> ✅ *"Let me tell you about my hometown. I'm from Thrissur, which is a city in central Kerala. To give you an idea, Thrissur is famous for a festival called Thrissur Pooram — every year, thousands of people come to see the elephants and the drums, and the whole city stays awake all night. The reason I like it is that it's not too big and not too small; I can get anywhere in fifteen minutes but still have everything I need. Honestly, whenever I come back from travelling, I feel relaxed the moment I reach Thrissur — it just feels like home. Overall, I'd say it's one of the best cities in Kerala to live in, and I'd like to settle there after a few years."*

*(Check the clock.)* **That's about 75 seconds of speaking — and roughly two minutes with natural pauses.** ✅

*(Point out.)* And look at the English used. Any **hard** words? Any **advanced** grammar? **No.** Simple English, five blocks. ✅

---

### Example 2 — "Talk about your favourite movie" *(faster, let them follow)*

*(Do it quickly — show the formula transferring to a new topic.)*

| Block | ✅ What to say |
|---|---|
| **P** | *"My favourite movie is Drishyam. It's a Malayalam thriller from 2013."* |
| **E** | *"For example, the whole story is about a normal family man who has to protect his family after an accident. What's clever is that he uses movies to plan everything."* |
| **R** | *"The reason I love it is the writing. Every small detail in the first half becomes important in the second half."* |
| **F** | *"Honestly, the first time I watched it, I couldn't move from my seat. And even after five or six times, I still enjoy it."* |
| **O** | *"Overall, I think it's one of the best Indian thrillers ever made. If you haven't seen it, you should definitely watch it."* |

*(Point out.)* Same five blocks. Completely different topic. **The formula didn't change.** ✅

---

### Now — the formula on a topic you've never thought about 🎲

*(This is the real proof. Do it live.)*

*(Challenge tone.)* Let's try something random. Something you've genuinely never prepared:

> **"Talk about your favourite colour."** 🎨

*(Laugh.)* Ridiculous topic, right? Who has thoughts about a colour? But watch:

| Block | ✅ |
|---|---|
| **P** | *"My favourite colour is blue."* |
| **E** | *"For example, most of my shirts are blue, and even my phone case is blue. I didn't plan it — it just happened."* |
| **R** | *"The reason is probably that blue feels calm to me. When I look at the sky or the sea, I feel peaceful."* |
| **F** | *"Honestly, I feel more confident when I wear blue. I don't know why, but it just suits me better than bright colours."* |
| **O** | *"Overall, I'd say colours affect our mood more than we think. Blue works for me, but I know some people prefer brighter colours."* |

*(Triumphant.)* **Ninety seconds. About a colour.** 🎨

*(Point at camera.)* And notice something important — I said *"I don't know why"* and *"probably."* You are **allowed** to be uncertain! You don't need brilliant answers. You need **structure**. ✅

---

### The 5 emergency phrases when a block runs dry 🆘

*(Extremely practical.)*

Sometimes a block empties before you're done. Use these to buy time and keep talking:

| Phrase | When to use |
|---|---|
| **"Let me think for a second..."** ⭐ | buys 3 seconds legally |
| **"Actually, there's one more thing..."** | restarts a block |
| **"I'm not sure how to explain this, but..."** | honest and natural |
| **"It's hard to describe, but basically..."** | for complex ideas |
| **"How can I put this..."** | very natural filler |

*(Emphasise.)* These are **not** mistakes. Native speakers use them constantly! Silence sounds like failure. **Thinking out loud sounds like fluency.** ✅

*(Warning about the wrong fillers.)* ❌ *"Um... um... uh..."* sounds nervous. ❌ *"Actually actually..."* sounds like a tic. ✅ Use full **phrases** instead — they sound intentional. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:30)

### Trap 1: Answering with a FACT and stopping

*(The core cultural difference — explain this carefully.)*

*(Insight.)* In Malayalam conversation, giving a **short, direct answer** is polite. Talking a lot about yourself can feel like showing off. **വിനയം** — modesty — is a value. 🙏

So when someone asks *"Tell me about your hometown,"* your instinct says: *give the fact, don't over-talk.* ✅ In Malayalam, that's **good manners.**

*(But.)* In English — especially in **interviews** and **IELTS** — a short answer is read as *"this person has nothing to say"* or *"this person can't speak English."* ❌

*(Key message — say it warmly but firmly.)*

> ### 👉 In English, talking more is not showing off. It's cooperating.

*(Explain.)* When someone asks you an open question in English, they are **giving you the floor**. Staying silent doesn't look humble — it looks like you **refused the invitation**. 🎁

*(Permission.)* So give yourself permission. **Two minutes about yourself is not arrogance in English. It's the expected answer.** ✅

### Trap 2: Translating a long Malayalam speech in your head

*(The freeze mechanism.)*

Here's what happens. You think of a beautiful, complex Malayalam sentence — then try to translate it perfectly. And you freeze mid-sentence. 🧊

*(The fix — connect to Day 32.)* **Think in blocks, not in translations.** 🔗

- ❌ *Think a big Malayalam sentence → translate it → speak*
- ✅ *Think one small idea → say one short English sentence → next idea* ✅

*(Rule.)* **One idea = one short sentence.** Then the next. Five short sentences per block beats one perfect long one. ✅

### Trap 3: Trying to be perfect

*(Genuinely important — say it kindly.)*

*(Observation.)* Many learners stop speaking mid-sentence to correct a small error:

> *"I go— I went— I have gone to Bangalore last— no, in 2019..."* 😰

*(Firmly.)* **Stop doing that.** A small tense slip costs you very little. Repeated **stopping** costs you a lot. 🛑

*(The rule that matters.)*

> ### 👉 While you're speaking, FLOW comes first.

*(Explain.)* A listener is mostly following your **meaning** — not auditing whether you said *"since"* instead of *"for."* Say it slightly wrong and **keep going**. Fix it later, in your practice — not live. ✅

*(One important limit.)* ⚠️ If the error changes your **meaning** — a wrong number, a missing *not*, the wrong name — **do** correct it immediately. ✅ And in **exams**, accuracy is directly marked, so review your recordings afterwards. ⭐

*(Note.)* And if you truly must correct yourself, do it the **native** way:
- ✅ *"I went there in 2019 — **sorry, 2020**."* ✅ *(smooth, confident)*
- ❌ *"I went— I— sorry— in 2019— no—"* ❌ *(panicked)*

### Trap 4: The "I don't have anything to say" belief

*(Address the mindset — this genuinely blocks people.)*

*(Warmly.)* Some of you are thinking: *"But my life is not interesting. My hometown has nothing special."*

*(Firmly.)* **Wrong.** And here's the proof — we just did **ninety seconds about a colour**. 🎨

*(Explain the truth.)* Interesting is not in the topic. Interesting is in the **detail** and the **feeling**. ✅

- ❌ *"My village is small."* ← boring
- ✅ *"My village has one tea shop, and every evening the same eight men sit there and argue about football."* ← **fascinating** ⭐

*(Point at camera.)* Same village. The second one just used **E** and **F**. ✅

*(Advice.)* When you feel empty, ask yourself these three questions — they always produce content:
1. **"Can I give a specific example?"** 🔍
2. **"Why do I feel that way?"** 💭
3. **"How is it different from other places/things?"** ⚖️

Those three questions are an infinite content machine. ♾️
