# Day 24 — Another, Other, or Others? ഇനി Confusion വേണ്ട

**Target length:** 11–12 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *Another* = one more (singular). *Other* = different (needs a noun). *Others* = other people/things (stands alone).

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Restaurant scene energy.)*

You're at a tea shop. You finished your tea. You want one more. And you say:

> ❌ *"Give me other tea."*
> ❌ *"One more tea give."*

*(Smile.)* Understandable — but the natural sentence is:

> ✅ **"Could I have another tea, please?"**

*(Then this one.)* You're comparing two options and you say:

> ❌ *"This shirt is small. Show me the another one."*

*(Wince.)* **"The another"** — that combination doesn't exist in English. Ever.

> ✅ **"Show me the other one."**

*(Point at camera.)*

Here's the Malayali problem: **വേറെ, മറ്റേ, മറ്റുള്ളവർ, ഒന്നുകൂടി** — all of these blur into "other" in your head.

Today: three words, three clear jobs. And one combination you must **never** say. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow. Focus on the SHAPE.)*

### 1️⃣ ANOTHER = one more, or a different one (SINGULAR)

*(Show the secret.)* Look at the word: **an + other = another.** The **"an"** is hiding inside! And *an* means **one**. 💡

So *another* normally takes a **singular** noun, and it already contains its own article. That's why ❌ *"the another"* is impossible — you can't have *the* and *an* together!

- ✅ *Could I have **another** cup of tea?* → one more ☕
- ✅ *Let's meet **another** day.* → a different day
- ✅ ***Another** person called me.* → one more person

**ANOTHER = one more / a different one. SINGULAR. Malayalam: ഒന്നുകൂടി / വേറൊരു**

### 2️⃣ OTHER = different — and it needs a noun after it

As a **describing** word, *other* is followed by a **noun** (or the word *one*).

- ✅ *the **other** shirt* ✅
- ✅ *the **other** one* ✅
- ✅ ***other** people* ✅
- ✅ *Give me the other.* ← also fine when it's obvious what you mean; **"the other one"** is clearer ⭐

**OTHER = different + needs a noun. Malayalam: മറ്റേ / വേറെ**

### 3️⃣ OTHERS = other people/things — stands alone, no noun needed

*Others* is a **pronoun**. It replaces "other people" or "other things."

- ✅ *Some people like tea, **others** like coffee.* ✅
- ✅ *Don't worry about **others**.* ✅
- ✅ *Where are the **others**?* ✅

**OTHERS = other people/things. Never followed by a noun. Malayalam: മറ്റുള്ളവർ**

### 🔑 The 2-second decision

*(Show on screen.)*

> **Is it ONE more thing?** → **another** *(no "the"!)*
> **Is there a NOUN after it?** → **other**
> **Does it stand ALONE?** → **others**

*(Slogan.)*
> **another + singular noun. other + noun. others = alone.**

### The forbidden combinations

*(Big red X on screen.)*

| ⚠️ Avoid | ✅ Say instead |
|---|---|
| the another | **the other** |
| another ones | **other ones** / **another one** |
| others people | **other people** |
| an other | **another** |
| other one *(without the)* | **another one** |

*(Emphasise.)* **"Others" NEVER takes a noun.** If a noun follows, drop the -s: *other **people***, not "others people." ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഒരു ചായ കൂടി തരാമോ?

*(Find the clue.)* **കൂടി** = "more, in addition." That means **one more** → **ANOTHER** ✅

Build it:

1. **One more?** → Yes → **another** *(and remember — "an" is inside, so NO "a" or "the"!)*
2. **Polite request** *(Day 08!)* → Could I have
3. **What** → another tea
4. **Politeness** → please

> ✅ **"Could I have another tea, please?"**

*(Other natural versions.)*
- ✅ *One more tea, please.* ✅ *(also perfect and very common!)*
- ✅ *Can I get another cup, please?*
- ✅ *I'll have another, thanks.* ← *another* alone is okay when the noun is obvious ✅

*(Careful with the common error.)*
- ❌ *Could I have a another tea?* ← "a" + "an" = double article ❌
- ❌ *Could I have the another tea?* ← "the" + "an" ❌
- ✅ **Could I have another tea?** ✅

*(Memory trick.)* Say to yourself: **"another already has its 'an' — don't feed it more articles!"** 🍽️

*(Bonus — another with numbers!)*
Surprisingly, **another** can go with plural numbers:
- ✅ *I need **another two days**.* = two more days ✅
- ✅ *Wait **another five minutes**.* ✅
- ✅ *He scored **another 50 runs**.* ✅

*(Explain.)* Here *another* means "an additional set of," so the group counts as **one unit**. ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഈ ഷർട്ട് ചെറുതാണ്, ആ മറ്റേ ഷർട്ട് കാണിക്കാമോ?

*(Find the clue.)* **മറ്റേ** = "the other" — a **specific** different one. There are two shirts, and you want the second one. → **THE OTHER** ✅

Build it:

1. **A specific different one?** → Yes → **the other**
2. **Noun after it?** → Yes → shirt / one
3. **Polite request** → Could you show me
4. **What** → the other one

> ✅ **"This shirt is small. Could you show me the other one?"**

*(The key difference — feel it!)*

| Sentence | Situation |
|---|---|
| *Show me **another** shirt.* | There are **many** shirts. Any different one. 🤷 |
| *Show me **the other** shirt.* | There are exactly **two**. I want the second. ☝️ |

*(Explain.)* **"The other"** = there's only **one option left**. You both know which one.
**"Another"** = there are **several options**, pick any.

*(Great examples.)*
- ✅ *I have two brothers. One is a doctor; **the other** is an engineer.* ← only two, so *the other* ✅
- ✅ *I have three brothers. One is a doctor; **the others** are engineers.* ← more than two remaining ✅

*(Connect to Day 20.)* See how **"the"** carries the "we both know" meaning again? Everything connects. 🔗

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Word & why |
|---|---|---|
| Rescheduling a meeting | **Can we meet another day?** | a different day, many options |
| Comparing two hands | **My right hand hurts, so I write with the other one.** | only two → the other |
| Talking about people generally | **Some people are shy; others are outgoing.** | stands alone → others |
| Waiting for your group | **Where are the others?** | other people → others |
| Being considerate | **You should respect other people's opinions.** | noun follows → other |

*(Highlight row 3 — the classic pattern!)*

> **"Some ... others ..."**

This is a beautiful, natural English structure for comparing groups:
- ✅ *Some like tea, others prefer coffee.*
- ✅ *Some students learn fast, others need more time.*
- ✅ *Some days are good, others are difficult.*

*(Write it down.)* Use this in IELTS essays and interviews — it sounds sophisticated. ⭐

*(Highlight row 5.)* Note: ***other* people's** — the apostrophe goes after *people*, and *other* has **no -s**. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "വേറെ" → "other" for everything

**വേറെ** covers a huge range in Malayalam:

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| വേറെ ഒന്ന് തരൂ | Give other one | **Give me another one.** |
| വേറെ എന്തെങ്കിലും? | Other something? | **Anything else?** |
| വേറെ ആരുമില്ല | No other is there | **There's no one else.** |
| വേറെ വഴിയില്ല | No other way | **There's no other way.** ✅ |
| വേറെ ദിവസം | Other day | **Another day.** |

*(Big discovery.)* Notice rows 2 and 3 — **വേറെ** often means **"else,"** not "other"!

### 🌟 The word Malayalis never use: ELSE

*(Get excited about this.)* **"Else"** is the missing word in most Malayalis' English. It means "in addition / different," and it goes **after** question words:

- ✅ *What **else**?* = വേറെ എന്ത്? ✅
- ✅ *Who **else** is coming?* = വേറെ ആര്? ✅
- ✅ *Anything **else**?* = വേറെ എന്തെങ്കിലും? ✅
- ✅ *Somewhere **else*** = വേറെ എവിടെയോ ✅
- ✅ *Nobody **else** knows.* = വേറെ ആർക്കും അറിയില്ല ✅
- ✅ *Let's talk about something **else**.* ✅

*(Practise these — they'll transform your speaking!)*
1. **Anything else?** ← every shopkeeper says this 🛍️
2. **What else did he say?**
3. **Is there anyone else?**
4. **Let's go somewhere else.**

*(Rule.)* **Else goes AFTER the question word or "some/any/no" word.** Never before:
- ✅ *something else* ✅
- ❌ *else something* ❌

### Trap 2: "Others" with a noun

- ❌ *others people* → ✅ **other people**
- ❌ *others students* → ✅ **other students**
- ❌ *others things* → ✅ **other things**

*(Rule — say it clearly.)* **The moment a noun appears, drop the -s.**
- ✅ *other people* ✅ *(noun present → no -s)*
- ✅ *the others* ✅ *(no noun → -s needed)*

*(Test yourself.)*
- ✅ *Some people came early; **the others** came late.* ← no noun ✅
- ✅ *Some people came early; **other people** came late.* ← noun ✅

### Trap 3: "The other day" — a hidden idiom!

*(Fun one.)* Careful — **"the other day"** doesn't mean "a different day." It's an **idiom** meaning **"recently, a few days ago"**:

- ✅ *I met him **the other day**.* = a few days ago ✅ *(കഴിഞ്ഞ ദിവസം)*
- ✅ *I saw that movie the other day.*

So:
- **another day** = a future different day → *Let's meet another day.* 📅
- **the other day** = recently in the past → *I saw him the other day.* 🕐

*(Very useful for conversation!)* Native speakers use *"the other day"* constantly. Learn it. ⭐

### Trap 4: "Each other" vs "one another"

*(Quick bonus.)*
- ✅ *We help **each other**.* = രണ്ടുപേരും പരസ്പരം ✅
- ✅ *They love **each other**.* ✅

*(Note.)* ❌ *"We help each others"* → no -s! It's always **each other**. ✅

And the common Malayali error:
- ❌ *We are helping to each other* → ✅ **We help each other.** *(no "to"!)*
- ✅ *They talked with each other.* ✅ *(also: talked **to** each other — both correct)*
