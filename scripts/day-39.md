# Day 39 — Build Long English Sentences Without Getting Confused

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** Start with a short core sentence, then **add layers**. Never build a long sentence from the start.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confident, building.)*

Yesterday I told you to speak in **short** sentences. And that's genuinely the right advice when you're nervous. ✅

But now let's be honest about something. Listen to these two answers to the same interview question:

**Answer A:** *"I worked at a company. It was a small company. I did accounts. I worked there two years."*

**Answer B:** *"I worked at a small company in Kochi, where I handled accounts for about two years."*

*(Pause.)*

Both **correct**. But B sounds like a **professional**. A sounds like a **beginner**. 📊

*(Point at camera.)*

Same information. Same vocabulary level. The difference is that B **connected** the ideas instead of listing them.

*(Lean in.)*

But here's the danger — when Malayalis try to build long sentences, they usually collapse:

> ❌ *"I worked in a small company which is in Kochi and there I was doing the accounts work and it was for two years I worked there."* 😰

*(Wince.)* And-and-and. 🔗🔗🔗

Today: how to build long sentences **safely**. There's a technique, and it's the opposite of what you'd expect. Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. This principle is counter-intuitive and important.)*

### The mistake: building long from the start ❌

*(Explain what learners do wrong.)*

When you decide *"I'll say something impressive,"* your brain tries to hold the **whole long sentence** in memory before speaking. Four ideas, all at once, plus grammar. 💥

And that's when it collapses. Not because the English is hard — because **your working memory is full**. 🧠

### 🔑 THE METHOD: Core first, then layers

*(Write it big.)*

> ### 👉 Say a SHORT core sentence in your head. Then ADD pieces to it.

*(Demonstrate the growth.)*

Watch this sentence grow:

| Layer | Sentence |
|---|---|
| **Core** | *I worked at a company.* ✅ |
| **+ description** | *I worked at a **small** company.* |
| **+ place** | *I worked at a small company **in Kochi**.* |
| **+ what you did** | *I worked at a small company in Kochi, **where I handled accounts**.* |
| **+ duration** | *I worked at a small company in Kochi, where I handled accounts **for two years**.* ⭐ |

*(Point out.)* **Five steps. Each step adds ONE thing.** At no point did I hold a long sentence in memory. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Don't build long. Build short, then GROW.**
> **നീളമുള്ള വാക്യം ഉണ്ടാക്കരുത് — ചെറുത് ഉണ്ടാക്കി വളർത്തുക.** 🌱

### The 4 layers you can add to ANY sentence

*(This is the practical toolkit. Very valuable.)*

Every English sentence can accept these four additions:

| Layer | Question it answers | Example addition |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Description** | *what kind?* | a **small** company, my **younger** brother |
| **2. Place / Time** | *where? when?* | **in Kochi**, **last year**, **at 6 p.m.** |
| **3. Extra info** | *which one? who?* | **which is near the metro**, **where I studied** |
| **4. Reason / Result** | *why? so what?* | **because it was closer**, **so I accepted** |

*(Emphasise.)* Four layers. That's the whole system. And you add them **one at a time**, in that order. ✅

### Why the ORDER matters

*(Important structural point.)*

English has a **preferred order** for these layers:

> **[Who] [Verb] [What] — [Description] — [Place] — [Time] — [Extra] — [Reason]**

*(Compare to Malayalam.)* Malayalam puts **everything before the verb**:
- **ഞാൻ ഇന്നലെ കൊച്ചിയിലെ ഒരു ചെറിയ കമ്പനിയിൽ ജോലി ചെയ്തു** ← all layers first, verb last

*(Point out.)* So the layers are **stacked at the front** in Malayalam and **trail behind** in English. 🔄

*(The practical instruction.)* **Get the person + verb out of your mouth first. Then hang the layers behind it.** 🧥

*(Demo.)*
- 🧠 Malayalam order: *yesterday — in a small Kochi company — I — worked*
- ✅ English order: **I worked** — at a small company — in Kochi — yesterday ✅

*(Connect to Day 32.)* Same core habit: **person + verb first.** 🔗 That one reflex fixes both long and short sentences. ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — grown layer by layer

**The idea you want to express (in Malayalam):**
> **എന്റെ അനിയൻ ബാംഗ്ലൂരിലെ ഒരു വലിയ IT കമ്പനിയിൽ software engineer ആയി ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു, അവൻ 2021-ൽ അവിടെ ചേർന്നു.**

*(Don't panic — grow it.)*

**🌱 Layer 0 — The CORE** *(person + verb + what)*

Find the main action: **ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു → works** ✅

> ✅ *"My brother works."*

*(Point out.)* **Three words.** Ugly, boring, and **completely correct**. That's your foundation. ✅

**🌿 Layer 1 — Add description**
> ✅ *"My **younger** brother works."*

*(Note.)* ❌ Not *"my small brother"* — say **younger brother** ✅ *(or just "my brother")*

**🌿 Layer 2 — Add what he is / where**
> ✅ *"My younger brother works **as a software engineer**."*

*(Note the preposition!)* **work AS a job title.** ⭐
- ✅ *He works **as** an engineer.* ✅
- ✅ *He works **at** Infosys.* ✅ *(company)*
- ✅ *He works **in** IT.* ✅ *(field)*
- ❌ *He works engineer.* ❌

**🌿 Layer 3 — Add place**
> ✅ *"My younger brother works as a software engineer **at a big IT company in Bangalore**."*

**🌿 Layer 4 — Add time / extra info**
> ✅ *"My younger brother works as a software engineer at a big IT company in Bangalore. **He joined in 2021.**"* ✅

**OR — join it into one sentence:**
> ✅ *"My younger brother works as a software engineer at a big IT company in Bangalore, **which he joined in 2021**."* ⭐

*(Honest advice.)* **Both are excellent.** The two-sentence version is easier to say and just as professional. **You never HAVE to make it one sentence.** ✅

---

### Sentence 2 — grown layer by layer *(a work example)*

**The idea:**
> **ഞാൻ റിപ്പോർട്ട് പൂർത്തിയാക്കി, പക്ഷേ manager-ന് അയക്കാൻ പറ്റിയില്ല, കാരണം internet പോയി.**

**🌱 Core** → find the main verb: **പൂർത്തിയാക്കി → finished** ✅
> ✅ *"I finished the report."*

**🌿 + the problem** *(Layer 4 — reason/result)*
> ✅ *"I finished the report, **but I couldn't send it**."*

**🌿 + the reason**
> ✅ *"I finished the report, but I couldn't send it **because the internet was down**."* ⭐

*(Note the natural phrase.)* **"the internet was down"** ⭐ — not ❌ *"internet went"* or ❌ *"internet was not there."* ✅

**🌿 + who / when** *(if needed)*
> ✅ *"I finished the report yesterday, but I couldn't send it to the manager because the internet was down."* ✅

*(Point out.)* **Four layers, added one at a time, and the sentence never collapsed.** ✅

---

### The 5 connectors that make sentences longer safely 🔗

*(Very practical — these are the "growth points.")*

#### 1️⃣ **which / who / where** — adds extra info *(Day 58 will go deep on this)*

- ✅ *I work at a company **which** makes mobile apps.*
- ✅ *My friend, **who** lives in Dubai, is coming next week.*
- ✅ *I studied at a college **where** the classes were all in English.*

*(Malayali tip.)* **which** = things · **who** = people · **where** = places ✅

#### 2️⃣ **because / so** — adds reason and result

- ✅ *I left early **because** I had a class.*
- ✅ *It was raining, **so** we cancelled the trip.*

*(Warning.)* ❌ Never *"Because it rained, **so** we stayed."* — **one connector per joint!** ✅ *(Day 32)*

#### 3️⃣ **although / even though** — adds contrast ⭐

- ✅ ***Although** I was tired, I finished the work.*
- ✅ *I enjoyed the movie, **even though** it was very long.*

*(Emphasise.)* Malayalis rarely use **although** — and it instantly makes you sound more advanced. Practise it! ⭐

*(Warning.)* ❌ Never *"Although I was tired, **but** I finished."* — pick **one**! ✅

#### 4️⃣ **while / when / after / before** — adds time

- ✅ ***While** I was waiting, I read the news.*
- ✅ *I'll call you **after** I get home.*

*(Remember Day 26!)* After time words → **present tense** for future. ✅ *"after I **get** home"*, not "will get home." 🔗

#### 5️⃣ **-ing phrases** — the elegant one ⭐

*(This is the most sophisticated tool — teach it well.)*

Instead of two sentences, start one with **-ing**:

| Two sentences | ⭐ One elegant sentence |
|---|---|
| *I finished the work. Then I went home.* | ***Having finished** the work, I went home.* |
| *I was waiting for the bus. I met an old friend.* | ***While waiting** for the bus, I met an old friend.* |
| *He didn't know the answer. He stayed quiet.* | ***Not knowing** the answer, he stayed quiet.* |

*(Say honestly.)* These sound **beautiful in writing** — perfect for IELTS essays and emails. ✅ But in **speaking**, don't force them. Two sentences are fine. ✅

---

### The safety habit: aim for about 15 words ✂️

*(Practical and important.)*

*(Give them a hard rule.)*

> ### 👉 In speaking, stop at about 15 words. Then breathe and start a new sentence.

*(Explain.)* Why 15? It's not a grammar rule — it's a **practical** target. ✅ It's roughly one comfortable breath, and it keeps you from building sentences you can't finish. ✅ Skilled speakers go much longer; while you're building the habit, short is safer. ⭐

*(Demo the limit.)*
- ✅ *"I worked at a small company in Kochi, where I handled accounts for two years."* → **14 words** ✅ perfect
- ❌ *"I worked at a small company in Kochi where I was handling accounts and also doing some billing work and it was for two years and after that I moved..."* → **32 words** ❌ collapsed

*(Rule.)* **Two sentences of 12 words beat one sentence of 24.** ✅ Always.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: The "and-and-and" chain 🔗🔗🔗

*(The most common long-sentence failure.)*

*(Explain the cause.)* Malayalam connects clauses with **verb endings** — and there are many of them: **-ഉം, -ഇട്ട്, -അപ്പോൾ, -കൊണ്ട്**. Each one is a different connector, but they all feel like "and" when you translate. 🔄

So one Malayalam sentence with **four different** connectors becomes English with **four "and"s**:

❌ *"I went to the shop and I bought vegetables and I came home and I cooked."*

*(The fix — use the RIGHT connector for each joint.)*

✅ *"I went to the shop and bought some vegetables. **After** I came home, I cooked dinner."* ⭐

*(Point out.)* Notice two upgrades:
1. **"and bought"** — no repeated *"I"*! ✅ *(same subject → drop it)*
2. **"After I came home"** — a real connector instead of *and* ✅

*(The subject-dropping rule — very useful!)*
- ❌ *I went and I bought and I came.* ❌
- ✅ **I went, bought some vegetables, and came home.** ✅

*(Rule.)* **Same person doing all the actions? Say the person ONCE.** ✅

### Trap 2: The verb-last collapse in long sentences

*(The pressure leak from Day 32.)*

Short sentences? Your word order is fine now. But in **long** sentences, when memory fills up, Malayalam order leaks back:

- ❌ *"I yesterday my friend who is in Dubai to meet went."* 😰
- ✅ **"Yesterday I went to meet my friend, who lives in Dubai."** ✅

*(The fix.)* **Get the core out FIRST.** *"I went..."* — say it, commit to it. Then the layers have nowhere to go but behind. ✅

*(Drill.)* Practise saying just the cores fast:
**I went · She said · They finished · We decided · He joined · I handled** ✅

### Trap 3: Over-using "which"

*(A specifically Indian-English pattern.)*

❌ *"I have a friend which is a doctor which is working in a hospital which is in Kochi."* 😄

*(Fix.)*
- **which** = things only ✅
- **who** = people ✅
- And **don't chain them!** ✅

✅ *"I have a friend **who** is a doctor. She works at a hospital in Kochi."* ⭐

*(Rule.)* **Maximum ONE "which/who" per sentence.** Two means you should have used a full stop. ✅

### Trap 4: Making it long just to sound impressive

*(An honest correction.)*

*(Firmly but kindly.)* Some learners believe long sentences = advanced English. **That's backwards.** 🔄

*(The truth.)* Professional English writing — business, journalism, even academic — has been getting **shorter** for fifty years. Clear beats complicated. ✅

*(The real skill.)* Look at these two:
- *"I have been working in this field for a considerable duration of time."* ❌ *(long and empty)*
- *"I've worked in this field for six years."* ⭐ *(short and specific)*

*(Point out.)* The **second** one sounds more professional. Because **specific** beats **long**. ✅

*(Rule to remember.)*

> ### 👉 Add layers to add INFORMATION, never to add length.

If a layer doesn't tell the listener something new, **delete it**. ✂️
