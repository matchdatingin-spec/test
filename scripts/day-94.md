## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 5:00)

*(Explain what levels mean and why four skills matter.)*

### 📊 The four skills are usually UNEVEN

*(The key insight of this video.)*

*(Explain.)* Most Malayali learners have a very **uneven** profile: ✅

| Skill | Typical Malayali level |
|---|---|
| **Reading** 📖 | strongest ⭐ *(years of textbooks)* |
| **Grammar** ✍️ | strong ✅ *(exam-focused schooling)* |
| **Listening** 👂 | medium ⚠️ *(few native-speed inputs)* |
| **Speaking** 🗣️ | weakest ⚠️ *(almost no practice)* |
| **Writing** 📧 | medium ⚠️ *(but old-fashioned style)* |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Reading high, speaking low** ✅ is the classic Kerala profile. ✅

*(Explain why.)* You were taught English to **pass exams**, which are reading and writing. ✅ Nobody assessed whether you could **talk**. ⚠️

*(The consequence.)* ⭐ So a single "level" is misleading. ✅ You need a **profile**. ⭐

### 🎯 The levels, described honestly

*(Show the reference table.)*

| Level | Speaking | Writing | Real-world equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A2** | simple sentences, frequent pauses | short messages | basic ✅ |
| **B1** | can explain and discuss with effort ⭐ | clear emails ✅ | **IELTS 5.5–6** · most jobs in India ✅ |
| **B2** | fluent on familiar topics, few pauses ⭐ | professional, structured ✅ | **IELTS 6.5–7** · Gulf/abroad jobs ⭐ |
| **C1** | flexible, near-effortless ✅ | nuanced ✅ | **IELTS 7.5–8** · client-facing, academic ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **B2 is the target for most people watching.** ✅ It's what most overseas jobs and IELTS 7 require. ✅

*(Firmly.)* And B2 is genuinely **reachable** from B1 in a few months of the right practice. ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 45)*

## 🔍 3. THE FOUR TESTS (5:00 – 15:00)

---

### 🗣️ TEST 1 — SPEAKING *(10 marks)*

*(The most important test — and the one people skip. Insist on it.)*

*(Firmly.)* **You must actually speak.** ✅ Out loud. Recorded on your phone. ✅ Reading silently gives you nothing. ⚠️

#### 📱 The task

**Record yourself for 60 seconds** on this topic: 🎙️

> **"Describe your typical working day, and one thing you would change about it."**

*(Give a genuine 60-second countdown on screen.)* ⏱️

#### 📊 Now score yourself — listen back once

| # | Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Did you speak for the **full 60 seconds**? | 2 |
| 2 | Did you answer **both** parts *(day + one change)*? | 2 |
| 3 | How many times did you **stop completely**? *(0–1 = 2 marks · 2–3 = 1 · 4+ = 0)* | 2 |
| 4 | Did you use any **linking words** *(because, so, although, which)*? | 2 |
| 5 | Did you use **past or future** tense correctly at least once? | 2 |

**Speaking total: ___ / 10** 🗣️

*(Emphasise criterion 3!)* ⭐ **Stopping** is the clearest fluency marker. ✅ Not accent, not vocabulary — **whether the speech keeps moving.** 🔗 *(Day 71)*

*(Warm note.)* ✅ If you scored low here, that's **expected** ✅ — speaking is almost everyone's weakest skill. It's also the fastest to improve. ⭐

---

### ✍️ TEST 2 — WRITING *(10 marks)*

#### 📧 The task

**Write this email in 5 minutes.** ⏱️ On paper or on your phone. ✅

> **Situation:** You need two extra days for a report that's due tomorrow. Two colleagues have been on leave. Write to your manager.

*(Give 5 minutes on screen.)* ⏱️

#### 📊 Score yourself

| # | Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is there a **specific subject line** with the request? 🔗 *(Day 83)* | 1 |
| 2 | Is the **ask** in the first two lines? ⭐ | 2 |
| 3 | Did you give a **specific new date**? 📊 | 1 |
| 4 | Did you avoid *"do the needful," "revert," "kindly"*? 🔗 | 2 |
| 5 | **One** apology or fewer? 🔗 *(Day 84)* | 1 |
| 6 | Under **90 words**? | 1 |
| 7 | No *"since 2 days"* / *"informations"* / *"I am having"*? 🔗 | 2 |

**Writing total: ___ / 10** ✍️

*(Emphasise #2.)* ⭐ If your ask was in the **last** line, you lose 2 marks — and in real life, you'd lose the reader. ⚠️

### 👂 TEST 3 — LISTENING *(10 marks)*

*(Critical: deliver this at genuine natural speed.)*

*(Instruct.)* I'll say five sentences at **normal** speaking speed. ✅ Each **once** only. ✅ Write what you hear. ✍️

*(⚠️ Production note: deliver these with real reductions and linking — as in Day 43. Do NOT slow down.)*

**1.** *"I'll get back to you by Thursday, but I'd rather confirm with the client first."* ⭐
**2.** *"She's been working there for about three years, hasn't she?"* ⭐
**3.** *"We were supposed to finish it yesterday, but the system was down."* ⭐
**4.** *"D'you know whatcha wanna do about the invoice?"* ⭐ *(reduced!)*
**5.** *"If I'd known earlier, I would've sorted it out myself."* ⭐ *(reduced!)*

#### 📊 Score yourself

**2 marks** per sentence where you got the **meaning** right ✅ *(exact words not required)*

**Listening total: ___ / 10** 👂

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Sentences **4 and 5** used reductions 🔗 *(Day 43)* ✅ If you missed those two but got 1–3, your **grammar listening** is fine and your **connected-speech listening** needs work. ✅ That's a useful distinction. ⭐

### ✅ TEST 4 — GRAMMAR *(10 marks)*

*(Quick — 10 sentences, 1 mark each.)*

**1.** *"I ___ here for three years."* → **have been working** ✅ 🔗 *(19, 27)*
**2.** *"He ___ like coffee."* → **doesn't** ✅ 🔗 *(53)*
**3.** *"I ___ with your opinion."* → **agree** *(no "am")* ✅ 🔗 *(52)*
**4.** *"If I had money, I ___ buy a car."* → **would** ✅ 🔗 *(51)*

**7.** *"I have three years of ___."* → **experience** ✅ 🔗 *(56)*
**8.** *"He asked me where ___."* → **I was going** ✅ 🔗 *(60)*
**9.** *"I got my hair ___ yesterday."* → **cut** ✅ 🔗 *(62)*
**10.** *"I'm ___ to finish it today."* → **supposed** ✅ 🔗 *(63)*

**Grammar total: ___ / 10** ✅

### 🔍 But the PROFILE matters more

*(This is the genuinely useful part.)*

*(Explain.)* Find **your** pattern below. ✅

#### 📖 Pattern 1: Grammar high, Speaking low ⚠️
*(The classic Kerala profile.)*

**Example:** Grammar 9, Writing 7, Listening 6, **Speaking 3** ⚠️

*(Diagnosis.)* ⭐ You **know** English. You can't **produce** it under time pressure. ✅

**Your plan:**
- ⭐ **Stop studying grammar entirely** ✅ Seriously.
- ✅ **Day 44** shadowing — 2 min daily 🗣️
- ✅ **Day 36** P-E-R-F-O — record 2 min daily 🎙️
- ✅ **Day 45** daily plan, 60% output ⭐

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ You need **mouth hours**, not more rules. ✅ Three months of daily speaking would move you to B2. ⭐

#### 👂 Pattern 2: Everything medium, Listening low ⚠️

**Example:** Grammar 7, Writing 7, **Listening 4**, Speaking 6 ⚠️

*(Diagnosis.)* ✅ You've had little exposure to **native-speed** English. ✅

**Your plan:**
- ⭐ **Day 43** — the 30-second decode, daily 👂
- ✅ **Day 71** — connected speech 🔗
- ⭐ Malayalam films with **English subtitles** ✅ *(Day 37's trick)*

*(Note.)* ⚠️ If you missed sentences 4 and 5 but got 1–3, it's specifically **reductions** ✅ — that's Day 43, and it improves fast. ⭐

#### ✍️ Pattern 3: Speaking okay, Writing low ⚠️

**Example:** Speaking 7, **Writing 4**, Listening 6, Grammar 7 ⚠️

*(Diagnosis.)* ✅ You likely write in the **old colonial style** ✅ — which the test penalised. ✅

**Your plan:**
- ⭐ **Days 83, 84, 85** — the writing block ✅
- ⭐ **Day 92** — the email makeover ✅
- ✅ Rewrite one real email weekly 📧

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ This is the **fastest** gap to close. ✅ It's vocabulary substitution, not skill-building — a couple of weeks. ⭐

#### 🎯 Pattern 4: Everything even, all medium ✅

**Example:** all around 6 ✅

*(Diagnosis.)* ⭐ Solid B1. You need **depth**, not repair. ✅

- ✅ **Days 74–77** vocabulary 📚
- ✅ **Day 58** relative clauses — biggest structural upgrade available 🔗

#### ⚠️ Pattern 5: Grammar low, others okay

**Example:** **Grammar 4**, Speaking 6, Writing 5 ⚠️

### 💙 And the honest part about levels

*(Close sincerely — this section matters most.)*

*(Warmly.)* Two things I want to say. ✅

**First** — if your score was lower than you hoped: ✅

*(Firmly.)* This test was **hard**. ✅ It included reduced native speech, a timed email, and a 60-second unprepared monologue. ✅ Most people who **live** in English-speaking countries would drop marks on it. ⭐

**Second** — and this is the important one: ✅

*(Slowly.)* Your level is **not** your worth. ✅
