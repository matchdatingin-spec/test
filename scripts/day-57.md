# Day 57 — Gerund or Infinitive? I Like Reading vs I Like to Read

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *-ing* points backwards to real experience. *To* points forwards to a goal. That single feeling explains almost every case.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Playful, curious.)*

Two sentences. They look almost identical:

> *"I stopped **smoking**."* 🚭
> *"I stopped **to smoke**."* 🚬

*(Pause.)* Same meaning? 🤔

**Completely opposite!**

- *I stopped **smoking**.* = I **quit**. No more cigarettes. ✅
- *I stopped **to smoke**.* = I was walking, and I **paused in order to** have a cigarette! 😄

*(Point at camera.)*

One is quitting. One is smoking. And the only difference is **-ing** versus **to**.

*(Lean in.)*

And then there's the everyday confusion:

> ❌ *"I enjoy to swim."*
> ❌ *"I am interested to learn English."* *(should be: interested **in learning**)*
> ❌ *"I look forward to meet you."*

*(Emphasise.)* All wrong — all need **-ing**. ✅

Today: one **feeling** that explains almost every case, plus the lists you genuinely need to memorise. Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. The "feeling" is the real teaching.)*

### First, the names *(then we forget them)* 📚

- **Gerund** = verb + **-ing** used as a thing → *swimming, reading, working* ✅
- **Infinitive** = **to** + verb → *to swim, to read, to work* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Forget the names. Focus on the **feeling**. ✅

### 🔑 THE FEELING: backwards vs forwards

*(This is the insight — spend time here.)*

> ### 👉 **-ING points BACKWARDS** ⬅️ — to something real, already experienced
> ### 👉 **TO points FORWARDS** ➡️ — to a goal, a purpose, something not yet done

*(Test it — this is genuinely clarifying.)*

| Sentence | Feeling |
|---|---|
| *I enjoy **swimming**.* | I've swum. I know it. ⬅️ real ✅ |
| *I want **to swim**.* | Not yet. It's a goal. ➡️ ✅ |
| *I remember **locking** the door.* | The memory is real ⬅️ ✅ |
| *I remembered **to lock** the door.* | It was a task ahead ➡️ ✅ |
| *I stopped **smoking**.* | The smoking was real, and it ended ⬅️ ✅ |
| *I stopped **to smoke**.* | Purpose ahead: in order to smoke ➡️ ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* **-ing = experience. To = intention.** ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **-ing looks back at reality ⬅️. To looks forward at purpose ➡️.**
> **-ing = അനുഭവം. To = ലക്ഷ്യം.** ✅

*(Honest framing — say it once.)* ⭐ This feeling is a **memory hook**, not a law. ✅ It explains the pairs above beautifully, but many verbs simply have a fixed habit you learn by ear — ✅ *avoid **driving** tomorrow* is *-ing* even though it's in the future. ✅ Use the feeling to remember, and the **lists** below to be sure. ⭐

### The three groups of verbs 📋

*(Now the practical part.)*

**Group 1: Verbs that take only -ING** ⬅️
*(These are about **real, experienced** things)*

**enjoy · avoid · finish · mind · suggest · recommend · practise · consider · imagine · admit · deny · keep · miss · risk**

- ✅ *I enjoy **reading**.* ✅
- ✅ *He avoided **answering**.* ✅
- ✅ *Would you mind **waiting**?* ✅ 🔗 *(Day 42)*

**Group 2: Verbs that take only TO** ➡️
*(These are about **goals, decisions, intentions**)*

**want · need · decide · hope · plan · promise · agree · refuse · learn · offer · manage · expect · afford · seem**

- ✅ *I want **to go**.* ✅
- ✅ *He decided **to leave**.* ✅
- ✅ *She promised **to call**.* ✅

**Group 3: Verbs that take BOTH** 🔀
*(Same meaning, or slightly different)*

**like · love · hate · prefer · begin · start · continue**

- ✅ *I like **reading**.* ✅ = ✅ *I like **to read**.* ✅ *(both fine!)*

*(And note these two — they take both, but the meaning CHANGES.)* ⭐ **stop** and **try** get their own section later in this class: ✅ *stop **smoking*** *(quit)* vs *stop **to smoke*** *(pause in order to)*; ✅ *try **to call*** *(attempt)* vs *try **calling*** *(experiment)*. ⭐

*(Point out the logic!)* Look at Groups 1 and 2 again:
- Group 1 verbs are about things you've **done** ⬅️
- Group 2 verbs are about things you **will do** ➡️

*(Emphasise.)* **It's not random!** The feeling predicts the group about 80% of the time. ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് നീന്താൻ ഇഷ്ടമാണ്.

*(Notice.)* **നീന്താൻ** — the **-ആൻ** ending. ✅

*(Important observation!)* Malayalam **-ആൻ** looks and feels like English **"to"** — *"to swim."* ✅

*(Point out.)* And that's exactly the trap! Because your brain sees **-ആൻ** and produces *"to"* every time — even when English wants **-ing**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **-ആൻ is not always "to."** Sometimes it's **-ing**. ✅

Build it:

1. **Which verb?** → *enjoy* or *like*
2. **enjoy → Group 1** → only **-ing** ✅
3. **Who** → I

> ✅ **"I enjoy swimming."** ✅
> ✅ **"I like swimming."** ✅ *(like = Group 3, both work)*
> ✅ **"I like to swim."** ✅ *(also fine!)*
> ❌ **"I enjoy to swim."** ❌ *(enjoy is Group 1!)*

*(Emphasise.)* **"Enjoy" NEVER takes "to."** ✅ Ever. Practise: **"I enjoy reading. I enjoy cooking. I enjoy travelling."** ✅

*(The nuance between "like reading" and "like to read"!)*

*(Explain — this is genuinely interesting.)*
- ✅ *I like **reading**.* → I **enjoy** the activity itself ⬅️ ✅
- ✅ *I like **to read** before bed.* → it's my **habit/choice** ➡️ ✅

*(Practical.)* The difference is **very** small. Native speakers use both freely. **Don't stress.** ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ഇംഗ്ലീഷ് പഠിക്കാൻ തീരുമാനിച്ചു.

*(Find the verb.)* **തീരുമാനിച്ചു** = *decided* ✅

*(Which group?)* **decide** is about a **future action** → Group 2 → **to** ✅ ➡️

Build it:

1. **Who** → I
2. **Verb, past** → decided
3. **to + plain verb** → to learn
4. **What** → English

> ✅ **"I decided to learn English."** ✅

*(Practise Group 2 — say these five.)*
1. *I want **to improve** my English.* ✅
2. *He decided **to resign**.* ✅
3. *She promised **to help** me.* ✅
4. *They refused **to pay**.* ✅
5. *I'm trying **to understand**.* ✅

*(Note "manage to" and "afford to"!)* ⭐
- ✅ *I **managed to** finish it on time.* ⭐ *(succeeded with difficulty)*
- ✅ *I can't **afford to** buy a car.* ✅ *(don't have money)*

### ⚠️ THE MEANING-CHANGERS

*(The most interesting section — five verbs where the choice changes everything.)*

#### 1️⃣ STOP

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I stopped **smoking**.* | I quit ✅ 🚭 |
| *I stopped **to smoke**.* | I paused in order to smoke 🚬 |

*(Rule.)* **stop + -ing** = end the activity ⬅️ · **stop + to** = pause for a purpose ➡️ ✅

#### 2️⃣ REMEMBER

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I remember **locking** the door.* | I have the memory ⬅️ ✅ |
| *I remembered **to lock** the door.* | I didn't forget the task ➡️ ✅ |

*(Practical.)* ✅ *"Remember **to send** the email"* = don't forget! ⭐ *(a reminder)* ✅

#### 3️⃣ FORGET

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I'll never forget **meeting** her.* | the memory ⬅️ ✅ |
| *I forgot **to call** her.* | I failed to do the task ➡️ ✅ |

*(Note.)* **"I forgot to..."** ⭐ is the useful everyday one: ✅ *"Sorry, I forgot to reply."* ✅

#### 4️⃣ TRY

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I tried **calling** him.* | I did it, as an experiment ⬅️ ✅ |
| *I tried **to call** him.* | I attempted, maybe failed ➡️ ✅ |

*(Explain.)*
- ✅ *I tried **to open** the jar* → I struggled, maybe couldn't ➡️
- ✅ *I tried **opening** it with hot water* → I experimented with a method ⬅️

#### 5️⃣ GO ON

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *He went on **talking**.* | he continued talking ⬅️ ✅ |
| *He went on **to become** a doctor.* | next, he became one ➡️ ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* Five verbs. And in each, **-ing looks back, to looks forward.** ✅ The feeling holds! 🎯

---

### 🔗 The preposition rule — this fixes many errors!

*(Extremely practical.)*

> ### 👉 When a VERB follows a preposition, use -ING. Never "to + verb."

*(Small precision note.)* ✅ Prepositions are usually followed by a **noun** — *in the house*, *after lunch*. ✅ This rule kicks in when what follows is a **verb**. ⭐

*(Explain.)* Prepositions are the small words: *in, on, at, for, of, about, after, before, without, by* ✅

- ✅ *interested **in** learn**ing*** ✅
- ✅ *good **at** cook**ing*** ✅
- ✅ *thank you **for** help**ing*** ✅
- ✅ ***before** go**ing*** ✅
- ✅ ***without** ask**ing*** ✅
- ✅ *tired **of** wait**ing*** ✅

*(And now the tricky ones — where "to" IS a preposition!)* ⚠️

*(Explain.)* In some phrases, **"to" is a preposition**, not part of an infinitive. And then it takes **-ing**! 🤯

| ✅ Correct | ⚠️ Common error or different meaning |
|---|---|
| **look forward to** meet**ing** you ⭐ | look forward to meet |
| **used to** work**ing** here ⭐ 🔗 | used to work *(different meaning!)* |
| **object to** be**ing** treated like that | object to be treated |
| **in addition to** be**ing** late | in addition to be late |

*(Highlight the first one!)* ⭐

> ✅ **"I look forward to hearing from you."** ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* This is the **standard email closing** — and Malayalis get it wrong constantly:
- ❌ *I look forward to hear from you.* ❌
- ✅ **I look forward to hearing from you.** ✅

*(Say it three times.)* **"Looking forward to hearing from you."** ⭐ 📧

*(And "used to" — the Day 16 connection!)* 🔗
- ✅ *I **used to work** here.* → past habit ⬅️ *(plain verb!)*
- ✅ *I **am used to working** here.* → comfortable ✅ *(-ing!)*

*(Point out.)* **The "be" decides!** 🔗 With *be*, "to" is a preposition → *-ing*. Without *be*, it's an infinitive → plain verb. ✅

---

### 🎯 The -ING as a SUBJECT

*(A useful bonus.)*

*(Explain.)* **-ing** forms can act as **nouns** — as the subject of a sentence:

- ✅ ***Smoking** is bad for your health.* ✅
- ✅ ***Learning** English takes time.* ✅
- ✅ ***Waking up** early is difficult.* ✅
- ✅ ***Speaking** in public makes me nervous.* ✅

*(Malayali note.)* ❌ *"To smoke is bad"* is grammatically possible but sounds formal/old. ✅ Use **-ing** for subjects. ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: "-ആൻ" → always "to"

*(The root cause.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayalam **-ആൻ** *(പഠിക്കാൻ, പോകാൻ, ചെയ്യാൻ)* feels **exactly** like English *"to."* ✅ So we produce:

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| നീന്താൻ ഇഷ്ടമാണ് | I enjoy to swim | **I enjoy swimming** |
| പഠിക്കാൻ താൽപ്പര്യമുണ്ട് | I'm interested to learn | **I'm interested in learning** |
| കാണാൻ കാത്തിരിക്കുന്നു | I look forward to meet you | **I look forward to meeting you** |
| ചെയ്യാൻ ഒഴിവാക്കി | He avoided to do it | **He avoided doing it** |
| സംസാരിക്കാൻ നിർത്തി | He stopped to talk ⚠️ | **He stopped talking** |

*(The fix — check the verb, not the Malayalam!)*

> ### 👉 Malayalam **-ആൻ** tells you nothing. Look at the **English verb** and its group. ✅

*(The five you must never get wrong.)* 🔗
1. **enjoy + -ing** ✅
2. **avoid + -ing** ✅
3. **interested in + -ing** ✅
4. **look forward to + -ing** ✅
5. **finish + -ing** ✅

*(Say each three times.)* 🗣️

### Trap 2: "interested to" instead of "interested in"

*(Very common and worth its own slot.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I'm interested to learn | **I'm interested in learning** ⭐ |
| I'm interested to know | **I'd like to know** ✅ / **I'm interested in knowing** ✅ |
| Interested to join | **Interested in joining** ✅ |

*(One genuine exception, so you're not confused.)* ⭐ With **hear, know, learn, see**, *interested to* is correct English when you mean a **reaction to news**: ✅ *"I'd be interested to hear what he said."* ✅ But for interests and hobbies, always **interested in + -ing**. ⭐

*(Note.)* ✅ *"I'd be interested to know..."* ✅ **does** exist and is correct in formal English! But **"interested in + -ing"** is safer. ⭐

### Trap 3: The adjective + preposition list

*(Practical — these all take -ing.)*

| Phrase | ✅ Example |
|---|---|
| **good at** | *She's good at **explaining** things.* ✅ |
| **interested in** | *I'm interested in **learning** Spanish.* ✅ |
| **afraid of** | *He's afraid of **flying**.* ✅ |
| **tired of** | *I'm tired of **waiting**.* ✅ |
| **used to** 🔗 | *I'm used to **working** late.* ✅ |
| **capable of** | *She's capable of **handling** it.* ✅ |
| **responsible for** | *I'm responsible for **managing** the team.* ⭐ |
| **worried about** | *I'm worried about **failing**.* ✅ |

*(Highlight "responsible for."*) ⭐ Interview gold: ✅ *"I was responsible for **managing** a team of five."* ✅

### Trap 4: Making it complicated when you don't have to 😌

*(An honest, freeing note.)*

*(Warmly.)* You don't have to memorise all these lists perfectly. Here's a practical shortcut:

*(Give them the safe defaults.)*

| Situation | Safe choice |
|---|---|
| After a **preposition** *(in, on, at, for, of, about)* | **always -ing** ✅ |
| After **want, need, decide, plan, hope, try, promise** | **to** ✅ |
| After **enjoy, avoid, finish, stop, mind, suggest** | **-ing** ✅ |
| After **like, love, hate, prefer, start** | **either!** 😌 ✅ |
| **Not sure?** | don't guess — **rephrase**: *"I'd like to..."* / *"I enjoy..."* with a verb you know ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* And honestly? If you say ❌ *"I enjoy to swim,"* everyone understands you perfectly. ✅ This is a **polish** issue, not a communication issue. Don't let it slow your speaking. 🔗 *(Day 36 — flow beats accuracy!)*

## 📝 5. HOMEWORK TEST (13:00 – End)

*(Energetic close.)*

**Question 1 — translate these three.** Same **-ആൻ** ending, three different English forms:
1. **എനിക്ക് സിനിമ കാണാൻ ഇഷ്ടമാണ്.** *(enjoy)*
2. **ഞാൻ ഇംഗ്ലീഷ് പഠിക്കാൻ തീരുമാനിച്ചു.** *(decided)*
3. **നിങ്ങളെ കാണാൻ ഞാൻ കാത്തിരിക്കുന്നു.** *(look forward to — careful!)*

**Question 2 — *-ing* or *to*?**
1. I enjoy ___ (read / reading) at night.
2. She decided ___ (leave / to leave) the job.
3. I'm interested in ___ (learn / learning) French.
4. Would you mind ___ (wait / waiting) a minute?
5. I look forward to ___ (hear / hearing) from you.
6. He stopped ___ (smoke / smoking) last year. *(= he quit)*

*(Warm close.)*

*(Lean in.)*

And the **one phrase** to fix permanently today — it's in every professional email you'll ever write:

> ### ⭐ **"I look forward to hearing from you."**

*(Firmly.)* Not ❌ *"to hear."* ✅ **"to hearing."** 📧

*(Practise it five times.)* This one phrase, said correctly, quietly signals that your English is solid. ✅

*(Big close.)*

Tomorrow, Day 58 — **who, which, that, whose.** 🔗

These are the words that let you **join** two sentences into one — and turn *"I have a friend. He is a doctor."* into *"I have a friend **who** is a doctor."* ⭐

This is the single biggest upgrade available to an intermediate speaker. It's how you stop sounding like a beginner making short statements and start sounding like someone who **connects** ideas.

**-ing looks back at reality ⬅️. To looks forward at purpose ➡️.** See you tomorrow! 👋
