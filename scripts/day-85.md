# Day 85 — Professional WhatsApp English: Stop Sending Awkward Work Messages

**Target length:** 14–16 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** WhatsApp has completely different rules from email — and in Kerala, more work happens here than in your inbox.

**📌 Format note:** A **practical** video in the workplace-writing block. Section 3 groups messages by situation with before/after pairs. Section 4 covers WhatsApp-specific cultural habits — the good-morning image, the "hi" message, voice notes.

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Recognisable, slightly funny.)*

Your manager gets this at 9:15 in the morning:

> ❌ **"Hi sir"** 👋

*(Pause.)*

And then... **nothing.** ⏳

*(Explain.)* He's now waiting. Is something wrong? Is it urgent? Should he reply? ✅ Two minutes later:

> ❌ **"Sir are you there"**

*(Then another minute.)*

> ❌ **"Sir one small doubt"** 🔗

*(Laugh warmly.)* Four messages. Ninety seconds of his attention. **Zero information.** ✅

*(Point at camera.)*

The professional version is **one** message:

> ✅ **"Hi Rajesh — quick question: should the March invoice go to accounts or directly to the client?"** ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* One message. Complete. He can answer in five seconds. ✅

*(Lean in.)*

*(Explain.)* And here's why this matters in Kerala specifically: **more work happens on WhatsApp than on email.** ✅ Client groups, team groups, your manager at 10 pm. 📱

*(Confident.)* So your WhatsApp English **is** your professional reputation. Today, let's fix it. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:30)

*(Explain how WhatsApp differs from email — this is the key framing.)*

### 📱 WhatsApp is not email

*(The core distinction.)*

*(Explain.)* People apply **email** rules to WhatsApp, or **friend-chat** rules to work messages. ✅ Both are wrong. ✅

| | 📧 Email | 📱 Work WhatsApp |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting | always ✅ | often skip ✅ |
| Length | up to 100 words | **1–3 lines** ⭐ |
| Sign-off | yes ✅ | never ✅ |
| Formality | medium-high | **medium-low** ✅ |
| Response expectation | hours ⏰ | minutes ⚡ |
| Best for | records, formal requests, attachments 📎 | quick questions, updates, coordination ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ WhatsApp is **spoken English, typed.** ✅ Short, direct, warm. ✅

### 🔑 THE ONE RULE

*(The most important idea today.)*

> ### 👉 **One message. Complete thought. Never "hi" alone.** ✅

*(Explain.)* Every message should be **answerable** on its own. ✅ If your reader has to ask *"what do you need?"* — you failed. ⚠️

*(Show it.)*
| ⚠️ Split across messages | ✅ One complete message |
|---|---|
| *"Hi sir"* → *"Are you free"* → *"One doubt"* ⚠️ | *"Hi Rajesh — quick question about the March invoice: accounts or client directly?"* ⭐ |

*(Slogan.)*
> **One message. One complete thought.** ✅
> **ഒരു message — ഒരു പൂർണ്ണ ചിന്ത.** ⭐

### 🎯 The 3-part message shape

*(Practical structure.)*

> ### **1. Who/greeting** *(optional)* + **2. The point** + **3. What you need** ✅

**Examples:**
- ✅ *"Hi Anu — the client called about invoice 4471. Could you check if it was sent?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Quick update: reconciliation is done. Sending the file in 10 minutes."* ⭐
- ✅ *"Rajesh, I'm running about 15 minutes late for the 3 pm — starting without me is fine."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **One or two lines.** ✅ Complete. ✅

### ⏰ And the timing question

*(Practical and culturally relevant.)*

*(Explain.)* Work WhatsApp has **timing** norms: ✅

| Time | ✅ Okay? |
|---|---|
| 9 am – 7 pm | ✅ normal |
| 7 pm – 9 pm | ⚠️ okay if genuinely needed |
| After 9 pm | ⚠️ only if urgent — and **say** it's urgent ✅ |
| Sunday / holidays | ⚠️ avoid unless urgent ✅ |

*(The technique for late messages.)* ⭐
> ✅ *"Sorry for the late message — no need to reply tonight. Just so you have it for tomorrow: the client approved the revised quote."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **"No need to reply tonight"** ✅ is a genuinely considerate phrase. ✅ It removes the pressure. ⭐

## 🔍 3. MESSAGES BY SITUATION (4:30 – 13:00)

---

### 1️⃣ Asking a quick question ❓

| ❌ Before | ✅ After |
|---|---|
| *"Hi sir"* + *"one doubt"* 🔗 | *"Hi Rajesh — quick question: does the GST report need the client's PAN as well?"* ⭐ |
| *"Sir available?"* | *"Rajesh, when you get a moment — is the Alpha invoice approved?"* ✅ |
| *"Sir I have a doubt regarding the matter we discussed"* | *"About the reconciliation we discussed — should I include the Kochi branch or keep it separate?"* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Say **"question,"** not **"doubt"** ✅ 🔗 *(Day 02!)* ✅

---

### 2️⃣ Giving an update 📊

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Sir work is going on"* ⚠️ | *"Update: reconciliation is about 70% done. Should finish by 4."* ⭐ |
| *"Almost finished sir"* | *"Nearly done — I'll send it in about 30 minutes."* ✅ |
| *"Sir I have completed the same"* 📜 | *"Done — file sent to your email."* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Add a number or a time.** ✅ *"Going on"* tells your manager nothing. ✅

---

### 3️⃣ Being late or absent ⏰

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Sir I am coming"* 🔗 | *"On my way — about 20 minutes."* ⭐ |
| *"Sir traffic is there"* | *"Stuck in traffic on Bypass — I'll be about 15 minutes late for the 10 am."* ⭐ |
| *"Sir today I will not come, fever"* | *"Hi Rajesh — I'm unwell today; I've had a fever since last night. Won't be able to come in. Anu has my urgent items."* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Note the pattern: **the problem + the impact + what you've done about it.** ✅

---

### 4️⃣ Following up on no reply 🔁

*(The delicate one.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Sir?"* ⚠️ | *"Just following up on the approval — no rush, but I'm blocked until it's through."* ⭐ |
| *"Sir any update?"* | *"Any update on the SAP access request? Happy to wait if it's still in process."* ✅ |
| *"Sir you didn't reply"* ⚠️ | *"Nudging this one gently — did you get a chance to look at the quote?"* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **"Just following up"** ✅ and **"nudging this gently"** ⭐ — both are soft and professional. ✅

*(⚠️ Never.)* ❌ *"You didn't reply"* ✅ or ❌ *"I sent it yesterday"* ✅ — both read as accusations. ⚠️

---

### 5️⃣ In a client group 👥

*(Higher stakes — everyone is watching.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Noted sir"* *(to everything)* ⚠️ | *"Noted — I'll have the revised figures by Thursday."* ⭐ |
| *"Ok sir will do the needful"* 📜 | *"Sure — I'll update the sheet and share it today."* ⭐ |
| *"Sir sorry sorry my mistake, extremely sorry"* ⚠️ | *"That's my error — correcting it now and will resend within the hour."* ⭐ 🔗 |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ In a **client group**, always add **what you'll do and when.** ✅ *"Noted"* alone leaves the client uncertain. ✅

*(And on mistakes in groups.)* 🔗 *(Day 59)* ⭐ **One** clear ownership line + the fix. ✅ Never a string of apologies in front of a client — it looks worse than the error. ⚠️

---

### 6️⃣ Declining or pushing back 🚫

*(Uses Day 42's structure.)* 🔗

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Sir not possible"* ⚠️ | *"That's tight for today — could it work by tomorrow noon instead?"* ⭐ |
| *"Sir I have too much work"* | *"I've got the month-end closing today. Could this wait till tomorrow, or should I prioritise it over that?"* ⭐ |
| *"Sir I can't"* | *"I won't be able to make it today, unfortunately. Would Thursday work?"* ✅ |

*(Highlight the second one!)* ⭐ **"Should I prioritise it over that?"** ✅ — brilliant workplace English. ✅ You're not refusing; you're asking them to **choose**. ⭐

---

### 7️⃣ Asking for something 🙏

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Sir kindly send the file"* 📜 | *"Could you send me the March invoice when you get a chance?"* ⭐ 🔗 |
| *"Send asap"* ⚠️ | *"Could you send it by 4 if possible? Need it for the closing."* ✅ |
| *"Please do the needful"* 📜 | *"Could you approve this when you get a moment?"* ⭐ |

*(⚠️ On "ASAP".)* ⭐ It reads as **demanding** in English. ✅ Give a **real time** instead: *"by 4,"* *"before Friday,"* *"today if possible."* ✅

---

### 8️⃣ Thanking and closing 🙏

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Thank you so much sir, thanks a lot sir"* ⚠️ | *"Thanks, Rajesh — appreciated."* ⭐ |
| *"Ok sir ok sir"* | *"Got it, thanks."* ⭐ 🔗 |
| *"Thanks and regards"* 📜 | *(nothing needed — WhatsApp doesn't need sign-offs)* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **No sign-offs on WhatsApp.** ✅ They already know it's you. ✅

---

### 🎙️ Bonus: when to send a VOICE NOTE

*(Genuinely useful and Kerala-relevant.)*

*(Explain.)* Voice notes are common in Kerala work culture — and they're **sometimes** better, sometimes worse. ✅

| ✅ Voice note is good for | ❌ Voice note is bad for |
|---|---|
| explaining something **complex** ✅ | anything that needs to be **searched later** ⚠️ |
| when you're **driving** or busy ✅ | numbers, dates, IDs ⚠️ |
| showing **tone** *(reassurance, urgency)* ⭐ | client groups *(usually)* ⚠️ |
| long explanations you'd type badly ✅ | your **manager's boss** ⚠️ |

*(The rules for professional voice notes.)* ⭐
1. ✅ Keep it **under 45 seconds** ✅
2. ⭐ Start with the **point**: *"Quick one about the invoice —"* ✅
3. ✅ **Type the key details** as well *(numbers, dates)* ⭐
4. ✅ Don't send **five** in a row ⚠️

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Best practice: voice note for the explanation + a typed line with the key facts.** ✅

*(Example.)*
> 🎙️ *(30-second voice note explaining the mismatch)*
> ✅ *"Summary: ₹42,000 mismatch in July, two Beta invoices double-entered. Sheet attached."* ⭐

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ That combination is genuinely professional. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (13:00 – 15:30)

### Trap 1: The "hi" message 👋

*(The main one — explain why it's a problem.)*

*(Explain.)* Sending *"Hi sir"* and waiting is a **politeness** instinct — you're checking whether it's a good time before you intrude. ✅ It's genuinely considerate in intention. 🙏

*(But.)* On WhatsApp it has the **opposite** effect: ⚠️
- your reader is now **waiting** and **anxious** ⏳
- you've used **two** interactions instead of one ✅
- if they're busy, they may reply hours later — and then you **still** have to ask ⚠️

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 **Ask your question in the first message.** ✅ Politeness goes in the WORDS, not the sequence. ⭐

*(Show it.)*
- ✅ *"Hi Rajesh — when you get a moment, quick question: ___"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ *"When you get a moment"* ✅ gives them the same courtesy you intended — **without** making them wait. ⭐

### Trap 2: The good morning image 🌅

*(A real Kerala WhatsApp phenomenon. Handle it with humour and honesty.)*

*(Explain warmly.)* You know the ones — a flower, a sunrise, *"Good Morning! Have a blessed day!"* forwarded to every group at 6 am. 🌸

*(Warmly.)* In **family** and **friend** groups: completely fine. ✅ It's a way of showing care, and there's nothing wrong with it. 💙

*(But.)* In **work** groups and **client** groups: ⚠️
- it fills professional chats with **noise** ✅
- international colleagues find it **puzzling** ✅
- it can make you look **less serious** — unfairly, but it does ⚠️

*(The guideline.)*

| Group | Good morning images? |
|---|---|
| Family, friends 💙 | ✅ absolutely fine |
| Team group *(if everyone does it)* | ⚠️ follow the group norm |
| Client group 💼 | ❌ avoid |
| Your manager individually | ❌ avoid |
| International colleagues 🌍 | ❌ avoid |

*(Firmly, warmly.)* ⭐ **Read the room.** ✅ That's all. ✅

*(And a genuine alternative.)* If you want to be warm in a work group, use **words**:
- ✅ *"Morning all — hope everyone had a good weekend."* ⭐

### Trap 3: "Sir" overload 🙏 🔗 *(Days 80, 82)*

*(The written version of the same habit.)*

- ⚠️ *"Ok sir"* · *"Yes sir"* · *"Thank you sir"* · *"Noted sir"* · *"Sure sir"* ✅

*(Explain.)* Five messages, five *"sirs."* ✅ In writing, it's even more visible than in speech. ✅

*(The fix.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *Ok sir* | **Got it** ⭐ |
| *Yes sir* | **Sure** ✅ |
| *Noted sir* | **Noted — will do** ⭐ |
| *Thank you sir* | **Thanks, Rajesh** ✅ |
| *Sure sir I will do* | **Sure, I'll handle it** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Use their name instead.** ✅ *"Thanks, Rajesh"* is warmer **and** more professional than *"Thank you sir."* ✅

### Trap 4: Message-splitting 📱📱📱

*(A practical habit worth breaking.)*

*(Explain.)* Many of us type the way we speak — one thought per message: ✅

> ⚠️ *"Rajesh"*
> ⚠️ *"about the invoice"*
> ⚠️ *"the one for Alpha"*
> ⚠️ *"client is asking"*
> ⚠️ *"can you check"*

*(Emphasise.)* ⚠️ **Five notifications.** ✅ Five buzzes on his phone. ✅ And he has to read all five to understand one question. ⚠️

*(The fix.)*
- ✅ *"Rajesh — the Alpha client is asking about their invoice. Could you check if it was sent?"* ⭐

*(Practical tip.)* ⭐ Use **Shift+Enter** *(or the newline key)* to add lines **within** one message ✅ — instead of sending each line separately. ⭐

### Trap 5: Formality mismatch 📜

*(Applying email English to WhatsApp.)*

| ⚠️ Email English on WhatsApp | ✅ WhatsApp English |
|---|---|
| *Respected Sir, With reference to...* 📜 | *Hi Rajesh —* ⭐ |
| *Kindly do the needful* 📜 | *Could you check this?* ✅ |
| *Please find the attached herewith* 📜 | *Sending it now* ⭐ |
| *Thanking you, Yours faithfully* 📜 | *(nothing)* ✅ |
| *I am in receipt of the same* 📜 | *Got it* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ WhatsApp is **conversational**. ✅ Write the way you'd **speak** to that person. ✅

*(But careful the other way too!)* ⚠️ Don't go too casual with clients:
- ⚠️ *"ok bro"* · *"k"* · *"👍"* *(alone, to a client)* ⚠️
- ✅ *"Sure — will do."* ⭐

### ✅ The professional WhatsApp checklist

| ✅ | Check |
|---|---|
| ⬜ | **One** message, complete thought ⭐ |
| ⬜ | The **point** is in the message *(no lone "hi")* ✅ |
| ⬜ | Numbers, dates, names included ✅ |
| ⬜ | Their **name** instead of "sir" ✅ |
| ⬜ | Update includes a **time** or **number** ⭐ |
| ⬜ | Zero *"needful," "revert," "the same"* ✅ |
| ⬜ | Appropriate **time of day** ⏰ |
| ⬜ | Read it once before sending ✅ |
