## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Energy high. Look straight into the camera. Smile.)*

Listen to this sentence.

> ❌ *"Yesterday night I saw one movie in theatre."*

Sounds okay, right? It sounds okay because your brain is not listening to English. Your brain is listening to Malayalam.

**ഇന്നലെ രാത്രി ഞാൻ ഒരു സിനിമ കണ്ടു.**

Word by word, you translated it. Yesterday. Night. One. Movie. Saw.

But a native speaker will never say that. They say:

> ✅ *"Last night I watched a movie at the theatre."*

Three real changes. *Last* night, not yesterday night. *A* movie, not one movie. *At the* theatre, not in theatre.

*(And one that isn't an error.)* ⭐ I also said *watched* instead of *saw* — but **"I saw a movie" is perfectly correct English.** ✅ *Watched* fits viewing at home; *saw* is completely natural for going out to a film. So that one's a **choice**, not a mistake. ⭐

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow down here. This is the most important part of the whole video.)*

Before the sentences, understand one thing. **Why** does direct translation fail?

There are three reasons. Only three. Every mistake today comes from one of these.

### Reason 1: The verb sits in a different place

In Malayalam, the verb comes at the **end**. Always.

**ഞാൻ ചായ കുടിച്ചു.**
I — tea — drank.
Subject – Object – **Verb**. We call this **S-O-V**.

In English, the verb comes in the **middle**. Right after the person.

*I drank tea.*
Subject – **Verb** – Object. We call this **S-V-O**.

So English is like a train: **Who → Did what → To what.** The verb is the engine, and the engine is in front, pulling everything behind it.

Malayalam is the opposite. The engine is at the back, pushing.

That is why, when a Malayali speaks fast, the sentence comes out backwards: *"I to Kochi tomorrow going."* Your mouth is speaking English, but your grammar is still Malayalam.

### Reason 2: One Malayalam word = many English words

**അടിച്ചു.** Now look:

- Petrol അടിച്ചു → *filled* petrol
- Photo അടിച്ചു → *took* a photo
- അവനെ അടിച്ചു → *hit* him
- Lottery അടിച്ചു → *won* the lottery

Same Malayalam verb. Four different English verbs. So you cannot keep a one-to-one dictionary in your head. It will break.

### Reason 3: Malayalam has no articles, English is addicted to them

There is no word for *a*, *an*, *the* in Malayalam. So your brain simply forgets them. And then instead of "a", you say "one", because ഒരു feels like "one".

**ഒരു** is not always "one". Most of the time, **ഒരു = a**.

Remember these three reasons. Now the sentences.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് നാളെ ബാങ്കിൽ പോകണം.

Now watch what your brain does. It picks the words in Malayalam order:

- എനിക്ക് → *to me*
- നാളെ → *tomorrow*
- ബാങ്കിൽ → *in bank*
- പോകണം → *want to go / should go*

And out comes: ❌ *"To me tomorrow in bank should go."* Or the softer version: ❌ *"I want to go bank tomorrow."*

Now let's build it the **English** way. English asks questions in a fixed order.

1. **Who?** → I
2. **Doing what?** (the verb, immediately!) → have to go
3. **Where?** → to the bank
4. **When?** → tomorrow

> ✅ **"I have to go to the bank tomorrow."**

Notice the order: **Who – What – Where – When.** Person, action, place, time. Time goes at the **end** in English, but at the **beginning** in Malayalam. Feel that difference. That one switch will fix hundreds of your sentences.

Also notice: *to the bank*. Not "in bank." When you are moving, you use **to**. When you are already inside, you use **in**.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് ഉറക്കം വരുന്നു.

Direct translation: ⚠️ *"Sleep is coming to me."*

I know. It sounds so logical. In Malayalam, sleep is a guest — it *comes*. Hunger comes. Anger comes. Sleep comes.

But in English, **you don't receive the feeling. You become the feeling.**

That is the whole logic. Malayalam says *"To me, sleep is coming."* English says *"I am sleepy."* The feeling is not a visitor. The feeling is you.

1. **Who?** → I
2. **Am** (because I am describing myself, not doing an action)
3. **The feeling as a describing word** → sleepy

> ✅ **"I am sleepy."** or **"I am feeling sleepy."**

Now apply the same logic:

- എനിക്ക് വിശക്കുന്നു → ⚠️ *Hunger is coming* → ✅ **I am hungry.**
- എനിക്ക് ദേഷ്യം വരുന്നു → ⚠️ *Anger is coming to me* → ✅ **I am getting angry.**
- എനിക്ക് പേടിയാകുന്നു → ⚠️ *Fear is coming* → ✅ **I am scared.**
- എനിക്ക് ബോറടിക്കുന്നു → ❌ *Bore is coming* → ✅ **I am bored.**

One rule. Five sentences fixed. This is how you learn fast — learn the logic, not the list.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

Now, the biggest trap of all. Number 3 on that list. **"I am having."**

Almost every Malayali says this:

> ❌ *"I am having a car."*
> ❌ *"I am having a headache."*
> ❌ *"She is having long hair."*

Why do we all make the same mistake? Because of one Malayalam word: **ഉണ്ട്.**

**എന്റെ കയ്യിൽ ഒരു car ഉണ്ട്.** — ഉണ്ട് means "exists / is there." It feels like a *present, right now* thing. And in your English class, they taught you that "right now" means **-ing**. So your brain says: right now + have = *am having*. Perfectly logical. Completely wrong.

Here is the real English logic. **"Have" for owning is not an action.** You are not *doing* anything. The car just belongs to you, while you sleep, while you eat, while you do nothing. Nothing is happening. And the **-ing** tense is for things **in progress** — not for states like owning. *(The **-ing** form does other jobs too, which we'll meet later.)*

So:
- Owning → **I have a car.** ✅
- Family → **I have two sisters.** ✅
- Pain → **I have a headache.** ✅
- Appearance → **She has long hair.** ✅

Then when *is* "having" correct? Only when "have" stops meaning *own* and starts meaning a real action — usually **eating, drinking, or experiencing**.

- ✅ *I am having lunch.* (= eating)
- ✅ *We are having a great time.* (= experiencing)
- ✅ *They are having a meeting.* (= doing)

**Quick test:** Can you *see* it happening from outside? Then -ing is fine. Can't see it? Use plain *have*.

Nobody can look at you and see you owning a car. So: **I have a car.**

*(Point at camera.)* From today, "I am having a car" is dead. Finished. Never again.
