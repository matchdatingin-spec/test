# Day 23 — Each, Every, or All? Stop Using Them Incorrectly

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *Each* = one by one. *Every* = the whole group as a rule. *All* = the group together. And two of them are secretly **singular**.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Quick quiz energy.)*

Which one is correct?

> *"Every student are present."*
> *"Every student is present."*

*(Pause.)* If you said the first one — you're in the majority. And you're wrong. 😊

It's **"Every student IS present."**

*(Confused gesture.)* But wait — "every student" means **many** students! Fifty students! How can it be **is**?

*(Point at camera.)*

And then this one:

> ❌ *"All the students is present."*

Now it flips! **All** takes *are*. 😄

*(Lean in.)* I know. It feels random. But it's not — there's a beautiful logic hiding here. In Malayalam, **എല്ലാ വിദ്യാർത്ഥികളും** and **ഓരോ വിദ്യാർത്ഥിയും** both feel plural. English sees them completely differently.

Today, 10 minutes, permanent clarity. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Use your hands to show the pictures.)*

### 👤👤👤 EACH = one by one, separately. I see them individually.

Picture a teacher walking to **each desk, one at a time**.

- ✅ *Each student got a certificate.* → I gave them one at a time
- ✅ *Each of us has a different opinion.* → separately
- ✅ *She kissed each child on the forehead.* → one, then the next

**EACH = individually. Malayalam: ഓരോ / ഓരോരുത്തരും**
**Grammar: SINGULAR** → *each student **is***

### 💯 EVERY = all of them, as a general rule. No exceptions.

Picture a **rule on the wall** that applies to the whole group.

- ✅ *Every student must wear a uniform.* → the rule, 100%, no exceptions
- ✅ *Every Sunday I visit my parents.* → always, without fail
- ✅ *Every human needs water.* → a universal truth

**EVERY = all, without exception, as one rule. Malayalam: എല്ലാ / ഓരോ**
**Grammar: SINGULAR** → *every student **is***

### 👥 ALL = the whole group together, as one crowd.

Picture the **whole class in one photo**.

- ✅ *All the students are present.* → the group ✅
- ✅ *All my friends came.* → the whole gang
- ✅ *I ate all the food.* → the whole amount

**ALL = the whole group / whole amount. Malayalam: എല്ലാം / മുഴുവൻ**
**Grammar: PLURAL** → *all students **are***

### 🔑 The grammar secret — this is the key

*(Explain it slowly. This is the "aha" moment.)*

Why are *each* and *every* **singular**?

Because English looks at the **noun right after them**, and that noun is **singular**:

- *each **student*** ← one student! 👤
- *every **student*** ← one student! 👤
- *all **students*** ← many students! 👥

*(Excited.)* That's it! **Each** and **every** literally point at **one** person at a time. Even though the total is many, the grammar follows the word beside it.

*(Slogan — say it three times.)*
> **Each + singular. Every + singular. All + plural.**
> **each student is · every student is · all students are**

### The quick comparison table

| Word | Picture | Noun | Verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| **each** | 👤 one at a time | singular | **is / has** |
| **every** | 💯 the whole rule | singular | **is / has** |
| **all** | 👥 the crowd | plural | **are / have** |

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എല്ലാ വിദ്യാർത്ഥികളും യൂണിഫോം ഇടണം.

*(Look at the Malayalam.)* **വിദ്യാർത്ഥികളും** — that **-കൾ** is a **plural** marker! Malayalam is clearly saying "students," many of them.

So your brain says: many → ❌ *"Every students are wearing uniform."*

*(Two errors.)* Wrong plural, wrong verb.

Build it *(using EVERY — a rule)*:

1. **Is this a rule for all, with no exceptions?** → Yes → **every**
2. **Every takes SINGULAR** → student *(no -s!)*
3. **Verb** → must wear
4. **What** → a uniform

> ✅ **"Every student must wear a uniform."**

Now the same idea with **ALL**:

> ✅ **"All students must wear a uniform."** ← plural ✅

*(Show them side by side.)*

| ✅ Correct | Note |
|---|---|
| **Every student must wear a uniform.** | singular noun, singular verb |
| **All students must wear a uniform.** | plural noun, plural verb |
| **All the students must wear uniforms.** | with "the" = a specific group |
| ❌ Every students... | never! *every* + plural is impossible |
| ❌ All student... | never! *all* + singular countable is impossible |

*(Rule.)* **Every takes ONE countable thing. All takes a plural — or an uncountable amount** *(all the rice, all day)*. ✅ That's why they don't swap.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഓരോ വിദ്യാർത്ഥിക്കും ഒരു സർട്ടിഫിക്കറ്റ് കിട്ടി.

*(Find the clue.)* **ഓരോ** = "each, one by one." Malayalam is marking **individuality** here! → **EACH** ✅

Build it:

1. **One by one?** → Yes → **each**
2. **Each takes SINGULAR** → student
3. **Verb, singular + past** → got / received
4. **What** → a certificate

> ✅ **"Each student got a certificate."**

*(The "each of" pattern — very useful.)*

> ✅ **"Each of the students got a certificate."**

*(Note carefully!)* After **"each of,"** the noun is **plural** — but the **verb stays singular**!

- ✅ *Each of the students **is** present.* ← plural noun, singular verb 🤯
- ✅ *Each of us **has** a different opinion.*
- ✅ *Each of them **wants** something different.*

*(Explain.)* Because the real subject is **"each"** — one at a time — not "the students." The **"of the students"** part is just extra information.

*(Same for "every one of.")*
- ✅ *Every one of them **is** talented.*
- ✅ *Every one of us **makes** mistakes.*

*(Note the spelling.)* **"every one"** *(two words)* = each individual. **"everyone"** *(one word)* = all people.
- ✅ *Every one of the mangoes was sweet.* ← individual mangoes
- ✅ *Everyone came to the party.* ← all people

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Word & why |
|---|---|---|
| Describing your routine | **I go to the gym every morning.** | routine → every |
| Distributing sweets | **I gave each child two sweets.** | one by one → each |
| Describing your team | **All my colleagues are very helpful.** | whole group → all |
| A universal truth | **Every human being makes mistakes.** | no exceptions → every |
| Finishing your food | **I ate all the rice.** | whole amount → all |

*(Highlight row 1.)* **Time expressions love "every":**
- ✅ *every day, every morning, every week, every year*
- ✅ *every two hours* ← "every" + number = repeated interval ✅
- ✅ *every other day* = alternate days ✅

*(Careful!)* ❌ *"all days"* → ✅ **"every day."** ❌ *"all mornings"* → ✅ **"every morning."** ✅

*(Highlight row 5.)* **"All" with uncountable nouns** takes singular:
- ✅ *All the rice **is** finished.* ← rice is uncountable ✅
- ✅ *All the money **was** spent.*
- ✅ *All the work **is** done.*

*(Rule.)* **All follows the noun after it.** Plural noun → *are*. Uncountable noun → *is*.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "എല്ലാവരും" → plural verb with "everyone"

*(A huge one.)* **എല്ലാവരും** feels very plural — "all people." So we say:

- ❌ *Everyone are here* → ✅ **Everyone is here.**
- ❌ *Everybody have a phone* → ✅ **Everybody has a phone.**
- ❌ *Everything are ready* → ✅ **Everything is ready.**

*(Explain.)* **Every-** words are **always singular** in English. Every single one:

| Word | Verb |
|---|---|
| **everyone** | **is / has / was** |
| **everybody** | **is / has / was** |
| **everything** | **is / has / was** |
| **someone / somebody** | **is / has** |
| **anyone / anybody** | **is / has** |
| **nobody / no one** | **is / has** |

*(Memory trick.)* Look at the end of the word — **-one, -body, -thing.** All singular words! 👤 That's your clue.

*(Practise — say these with me.)*
1. Everyone **is** welcome. ✅
2. Everybody **has** a chance. ✅
3. Everything **is** fine. ✅
4. Nobody **knows** the answer. ✅

*(Note the exception in casual speech.)* When you refer back to *everyone*, English uses **they** *(not "he" or "she")*:
- ✅ *Everyone **is** here, and **they** are ready.* ✅
- ✅ *Everybody should bring **their** ID.* ✅

Yes — singular verb, plural pronoun. English is beautifully inconsistent. 😄

### Trap 2: "All" placed like Malayalam

Malayalam puts **എല്ലാം** in flexible positions, so we produce:

- ❌ *All we are going* → ✅ **We are all going.** / **All of us are going.**
- ❌ *All they came* → ✅ **They all came.** / **All of them came.**
- ❌ *All you must submit* → ✅ **You must all submit.** / **All of you must submit.**

*(Rule for pronouns.)* With **we/they/you**, *all* goes **after** the pronoun, or use **"all of":**
- ✅ *We **all** know that.* ✅
- ✅ ***All of us** know that.* ✅
- ❌ *All we know that.* ❌

*(Practise the two shapes.)*
- **[pronoun] + all** → *they all, we all, you all* ✅
- **all of + [pronoun]** → *all of them, all of us, all of you* ✅

### Trap 3: "All" for "every day"

- ❌ *I study English all days* → ✅ **I study English every day.**
- ❌ *He comes all mornings* → ✅ **He comes every morning.**
- ❌ *All time I forget* → ✅ **I always forget.** / **I forget every time.**

*(Rule.)* **Repeated time → EVERY. Whole quantity → ALL.**
- ✅ *every day* = each day, repeatedly 🔄
- ✅ *all day* = from morning to night, one long day ☀️

*(Feel the difference — this is important!)*
- ✅ *I work **every day**.* = Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday... 🔄
- ✅ *I worked **all day**.* = from 9 AM to 9 PM, one day 😩

*(Same pattern.)*
- *all night* = the whole night 🌙 vs *every night* = each night 🔄
- *all week* = the whole week vs *every week* = each week

### Trap 4: "Each and every" — is it wrong?

*(Fun question.)* No! It's correct — and it's an **emphasis** phrase:
- ✅ *Each and every student must attend.* ✅ = absolutely all of them, strongly

*(But note.)* It's still **singular**: *each and every student **is**...* ✅

*(Advice.)* Use it for emphasis in speeches. In normal conversation, just say *every*.

### Bonus: "both" — the two-person version

*(Quick expansion.)*
- **both** = exactly two ✌️ → *Both my brothers **are** engineers.* ← plural ✅
- **all** = the whole group 👥 *(usually three or more — but it can cover two as well)*
- **each** = any number, one at a time

- ✅ *Both of them are coming.* ← two people
- ✅ *All of them are coming.* ← the whole group *(use **both** when you know it's exactly two)*
