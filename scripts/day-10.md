# Day 10 — Should vs Must vs Have To: How Strong Is Each One?

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** A pressure meter from 40% to 100% — so you never sound rude when you meant to be helpful.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Slightly shocked tone.)*

Your colleague looks tired. You want to help. You want to say **നീ കുറച്ച് വിശ്രമിക്കണം.**

And you say:

> ❌ *"You must take rest."*

*(Pause.)* You **meant**: "Please rest, I care about you."
They **heard**: "Take rest. That's an order."

*(Laugh a little.)* And then this one, in a job interview:

> ⚠️ *"I must get this job."*

That sounds desperate! Not confident. Desperate.

*(Lean in.)*

Here's the problem. Malayalam has one main ending for all of this — **-ണം** (വേണം, പോകണം, ചെയ്യണം). One ending. But English has a whole **staircase** of strength: *should, ought to, need to, have to, must.*

Choose the wrong step, and you sound rude, or weak, or desperate. Today we build the staircase. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

Picture a **pressure meter**.

| Word | Pressure | What it really means |
|---|---|---|
| **could** | 20% | Just an option |
| **should** | 50% | Good advice. Your choice. |
| **need to** | 70% | Important. Please do it. |
| **have to** | 90% | No real choice — a rule or situation forces you |
| **must** | 100% | Absolute. Law, safety, or my strong personal feeling |

*(Say clearly.)* Now, the **two most important** things to understand:

### 1. SHOULD = advice. The person can say no.

- ✅ *You should drink more water.* → Good idea. Up to you.
- ✅ *You should apply for that job.* → My suggestion.
- ✅ *We should leave early.* → Sounds sensible.

**Should is a friend giving advice.** Warm. Safe. Use it 80% of the time.

### 2. HAVE TO = the rule forces you. MUST = I force you.

This is the difference nobody explains. Listen:

- ✅ *I **have to** wear a helmet.* → **The law** says so. Outside pressure. Not my choice.
- ✅ *You **must** wear a helmet.* → **I** am telling you. Inside pressure. My authority.

*(Slogan.)* **HAVE TO often = pressure from outside. MUST often = pressure from me.** ✅ *(A useful tendency — written rules use "must" too: "Passengers must wear seatbelts.")*

That's why *"You must take rest"* sounds like an order — you put **yourself** in charge of them.

And that's why, in real spoken English, **native speakers use "have to" far more than "must."** *Must* sounds heavy, formal, almost like a signboard: *"Passengers must show their tickets."*

### The safe strategy

*(Point at camera.)*

> **Giving advice to a person → SHOULD.**
> **Talking about rules and necessities → HAVE TO.**
> **Save MUST for signs, laws, and very strong feelings.**

Follow that, and you will never sound rude again.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** നീ കുറച്ച് വിശ്രമിക്കണം.

Look at **വിശ്രമിക്കണം** — the *-ണം* ending. Notice: in Malayalam, this **one ending** covers "should," "have to," and "must." The listener figures out the strength from your **tone**, your face, your relationship.

English can't rely on tone that much. English needs you to **choose the word**.

So ask: am I ordering, or advising? → **Advising.** → **should**

Build it:

1. **Who** → You
2. **Advice helper** → should
3. **Plain verb** → get / take *(careful — see below!)*
4. **What** → some rest

> ✅ **"You should get some rest."**

*(Vocabulary fix inside the fix!)* ❌ *"Take rest"* is Indian English. Native English says:
- ✅ **get some rest**
- ✅ **take a break**
- ✅ **rest for a while**

Never ❌ *"take rest."* Say **"get some rest."** Practise it: *"You should get some rest."*

*(Now soften it even more.)* Real caring English uses softeners:
- ✅ *You should probably get some rest.*
- ✅ *Maybe you should get some rest.*
- ✅ *I think you should get some rest.*

*(Big tip.)* **"I think you should..."** is the kindest advice phrase in English. It says "this is only my opinion." Malayalis rarely use it — start today.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് നാളെ ഓഫീസിൽ പോകണം.

Same *-ണം* ending. But now the meaning is different! This isn't advice — **the job requires it.** There is outside pressure.

So: **have to**.

Build it:

1. **Who** → I
2. **Necessity from outside** → have to
3. **Plain verb** → go
4. **Where** → to the office
5. **When** → tomorrow

> ✅ **"I have to go to the office tomorrow."**

*(Compare the three versions and feel the difference.)*

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I **should** go to the office tomorrow.* | It'd be a good idea. Maybe I won't. |
| *I **have to** go to the office tomorrow.* | No choice. My job requires it. |
| *I **must** go to the office tomorrow.* | Very urgent — I feel strongly about it. |

*(Grammar note — important.)* **have to** changes form with the person and the tense. *must* never changes:

| | Present | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have to** go | **had to** go | **will have to** go |
| he / she / it | **has to** go | **had to** go | **will have to** go |
| must | **must** go | ❌ *no past form!* → use *had to* | *"must go tomorrow"* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* **Must has no past form.** So for past obligation, use *had to*:
- ❌ *Yesterday I must go to hospital.*
- ✅ **Yesterday I had to go to the hospital.**

This is a very common Malayali error. **Past necessity = had to.** Always.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Which word & why |
|---|---|---|
| Friend has a bad cough | **I think you should see a doctor.** | advice → should |
| Explaining office rules | **We have to submit the report by Friday.** | outside rule → have to |
| Airport signboard | **Passengers must carry a valid ID.** | law/formal → must |
| Cancelling a plan | **Sorry, I can't come — I have to work late.** | necessity → have to |
| Strong personal feeling | **You must try this biryani!** | enthusiasm → must ✅ |

*(Highlight row 5.)* Yes! **Must** has one lovely friendly use — **strong recommendation with excitement**:
- ✅ *You must watch this movie!*
- ✅ *You must visit Munnar!*
- ✅ *This is a must-try dish.*

Here *must* isn't an order — it's enthusiasm. Tone changes everything.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: The negative flip — this one is dangerous

*(Serious tone.)* Listen carefully. This is the most important 60 seconds of today's video.

*must* and *have to* are close in the positive. But in the **negative**, they mean **opposite things**:

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ *You **mustn't** tell him.* | **It is forbidden.** Do NOT tell him. 🚫 |
| ✅ *You **don't have to** tell him.* | **It's not necessary.** You can if you want. 🆗 |

*(Repeat with force.)* **mustn't = forbidden. Don't have to = optional.**

Now imagine the mistake. Your friend asks *"Should I bring food?"* and you want to say **ഭക്ഷണം കൊണ്ടുവരേണ്ട ആവശ്യമില്ല** — "no need."

- ⚠️ *You mustn't bring food.* → "Food is banned!" 😳
- ✅ **You don't have to bring food.** → "No need, but you can." ✅

*(Point at camera.)* Learn this phrase perfectly: **"You don't have to."** It is how English says ആവശ്യമില്ല.

- ✅ *You don't have to wait for me.*
- ✅ *You don't have to pay now.*
- ✅ *You don't have to come if you're busy.*

And *"shouldn't"* sits in between — gentle warning:
- ✅ *You shouldn't eat so much sugar.* = it's not good for you *(advice, not a ban)*

### Trap 2: "വേണം" is not always "must"

**വേണം** appears everywhere in Malayalam, and it becomes different English things:

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| എനിക്ക് ഒരു ചായ വേണം | I must a tea | **I'd like a tea.** / **Can I get a tea?** |
| എനിക്ക് പോകണം | I must go *(too strong)* | **I have to go.** / **I need to go.** |
| നിനക്ക് ഇത് വായിക്കണം | You must read this | **You should read this.** |
| എനിക്ക് English പഠിക്കണം | I must learn English | **I want to learn English.** |

*(Key insight.)* Notice: **വേണം** sometimes means **want**, sometimes **need**, sometimes **should**. So before translating, ask yourself:

> *"Is this a **desire**, a **necessity**, or **advice**?"*

- Desire → **want to / would like to**
- Necessity → **have to / need to**
- Advice → **should**

### Trap 3: Forgetting "to"

- ❌ *I must to go* → ✅ **I must go.** *(must takes NO to)*
- ❌ *I have go* → ✅ **I have to go.** *(obligation *have to* always keeps its *to*)*
- ❌ *I should to study* → ✅ **I should study.** *(should takes NO to)*
- ❌ *I need go* → ✅ **I need to go.** *(need takes to)*

*(The real rule — it's about word type, not word length.)* **Modals take a plain verb: must, should, can, will, might, could.** ✅ **Semi-modals keep "to": have to, need to, want to — and *ought to*, which is a modal that keeps its *to*.** ✅
