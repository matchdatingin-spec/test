# Day 27 — Have Been, Has Been, or Had Been? Easy Timeline Method

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Draw one line. *Has/have been* touches NOW. *Had been* stops **before** another past event. That's it.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confident, mystery-solving energy.)*

Three sentences. Which is correct?

> *"I **have been** working here since 2020."*
> *"He **has been** working here since 2020."*
> *"They **had been** working here since 2020."*

*(Pause.)* Trick question — **all three can be correct!** They just mean different things, and they need different subjects.

*(Then the real problem.)* But here's what Malayalis actually say:

> ❌ *"He have been working here."*
> ❌ *"I had been living here since 2020."* *(when you still live there!)*
> ❌ *"I am working here since 2020."*

*(Point at camera.)*

Three completely different errors. And they all come from **one gap**: Malayalam doesn't split time this finely. **ചെയ്യുന്നു, ചെയ്തു, ചെയ്തിരുന്നു** — three broad buckets. English has **twelve**.

*(Lean in, reassuring.)*

But today, I'm not going to give you twelve rules. I'm going to give you **one line** — a timeline you can draw with your finger. And every sentence will find its place on it. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Draw a horizontal line in the air. Left = past. Right = now.)*

```
PAST ──────────────────── NOW ────► FUTURE
```

### 1️⃣ HAVE / HAS BEEN = started in the past, TOUCHES NOW ✋

The action **reaches** the present moment. It's **still happening** *(or just stopped)*.

```
PAST ══════════════✋NOW
      (started)      (still going!)
```

- ✅ *I **have been** working here for 3 years.* → I still work here ✅
- ✅ *She **has been** waiting since this morning.* → she's still waiting ✅

*(Dialect note.)* ⚠️ Bare *"since morning"* is common **Indian English**; broader international English adds the determiner: **since *this* morning**. ✅ Same for *since evening → since this evening*. ⭐
- ✅ *It **has been** raining all day.* → it's still raining ✅

**HAVE/HAS BEEN = started before, and it reaches now.**

*(Small precision note.)* ✅ It can also mean the action **just** stopped, with the result still visible: ✅ *I'm tired — I've been running.* ✅ The link to **now** is what matters, not that it's still in progress.

### 2️⃣ HAD BEEN = it was already going before another PAST event ⛔

There are **two** past moments. *Had been* covers the **earlier** one.

```
PAST ═══════⛔ (2nd past event) ──── NOW
   (started)  (then this happened)
```

- ✅ *I **had been** waiting for an hour when he finally came.* → the hour of waiting was already done by then ⛔
- ✅ *She **had been** working there for 5 years before she resigned.* → then she left
- ✅ *It **had been** raining, so the road was wet.* → rain came first, road stayed wet

**HAD BEEN = it was already under way before that past moment. It doesn't connect to now.**

*(Careful — one honest correction to a myth.)* ⚠️ *Had been* does **not** always mean the action stopped. ✅ *She had been working there for five years when she was promoted — and she stayed another ten.* ✅ It tells you the action was **already running** at that past point; whether it continued afterwards depends on the story. ⭐

### 🔑 The 3-second decision

*(Big on screen.)*

> **Does it connect to NOW?** → **have / has been** ✅
> **Was it already going before another PAST event?** → **had been** ⛔

*(Slogan.)*
> **HAVE BEEN touches now. HAD BEEN stops in the past.**

### And have vs has? — the easy part

| Subject | Word |
|---|---|
| I, you, we, they | **have** been |
| he, she, it, *one person's name* | **has** been |
| everyone / nobody / each *(Day 23!)* | **has** been |

*(Memory trick.)* **He, she, it → add the S.** *He **has**, she **has**, it **has**.* Everyone else → *have*. ✅

*(Note.)* **Had** is easy — it **never** changes! *I had, he had, they had.* Same for everyone. ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ 2020 മുതൽ ഇവിടെ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു. *(and I still work here)*

*(Notice the trap.)* **ചെയ്യുന്നു** looks **present**. So we say ❌ *"I am working here since 2020."* *(Day 19's error!)*

Build it:

1. **Does it touch NOW?** → Yes, I still work here ✋ → **have been**
2. **Who** → I → **have** *(not has)*
3. **been** → been
4. **Verb + ing** → working
5. **Where** → here
6. **Starting point** *(Day 19!)* → since 2020

> ✅ **"I have been working here since 2020."**

*(Spoken form.)*
> ✅ **"I've been working here since 2020."** ✅

*(The he/she version.)*
> ✅ **"He has been working here since 2020."**
> ✅ **"He's been working here since 2020."** ✅

*(Emphasise the contraction!)* Notice: **"he's been"** — that **'s** is *has*, not *is*! Malayalis often misread this.
- *He's been working* = He **has** been working ✅
- *He's working* = He **is** working ✅

*(Practise the sounds.)* **I've been. He's been. They've been. She's been.** Say each three times. These contractions are the sound of fluent English. 🔊

*(Common error.)*
- ❌ *He have been working* → ✅ **He has been working.** ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** അവൻ വരുമ്പോൾ ഞാൻ ഒരു മണിക്കൂറായി കാത്തിരിക്കുകയായിരുന്നു.

*(Notice — two past events!)*
1. I was waiting *(earlier)* ⏳
2. He came *(later)* ✋

The waiting **stopped** when he came. It does **not** touch now. → **HAD BEEN** ⛔

Build it:

1. **Two past events, earlier one?** → **had been**
2. **Who** → I
3. **had been** → had been
4. **Verb + ing** → waiting
5. **How long** → for an hour
6. **The second event** *(simple past!)* → when he came

> ✅ **"I had been waiting for an hour when he came."**

*(Very important structure note!)*

Notice the **two different tenses** in one sentence:
- The **earlier** action → *had been waiting* ⏳
- The **later** action → *came* *(simple past)* ✋

*(Rule.)* **The earlier past action gets "had." The later one gets simple past.** ✅

*(Practise the shape.)*
- ✅ *I **had been** studying for 3 hours when the power **went** off.*
- ✅ *She **had been** crying before I **got** there.*
- ✅ *They **had been** playing when it **started** to rain.*

*(Simple version — very useful!)* And honestly? In everyday speech, native speakers often just use the simpler form:
- ✅ *I **was waiting** for an hour when he came.* ✅ Also perfectly natural!

*(Reassurance.)* So don't panic about *had been*. It's mostly for **writing** and **storytelling**. In conversation, *was waiting* is fine. ✅

*(But you MUST know it for:)* IELTS writing, formal emails, and storytelling. ⭐

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Which & why |
|---|---|---|
| Current job (interview!) | **I've been working in sales for five years.** | still true ✋ |
| Explaining a delay | **She's been feeling unwell since Monday.** | still true ✋ |
| Telling a story | **I had been saving money for a year before I bought the bike.** | stopped in past ⛔ |
| Weather right now | **It's been raining since this morning.** | still raining ✋ |
| Explaining why you're tired | **I'd been travelling all night, so I was exhausted.** | stopped in past ⛔ |

*(Highlight row 1.)* **"I've been working in ___ for ___ years."** ⭐ Write this down. Memorise it. Use it in **every** interview. It's the single most valuable sentence pattern for describing your career. ✅

*(Highlight row 4.)* **"It's been raining since this morning."** So natural! Not ❌ *"Rain is coming from morning."* ✅

### The three-way comparison

*(This clarifies everything — show it.)*

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I **work** here.* | general fact, present ✅ |
| *I **am working** here.* | right now, this moment ✅ |
| *I **have been working** here for 3 years.* | started 3 years ago, still here ✋ |
| *I **worked** here.* | past, finished, I left ❌ |
| *I **had been working** here before I moved.* | earlier past, then something changed ⛔ |

*(Point out.)* Look at the **difference in your career story!**
- *I **work** at TCS.* → I'm there now ✅
- *I **worked** at TCS.* → I left ❌

*(Interview warning!)* If you say ⚠️ *"I worked at Infosys"* about your **current** job, the interviewer thinks you **quit**! Say **"I work at Infosys"** or **"I've been working at Infosys."** ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "-ചെയ്യുന്നു" for continuing actions

*(The root cause.)*

Malayalam uses **present tense** for actions that started in the past and continue:

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| ഞാൻ 3 വർഷമായി പഠിക്കുന്നു | I am studying since 3 years | **I've been studying for 3 years.** |
| അവൾ രാവിലെ മുതൽ കാത്തിരിക്കുന്നു | She is waiting from morning | **She's been waiting since this morning.** |
| മഴ പെയ്യുന്നു കുറേ നേരമായി | Rain is coming long time | **It's been raining for a long time.** |

*(The formula — write it down.)*
> ### 👉 Duration + still happening = HAVE/HAS BEEN + verb-ing

*(Say it three times.)* **Have been + ing. Have been + ing. Have been + ing.** ✅

### Trap 2: "Had been" over-used

*(A very Malayali habit!)* Many learners think *had been* sounds "more advanced," so they use it everywhere:

- ❌ *I had been living in Kochi since 2020.* *(if you still live there!)* → ✅ **I've been living in Kochi since 2020.** ✅
- ❌ *He had been my friend for 10 years.* *(if still friends!)* → ✅ **He's been my friend for 10 years.** ✅

*(Rule — say it firmly.)* **"Had" moves your viewpoint back to a PAST moment.** If you're speaking from **today**, use *have/has been*. ⛔

*(Self-check.)* Ask: **"Am I measuring up to today, or up to a moment in the past?"**
- Up to today → **have/has been** ✅
- Up to an earlier past moment → **had been** ✅

*(Advanced flag — say it once.)* ⭐ *Had been* is fine even for things that are still true, **if** your viewpoint is in the past: ✅ *By 2020, I **had been** living in Kochi for ten years* — and I still live here. ✅ The test is your **viewpoint**, not whether the fact ended.

### Trap 3: "Has" vs "have" with names and groups

- ❌ *My brother have been working there* → ✅ **My brother has been working there.** ✅
- ❌ *Everyone have been waiting* → ✅ **Everyone has been waiting.** ✅ *(Day 23!)*
- ❌ *My parents has been living there* → ✅ **My parents have been living there.** ✅

*(Rule.)* **One person/thing → has. More than one → have.** ✅

*(Careful with these.)*
- *my family* → **has** ✅ *(one unit)* — but *my family **are** all doctors* is also used in British English 😄
- *the police* → **have** ✅ *(always plural in English!)*
- *the news* → **has** ✅ *(singular, Day 21!)*

### Trap 4: "-ing" with state verbs

*(Connects to Day 19.)* Some verbs are **states**, not actions. They skip the *-ing*:

**know, have (own), understand, believe, like, love, want, need, be**

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I have been knowing him for 5 years | **I have known him for 5 years.** |
| She has been having a car since 2020 | **She has had a car since 2020.** |
| I have been wanting... *(actually okay!)* | ✅ *I've been wanting to tell you something.* ✅ |

*(Rule.)*
- **Action** *(work, study, wait, play, rain)* → **have been + ing** ✅
- **State** *(know, own, be)* → **have + V3** ✅

*(Examples.)*
- ✅ *I've **been working** here for 3 years.* ← action
- ✅ *I've **known** him for 3 years.* ← state
- ✅ *She's **been living** in Dubai for 2 years.* ← action
- ✅ *She's **been** sick for 2 days.* ← state *(be → been)*

### The one-question self-check

*(Give them a practical tool.)*

Before you speak, ask **three** questions:

1. **Is it still true now?** → *have/has been* ✋
2. **Did it stop before another past event?** → *had been* ⛔
3. **Is my subject he/she/it?** → *has*, otherwise *have* ✅

*(Drill — say these six with me!)*
1. I've been waiting for an hour. ✅
2. She's been living here since 2019. ✅
3. They've been friends for years. ✅
4. He has been working hard. ✅
5. I had been sleeping when you called. ✅
6. It's been raining all day. ✅
