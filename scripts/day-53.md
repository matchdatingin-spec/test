# Day 53 — Do, Does, and Did: Questions and Negatives Made Easy

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** Three helper words that build every question and every negative in English — and one rule that makes them foolproof.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Quick, energetic.)*

Yesterday: **am, is, are, was, were.** ✅ Today: the other three helpers.

Listen to these:

> ❌ *"Where you going?"*
> ❌ *"He don't like coffee."*
> ❌ *"I didn't went there."*
> ❌ *"Does he knows the answer?"*
> ⚠️ *"You like tea?"* *(only works as a "let me check" question)*

*(Point at camera.)*

Five sentences. All wrong. And all fixed by **three words**: **do, does, did.** ✅

*(Lean in.)*

Here's why this is hard for us. In Malayalam, questions and negatives are built **inside the verb**:

- **വരുന്നു** → statement
- **വരുന്നുണ്ടോ?** → question *(add -ഓ)*
- **വരുന്നില്ല** → negative *(add -ഇല്ല)*

*(Emphasise.)* **No extra word.** Everything happens in the verb's tail. ✅

But English **hires a helper** — a separate word that arrives and takes over the job. Today, we learn to hire it properly. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. This is foundational.)*

### The table 🔧

| Subject | Present | Past |
|---|---|---|
| **I / you / we / they** | **do** | **did** |
| **he / she / it** | **does** | **did** |

*(Emphasise.)* **Did** works for **everyone** in the past — no changes! ✅ Easy. 😌

### 🔑 THE BOSS RULE *(from Day 03 — the most important rule in English grammar)*

*(Bring it back and make it central.)*

> ### 👉 When do/does/did arrives, it takes ALL the work. The main verb goes PLAIN.

*(Explain.)* Think of *do/does/did* as the **boss** and the main verb as the **worker**. When the boss walks in, the boss handles the **tense** and the **person**, and the worker relaxes into its simplest form. 👔

*(Show it.)*

| Statement | Question | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| He **likes** tea. | **Does** he **like** tea? | He **doesn't like** tea. |
| They **went** home. | **Did** they **go** home? | They **didn't go** home. |
| She **knows** him. | **Does** she **know** him? | She **doesn't know** him. |

*(Point out.)* Look at what happened to the main verb:
- *likes* → **like** *(the -s moved to "does")* ✅
- *went* → **go** *(the past moved to "did")* ✅
- *knows* → **know** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **The tense and the -s move to the boss. The worker becomes plain.** ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **One boss, one job. Never two.**
> **Boss വന്നാൽ verb ചെറുതാകും.** ✅

### 🚫 The critical warning: don't use "do" when "be" is already there

*(This prevents a big error.)*

*(Explain.)* If your sentence already has **am/is/are/was/were**, or **can/will/should**, or **have/has as the perfect helper** — **do not** add *do*! Those words are **already** helpers, and they do the job themselves. ✅

*(One trap.)* ⚠️ When *have* is the **main verb** meaning "own," it **does** need *do* in modern English: ✅ *"**Do** you **have** a car?"* ⭐ *("Have you a car?" is correct but old-fashioned.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Do you are ready? | **Are you ready?** ✅ |
| Does he is coming? | **Is he coming?** ✅ |
| Do you can swim? | **Can you swim?** ✅ |
| Did you have finished? | **Have you finished?** ✅ |

*(Rule.)*

> ### 👉 Only hire "do" when there is NO other helper.

*(The test.)* Look at your statement:
- *He **is** tired.* → has a helper ✅ → *Is he tired?* ✅
- *He **works** here.* → no helper → **hire "does"** ✅ → *Does he work here?* ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** നിങ്ങൾ എവിടെ പോകുന്നു?

*(Notice the Malayalam.)* **പോകുന്നു** with a question word — but the word **order doesn't change** in Malayalam. ✅ That's why we produce ❌ *"Where you going?"* ✅

Build it:

1. **Question word** → Where
2. **Is there a helper in the statement?** *(You go...)* → No! → **hire "do"** ✅
3. **Helper for "you"** → do
4. **Person** → you
5. **Plain verb** → go

> ✅ **"Where do you go?"** ← habit 🔁

*(But wait — check the meaning!)* If you mean **right now**, you need *be + -ing* instead:

> ✅ **"Where are you going?"** ← right now ⏺️ ✅

*(Emphasise the difference.)* 🔗 *(Day 47)*
- *Where **do** you **go** for lunch?* → habit ✅
- *Where **are** you **going**?* → right now ✅

*(Practise the question shapes — say each.)*

| Question word | + helper + person + plain verb |
|---|---|
| **What** | **do** you do? ⭐ |
| **Where** | **do** you live? |
| **When** | **did** he call? |
| **Why** | **does** she look sad? |
| **How** | **do** you know him? |
| **How much** | **does** it cost? |
| **How long** | **did** it take? |

*(Note the pattern.)* **Q-word → helper → person → plain verb.** ✅ Always that order. 🔗 *(Day 35)*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** അവന് കാപ്പി ഇഷ്ടമല്ല.

*(Notice.)* **ഇഷ്ടമല്ല** — the negative is **inside** the word ✅ *(ഇഷ്ടം + അല്ല)*

So we produce: ❌ *"He don't like coffee."* ← wrong helper! ✅

Build it:

1. **Who** → He
2. **Which helper?** *(he/she/it → does)* → does
3. **not** → does + not = **doesn't**
4. **Plain verb** *(the -s moved to "does"!)* → like
5. **What** → coffee

> ✅ **"He doesn't like coffee."**

*(Emphasise the -s transfer!)*
- *He like**s** coffee.* ← the -s is on the verb ✅
- *He **does**n't **like** coffee.* ← the -s moved to *does* ✅

*(Point out.)* ❌ *"He doesn't likes"* ❌ — **two** -s's! Only one allowed. ✅ *(Same economy as one-past-per-sentence!)* 🔗

*(The negative family — practise all.)*

| Subject | Present negative | Past negative |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **don't** *(do not)* | **didn't** |
| he / she / it | **doesn't** *(does not)* | **didn't** |

*(Say these five.)*
1. *I don't know.* ✅
2. *He doesn't work here.* ✅
3. *They don't have time.* ✅
4. *She didn't call me.* ✅
5. *We didn't go.* ✅

*(Malayali warning.)* ❌ *"He don't"* → ✅ **"He doesn't."** ✅ This is one of the most noticed errors in English — worth fixing carefully. ⚠️

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Asking about someone's job | **What do you do?** ⭐ | fixed expression |
| Saying you don't understand | **I don't understand.** | no *be*! 🔗 |
| Asking about the past | **Did you get my message?** | *did* + plain |
| Correcting someone | **He doesn't work there anymore.** | doesn't + plain |
| Checking a fact | **Does this bus go to Kaloor?** | does + plain |

*(Highlight row 5.)* **"Does this bus go to...?"** ⭐ — genuinely useful travel English, and the form to build. ⚠️ *"This bus goes to Kaloor?"* is what people say when they're **checking** something they already half-believe — real English, but not the neutral question. ✅

### 🎁 Bonus 1: The emphasis "do" 💪

*(A lovely use Malayalis rarely know.)*

*(Explain.)* You can add *do/does/did* to a **positive** sentence for **strong emphasis**:

- ✅ *I **do** understand your problem.* = I **really** do ✅
- ✅ *She **did** call you — check your phone!* = she really did ✅
- ✅ *He **does** work hard.* = despite what you think ✅
- ✅ *Please **do** come!* = warm invitation ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* This is how English **argues** or **insists**. Very useful when someone doubts you:
- ✅ *"I **did** send it! Check your spam folder."* ⭐

*(Note.)* The main verb still stays **plain**! ✅ *"She **did call**"* not "did called." 🔗

### 🎁 Bonus 2: Short answers ✅

*(Very practical — Malayalis often over-answer.)*

*(Explain.)* When answering a yes/no question, English uses a **short answer** with the same helper:

| Question | ✅ Short answer |
|---|---|
| *Do you like tea?* | **Yes, I do.** / **No, I don't.** |
| *Does he work here?* | **Yes, he does.** / **No, he doesn't.** |
| *Did you call her?* | **Yes, I did.** / **No, I didn't.** |
| *Are you ready?* | **Yes, I am.** / **No, I'm not.** |
| *Can you swim?* | **Yes, I can.** / **No, I can't.** |

*(Point out.)* **Copy the helper from the question.** ✅ That's the whole rule. 🔗 *(Same as Day 14's "So do I"!)*

*(Malayali note.)* We often answer with a bare *"Yes"* — which is **fine**, just a bit flat — or we repeat everything: ❌ *"Yes, I am liking tea."* ⚠️ Say **"Yes, I do."** ✅ Short, natural, and it sounds engaged.

### 🎁 Bonus 3: Question tags with do 🏷️

*(Day 61 goes deep — but here's the connection.)* 🔗

- ✅ *You like coffee, **don't you**?* ✅
- ✅ *He works here, **doesn't he**?* ✅
- ✅ *You called her, **didn't you**?* ✅

*(Rule.)* **Positive statement → negative tag with the same helper.** ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: No helper at all — the missing-word error

*(The most common, because Malayalam needs no helper.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Where you going? | **Where are you going?** |
| What you said? | **What did you say?** |
| You like tea? | **Do you like tea?** |
| He came yesterday? | **Did he come yesterday?** |
| Why you didn't call? | **Why didn't you call?** |

*(Highlight the last one!)* ⚠️ Note the order in negative questions:
- ✅ *Why **didn't you** call?* ✅ *(helper + not, then person)*
- ❌ *Why you didn't call?* ❌

*(Rule.)* **In a direct question, the helper comes BEFORE the person.** ✅ 🔗 *(Day 35's swap)*

*(Two exceptions you've already met.)* ⭐ Subject questions don't swap — ✅ *"Who called?"* ✅ And inside a polite opener you keep statement order — ✅ *"Could you tell me why he didn't call?"* ⭐

### Trap 2: Double tense — "didn't went"

*(The Day 03 classic, worth drilling again.)* 🔗

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I didn't went | **I didn't go** |
| He didn't came | **He didn't come** |
| Did you saw him? | **Did you see him?** |
| She didn't told me | **She didn't tell me** |
| Did you got it? | **Did you get it?** |

*(The self-check.)*

> **Did I say "did"? Then the next verb must be a BABY verb — plain and simple.** ✅

*(Drill — say these fast.)*
1. Did you **go**? ✅
2. I didn't **eat**. ✅
3. He didn't **come**. ✅
4. Did she **say** that? ✅
5. We didn't **see** it. ✅

### Trap 3: Double -s — "doesn't likes"

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He doesn't likes it | **He doesn't like it** |
| Does she works here? | **Does she work here?** |
| Doesn't he knows? | **Doesn't he know?** |

*(Rule.)* **One -s per verb phrase.** It goes on *does*, not on the main verb. ✅ *(A sentence can have more than one: ✅ "He **works** here and **knows** everyone.")*

### Trap 4: Mixing "do" with "be"

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Do you are coming? | **Are you coming?** |
| Does he is a doctor? | **Is he a doctor?** |
| I don't am ready | **I'm not ready** |
| Do you have finished? | **Have you finished?** |

*(The self-check — one question.)*

> **Does my sentence already have a helper?** *(am/is/are/was/were/can/will/have/should)*
> **Yes → use THAT one, don't add "do."** ✅

*(Practise the two paths.)*
| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| He **is** tired. | ***Is** he tired?* ✅ *(reuse "is")* |
| He **works** here. | ***Does** he work here?* ✅ *(hire "does")* |
| He **can** swim. | ***Can** he swim?* ✅ *(reuse "can")* |
| He **came** yesterday. | ***Did** he come yesterday?* ✅ *(hire "did")* |

*(Emphasise.)* **Reuse if there's a helper. Hire "do" if there isn't.** ✅

### The 3-second self-check 🎯

*(Practical tool.)*

Before every question or negative:

1. **Does my statement have a helper?** *(is/are/can/will/have)*
   → **Yes** → use it, swap it to the front ✅
   → **No** → hire **do/does/did** ✅

2. **Which one?** → he/she/it = **does** · past = **did** · everyone else = **do** ✅

3. **Is my main verb PLAIN?** → it must be! ✅

*(Drill — I say the statement, you say the question.)*
- *He likes it.* → **Does he like it?** ✅
- *They went home.* → **Did they go home?** ✅
- *She is ready.* → **Is she ready?** ✅
- *You have finished.* → **Have you finished?** ✅
- *He can drive.* → **Can he drive?** ✅
- *I said that.* → **Did I say that?** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Do that drill for **one week**, every morning. Thirty seconds. It builds the reflex. ✅
