# Day 75 — Stop Saying Good and Bad: 30 Better Words for Speaking and Writing

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *"Good"* can mean kind, skilled, useful, tasty, or enjoyable. English has a different word for each — and precision is what sounds fluent.

**📌 Format note:** Second video of the **vocabulary block**. Section 3 groups the upgrades by *what you're describing* rather than by strength, so the choice becomes a decision rather than a memory task.

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Explain the principle — this makes it usable in real time.)*

### 🔑 Why "good" and "bad" limit you

*(Explain.)* **"Good"** and **"bad"** are **judgement** words. ✅ They tell your listener *whether* you approve — but not **why**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* And the *"why"* is the interesting part. ✅

*(Show it.)*
- ⚠️ *"The service was bad."* → I disapprove. Of what? 🤔
- ✅ *"The service was **slow**."* → now I know! ⭐
- ✅ *"The service was **rude**."* → different problem! ⭐
- ✅ *"The service was **careless**."* → different again! ⭐

*(The principle.)*

> ### 👉 Don't say whether it was good. Say **what** it was. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Replace the judgement with the reason.** 🎯
> **"Good" അല്ല — എന്താണ് നല്ലത് എന്ന് പറയുക.** ✅

### 🎯 The 5-question method

*(A practical tool for real-time choosing.)*

*(Explain.)* Before you say *"good,"* ask: **what am I describing?** ✅

| What you're describing | Ask yourself | Example word |
|---|---|---|
| **a person** 👤 | kind? clever? reliable? | **kind, brilliant, reliable** ✅ |
| **a skill** 🎯 | skilled? experienced? | **skilled, talented, capable** ✅ |
| **a thing** 📦 | useful? well-made? | **useful, practical, reliable** ✅ |
| **food** 🍛 | tasty? fresh? | **delicious, tasty, fresh** ✅ |
| **an experience** 🎬 | enjoyable? interesting? | **enjoyable, fascinating, fun** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Five categories.** ✅ Identify the category, and the word appears. ✅

*(That's the skill.)* Not memorising 30 words — **noticing** what you're describing. ⭐

### ⚠️ And when IS "good" fine?

*(Important — don't over-correct.)*

*(Explain honestly.)* **"Good"** is a perfectly normal English word. ✅ Native speakers use it constantly. ✅

*(When it's genuinely right.)*
- ✅ *"Good morning!"* ⭐ *(fixed phrase)*
- ✅ *"That's a good point."* ⭐ *(fixed phrase)*
- ✅ *"Sounds good."* ⭐ *(fixed phrase)*
- ✅ *"Good luck!"* ✅
- ✅ *"I'm good, thanks."* ⭐ *(= I'm fine)*

*(Emphasise.)* Don't eliminate *"good."* ✅ Just stop using it as your **only** positive word. ✅

*(The target.)* Aim for **variety**, not avoidance. ⭐

### 💼 Bonus: the interview upgrade ⭐

*(High value — connect to Days 78–81.)* 🔗

*(Explain.)* In an interview, *"good"* makes you sound vague. Watch: ✅

| ❌ Vague | ✅ Precise |
|---|---|
| *I'm good at communication* | *I'm **comfortable** presenting to clients* ⭐ |
| *I was good at my job* | *I consistently met my targets* ⭐ |
| *My English is good* | *I'm **confident** in written and spoken English* ⭐ |
| *I'm a good team player* | *I work well in a team and I'm **reliable** under pressure* ⭐ |

*(Highlight the last one!)* ⭐ **"It wasn't the right fit"** ✅ — the professional way to criticise a former employer **without** sounding negative. 💼 ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 14:00)

### Trap 1: "നല്ല" and "ചീത്ത" cover everything

*(The root cause.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayalam **നല്ല** is genuinely enormous in range: ✅

| Malayalam | ✅ English |
|---|---|
| നല്ല മനുഷ്യൻ | a **kind** man ✅ |
| നല്ല അധ്യാപകൻ | an **excellent** teacher ✅ |
| നല്ല ഭക്ഷണം | **delicious** food ✅ |
| നല്ല ഫോൺ | a **reliable** phone ✅ |
| നല്ല ക്ഷീണം | **exhausted** 🔗 *(intensifier!)* ✅ |
| നല്ല മഴ | **heavy** rain ✅ |
| നല്ല ഉറക്കം | a **deep** sleep ✅ |
| നല്ല വില | an **expensive** price ⚠️ *(or reasonable — context!)* |

*(Emphasise.)* **Eight different English words.** ✅ From one Malayalam word. ✅

*(Point out something interesting.)* Look at rows 5, 6, 7 — **നല്ല** isn't even "good" there! It's an **intensifier** meaning "a lot of." ✅

*(The fix — one question.)*

> ### 👉 Before saying *"good,"* ask: **"good in WHAT WAY?"** 🤔 ✅

*(Practise.)*
- *നല്ല ആൾ* → good in what way? → **kind** ✅
- *നല്ല ജോലിക്കാരൻ* → good in what way? → **hard-working** ✅
- *നല്ല സിനിമ* → good in what way? → **enjoyable** ✅

### Trap 2: "Not good" instead of a real word ⚠️

*(A very common pattern.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayalis often build negatives with *"not good"*: ✅

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| My English is not good | **I'm still improving my English** ⭐ |
| The food was not good | **The food was bland** ✅ |
| He is not good | **He's unreliable** ✅ |
| The signal is not good | **The signal is weak** ✅ |
| My health is not good | **I haven't been well** ⭐ |

*(Highlight row 1!)* ⭐ **Very important.**

*(Explain.)* ❌ *"My English is not good"* ✅ is a **self-damaging** sentence. 🔗 *(Day 38!)* ✅

*(The upgrades.)*
- ✅ *"I'm still improving my English."* ⭐
- ✅ *"I'm working on my English."* ✅
- ✅ *"My English is getting better."* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Same honesty. ✅ Completely different impression — **progress** instead of **deficit**. ⭐

### Trap 3: "Bad" for people — it's harsher in English ⚠️

*(A genuine cultural difference.)*

*(Explain.)* In Malayalam, **ചീത്ത മനുഷ്യൻ** is strong but usable. ✅

*(But.)* In English, calling someone **"a bad person"** is **very** strong — almost a moral condemnation. ✅ Native speakers rarely say it. ✅

*(The natural alternatives.)*
| What you mean | ✅ Say |
|---|---|
| impolite | **rude** ✅ |
| untrustworthy | **I don't trust him** ⭐ |
| difficult to work with | **difficult** ✅ |
| unkind | **not very nice** ⭐ *(softer!)* |
| unprofessional | **unprofessional** ✅ 💼 |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **"He's not very nice"** ✅ is how English criticises people gently. 🔗 *(Day 42)* ✅

### Trap 4: Over-using strong words 🔗 *(Day 74)*

*(The same warning, briefly.)*

*(Explain.)* Don't replace **every** "good" with "outstanding." ✅

- ⚠️ *"The tea was **exquisite**. My colleague is **phenomenal**. The app is **revolutionary**."* 😅

*(Emphasise.)* **Precise, not dramatic.** ✅ Most things in life are *"fine," "okay," "pretty good"* ✅ — and saying so is **accurate**, which is the real goal. ⭐

*(The everyday middle words!)* ⭐ Malayalis under-use these:

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **fine** ✅ | acceptable |
| **okay** ✅ | acceptable |
| **decent** ⭐ | reasonably good |
| **not bad** ⭐ | better than expected! |
| **alright** ✅ | fine |

*(Note.)* ⭐ **"Not bad"** ✅ is a **compliment** in English! 😄
- ✅ *"How was the food?"* → *"Not bad at all!"* ⭐ *(= quite good)*

### Bonus: describing your own English honestly 💬

*(Genuinely useful — connects to interviews.)*

*(Explain.)* When someone asks about your English level, use these: ✅

| Level | ✅ How to say it |
|---|---|
| Basic | *"I can manage basic conversation."* ✅ |
| Improving | *"I'm still improving, but I can handle work discussions."* ⭐ |
| Comfortable | *"I'm comfortable with both written and spoken English."* ⭐ |
| Strong | *"I'm fluent in English."* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Never ❌ *"My English is poor"* ✅ or ❌ *"I'm weak in English."* ✅

*(Firmly.)* Say **"I'm still improving"** ✅ — it's honest, positive, and it's what a **confident** learner says. ⭐
