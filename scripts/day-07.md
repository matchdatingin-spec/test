# Day 07 — Will vs Would: The Difference Nobody Explained Clearly

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *Will* lives in the real world. *Would* lives in the imaginary world. That's the whole secret.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Serious, a little mysterious.)*

Somebody told you *would* is "the past tense of will."

That person was not lying. But they gave you only 10% of the truth. And that 10% is exactly why you still make this mistake:

> ❌ *"If I had money, I will buy a car."*
> ❌ *"I will like to join your class."*
> ⚠️ *"He said he will come tomorrow."*

*(Pause.)*

Three sentences. The first two are simply **wrong** — they need *would*. The third is the interesting one: it's **acceptable** in real speech, but *would* is the safer, exam-correct choice, and we'll see exactly why.

But here's the confusing part — *would* is NOT past in any of those sentences. Sentence one is about the future. So how can a "past tense" word talk about the future?

*(Lean in.)*

Because *would* was never really about **time**. It's about something else entirely. Today I'll show you what. And after this, all those "if" sentences that scared you in school will feel easy. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

Here is the real difference. Two worlds.

### 🌍 WILL = THE REAL WORLD

Use *will* when you believe the thing is **actually going to happen.** It's real. It's planned. It's certain.

- *I will call you tonight.* → It's happening. Real.
- *It will rain tomorrow.* → I expect real rain.
- *She will pass the exam.* → I believe it truly.

**WILL = you're talking about the real world.**

### 💭 WOULD = THE IMAGINARY WORLD

Use *would* when the situation is **not real.** It's a dream, a condition, a "what if," a maybe-never.

- *I would buy a car... (if I had money).* → I don't have money. **Not real.**
- *That would be nice.* → In that imagined situation.
- *I would go, but I'm busy.* → I'm not going. **Not real.**

**WOULD = the world of imagination.**

*(Big point.)* So the question is never "past or future?" The question is:

> ### 👉 "Is this REAL, or am I IMAGINING it?"

Real → **will.** Imagining → **would.**

### And the "past" part?

Yes, *would* also does two past jobs: **reported speech** and **past habits**.

- ✅ *He said he **would** come.* ← he said "I will come," but we report it in the past
- ✅ *When I was a child, we **would** go to the beach every Sunday.* ← old habit

*(Be honest about this.)* ⚠️ Those two are **real** — the beach trips genuinely happened! ✅ So *would* doesn't always mean "imaginary." The one thing all its jobs share is **distance**: distance from the real world *(a dream)*, or distance in **time** *(a past habit, a past report)*.

*(Slogan.)* **Will = close and direct. Would = one step away** — away in reality, or away in time. ⭐

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് പണം ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ ഒരു കാർ വാങ്ങുമായിരുന്നു.

*(Read the Malayalam slowly.)* Now, notice something beautiful. Malayalam **already marks the imagination** — twice!

- **ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ** → the *-എങ്കിൽ* = "if"
- **വാങ്ങുമായിരുന്നു** → this long tail is the "imaginary" marker

So Malayalam is smarter than you think. You already know this concept. You just have to move the marker from the **verb tail** to a **separate word at the front**.

Build in English:

**Part 1 — the condition:**
1. **If** → If
2. **Who** → I
3. **Verb in past form** *(this is the "imaginary" signal in English)* → had
4. **What** → money

→ *If I had money...*

**Part 2 — the result:**
5. **Who** → I
6. **Imaginary helper** → would
7. **Plain verb** → buy
8. **What** → a car

> ✅ **"If I had money, I would buy a car."**

*(Critical teaching point.)* Look at *had*. It's a past form — but I'm talking about **today**! Why past?

Because English uses the **past form as a costume for "unreal."** Pushing the verb one step back in time is English's way of saying *"this isn't true."*

That's the secret nobody tells you: **in English, past form = distance from reality.**

So the rule for these sentences:

> ### if + past form ... would + plain verb

- ✅ *If I **were** rich, I **would** travel the world.*
- ✅ *If she **studied** more, she **would** pass.*
- ✅ *If he **called** me, I **would** help him.*

And the deadly mistake: ❌ *"If I had money, I **will** buy a car."* You mixed the imaginary world with the real world. Pick one world and stay in it.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** അവൻ പറഞ്ഞു അവൻ നാളെ വരുമെന്ന്.

His original words were: *"I will come tomorrow."* — real world, *will*.

But now **you** are reporting it, and you're reporting it in the **past** (അവൻ പറഞ്ഞു = he said). So everything shifts one step back:

1. **Who** → He
2. **Reporting verb, past** → said
3. **that** → (that)
4. **Who** → he
5. **will → would** *(because the reporting is in the past)*
6. **Plain verb** → come
7. **When** → tomorrow

> ✅ **"He said (that) he would come tomorrow."**

*(Very important note.)* Notice: **tomorrow is still in the future!** The event hasn't happened. But we still use *would*, because the **saying** happened in the past. The helper follows the reporting verb, not the event.

Compare:
- ✅ *He **says** he **will** come.* ← reporting now → will
- ✅ *He **said** he **would** come.* ← reported in the past → would

**Safe default: said → would. Says → will.** ⭐

*(One honest caveat.)* ⚠️ If the plan is **still** true and still in the future when you report it, native speakers do also say ✅ *"He said he **will** come tomorrow."* ✅ It isn't a crime. But **would** is the safer, exam-correct default — so make *would* your habit.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Which world? |
|---|---|---|
| Promising your friend | **I'll call you when I get there.** | real |
| Dreaming about a job abroad | **I would love to work in Canada.** | imaginary |
| Ordering at a restaurant | **I'd like a masala dosa, please.** | polite = softened, imaginary |
| Refusing gently | **I would come, but I have a class.** | imaginary (I'm not coming) |
| Childhood memory | **Every summer we would visit my grandmother.** | past habit |

*(Highlight row 3.)* This is worth pure gold: **"I'd like..."** Never say ❌ *"I will like to..."* — it doesn't exist. Never say ❌ *"I am liking to..."*.

Memorise the sound: **"I'd like to..."**
- ✅ *I'd like to join your class.*
- ✅ *I'd like to ask a question.*
- ✅ *I'd like to apply for this job.*

*(Highlight row 4.)* And this pattern is so useful in real life: **"I would ___, but ___."** It's how English politely says no.
- ✅ *I would help you, but I'm not free today.*
- ✅ *I would come, but it's too far.*

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### The trap: Malayalam mixes the two worlds freely, English does not

In spoken Malayalam, we constantly say things like:

**എനിക്ക് പണം ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ കാർ വാങ്ങും.**

Here, ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ (real-ish "if") + വാങ്ങും (plain future). And we use this **both** for real plans **and** for daydreams. Context decides. Malayalam is relaxed.

English is not relaxed. English demands you **declare your world** and stay in it:

| Meaning | English structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Real, possible** | if + present … **will** | If I **get** the money, I **will** buy a car. *(maybe I really will)* |
| **Imaginary, unlikely** | if + past … **would** | If I **had** the money, I **would** buy a car. *(I don't have it)* |
| **Impossible, past regret** | if + had + V3 … **would have** | If I **had had** the money, I **would have** bought a car. *(too late)* |

*(Say clearly.)* Three levels: **maybe → dream → too late.** These three are your safe **default patterns** — learn them as templates.

**Quick self-check while speaking:** the two halves normally **match**.
- ✅ present + will
- ✅ past + would
- ❌ past + will ← the classic Malayali error
- ⚠️ present + would ← usually wrong in these patterns

*(Advanced note — say it once, don't dwell.)* ⭐ The result half can also take **could** or **might** when you're less sure: ✅ *If I had the money, I **might** buy a car.* ✅ So the three rows are reliable templates, not the only legal combinations.

### Trap 2: The invisible "be"

Same as Day 04 — because Malayalam often has no separate "be" verb, we drop it:

- ❌ *That would nice* → ✅ **That would be nice.**
- ❌ *It will difficult* → ✅ **It will be difficult.**
- ❌ *He will happy* → ✅ **He will be happy.**
- ❌ *I would grateful* → ✅ **I would be grateful.**

*(Chant.)* **will be. would be. will be. would be.** Burn that into your mouth.

### Trap 3: Using *will* for polite requests

Malayalis often say ⚠️ *"Will you please send me the file?"* — this is correct English, and with a warm tone it's perfectly polite. But it's **direct**, and with a flat tone it can land like an instruction from a boss. There are softer options.

The soft, safe versions:
- ✅ **Could you send me the file?** ← best
- ✅ **Would you mind sending me the file?** ← very polite
- ✅ **Would it be possible to send me the file?** ← formal email

*(Note.)* Careful with *"Would you mind..."* — the verb after it takes **-ing**: *Would you mind **waiting**?* Not "to wait."
