# Day 68 — V vs W Pronunciation: The Malayalam Speaker's Masterclass

**Target length:** 14–16 minutes
**Level:** All levels
**One-line promise:** Malayalam has **one** letter where English has **two** sounds. Learn the two mouth positions, and this fixes forever.

**📌 Format note:** A **physical-technique** video. The Malayalam sound-system analysis moves to Section 2 (it *is* the core logic here), and Section 4 becomes the drill and self-testing method.

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC — Why this happens (1:00 – 5:00)

*(This IS the lesson. Spend real time here.)*

### 🪷 The Malayalam sound system

*(Explain properly — understanding removes the frustration.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayalam has the letter **വ**. ✅

And **വ** is a single sound — a soft, gentle sound made with the lips **and** a slight touch of the teeth. Linguists call it a **labiodental approximant**. 📖

*(Emphasise.)* It sits **exactly in the middle** between English **/v/** and **/w/**. ✅

*(Show it.)*

```
English /v/  ←—— Malayalam വ ——→  English /w/
(teeth bite lip)   (in between)     (lips round)
```

*(The consequence.)* So when you speak English, your mouth produces **വ** for both — and to an English ear, it sounds like the **wrong one**, roughly half the time. ✅

*(Firmly, kindly.)* **This is not carelessness.** Your mouth has never been asked to make these two sounds separately. It's a **muscle** that was never trained. 💪 ✅

### 🔊 The two mouth positions

*(Now the technique. Be very precise.)*

#### 🦷 SOUND 1: /v/ — teeth on lip

> ### **Top teeth TOUCH the bottom lip.** 🦷 Then push voice through. ✅

*(Step by step.)*
1. Put your **top front teeth** lightly on your **bottom lip** 🦷
2. Now **hum** — turn your voice on 🔊
3. Feel the **buzz** and the **vibration** in your lip ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **/v/ is a BUZZING sound.** 🐝 There's friction. Your lip **vibrates**. ✅

*(Test.)* Say **"vvvvvv"** — hold it for three seconds ✅ Your lip should be **tickling**. ✅

*(Malayalam comparison.)* This is close to **ഫ** in *some* positions, but with **voice on**. ✅ It's a **stronger, buzzier** version of വ. ✅

#### 👄 SOUND 2: /w/ — round lips, no teeth

> ### **Lips make a small round hole. Teeth touch NOTHING.** 👄 ✅

*(Step by step.)*
1. Make your lips into a small **round O** shape 👄 — like you're about to whistle 😗
2. Your teeth touch **nothing** ✅
3. Now push voice and **open** the lips as you say it ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **/w/ is a GLIDING sound.** 🌊 No friction, no buzz. Your lips **move** from round to open. ✅

*(Test.)* Say **"wwwww"** ✅ It should feel like the start of **"oo"** ✅ Actually — say **"oo-ee, oo-ee"** ✅ That *"oo"* start **is** the /w/ sound! ⭐

*(The shortcut!)* 🔑

> ### **/w/ = start with "oo."** ✅

- **water** → say **"oo-ater"** → **"wa-ter"** ✅
- **west** → say **"oo-est"** → **"west"** ✅
- **we** → **"oo-ee"** ✅

*(Practise that.)* **oo-ater · oo-est · oo-ee** ✅ Then speed it up. ⚡

### ✋ THE HAND TEST — feel the difference

*(A physical test they can do right now.)*

*(Explain.)* Hold your **hand** about 5 cm in front of your mouth. ✋

**Say "vvvv"** → 🦷 Air **scrapes** out between your teeth and lip — you feel a thin, buzzy stream ✅
**Say "wwww"** → 👄 Air flows out **freely** through rounded lips — smooth, no scraping ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **/v/ = teeth on lip, buzzing friction 🦷 · /w/ = rounded lips, nothing touching 👄** ✅

*(The real test is TOUCH, not air.)* ⭐ Put a finger on your top teeth: for **/v/** your bottom lip **touches** them. For **/w/** it touches **nothing**. ✅ That's the check that never fails. ⭐

*(Do it together on camera.)* Try it now. **"vvvv"** ... **"wwww"** ✅ Different, isn't it? ⭐

### 🪞 The mirror test

*(A second physical check.)*

*(Explain.)* Get a mirror — or your phone's front camera. 🪞

**Say "very"** → you should **see your teeth** touching your lip 🦷 ✅
**Say "water"** → you should see your lips **rounded**, teeth **hidden** 👄 ✅

*(Emphasise.)* If your mouth looks the **same** for both — that's the problem, and now you can **see** it. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **/v/ = teeth bite lip, buzzing 🦷🐝. /w/ = lips round, gliding 👄🌊.** ✅

## 🔍 3. THE PRACTICE (5:00 – 12:00)

*(This is a drill section. Keep energy up and leave real gaps.)*

### 🅐 Step 1 — Isolate the sounds

*(Say each for 3 seconds, five times.)*

> 🦷 **vvvvv** *(teeth on lip, buzz — this one you CAN hold)* — ×5
> 👄 **oo–wa, oo–wa** *(round lips, then glide open)* — ×5

*(Why the difference?)* ⚠️ **/v/ can be held; /w/ cannot.** ✅ /w/ is a **glide** — it only exists while your lips are *moving*. If you try to hold *"wwwww"* you're really just holding the vowel **"oo."** ⭐ So practise /w/ as a **movement**: *oo → wa*. ✅

*(Then alternate!)*
> **vvv — www — vvv — www** ✅ ×10

*(Emphasise.)* Feel your mouth **change shape**. ✅ That movement is the whole skill. ⭐

### 🅑 Step 2 — Single words

#### 🦷 The /v/ words *(teeth on lip!)*

| Word | Sentence to practise |
|---|---|
| **very** | *It's **very** good.* ✅ |
| **video** | *Watch the **video**.* ✅ |
| **visit** | *I'll **visit** tomorrow.* ✅ |
| **voice** | *Your **voice** is clear.* ✅ |
| **value** | *Great **value**.* ✅ |
| **vegetable** 🥬 | *Fresh **vegetables**.* 🔗 *(Day 66!)* |
| **vehicle** 🚗 | *Park the **vehicle**.* 🔗 *(Day 67!)* |
| **available** | *Is it **available**?* ✅ |
| **service** | *Good **service**.* ✅ |
| **love** ❤️ | *I **love** it.* ✅ |

*(Note.)* ⚠️ *love, have, give, leave* — the **/v/** at the **end** is often dropped by Malayalis ✅ Make sure the teeth touch! 🦷

#### 👄 The /w/ words *(round lips, start with "oo"!)*

| Word | Sentence to practise |
|---|---|

| **welcome** | *You're **welcome**.* ⭐ |
| **which** | ***Which** one?* ✅ |

*(Note.)* ⭐ **"You're welcome"** ✅ — a phrase you say constantly. Get that *w* right! 👄

### 🅒 Step 3 — MINIMUM PAIRS *(the real training)*

*(This is the most important drill. Slow, then fast.)*

*(Explain.)* These are pairs where **only** the v/w changes — so your mouth **must** do something different. ✅

| 🦷 /v/ | 👄 /w/ |
|---|---|
| **vest** 🦺 | **west** 🧭 |
| **vine** 🍇 | **wine** 🍷 |
| **veil** | **whale** 🐋 |
| **vet** 🐕 | **wet** 💧 |
| **verse** | **worse** |

*(Drill instruction.)* Say each **pair** slowly, feeling your mouth change: 🐢

*(Then all ten pairs, once each.)* ⚡

*(Emphasise.)* Your **mouth must visibly change** between the two. ✅ Watch in the mirror. 🪞

### 🅓 Step 4 — Both sounds in ONE word 🔥

*(The hard level — and the most useful.)*

*(Explain.)* These words contain **both** sounds. Your mouth has to switch **mid-word**. ✅

| Word | The switch |
|---|---|

| **weave** | **W**ea-**v**e → 👄 then 🦷 |

1. ✅ *"I **w**ant a **v**ery good job."* 👄🦷

## ⚠️ 4. THE DRILL AND SELF-TEST (12:00 – 14:30)

*(Section 4 adapts here — the trap analysis was in Section 2, so this is the practice method.)*

### 🔁 The 2-minute daily drill

*(Give them a specific, repeatable routine.)*

*(Explain.)* Do **exactly** this, every day, for **two weeks**: ✅

| Step | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 sec | **vvv — www** alternating ✅ |
| 2 | 30 sec | 5 minimum pairs *(vest/west, vine/wine...)* ✅ |
| 3 | 30 sec | *"wave"* and *"vowel"* ×10 ⭐ |
| 4 | 40 sec | The tongue twister ×5 ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* **Two minutes.** Fourteen days. ✅ That's roughly **half an hour** of total practice to fix a lifetime habit. ⭐

### 🎙️ The self-test — how to know it's working

*(Practical and honest.)*

*(Explain.)* You **cannot** hear your own errors reliably — your brain corrects them automatically. ✅ So you need evidence. 📊

**Test 1: Record and wait** ⏰
- Record yourself saying the ten minimum pairs 🎙️
- Listen **the next day** *(not immediately!)* ✅
- The delay lets you hear yourself as a **stranger** would ⭐

**Test 2: The phone dictation test** 📱 ⭐
- Open any voice-typing app
- Say: **"vest"** ... then **"west"** ✅
- If the phone types the **right** word both times — your sounds are distinct! ✅
- If it types the same word twice — keep drilling ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **This is brilliant.** ⭐ Your phone is an honest, patient pronunciation teacher. And it never gets tired of you. 😄

*(Try these five in dictation.)*
**very · water · vest · west · wave** ✅

**Test 3: The hand test, mid-sentence** ✋
- Say a real sentence with your hand in front of your mouth
- Feel the **puffs** — they should come **only** on the /w/ words ✅

### ⚠️ The two errors to watch for

**Error 1: Over-correcting into /v/** 🦷

*(Explain.)* Once you learn the teeth position, some learners use it **everywhere**:
- ❌ *"I **v**ant some **v**ater"* ❌ 😅

*(Emphasise.)* **Don't over-correct.** ✅ /w/ is the **more common** sound in everyday English words — *we, will, would, what, when, where, why, work, want, week* ✅ All /w/. 👄

**Error 2: Forgetting the /v/ at word ends** 🦷

*(Explain.)* Malayalis often soften the final /v/:
- ❌ *"lo-oo"* → ✅ **love** ✅
- ❌ *"gi-oo"* → ✅ **give** ✅
- ❌ *"lea-oo"* → ✅ **leave** ✅

*(Practise the final /v/.)* **love · give · leave · have · move · five** ✅ Teeth must touch at the **end**. 🦷

### 🔑 The quick mental check while speaking

*(A practical in-the-moment tool.)*

*(Explain.)* You can't analyse every word while talking. So use this: ✅

> ### 👉 Many of English's most common **little** words start with **w**. 👄
> *we, what, when, where, which, why, will, would, want, work, week, with* ✅

> ### 👉 **V** turns up a lot in longer, borrowed words. 🦷
> *very, video, visit, value, vehicle, available, service, develop* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Most **wh-** words start with **/w/** 👄 ✅ — but watch the exceptions: **who, whose, whole** start with an **h** sound! ⚠️ ⭐
