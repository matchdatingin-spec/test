# Day 80 — 10 Job Interview Questions and Answers You Must Prepare

**Target length:** 16–18 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** Ten questions cover most interviews. For each one, what they're **actually** testing — and the answer shape that works.

**📌 Format note:** Third video of the **career block**. Section 3 becomes the ten questions, each with *what they're testing → the shape → a model answer*. Section 4 covers the Malayali answering habits that undermine good content.

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confident, practical.)*

The interviewer asks:

> *"What is your biggest weakness?"* 🤔

And you say:

> ❌ *"Sir, my weakness is that I am a perfectionist. I work too hard and I care too much."* 😐

*(Pause. Flat expression.)*

*(Explain.)* Every interviewer has heard that **five hundred** times. ✅ And what they hear is: *"this person won't be honest with me."* ⚠️

*(Point at camera.)*

Or this classic:

> *"Where do you see yourself in five years?"*
> ❌ *"Sir, I want to see myself in a higher position in your esteemed organisation."* 📜

*(Emphasise.)* Empty. ✅ It could be said by anyone, about any company. ✅

*(Lean in, confident.)*

*(Explain.)* Here's the thing — interview questions are **not** random. Each one is testing something **specific**. ✅ And once you know **what** is being tested, the answer becomes obvious. ⭐

Today: **ten questions**, what each is really asking, and the shape that works. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Explain the framework — this makes all ten answerable.)*

### 🎯 The four things every interviewer is checking

*(This is the key insight.)*

*(Explain.)* Behind all ten questions, they're assessing just **four** things: ✅

| # | What they're checking | The question behind the question |
|---|---|---|
| 1️⃣ | **Can you do the job?** 🎯 | skills, experience |
| 2️⃣ | **Will you actually do it?** 💪 | motivation, reliability |
| 3️⃣ | **Will you fit the team?** 🤝 | attitude, communication |
| 4️⃣ | **Will you stay?** ⏳ | commitment, realistic expectations |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Every answer you give should serve **at least one** of these. ✅

*(The test.)* Before answering, ask: *"Which of the four am I proving right now?"* ✅

### 🔑 The universal answer shape: C-E-R

*(A simple pattern that works for almost every question.)*

> ### **C** = **Claim** — a direct, short answer ✅
> ### **E** = **Evidence** — a real example with a number ⭐
> ### **R** = **Relevance** — link it to this job ✅

*(Show it.)*

**Question:** *"Are you good at handling pressure?"*

- **C:** ✅ *"Yes, I'm comfortable under pressure."*
- **E:** ⭐ *"In my current role, month-end is always tight — I handle around 200 accounts and the closing window is two days."*
- **R:** ✅ *"So the deadline pressure in this role wouldn't be new to me."*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Claim. Evidence. Relevance.** ✅ Thirty seconds. ✅

*(Point out.)* Most candidates give only **C** ✅ — *"Yes sir, I can handle pressure."* ✅ Which proves nothing. ⚠️

*(Slogan.)*
> **Never claim without evidence.** 📊
> **തെളിവില്ലാതെ ഒന്നും പറയരുത്.** ✅

### ⏱️ And the length rule

*(Practical.)*

| Question type | Answer length |
|---|---|
| "Tell me about yourself" | **90 seconds** ⭐ 🔗 |
| Behavioural *("tell me about a time...")* | **60–90 seconds** ⭐ *(use S-P-A-R-K)* 🔗 |
| Direct/factual *("do you know Excel?")* | **20–30 seconds** ✅ |
| Closing *("any questions?")* | **ask 2 questions** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⚠️ Under 15 seconds = you seem uninterested ✅ Over 2 minutes = you're rambling ✅

## 🔍 3. THE 10 QUESTIONS (4:00 – 14:00)

---

### 1️⃣ "Tell me about yourself." 🎤

**Testing:** 1️⃣ can you do the job + 2️⃣ motivation ✅
**Shape:** 🔗 P-A-S-S *(freshers, Day 78)* or N-E-S-T *(experienced, Day 79)* ✅
**Length:** 90 seconds ⭐

*(Quick recap.)* ✅ Present/Now → Ability/Expertise → **Story with a number** → Target ✅

*(⚠️ Don't include.)* family, marks, hobbies, chronological history ✅

---

### 2️⃣ "Why should we hire you?" 💼

**Testing:** 1️⃣ skills + 4️⃣ fit ✅

*(Explain.)* This is an **invitation** — they're asking you to sell yourself directly. ✅ Don't be shy here. 🔗 *(Day 78's വിനയം point!)*

**Shape:** ⭐ Pick the **top 2** requirements from the job description and match them with evidence ✅

**Model *(experienced)*:**
> ✅ *"From the job description, the two main things you need are reconciliation experience and someone who can improve processes. On the first — I've done month-end closing for eight years, currently for around 200 clients. On the second — I reduced our closing cycle from five days to two by automating three manual reconciliations. So I'd be able to contribute on both from fairly early on."* ⭐

**Model *(fresher)*:**
> ✅ *"I know I don't have work experience yet, so I'd say two things. First, I've already built the core skills — Excel to an advanced level and basic Tally. Second, I learn fast — I taught myself Tally in three weeks for my project. I'd need training on your systems, but I don't think I'd need it twice."* ⭐

*(Highlight that last line!)* ⭐ **"I wouldn't need it twice."** ✅ Memorable and confident. ✅

---

### 3️⃣ "What is your greatest strength?" 💪

**Testing:** 1️⃣ + 3️⃣ ✅

**Shape:** ⭐ **One** strength + evidence + relevance ✅ *(not a list of five!)*

**Model:**
> ✅ *"I'd say it's attention to detail. In accounts that matters — in the last two years I haven't had a reconciliation query come back from audit. My colleagues actually send me their statements to check before submission. I know this role involves client reporting, so that habit would carry over."* ⭐

*(⚠️ Avoid.)* ❌ *"I am hard-working, punctual, sincere and honest"* ✅ — four claims, zero evidence ⚠️

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **One strength, proven** beats five strengths, claimed. ✅

---

### 4️⃣ "What is your greatest weakness?" 🤔

**Testing:** 3️⃣ self-awareness + honesty ✅

*(Explain the real test.)* They're **not** looking for a flaw. ✅ They're checking whether you can **look at yourself honestly** and **act** on it. ✅

**Shape:** ⭐ A **real** weakness + what you're **doing** about it + evidence of progress ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"Honestly, my weakness has been delegation. I tend to take on tasks myself because it feels faster, and that became a problem when my team grew to four people. What I've been doing about it is deliberately handing over one recurring task each month and letting people learn it, even if it's slower initially. Two of my juniors now handle the weekly reports independently, which they didn't six months ago."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Real weakness → real action → real result.** ✅

*(⚠️ Never say.)*
- ❌ *"I'm a perfectionist"* ✅ *(everyone says it)*
- ❌ *"I have no weaknesses"* ⚠️ *(arrogant or unreflective)*
- ❌ *"I get angry"* / *"I'm lazy"* ⚠️ *(too damaging!)*

*(The safe zone.)* ✅ Pick a **skill** or **work-habit** weakness, never a **character** or **attitude** one. ⭐

*(Good options.)* delegation · public speaking · saying no · over-checking work · asking for help too late ✅

---

### 5️⃣ "Where do you see yourself in five years?" 🔮

**Testing:** 4️⃣ will you stay + 2️⃣ ambition ✅

*(Explain the real test.)* They want to know: *"is this job a step in your plan, or just something you'll leave in six months?"* ✅

**Shape:** ⭐ A **realistic** progression in **this** field ✅ *(not "your position, sir"!)* 😄

**Model:**
> ✅ *"In five years I'd like to be handling reporting and analysis rather than only transaction-level work — ideally leading a small team. I'm not in a hurry about titles; I'd rather build genuine depth in financial reporting first. This role looked like the right next step because it moves me towards analysis while still using what I already know."* ⭐

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *"I want your position"* 😄 ⚠️
- ❌ *"I want to go abroad"* ⚠️ *(tells them you'll leave!)*
- ❌ *"I want to start my own business"* ⚠️ *(same problem)*
- ❌ *"I haven't thought about it"* ⚠️

---

### 6️⃣ "Tell me about a difficult situation you handled." 🔥

**Testing:** 1️⃣ + 3️⃣ problem-solving and temperament ✅
**Shape:** 🔗 **S-P-A-R-K** *(Day 40!)* ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"Last year we had a client dispute over an invoice — they said they'd been double-billed for a full quarter, about ₹4 lakhs. It was escalated and the relationship was at risk.*
>
> *I went through six months of entries line by line and found the actual issue: two of their locations had been set up as separate accounts, so some charges genuinely appeared twice. It was our error.*
>
> *I prepared a clear statement showing exactly what happened, we issued a credit note, and I suggested merging the two account codes so it couldn't recur.*
>
> *The client stayed, and they actually said they appreciated getting a straight answer. What I took from it is that admitting an error quickly costs much less than defending it."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice — **he admitted his company's mistake.** ✅ That reads as **integrity**, not weakness. 🔗 *(Day 59's active-voice ownership!)* ✅

---

### 7️⃣ "Tell me about a conflict with a colleague." 🤝

**Testing:** 3️⃣ team fit — this is the **highest-risk** question ⚠️

*(Explain the trap.)* If you **blame** the colleague, you fail. ✅ If you say *"I never have conflicts,"* you seem evasive. ⚠️

**Shape:** ⭐ A **real** disagreement + how you **resolved** it + no blame ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"There was a genuine disagreement with a colleague in sales about invoice timing. He wanted invoices raised before the paperwork was complete so his targets closed on time; I wasn't comfortable doing that.*
>
> *Rather than escalating it, I asked him to sit with me for ten minutes so I could show why it created audit problems later. It turned out he had no idea about the audit side — he just had a monthly target.*
>
> *We agreed on a provisional entry that let him track the deal without a formal invoice. It solved both problems. I learned that most conflicts at work are actually information gaps."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **No villain in that story.** ✅ Both people had valid needs. ✅ That's what a mature answer looks like. ⭐

---

### 8️⃣ "Why do you want to leave your current job?" 🚪

**Testing:** 4️⃣ will you stay + 3️⃣ attitude ⚠️
**Shape:** 🔗 Frame **forward** *(Day 79!)* ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"I've enjoyed my time there — I've learned a lot and I've been given real responsibility. But I've now done reconciliation well for several years, and I want to move into analysis and reporting. That path isn't really available in my current structure, so I started looking."* ⭐

*(⚠️ Never.)* criticise your manager, company, salary, or colleagues ✅ **Even if it's true.** ⚠️ 🔗 *(Day 79)*

---

### 9️⃣ "What are your salary expectations?" 💰

**Testing:** 4️⃣ realistic expectations ✅
*(Full treatment on Day 81.)* 🔗

**Shape:** ⭐ A **range**, with reasoning ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"My current CTC is ₹6.5 lakhs. Given that this role includes reporting responsibility beyond what I handle now, I'd be looking at somewhere in the ₹8 to ₹9 lakh range. But I'm open to discussing it based on the overall package."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Have a number.** ✅ ❌ *"Sir, as per company norms"* ✅ makes you sound passive and often costs you money. ⚠️

---

### 🔟 "Do you have any questions for us?" ❓

**Testing:** 2️⃣ genuine interest ✅

*(Explain.)* ❌ *"No sir"* ✅ is a **wasted** opportunity — and it signals low interest. ⚠️

**Shape:** ⭐ Ask **two** thoughtful questions ✅

**Good questions:**
- ✅ *"What would success in this role look like after the first year?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"How is the team structured — who would I be working with most closely?"* ✅
- ✅ *"What's the biggest challenge the person in this role would face early on?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Is there scope to move towards analysis over time?"* ✅ *(shows your plan!)*
- ✅ *"What are the next steps in the process?"* ⭐ *(always ask this!)*

*(⚠️ Don't ask first.)* salary, leave policy, working hours, work-from-home ✅ *(save for the offer stage)* ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (14:00 – 17:00)

### Trap 1: "Sir" overload 🙏

*(A specific and noticeable habit.)*

*(Explain warmly.)* In Kerala, addressing someone as **"sir"** repeatedly shows respect. ✅ It's genuine politeness. ✅

*(But.)* In an interview — especially international — saying *"sir"* in every sentence reads as **nervous** or **subordinate**, not respectful: ✅
- ⚠️ *"Yes sir. Sir, my strength is... Sir, I think... Thank you sir."* ✅

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 Use **"sir"** once at the start, once at the end. Not in between. ✅

*(Or better.)* Use their **name** if you know it ✅ — *"Thanks, Mr. Menon."* ⭐ That's how professional equals speak. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* You're being **interviewed**, not **judged as a person**. ✅ Speak as a professional talking to another professional. ⭐

### Trap 2: Answering too briefly 🤐

*(A cultural habit, from Day 36.)* 🔗

*(Explain.)* Short answers feel polite in Malayalam conversation. ✅ In an interview, they read as *"nothing to say."* ⚠️

| ⚠️ Too short | ✅ Complete |
|---|---|
| *"Yes sir, I know Excel."* | *"Yes — I use it daily for reconciliation. I'm comfortable with pivot tables, VLOOKUP and basic macros."* ⭐ |
| *"Yes, I can handle pressure."* | *"Yes. Month-end is a two-day window for 200 accounts, so tight deadlines are normal for me."* ⭐ |
| *"No sir, I haven't used SAP."* | *"I haven't used SAP directly, but I've worked in Tally for six years, and I picked that up quickly. I'd expect a similar learning curve."* ⭐ |

*(Highlight the third one!)* ⭐ **Never** answer a skills question with a flat *"no."* ✅ Always add **"but here's what I have."** ⭐

*(The formula.)*
> ### 👉 **"I haven't done X, but I've done Y, which is similar."** ✅ 🔗 *(Day 50!)*

### Trap 3: The memorised recital 🧊 🔗 *(Day 78)*

*(Brief reminder.)*

*(Explain.)* Memorising answers **word for word** makes you sound recited — and it breaks completely if they ask a variation. ✅

*(The fix.)* Memorise the **shape** *(C-E-R)* and your **evidence** *(the numbers and stories)*. ✅ Let the words vary. ⭐

*(Practical.)* Practise each answer **three** times, **differently** each time 🔗 *(Day 44)* ✅

### Trap 4: Not preparing evidence 📊

*(The biggest practical gap.)*

*(Explain.)* Most candidates prepare **answers**. ✅ Strong candidates prepare **evidence**. ⭐

*(The preparation method.)*

> ### 👉 Prepare **three stories** and **five numbers**. Then any question can be answered. ✅

*(Explain.)* Because the same story serves multiple questions: ✅

| Your automation story serves... |
|---|
| *"Tell me about yourself"* ✅ |
| *"Why should we hire you?"* ✅ |
| *"Greatest strength?"* ✅ |
| *"A difficult situation?"* ✅ |
| *"An achievement you're proud of?"* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **One good story answers five questions.** ✅ So prepare **three** stories properly rather than **ten** answers superficially. ⭐

*(The three stories to prepare.)*
1. 🔧 A **problem you solved** *(with a number)* ✅
2. 🤝 A **difficult person or conflict** you handled ✅
3. 📚 Something you **learned fast** ✅

### Trap 5: Body language and the closing ⚠️

*(Practical, often overlooked.)*

*(Explain.)* Three quick things that affect the outcome: ✅

**1. The freeze** 🧊 🔗 *(Day 38)*
If your mind goes blank: ✅ **"That's a good question — let me think for a second."** ⭐ Completely acceptable. ✅

**2. Asking for clarification** ✅
- ✅ *"Just to make sure I understand — are you asking about ___?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* This is a **strength**, not a weakness. ✅ Answering the wrong question is worse. ✅

**3. The close** ⭐
End with a clear, warm line: ✅
- ✅ *"Thank you for your time — I really enjoyed the conversation, and I'm genuinely interested in the role."* ⭐

*(And the follow-up!)* 📧 Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours ✅
> ✅ *"Thank you for your time today. I enjoyed learning more about the role, particularly the reporting side. Please let me know if you need anything further from me. Looking forward to hearing from you."* ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 57!)*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Very few Indian candidates do this. ✅ It genuinely makes you memorable. ⭐

## 📝 5. HOMEWORK TEST (17:00 – End)

*(Energetic, actionable.)*

**Question 1 — the weakness answer.** 🤔 Write **your** answer using the three-part shape:

> *"My weakness has been ___. What I've been doing about it is ___. As a result, ___."* ⭐

*(Say why.)* This is the question people most often answer badly. ✅ Get it written down properly, once. ⭐

**Question 2 — your three stories.** 📊 In the comments, write **one line** describing each of your three prepared stories:

1. A problem you solved *(with a number)* ✅
2. A conflict you handled ✅
3. Something you learned fast ✅

*(Warm close.)*

*(Lean in, sincere.)*

*(Warmly.)* And a thought about all of this.

*(Slowly.)* An interview is not a test of your **worth**. ✅ It's a **matching** exercise — they have a problem, and they're checking whether you're the solution. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* So your job isn't to be **impressive**. It's to be **clear** about what you can do. ⭐

*(Sincere.)* And if you don't get it — that often means the match wasn't right, not that you weren't good enough. ✅ Genuinely. 💙

*(Firmly.)* Prepare your three stories. Find your five numbers. And go in as a professional having a conversation — not a candidate asking for a favour. ⭐
