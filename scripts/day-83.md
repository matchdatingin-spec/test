# Day 83 — Professional Email Writing: The Complete Formula with Examples

**Target length:** 16–18 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** A five-part formula from subject line to sign-off — and a clear-out of the 1950s letter English still taught in Kerala schools.

**📌 Format note:** Opens the **workplace writing** sub-block (Days 83–87). Section 3 becomes the five email parts plus three full model emails. Section 4 covers the Indian-English business letter tradition and why it damages credibility internationally.

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Amused, then serious.)*

Here's an email a real Malayali professional might send:

> 📜 *"Respected Sir,*
>
> *With reference to the above cited subject, I am in receipt of your esteemed mail dated 14th instant. In this connection, I would like to bring to your kind notice that the said report is under process. I request you to kindly do the needful at the earliest and oblige.*
>
> *Thanking you,*
> *Yours faithfully,*
> *Vinod"* 😅

*(Pause.)*

*(Explain.)* Every word is real English. ✅ The grammar is correct. ✅ And it reads like a **government file from 1955**. 📜

*(Point at camera.)*

Now the modern version:

> ✅ *"Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *Thanks for your email. The report is in progress — I'll have it with you by Thursday.*
>
> *Let me know if you need anything before then.*
>
> *Best regards,*
> *Vinod"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* **Forty words instead of eighty.** ✅ Warmer. Clearer. And it makes Vinod look **senior**, not junior. ⭐

*(Lean in.)*

*(Explain.)* This matters more than you think. Your emails are read by people who've never met you — clients, managers abroad, recruiters. ✅ Your email **is** your professional impression. 📧

Today: the complete formula. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 5:00)

*(Explain the shift in what professional writing means.)*

### 📜 Why we write like this — the honest history

*(Explain warmly — this isn't a personal failing.)*

*(Explain.)* Kerala's English education inherited **British colonial administrative writing**. ✅ Letter-writing was taught from templates designed for 1900s government correspondence: ✅

- *"With reference to the above cited subject..."* ✅
- *"I beg to state that..."* ✅
- *"Kindly do the needful and oblige."* ✅
- *"Thanking you, Yours faithfully."* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* These were taught as **correct** — and marks were given for them. ✅ So there's nothing shameful here. It's what school rewarded. ⭐

*(But.)* Modern professional English moved on **decades** ago. ✅ And when you write in the old style today, international readers infer: ✅
- you're **unfamiliar** with modern business norms ⚠️
- you're **junior** *(senior people write short)* ⚠️
- you may be **hiding** the actual point ⚠️

### 🎯 The one principle of modern email

*(The core idea.)*

> ### 👉 **Modern professional English is SHORT and WARM.** ✅
> Not long and formal. ✅

*(Explain.)* Two things changed: ✅

| Old assumption 📜 | Modern reality ⭐ |
|---|---|
| Long = respectful | Long = **wasting the reader's time** ⚠️ |
| Formal = professional | **Clear** = professional ✅ |
| Elaborate = educated | Plain = **confident** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Senior people write the shortest emails.** ✅ A CEO's email is often three lines. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Short is respectful. Clear is professional.** ✅
> **ചെറുതാണ് മാന്യം. വ്യക്തതയാണ് പ്രൊഫഷണൽ.** ⭐

### 🔑 THE FORMULA: S-O-B-A-C

*(Write it big.)*

> ### **S** = **Subject** — specific, scannable 📌
> ### **O** = **Opening** — greeting + one warm line 👋
> ### **B** = **Body** — the point, in the first two lines ⭐
> ### **A** = **Action** — exactly what you want, and by when 🎯
> ### **C** = **Close** — sign-off ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **Five parts.** ✅ Most emails should be **under 100 words**. ⭐

*(Say it.)* **Subject · Opening · Body · Action · Close.** ✅

### ⚠️ And the rule that matters most

> ### 👉 **Put the point in the FIRST two lines.** ✅

*(Explain.)* Malayalam letter tradition — and Malayalam politeness generally — builds context **first** and arrives at the point **last**. 🔗 *(Day 40's storytelling difference!)* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* English business email does the **opposite**. ✅ The point comes **first**, the context after. 🔄

*(Show it.)*
| ❌ Context-first | ✅ Point-first |
|---|---|
| *"As you know, the quarter is ending and there have been several client queries, and considering the workload, I would like to request..."* ⚠️ | *"Could I take Friday off? I've cleared my pending work and Anu will cover urgent queries."* ⭐ |

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Reader's first question is "what do you want from me?"** ✅ Answer it immediately. ✅

## 🔍 3. THE FIVE PARTS + MODELS (5:00 – 14:00)

---

### 📌 Part S — SUBJECT LINE

*(The most under-used part.)*

*(Explain.)* The subject decides whether your email gets **opened** and **found again later**. ✅

| ❌ Weak | ✅ Strong |
|---|---|
| *Regarding* | **Q3 report — draft for your review** ⭐ |
| *Important* | **Leave request: 14–15 March** ✅ |
| *Hi* | **Invoice #4471 — clarification needed** ⭐ |
| *Please help* | **Access request: SAP reporting module** ✅ |
| *Meeting* | **Wednesday 3pm meeting — agenda attached** ⭐ |

*(The rules.)* ✅
1. ⭐ **Name the thing** *(report, invoice, leave, meeting)* ✅
2. ✅ Add the **detail** *(date, number, name)* ✅
3. ✅ Keep it under **8 words** ✅
4. ⭐ If action is needed, **say so**: *"Action needed: ..."* ✅

*(Useful prefixes.)* 💡
- ✅ **"Action needed:"** ⭐
- ✅ **"FYI:"** *(no reply expected)* ✅
- ✅ **"Reminder:"** ✅
- ✅ **"Follow-up:"** ✅ 🔗 *(Day 76!)*

---

### 👋 Part O — OPENING

*(Greeting + one line.)*

**The greeting — pick by relationship:**

| Situation | ✅ Greeting |
|---|---|
| Colleague, known | **Hi Rajesh,** ⭐ |
| Slightly formal | **Hello Rajesh,** ✅ |
| Client, first contact | **Dear Mr. Menon,** ✅ |
| Unknown name | **Hello,** ✅ / **Dear Sir/Madam,** ✅ |
| Team | **Hi all,** ⭐ / **Hi team,** ✅ |

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *Respected Sir* ✅ 📜
- ❌ *Dear Sir* *(when you know their name!)* ⚠️
- ❌ *Dear Respected Madam* ✅
- ❌ *Hai / Hii* ⚠️ *(spelling!)*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Use their name whenever you know it.** ✅ *"Hi Rajesh"* is warmer and more professional than *"Respected Sir."* ✅

**The one warm line:**

| Situation | ✅ Line |
|---|---|
| Replying | **Thanks for your email.** ⭐ |
| Following up | **Hope you're doing well.** ✅ |
| First contact | **I hope this finds you well.** ✅ |
| Quick internal | *(skip it — go straight to the point)* ⭐ |

*(Note.)* ⭐ For **internal, quick** emails, skip the warm line entirely. ✅ Colleagues appreciate directness. ✅

---

### ⭐ Part B — BODY *(the point, first)*

*(The core skill.)*

*(Explain.)* One or two sentences. **The point.** ✅ Then context if needed. ✅

**Examples:**

| Purpose | ✅ Opening body line |
|---|---|
| Request | **Could you send me the March invoice?** ⭐ |
| Update | **The report is ready — I've attached it.** ✅ |
| Problem | **There's a mismatch in the July figures.** ⭐ |
| Question | **Quick question about the new process.** ✅ |
| Bad news | **Unfortunately, we won't make Friday's deadline.** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice — **all under 10 words.** ✅ Context comes **after**. ✅

*(The structure inside the body.)*
1. **The point** ⭐ *(1 sentence)*
2. **Why / context** ✅ *(1–2 sentences)*
3. **Any detail** ✅ *(bullets if more than 2 items!)*

*(Use bullets!)* ⭐ If you have three or more items, **list** them:

> ✅ *"I need three things to close this:*
> *• the signed PO*
> *• the delivery date*
> *• the GST number"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Bullets get read. Paragraphs get skimmed.** ✅

---

### 🎯 Part A — ACTION

*(The part most people forget.)*

*(Explain.)* Say **exactly** what you want and **by when**. ✅

| ❌ Vague | ✅ Clear |
|---|---|
| *Please do the needful* 📜 | **Could you approve this by Thursday?** ⭐ |
| *Kindly look into it* | **Could you check the July entry and let me know?** ✅ |
| *Awaiting your reply* | **Let me know by Friday if that works.** ⭐ |
| *Please revert* 🔗 | **Please let me know your thoughts.** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **A deadline is not rude.** ✅ It's helpful — it lets the reader prioritise. ✅

*(Softening a deadline.)* 🔗 *(Day 42)*
- ✅ *"...by Thursday, if possible."* ⭐
- ✅ *"...whenever you get a chance — ideally before Friday."* ⭐
- ✅ *"No rush, but I'd need it before the 15th."* ✅

---

### ✅ Part C — CLOSE

**The sign-off:**

| Situation | ✅ Sign-off |
|---|---|
| Standard professional | **Best regards,** ⭐ |
| Slightly warmer | **Thanks,** ⭐ / **Many thanks,** ✅ |
| Internal, casual | **Cheers,** ✅ / **Thanks,** ✅ |
| Formal, first contact | **Kind regards,** ✅ |
| Awaiting something | **Looking forward to hearing from you,** ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 57!)* |

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *Thanking you* 📜 → ✅ **Thanks** ✅
- ❌ *Yours faithfully / Yours obediently* 📜 ⚠️
- ❌ *Awaiting your revert* 📜 🔗
- ❌ *With warm regards and best wishes always* ⚠️ *(too much)*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **"Best regards"** works for 90% of professional emails. ✅ Learn one and use it. ✅

---

### 📧 MODEL 1 — Requesting something

> ✅ **Subject:** March invoice — copy needed
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *Could you send me a copy of the March invoice for Alpha Traders? I need it to close the quarterly reconciliation.*
>
> *If you could get it to me by Wednesday, that would be great.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Word count: 42.)* ✅ Point-first, clear action, clear deadline. ⭐

---

### 📧 MODEL 2 — Reporting a problem

> ✅ **Subject:** Mismatch in July receivables — ₹42,000
>
> *Hi Priya,*
>
> *I've found a ₹42,000 mismatch in the July receivables figure and wanted to flag it early.*
>
> *It looks like two invoices for Beta Logistics were entered under both their Kochi and Calicut account codes. I've listed the entries in the attached sheet.*
>
> *Could we get 15 minutes tomorrow to confirm before I pass it to audit? I'm free after 11.*
>
> *Best regards,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice: the **number** is in the subject line. ✅ Priya knows the scale before she opens it. ⭐

*(And notice — he doesn't blame anyone.)* 🔗 *(Day 59!)* ✅ *"It looks like..."* ✅ Neutral. ⭐

---

### 📧 MODEL 3 — Following up on no reply

*(The most-needed email nobody teaches.)*

> ✅ **Subject:** Follow-up: SAP access request (sent 10 March)
>
> *Hi Suresh,*
>
> *Just following up on my request below for SAP reporting access — I know you've had a busy week.*
>
> *Is there anything else you need from my side to move it forward? I'm blocked on the monthly report until it's approved.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Three techniques: ✅
1. *"Just following up"* ✅ — soft, non-accusing 🔗 *(Day 76)*
2. *"I know you've had a busy week"* ⭐ — gives them a graceful excuse ✅
3. *"I'm blocked on..."* ⭐ — explains **urgency** without complaining ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ Never write *"I have not received any reply from your side"* ⚠️ It reads as an accusation. ✅ Say **"just following up."** ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (14:00 – 17:00)

### Trap 1: The colonial phrase book 📜

*(The main clear-out. Give the full replacement list.)*

| ❌ Old Indian business English | ✅ Modern |
|---|---|
| Respected Sir | **Hi [Name]** / **Dear Mr. [Name]** ⭐ |
| With reference to the above cited subject | **About the ___** ✅ |
| I am in receipt of your mail | **Thanks for your email** ⭐ |
| I would like to bring to your kind notice | **I wanted to flag that** ⭐ |
| Kindly do the needful | **Could you take care of this?** ⭐ |
| Please do the needful at the earliest | **Could you look at this by Thursday?** ✅ |
| Awaiting your revert | **Looking forward to your reply** ⭐ |
| Please find attached herewith | **I've attached the file** ⭐ |
| The same is being forwarded | **I'm forwarding it** ✅ |
| It is requested that | **Could you please** ✅ |
| Thanking you | **Thanks** ⭐ |
| Yours faithfully | **Best regards** ⭐ |
| Your good self | **you** ✅ |
| Please intimate | **Please let me know** ⭐ |
| As per our telephonic conversation | **As we discussed on the call** ✅ |
| At the earliest | **as soon as you can** / **by [date]** ⭐ |
| Do the necessary | **handle it** / **sort it out** ✅ |
| Revert back 🔗 | **get back to me** ⭐ |
| Prepone 🔗 | **move earlier** ✅ |
| Please acknowledge receipt | **Let me know you've got this** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Print this table. ✅ It's the single most useful page in this video. ⭐

### Trap 2: Context-first structure 🔄

*(The structural habit.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayalam politeness builds up to the request. ✅ Asking directly can feel abrupt. ✅

*(But.)* In email, **the reader is busy and scanning.** ✅ If your point is in paragraph three, it may never be read. ⚠️

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 **Point first. Context second. Politeness in the WORDS, not the ORDER.** ✅

*(Show it.)*

❌ **Context-first:**
> *"As you are aware, the month-end is approaching and there is considerable pressure on the accounts team. Additionally, two of my colleagues are on leave. In view of the above circumstances, I would like to request an extension for the reconciliation report."* ⚠️

✅ **Point-first:**
> *"Could I have two extra days for the reconciliation report? Two colleagues are on leave this week and month-end has been heavy. I'd have it with you by Thursday instead of Tuesday."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Same request. Same politeness. Half the words.** ✅ And the reader knows what you want in **six** words. ⭐

### Trap 3: Over-apologising in writing 🙏 🔗 *(Day 42)*

| ❌ Excessive | ✅ Right |
|---|---|
| *Sorry sorry, extremely sorry for the delay and inconvenience caused* | **Sorry for the delay.** ⭐ |
| *I apologise for troubling you with this matter* | **Quick question —** ✅ |
| *Sorry to disturb you sir* | **Sorry to bother you —** ✅ 🔗 |
| *Kindly excuse my mistake* | **That was my mistake — I've corrected it.** ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 59!)* |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **One apology + the fix.** ✅ Then move on. ✅

### Trap 4: Small mechanical errors that get noticed ⚠️

*(Practical checklist.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *Hai / Hii* | **Hi** ✅ |
| *Dear Sir/Mam* | **Dear Sir/Madam** ✅ *(Madam, not Mam!)* |
| *Please find the attachment* | **I've attached it** ⭐ |
| *Myself Vinod* 🔗 | **This is Vinod** / **I'm Vinod** ✅ |
| *Reply me* 🔗 | **Reply to me** / **Let me know** ✅ |
| *Discuss about* 🔗 | **discuss** ✅ |
| *I am having a doubt* 🔗 | **I have a question** ⭐ |
| *Updation* ⚠️ | **update** ✅ *(Indian-office English only)* |
| *Regards* + no name | **Regards, Vinod** ✅ |
| ALL CAPS ⚠️ | normal case ✅ *(caps = shouting!)* |

*(Note "updation.")* ⚠️ Very common in Indian offices, but it isn't standard English. ✅ Say **"update"** ✅ or **"updating."** ✅

### Trap 5: No proofread ✍️

*(Practical and easy.)*

*(Explain.)* Three checks before you hit send: ✅

1. ⭐ **Read the first two lines** — is the point there? ✅
2. ✅ **Check the name spelling** — nothing damages more than a misspelt name ⚠️
3. ⭐ **Check the attachment is actually attached** 📎 😄

*(The classic.)* ⭐ Write the word *"attached"* **last** — attach the file **first**. ✅ It prevents the most common professional embarrassment. ✅

### ✅ The 60-second email checklist

*(Give them a tool.)*

| ✅ | Check |
|---|---|
| ⬜ | Subject names the **thing** + a **detail** ✅ |
| ⬜ | Greeting uses their **name** ✅ |
| ⬜ | The **point** is in the first two lines ⭐ |
| ⬜ | Action + **deadline** stated ✅ |
| ⬜ | Under **100 words** *(if possible)* ⭐ |
| ⬜ | Zero *"needful," "revert," "herewith"* ✅ |
| ⬜ | Sign-off + your **name** ✅ |
| ⬜ | Attachment actually **attached** 📎 |

*(Warm close.)*

*(Lean in, sincere.)*

*(Warmly.)* And one thing I want to be clear about. 💙

*(Explain.)* If you've been writing *"kindly do the needful"* for fifteen years — that's not your mistake. ✅ It's what school taught, what your first manager wrote, and what everyone around you still uses. ✅

*(Firmly.)* And within India, it's genuinely understood. Nobody will misunderstand you. ✅

*(But.)* The moment your email goes to a client in Dubai, a manager in Bangalore, or a recruiter abroad — it **dates** you. ⚠️ Unfairly, but it does. ✅

*(Emphasise, warm.)* So learn both. ✅ Write the old style when it's expected locally, and the modern style when it counts. **That's professional range**, not disloyalty to how you were taught. ⭐
