# Day 84 — Leave Request Email: 10 Correct Templates for Work and College

**Target length:** 14–16 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Ten ready templates — and the one structural change that gets leave **approved** instead of questioned.

**📌 Format note:** A **template** video within the workplace-writing block. Section 3 becomes the ten templates; Section 4 covers the over-explaining and over-apologising habits that weaken leave requests.

**📅 DATE CONSISTENCY — check before filming:** All weekday/date pairs are anchored to **March 2025**, where **14 March is a Friday**. ✅ *(11 Tue · 12 Wed · 13 Thu · 14 Fri · 17 Mon · 20 Thu · 21 Fri.)* ⚠️ If you change any date, re-check the weekday — a subject line reading *"Thursday 14 March"* next to *"Friday 14 March"* is the kind of slip viewers screenshot. ⭐

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Practical, everyday.)*

You need Friday off. You write:

> 📜 *"Respected Sir, I am writing this mail to kindly inform you that due to some unavoidable personal reasons at home which I am unable to explain in detail, I will not be able to attend office on Friday. I am extremely sorry for the inconvenience caused to you and the team. Kindly grant me leave and do the needful. Thanking you, Vinod"* 😰

*(Pause.)*

*(Explain.)* Sixty-two words. ✅ One apology, buried in padding. ✅ And notice what's **missing**: ⚠️

- ❓ What **date** exactly? ✅
- ❓ How many **days**? ✅
- ❓ Who's covering your **work**? ⚠️
- ❓ Are you **reachable**? ✅

*(Point at camera.)*

*(Emphasise.)* Your manager now has to **reply and ask**. ✅ Which is why leave requests get delayed or questioned. ⚠️

*(Confident.)* Now the version that gets approved in one line:

> ✅ *"Hi Rajesh, I'd like to take Friday, 14 March off — a family matter. My pending work is up to date and Anu can cover urgent queries. I'll be reachable on my mobile if anything comes up. Let me know if that's okay. Thanks, Vinod"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* **Forty-five words. Zero apologies. Every question answered.** ✅

Today: ten templates. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Explain what a manager actually needs.)*

### 🎯 What your manager is actually thinking

*(The reframe.)*

*(Explain.)* When a leave request arrives, your manager has **exactly three** questions: ✅

| # | Their question |
|---|---|
| 1️⃣ | **When**, and for how long? 📅 |
| 2️⃣ | Who covers your **work**? 🤝 |
| 3️⃣ | Will this **break** anything? ⚠️ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Answer all three, and approval is easy.** ✅ Miss any one, and they have to come back to you — which is friction, and friction creates hesitation. ⚠️

*(Point out something important.)* Notice what is **not** on that list: ⭐

> ### 👉 **Your reason.** ✅

*(Explain.)* Your manager mostly doesn't need it, and often isn't allowed to require it. ✅ A **short** reason is polite. A **long** explanation is unnecessary — and it accidentally signals that you think your request needs defending. ⚠️

### 🔑 THE FORMULA: D-R-C-R

*(Write it big.)*

> ### **D** = **Dates** — exact, with the day name 📅
> ### **R** = **Reason** — one short phrase ✅
> ### **C** = **Cover** — who handles your work 🤝
> ### **R** = **Reachable** — how to contact you 📱

*(Emphasise.)* **Four elements. Usually four sentences.** ✅

*(Say it.)* **Dates · Reason · Cover · Reachable.** ✅

### ⭐ The element that changes everything: COVER

*(This is the highest-value idea.)*

*(Explain.)* Most leave emails ask for **permission**. ✅ A strong one presents a **plan**. ✅

*(Show the difference.)*
| ❌ Asking permission | ✅ Presenting a plan |
|---|---|
| *"Kindly grant me leave"* ⚠️ | *"My pending work is up to date and Anu can cover urgent queries"* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ The second version tells your manager: *"you don't need to worry about anything."* ✅ That's what makes approval automatic. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* **Never send a leave request without the cover line.** ✅ It's the single biggest improvement you can make. ⭐

*(Slogan.)*
> **Don't ask for permission. Present a plan.** ✅
> **അനുവാദം ചോദിക്കരുത് — ഒരു പ്ലാൻ അവതരിപ്പിക്കുക.** ⭐

### 📌 And the subject line

*(Quick but important.)* 🔗 *(Day 83)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *Leave* | **Leave request: Friday 14 March** ⭐ |
| *Regarding leave* | **Sick leave: 12–13 March** ✅ |
| *Please grant leave* | **Half-day request: Tuesday afternoon** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Put the dates in the subject.** ✅ Your manager can approve without opening the email. ✅

## 🔍 3. THE 10 TEMPLATES (4:00 – 13:00)

*(Each one short and ready to copy.)*

---

### 1️⃣ Planned casual leave — one day 📅

> ✅ **Subject:** Leave request: Friday 14 March
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *I'd like to take Friday, 14 March off for a family function.*
>
> *My pending work is up to date, and Anu has agreed to cover any urgent client queries. I'll be reachable on my mobile if anything comes up.*
>
> *Let me know if that works.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

---

### 2️⃣ Planned leave — multiple days ✈️

> ✅ **Subject:** Leave request: 17–21 March (5 days)
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *I'd like to apply for leave from Monday 17 to Friday 21 March — five working days. I'm travelling for a family wedding.*
>
> *To cover it:*
> *• The month-end reconciliation will be completed before I leave*
> *• Anu will handle daily client queries*
> *• I've documented the pending items in the shared sheet*
>
> *I'll have limited phone access but will check email once a day.*
>
> *Please let me know if this is okay.*
>
> *Best regards,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Note the **bullets** for a longer leave. ✅ Three lines that remove all your manager's doubt. ⭐

---

### 3️⃣ Sick leave — same day 🤒

*(Keep it very short. You're ill.)*

> ✅ **Subject:** Sick leave today
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *I'm unwell today — I've had a fever since last night — so I won't be able to come in.*
>
> *I've asked Anu to handle anything urgent. I'll be on my phone if something needs my input.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Note.)* ✅ **"I'm unwell"** ⭐ is the standard professional phrase. ✅

*(⚠️ Avoid.)* ❌ *"I am having fever"* 🔗 *(Day 54!)* → ✅ **"I have a fever"** ✅ or **"I'm running a temperature"** ✅

---

### 4️⃣ Sick leave — extending 🤒

> ✅ **Subject:** Sick leave — extending to Thursday
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *I'm still not well — the doctor has advised two more days' rest, so I'd need Wednesday and Thursday off as well.*
>
> *I'll send the medical certificate when I'm back. Anu is continuing to cover, and I've updated her on the pending reconciliation.*
>
> *Sorry for the short notice.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Note.)* ⭐ **One** apology is appropriate here — short notice genuinely affects people. ✅ But only one. ✅

---

### 5️⃣ Emergency leave — immediate 🚨

*(Very short. You have other things to deal with.)*

> ✅ **Subject:** Emergency leave — leaving now
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *There's a family emergency and I need to leave immediately. I'll keep you updated.*
>
> *Anu has my pending items. I'll be on my mobile.*
>
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ In a genuine emergency, **send this and go.** ✅ Details later. No apology needed. ✅

---

### 6️⃣ Half-day request 🕐

> ✅ **Subject:** Half-day request: Tuesday afternoon
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *Could I take a half-day on Tuesday 11 March? I have a medical appointment at 3 pm.*
>
> *I'll finish the morning's work before I leave and will be reachable if anything urgent comes up.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

---

### 7️⃣ Work-from-home request 🏠

> ✅ **Subject:** WFH request: Thursday 20 March
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *Could I work from home on Thursday? I have a plumbing repair at home that needs someone present.*
>
> *I'll be online for the full day and available on call. The only thing I'd need to shift is the 11 am team sync — happy to join that on video instead.*
>
> *Let me know if that's fine.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Note: he **flagged the one problem himself** *(the 11 am meeting)* ✅ and offered a solution. ✅ That's what makes it easy to approve. ⭐

---

### 8️⃣ Leave already refused once — asking again ⚠️

*(The tricky one. Handle carefully.)*

> ✅ **Subject:** Revisiting leave request: 18–22 March
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *I know you weren't able to approve my leave for 18–22 March because of the audit. I wanted to check whether there's any flexibility now that the audit dates have moved.*
>
> *If the full week isn't possible, would 20–22 March work instead? I'd manage the audit prep before I go, and Anu can handle the follow-ups.*
>
> *Completely understand if it's still difficult — just wanted to ask.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Three techniques: ✅
1. **Acknowledge** the earlier refusal without resentment ✅
2. Offer a **reduced** alternative ⭐
3. **Give them an exit** — *"completely understand if..."* ⭐

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Asking twice is fine.** ✅ Asking twice **politely** is professional. ✅

---

### 9️⃣ College leave — to a professor 🎓

> ✅ **Subject:** Leave request — 14 March — Vinod K, S5 B.Com
>
> *Dear Professor Menon,*
>
> *I won't be able to attend classes on Friday, 14 March, as I have a family function out of town.*
>
> *I'll collect the notes from my classmates and complete any assignments given that day. Please let me know if there's anything I need to submit in advance.*
>
> *Thank you,*
> *Vinod K*
> *S5 B.Com, Roll No. 23* ⭐

*(Note.)* ⭐ For college, **include your class and roll number** in the signature ✅ — professors teach hundreds of students. ✅

---

### 🔟 Leave for an exam or interview 🎯

*(Sensitive — you may not want to say it's an interview.)*

> ✅ **Subject:** Leave request: Wednesday 12 March
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *Could I take Wednesday 12 March off? I have a personal commitment I need to attend to.*
>
> *My pending items will be cleared by Tuesday evening, and Anu can cover anything urgent.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **"A personal commitment"** ✅ is a complete and professional reason. ✅ You are **not** obliged to say more. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* And you should **never** feel you must reveal an interview. ✅ *"Personal commitment"* is honest and sufficient. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (13:00 – 15:30)

### Trap 1: Over-explaining the reason 📖

*(The core habit.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayali leave emails often contain a **paragraph** of explanation: ✅

> ⚠️ *"Due to some unavoidable circumstances at home, as my mother is not well and there is nobody else available to accompany her to the hospital since my brother is away in Bangalore..."* ✅

*(Explain warmly.)* Why? Because culturally, asking for something feels like it needs **justification**. ✅ We're taught not to inconvenience others without good cause. 🙏

*(Firmly.)* But in professional email, a long reason has the **opposite** effect: ⚠️
- it suggests you think the request is **unreasonable** ⚠️
- it invites your manager to **judge** whether the reason is good enough ⚠️
- it's often **more personal** than they need or want ✅

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 **One short phrase.** That's the whole reason. ✅

*(The reason menu — all complete and professional.)*
- ✅ *a family function* ✅
- ✅ *a medical appointment* ✅
- ✅ *a family matter* ⭐
- ✅ *a personal commitment* ⭐
- ✅ *I'm unwell* ✅
- ✅ *travelling out of town* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Four words maximum.** ✅ If your manager needs more, they'll ask. ✅

### Trap 2: Over-apologising 🙏 🔗 *(Days 42, 83)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *Extremely sorry for the inconvenience caused* ⚠️ | *(usually nothing needed!)* ⭐ |
| *Sorry sorry, kindly excuse me* | **Sorry for the short notice.** ✅ *(only if genuinely short)* |
| *I apologise for troubling you with this request* | *(nothing)* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise strongly.)*

> ### 👉 **Taking leave is not a favour. It's part of your job terms.** ✅

*(Explain.)* You have leave **entitlement**. ✅ Using it is normal and expected. ✅ Apologising for it frames it as something you're getting away with. ⚠️

*(When an apology IS right.)*
- ✅ **Short notice** *(same-day, or under 24 hours)* ✅
- ✅ **A genuine problem you're causing** *(missing a key deadline)* ✅

*(Otherwise.)* ⭐ **No apology.** ✅ Just *"Thanks"* or *"Let me know if that works."* ✅

### Trap 3: "Kindly grant me leave" 📜 🔗 *(Day 83)*

| ❌ Old | ✅ Modern |
|---|---|
| Kindly grant me leave | **I'd like to take Friday off** ⭐ |
| I request you to sanction my leave | **Could I take Friday off?** ✅ |
| Kindly do the needful | **Let me know if that works** ⭐ |
| Awaiting your kind approval | **Let me know if that's okay** ✅ |
| I beg to state that | *(delete entirely)* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **"I'd like to take ___ off"** ✅ and **"Could I take ___ off?"** ✅ — learn these two shapes. ✅

*(The difference in tone.)*
- ✅ *"I'd like to take Friday off."* ⭐ *(confident, for entitled leave)*
- ✅ *"Could I take Friday off?"* ✅ *(slightly softer, for a favour or a busy period)*

*(Both are fine.)* Use the first for planned leave, the second when you know it's inconvenient. ✅

### Trap 4: Grammar errors specific to leave emails ⚠️

*(Practical checklist.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I am having fever 🔗 | **I have a fever** ✅ |
| I am in leave 🔗 | **I'm on leave** ✅ |
| I want to take leave on Friday | **I'd like to take Friday off** ⭐ |
| Kindly sanction 2 days leave | **Could I take two days' leave?** ✅ |
| I will be absent from office | **I won't be in the office** ✅ |
| Please adjust my work | **Anu will cover my work** ⭐ |
| Yesterday night I got fever 🔗 | **I've had a fever since last night** ✅ |
| I will join on Monday | **I'll be back on Monday** ⭐ |
| Leave letter | **leave request** ✅ |

*(Note the last one.)* ⭐ In modern professional English it's a **leave request** or **time-off request** ✅ — not a *"leave letter."* ✅

### Trap 5: Missing the "cover" line 🤝

*(The highest-value omission.)*

*(Explain.)* This is what separates a request that gets approved instantly from one that gets questioned. ✅

*(The cover-line options.)*
- ✅ *"My pending work is up to date."* ⭐
- ✅ *"Anu has agreed to cover urgent queries."* ⭐
- ✅ *"I'll complete the month-end before I leave."* ✅
- ✅ *"I've documented the pending items in the shared sheet."* ⭐
- ✅ *"There's nothing time-sensitive that week."* ✅

*(And the reachability line.)*
- ✅ *"I'll be reachable on my mobile."* ⭐
- ✅ *"I'll check email once a day."* ✅
- ✅ *"I'll be travelling, so phone access may be limited."* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Two extra sentences.** ✅ And they transform your request from a problem into a **plan**. ⭐

### ✅ The 30-second leave-email checklist

| ✅ | Check |
|---|---|
| ⬜ | Subject has the **dates** 📅 |
| ⬜ | Exact dates **and** day names in the body ✅ |
| ⬜ | Reason = **one short phrase** ✅ |
| ⬜ | **Cover** line included 🤝 ⭐ |
| ⬜ | **Reachability** stated 📱 |
| ⬜ | Apology only if notice is **genuinely** short ✅ |
| ⬜ | Zero *"kindly grant," "do the needful"* ✅ |
| ⬜ | Under **80 words** ⭐ |
