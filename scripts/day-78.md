# Day 78 — Job Interview Self-Introduction for Freshers | English Malayalam

**Target length:** 15–17 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** A 90-second structure — **P-A-S-S** — that turns "I have no experience" into a real answer.

**📌 Format note:** Opens the **career block** (Days 78–87). Section 3 becomes the structure plus two full model answers; Section 4 covers the specific cultural habits that weaken Malayali interview answers.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Warm, understanding — this is a high-anxiety topic.)*

You walk into the interview room. You sit down. And they say:

> *"So — tell me about yourself."* 🎤

*(Pause.)*

And you say:

> ❌ *"Myself Arun. I am from Thrissur. I passed out from Calicut University in 2024 with 72%. My father is a farmer and my mother is a housewife. I have one sister. My hobbies are reading and listening to music. That's all about me, sir."* 😰

*(Gently.)* Twenty-five seconds. And you just told them **nothing** about why they should hire you. ✅

*(Point at camera.)*

*(Explain.)* Look at what's wrong there — and none of it is your English: ✅
- ❌ *"Myself Arun"* 🔗 *(Day 02!)*
- ❌ *"passed out"* → sounds like you **fainted** 😄 🔗
- ❌ **Family details** — they didn't ask ✅
- ❌ **Percentage** — it's on your resume ✅
- ❌ **No mention of the job** ⚠️

*(Lean in, confident.)*

Today I'll give you a structure — **P-A-S-S** — for a **90-second** answer that actually makes them want you. ✅

*(Warm.)* And if you're thinking *"but I have no experience"* — that's exactly what we're going to fix. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 5:00)

*(Explain what the question is really asking — this is the key insight.)*

### 🎯 What "tell me about yourself" actually means

*(This reframe changes everything.)*

*(Explain.)* The interviewer is **not** asking about your life. ✅ They're asking:

> ### 👉 **"Why should I hire you for THIS job?"** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Every sentence in your answer should serve that question. ✅ If a sentence doesn't help them decide, **cut it**. ✂️

*(The test.)* Before including anything, ask: *"Does this make me a better candidate for this job?"* ✅
- Your degree → **yes** ✅
- Your internship → **yes** ✅
- Your father's job → **no** ❌
- Your hobbies → **only if relevant** ⚠️

### 🔑 THE STRUCTURE: P-A-S-S

*(Write it big.)*

> ### **P** = **Present** — who you are now *(15 sec)*
> ### **A** = **Ability** — what you can do *(30 sec)*
> ### **S** = **Story** — one proof example *(30 sec)* ⭐
> ### **S** = **So** — why this job, why now *(15 sec)*

*(Emphasise.)* **Ninety seconds. Four blocks.** ✅

*(Say it.)* **Present · Ability · Story · So.** ✅

### ⭐ The block that wins interviews: STORY

*(Spend time here — this is what freshers skip.)*

*(Explain.)* Almost every fresher gives **P** and stops. ✅ Name, degree, college. Done. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* But interviewers hear that from **fifty** candidates. ✅ What they remember is the **story**. 🔗 *(Day 40's S-P-A-R-K!)*

*(Point out.)* And a story doesn't need a **job**. It can come from: ✅

| Source | Example |
|---|---|
| **College project** 🎓 | *"For my final-year project, I..."* ⭐ |
| **Internship** 💼 | *"During my internship, I noticed..."* ⭐ |
| **Volunteering** 🤝 | *"I organised the college tech fest..."* ⭐ |
| **Freelance / part-time** 💰 | *"I did data entry work for a small shop..."* ⭐ |
| **Self-learning** 📚 | *"I taught myself Excel because..."* ⭐ |
| **Family business** 🏪 | *"I helped manage my uncle's shop accounts..."* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise strongly.)* ⭐ **All six of these count as experience.** ✅

*(Firmly.)* When you say *"I have no experience,"* you usually mean *"I have no salary slip."* ✅ Those are **different things**. ⭐

### 💪 The fresher's advantage — how to frame it

*(Genuinely useful reframing.)*

*(Explain.)* Being a fresher is not a weakness in an interview. It's a **different** offer: ✅

| ❌ What freshers say | ✅ What to say instead |
|---|---|
| *I have no experience* | *I'm at the start of my career, so I'm keen to learn your way of working* ⭐ |
| *I don't know that software* | *I haven't used it yet, but I picked up Excel in two weeks, so I learn fast* ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 50!)* |
| *I'm a fresher* | *I'm a recent graduate* ✅ |
| *I need a job* | *I'm looking for a role where I can build a career* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice the last row. ✅ **"I need a job"** puts your need first. **"I'm looking for a role where I can grow"** puts **value** first. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **They don't want experience. They want evidence that you can learn.** ✅
> **അനുഭവം അല്ല — പഠിക്കാൻ കഴിയും എന്ന തെളിവ്.** ✅

## 🔍 3. THE STRUCTURE AND MODEL ANSWERS (5:00 – 12:30)

---

### 🅿️ Block P — PRESENT *(15 seconds)*

*(What to include.)*

> ✅ Your **name** + your **qualification** + **one** positioning line ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"Good morning. I'm Arun. I completed my B.Com from Calicut University last year, and I've been focusing on accounting and data analysis since then."* ⭐

*(Break it down.)*
| Piece | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Greeting | *Good morning* ✅ |
| Name | *I'm Arun* ✅ *(not "Myself Arun")* 🔗 |
| Qualification | *I completed my B.Com* ✅ *(not "passed out")* 🔗 |
| Positioning | *I've been focusing on...* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise the last piece!)* ⭐ That positioning line is what separates you from the other fifty candidates. ✅ It tells them **what you're aiming at**. ✅

*(Options for the positioning line.)*
- ✅ *"...and since then I've been building my skills in ___."* ✅
- ✅ *"...and I've been working on ___ through online courses and projects."* ⭐
- ✅ *"...and I'm looking to start my career in ___."* ✅

*(⚠️ What NOT to include in P.)*
- ❌ your percentage ✅ *(it's on the resume)*
- ❌ your family ✅
- ❌ your date of birth ✅
- ❌ your native place ✅ *(unless asked)*

---

### 🅰️ Block A — ABILITY *(30 seconds)*

*(What you can do — skills, tools, strengths.)*

**Model:**
> ✅ *"On the technical side, I'm comfortable with Excel — including pivot tables and VLOOKUP — and I've worked with Tally for basic accounting. I'm also confident in written and spoken English, which I know matters for client communication. And I'd say my strength is that I'm organised — I'm the kind of person who keeps everything documented."* ⭐

*(Break it down.)*
| Piece | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Technical skills | *Excel, pivot tables, VLOOKUP, Tally* ✅ |
| Communication | *confident in written and spoken English* ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 75!)* |
| Personal strength | *organised, keeps things documented* ✅ |

*(The rules for Block A.)* ✅
1. ⭐ **Be specific** — not *"good at Excel"* ✅ but *"pivot tables and VLOOKUP"* ✅
2. ✅ **Name the tools** — software, systems, languages ✅
3. ⭐ **Connect to the job** — *"which matters for client communication"* ✅
4. ✅ **One personal strength**, with proof ✅

*(⚠️ Avoid these vague claims.)* 🔗 *(Day 75)*
| ❌ Vague | ✅ Specific |
|---|---|
| I'm a good communicator | **I'm comfortable presenting to a group** ⭐ |
| I'm hard-working | **I'm the person who stays until the numbers match** ⭐ |
| I'm a team player | **I work well in a team and I'm reliable under pressure** ⭐ |
| I'm a quick learner | **I taught myself Tally in three weeks** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Every claim needs evidence.** ✅ *"Quick learner"* is a claim. *"I taught myself Tally in three weeks"* is **proof**. ⭐

---

### ⭐ Block S — STORY *(30 seconds)*

*(The block that wins. Use S-P-A-R-K, compressed.)* 🔗 *(Day 40)*

**Model:**
> ✅ *"To give you an example — during my final semester, our college needed to track fees for about 400 students, and it was all being done on paper. I built a simple Excel system with formulas that flagged pending payments automatically. It cut the checking time from two days to about an hour, and the office still uses it. That's when I realised I actually enjoy solving problems with data."* ⭐

*(Break it down — it's S-P-A-R-K!)* 🔗
| Block | In the story |
|---|---|
| **S**etting | *during my final semester, our college...* ✅ |
| **P**roblem | *400 students, all on paper* ⚡ |
| **A**ction | *I built a simple Excel system with formulas* ✅ |
| **R**esult | *two days → one hour, still in use* ⭐ |
| **K**ey point | *that's when I realised I enjoy...* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise the RESULT!)* ⭐ **"Two days to one hour"** ✅ — a **number**. ✅

*(Firmly.)* Interviewers remember **numbers**. ✅ Find a number in your story, even an approximate one: ✅
- ✅ *"about 400 students"* ✅
- ✅ *"reduced errors by half"* ✅
- ✅ *"a team of six"* ✅
- ✅ *"in three weeks"* ✅

*(Story sources for freshers — pick ONE.)*
- 🎓 A **final-year project** you actually did ✅
- 💼 An **internship** task ✅
- 🤝 **Organising** a college event ✅
- 📚 **Teaching yourself** a skill ✅
- 🏪 Helping in a **family business** ✅

---

### 🆂 Block S — SO *(15 seconds)*

*(Why this job, why now.)*

**Model:**
> ✅ *"So when I saw this role, it felt like a good fit — it's accounts work with a data focus, which is exactly the direction I want to grow in. And I'd like to learn it properly in a company where the systems are already strong."* ⭐

*(The rules.)* ✅
1. ⭐ **Name something specific** about **this** job or company ✅
2. ✅ Show you want to **grow**, not just earn ✅
3. ⭐ Keep it **short** — 2 sentences ✅

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *"I need a job urgently"* ✅
- ❌ *"Your company is very reputed and famous"* ⚠️ *(everyone says this)*
- ❌ *"I want to utilise my skills for the growth of your esteemed organisation"* 📜 *(1960s letter English!)* 🔗

---

### 🎤 THE FULL 90-SECOND MODEL — Fresher, Commerce

*(Read it at natural speed. This is the deliverable.)*

> ✅ *"Good morning. I'm Arun. I completed my B.Com from Calicut University last year, and since then I've been building my skills in accounting and data work.*
>
> *On the technical side, I'm comfortable with Excel — pivot tables, VLOOKUP — and I've worked with Tally for basic accounting. I'm also confident in written and spoken English, which I know matters for client communication. I'd say my main strength is that I'm organised; I keep everything documented.*
>
> *To give you an example — in my final semester, our college was tracking fees for about 400 students entirely on paper. I built a simple Excel system that flagged pending payments automatically. It cut the checking time from two days to about an hour, and the office still uses it. That's when I realised I genuinely enjoy solving problems with data.*
>
> *So when I saw this role, it felt like a good fit — accounts work with a data focus is exactly where I want to grow. And I'd like to learn it properly somewhere the systems are already strong."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* **Ninety seconds. Zero family details. One number. One story.** ✅

---

### 🎤 SECOND MODEL — Fresher, Engineering / IT

*(A second template so it transfers.)*

> ✅ *"Good afternoon. I'm Meera. I finished my B.Tech in Computer Science from MG University this year, and I've been focusing on front-end development.*
>
> *I work mainly with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and React. I've built three projects on my own — the main one is a small booking app that I put on GitHub. I'm also comfortable with Git and basic SQL.*
>
> *For that booking app, the tricky part was that I'd never used React before I started. I gave myself three weeks, followed a course, and built it while learning. It's not perfect, but it works, and I understand every line of it. That process taught me I'm okay being uncomfortable while learning something new.*
>
> *So this role interested me because it's front-end focused and you mentioned mentoring for juniors — that's exactly what I need at this stage."* ⭐

*(Point out.)* ⭐ Notice — she **admitted** she didn't know React ✅ and turned it into evidence of learning ability. 🔗 *(Day 50's pattern!)* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **Honesty + a plan beats pretending.** ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (12:30 – 15:30)

### Trap 1: The family and marks introduction 👨‍👩‍👧

*(A real cultural habit — explain it kindly.)*

*(Explain warmly.)* In Kerala, introducing yourself **traditionally** includes family — where you're from, what your father does, how many siblings. ✅ It's how people place you socially, and it comes from a genuinely warm cultural instinct. ✅

*(But.)* In a **job interview**, it: ✅
- uses up your best 30 seconds ⏰
- tells them nothing about your **work** ✅
- can invite bias you don't want ⚠️

*(Firmly, kindly.)* **Cut it.** ✅ Unless they ask directly. ✅

*(Same for marks.)* Your percentage is **already on the resume**. ✅ Saying it aloud wastes time and — if it's not brilliant — draws attention to it. ⚠️

*(What to do if they ask about family.)*
- ✅ *"I'm from Thrissur, and my family is based there."* ✅ *(one line, move on)* ⭐

### Trap 2: "Passed out" and other resume-killers 🔗

*(Quick, high-value fixes.)* 🔗 *(Days 02, 28)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I passed out in 2024 | **I graduated in 2024** ⭐ |
| Myself Arun | **I'm Arun** ⭐ |
| I am having 2 years experience | **I have two years of experience** ✅ |
| I am fresher | **I'm a recent graduate** ⭐ |
| My hobbies are... *(unasked)* | *(skip unless relevant)* ✅ |
| I did my schooling in... | *(skip — too far back)* ✅ |
| That's all about me, sir | **That's a bit about my background.** ⭐ |
| I am interested to join | **I'm interested in joining** ✅ 🔗 *(Day 57!)* |
| Kindly consider my candidature | **I'd really like to be considered** ✅ |

*(Highlight the second-to-last!)* ⚠️ ❌ *"That's all about me"* ✅ sounds like you've run out. ✅ Say **"That's a bit about my background — happy to go into any of it in more detail."** ⭐

### Trap 3: Under-selling — the വിനയം reflex 🙏

*(A genuinely important cultural point.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayali culture values **വിനയം** — modesty. ✅ Talking about your own achievements can feel like showing off, which is genuinely uncomfortable. ✅

*(So we produce.)*
- ⚠️ *"I just did a small project."* ✅
- ⚠️ *"It's nothing much, but..."* ✅
- ⚠️ *"I only know basic Excel."* ✅

*(Point out.)* Every one of those **reduces** you. ⚠️ And the interviewer takes your word for it. ✅

*(The reframe — this matters.)*

> ### 👉 In an interview, describing your work accurately is **not** boasting. It's **answering the question**. ✅

*(Explain.)* They **asked**. ✅ Withholding the information isn't humility — it's leaving the question unanswered. ⭐

*(The upgrades.)*
| 🙏 Modest | ✅ Accurate |
|---|---|
| I just did a small project | **I built a system that saved the office a day's work** ⭐ |
| It's nothing much | **It's something I'm proud of, actually** ✅ |
| I only know basic Excel | **I'm comfortable with pivot tables and VLOOKUP** ⭐ |
| I somehow managed | **I completed it on time** ✅ |
| I think maybe I can do it | **Yes, I can handle that** ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 08!)* |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Accurate, not inflated.** ✅ Don't exaggerate — just stop **shrinking**. ⭐

### Trap 4: The memorised-and-frozen answer 🧊

*(A practical warning.)*

*(Explain.)* Some candidates memorise a self-introduction **word for word**, and then: ✅
- they sound **recited**, not spoken ⚠️
- if interrupted, they **freeze** completely 🧊

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 Memorise the **four blocks**, not the sentences. ✅

*(Explain.)* Know **what** each block contains. ✅ Let the exact words be slightly different every time. ✅ That's what natural speech is. ⭐

*(Practical.)* Practise it **five** times out loud, differently each time 🔗 *(Day 44)* ✅ Then stop. ✅

*(And prepare for interruption!)* If they cut in with a question — **answer it**, then say: ✅
- ✅ *"Should I continue with the rest of my background?"* ⭐

### Trap 5: No question at the end ⚠️

*(A missed opportunity.)*

*(Explain.)* At the end of the interview, they'll ask *"Do you have any questions?"* ✅ And most freshers say ❌ *"No sir."* ⚠️

*(Firmly.)* **Always** have two questions ready. ✅ It signals genuine interest. ⭐

*(Good fresher questions.)*
- ✅ *"What would a typical first three months look like in this role?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Is there training, or would I be learning on the job?"* ✅
- ✅ *"What does success look like in this role after one year?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Could you tell me a bit about the team I'd be working with?"* ✅

*(⚠️ Don't ask about salary first.)* ✅ Wait for them to raise it, or ask in a later round. 🔗 *(Day 81!)*

## 📝 5. HOMEWORK TEST (15:30 – End)

*(Energetic, actionable.)*

**Question 1 — write your four blocks.** In the comments, write **one sentence for each**: ⭐

> **P:** *I'm ___ and I completed ___*
> **A:** *I'm comfortable with ___*
> **S:** *For example, ___*
> **So:** *This role interests me because ___*

*(Say why.)* I'll read these and reply with specific feedback. ✅ This is the single most useful thing you can post in the comments all month. ⭐

**Question 2 — find your story.** Think hard and comment: **what is your ONE story?** 🔍

*(Prompt them.)* A project, an internship task, an event you organised, a skill you taught yourself, something you fixed. ✅ It doesn't need to be impressive — it needs to be **real** and have a **result**. ⭐

*(Warm close.)*

*(Lean in, sincere.)*

*(Warmly.)* And I want to say something directly to the freshers watching. 💙

*(Slowly.)* I know the feeling of sitting outside an interview room thinking *"I have nothing to offer."* ✅ Especially if your college wasn't famous, or your English was mocked, or you don't have an uncle who can make a call. 😔

*(Firmly.)* Here's the truth: **they are not looking for experience.** ✅ They're looking for someone who will **learn fast and not create problems**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* And you can **prove** that — with one honest story about something you figured out. ⭐

*(Sincere.)* Ninety seconds. Four blocks. One story with a number in it. That's genuinely all it takes to be the candidate they remember. ✅
