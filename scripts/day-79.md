# Day 79 — Tell Me About Yourself: Best Answer for Experienced Candidates

**Target length:** 15–17 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** With experience, the problem flips — too much to say. Here's how to compress a career into 90 seconds and explain a move without criticising anyone.

**📌 Format note:** Second video of the **career block**. Section 3 gives the structure plus two full model answers at different experience levels; Section 4 handles the job-change explanation, which is where experienced candidates most often damage themselves.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Direct, recognisable.)*

You have eight years of experience. The interviewer says:

> *"Tell me about yourself."* 🎤

And you begin:

> ❌ *"Sir, I started my career in 2016 in a small firm in Ernakulam doing data entry. Then in 2017 I moved to another company where I was handling billing. Then in 2019 I joined my current company as a junior executive, then I was promoted in 2021, and then in 2022 my reporting manager changed and..."* 😴

*(Pause. Look at watch.)*

**Four minutes.** ⏰ And the interviewer stopped listening at minute two. ✅

*(Point at camera.)*

*(Explain.)* Yesterday, freshers had the problem of **too little** to say. ✅ Your problem is the **opposite** — and it's actually harder. ⚠️

*(Emphasise.)* Because a **chronological** career history is the most boring possible answer. ✅ It's on your resume. They can **read**. ✅

*(Lean in, confident.)*

They want to know: **what are you good at now, and what will you bring us?** ✅

Today: a structure that compresses any career into 90 seconds. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 5:00)

*(Explain the shift in what's being asked.)*

### 🎯 What the question means when you have experience

*(The reframe.)*

*(Explain.)* For a fresher, the question means *"can you learn?"* ✅

For you, it means: ✅

> ### 👉 **"What are you expert in, and why are you leaving?"** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Two things. ✅ And the **second** one is where most experienced candidates damage themselves — we'll handle that properly in Section 4. ⚠️

### ⚠️ Why chronological order fails

*(Explain the mechanism.)*

*(Explain.)* Chronological order has three problems: ✅

**Problem 1: It buries your best material** 📦
Your strongest achievement might be from **last year** ✅ — and in chronological order, they hear it at minute three, if at all. ✅

**Problem 2: It sounds like a list, not a person** 📋
🔗 *(Day 40's lesson!)* Lists have no tension. Stories do. ✅

**Problem 3: It highlights every gap and short stint** ⚠️
If you left a job after eight months, chronological order **spotlights** it. ✅ Thematic order lets you place it in context. ✅

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 Organise by **THEME**, not by **TIME**. ✅

*(Explain.)* Instead of *"2016 then 2017 then 2019,"* say: *"I've spent eight years in accounts, and the part I'm strongest at is reconciliation."* ⭐

### 🔑 THE STRUCTURE: N-E-S-T

*(Write it big.)*

> ### **N** = **Now** — your current position and total experience *(15 sec)*
> ### **E** = **Expertise** — what you're genuinely strong at *(25 sec)*
> ### **S** = **Story** — one achievement with a number *(35 sec)* ⭐
> ### **T** = **Target** — why this role, now *(15 sec)*

*(Emphasise.)* **Ninety seconds.** ✅ Same length as a fresher's answer. ✅

*(Say it.)* **Now · Expertise · Story · Target.** ✅

*(Point out the difference from yesterday.)* ⭐ The **Story** block is **longer** here ✅ — because you have real, measurable results, and that's your biggest advantage over a fresher. ⭐

### 📊 The one thing that separates strong candidates: NUMBERS

*(This is the highest-value idea in the video.)*

*(Explain.)* Experienced candidates say things like: ✅
- ⚠️ *"I handled billing."* ✅ → so does everyone ✅
- ⚠️ *"I managed a team."* ✅ → how many? ✅
- ⚠️ *"I improved the process."* ✅ → by how much? ✅

*(Emphasise.)*

> ### 👉 Make your claims **measurable** wherever you truly can. ✅

*(Show the transformation.)*
| ⚠️ Vague | ⭐ With numbers |
|---|---|
| I handled billing | **I handled billing for about 200 clients a month** ⭐ |
| I managed a team | **I managed a team of six** ⭐ |
| I improved the process | **I cut the monthly closing time from 5 days to 2** ⭐ |
| I trained new staff | **I trained 12 new joiners over two years** ⭐ |
| I handled customers | **I handled around 40 support calls a day** ⭐ |

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ Before your interview, **sit down and find your real numbers.** ✅ How many clients, how many people, how much time saved, what percentage. ✅ Dig through your old emails, reports and appraisals — the figures are usually there.

*(Emphasise — and this matters.)* ⚠️ **Only ever say a number you could defend.** ✅ Approximate language is completely fine when you're genuinely estimating — *"about,"* *"roughly,"* *"around"* ✅ — but the estimate must be **honest**.

*(Serious for a moment.)* ⛔ Never invent a figure to sound impressive. A good interviewer will ask *"How did you measure that?"* or *"Walk me through it"* — and an invented number **collapses on the second question.** ✅ Reference checks and probation reviews find these things. ⭐

*(Reassuring.)* And if you honestly don't have a number? **Say the scope instead.** ✅ *"I was the only person handling billing for the branch"* is strong, specific and completely safe. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Themes, not timeline. Numbers, not adjectives.** 📊 ✅

## 🔍 3. THE STRUCTURE AND MODEL ANSWERS (5:00 – 12:30)

---

### 🅝 Block N — NOW *(15 seconds)*

> ✅ **Current role + total years + your field** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"I'm currently a Senior Accounts Executive at a logistics company in Kochi, and I've been in accounts for about eight years now."* ⭐

*(The rules.)* ✅
1. ✅ Start with **now**, not with your first job ✅
2. ⭐ Give **total** years in the field, not a job-by-job breakdown ✅
3. ✅ Keep it to **one** sentence ✅

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *"I started my career in 2016..."* ✅ *(chronological trap)*
- ❌ *"I am having 8 years experience"* ✅ 🔗 *(Day 54!)*
- ✅ **"I have eight years of experience"** ✅ *(note: experience, no -s!)* 🔗 *(Day 56)*

---

### 🅔 Block E — EXPERTISE *(25 seconds)*

> ✅ **What you're genuinely strong at + the tools + the scale** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"My core strength is month-end closing and reconciliation — that's the part I've done most and enjoy most. I work daily in Tally and SAP, and I'm comfortable with advanced Excel for reporting. At the moment I handle accounts for around 200 clients and I report directly to the Finance Manager."* ⭐

*(Break it down.)*
| Piece | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Core strength | *month-end closing and reconciliation* ⭐ |
| Tools | *Tally, SAP, advanced Excel* ✅ |
| Scale | *around 200 clients* ⭐ |
| Level | *report to the Finance Manager* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Naming one specific strength is more powerful than listing ten.** ✅

*(Explain.)* If you say *"I do everything — billing, GST, payroll, audit, reconciliation, MIS"* ✅ the interviewer hears **generalist**. ✅ If you say *"my core strength is reconciliation"* ✅ they hear **specialist**. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* Pick **one or two** things to be strong at. ✅ Mention the rest briefly. ⭐

---

### ⭐ Block S — STORY *(35 seconds)*

*(The longest block. S-P-A-R-K, compressed.)* 🔗 *(Day 40)*

**Model:**
> ✅ *"To give you a concrete example — when I joined my current company, month-end closing was taking five working days, and we were regularly late with client reports. I went through the process and found we were doing three reconciliations manually that could be automated in Excel. I built the templates, trained two colleagues, and we brought closing down to two days. That's held for the last three years, and it's probably the work I'm most proud of."* ⭐

*(Break it down.)* 🔗
| Block | In the story |
|---|---|
| **S**etting | *when I joined my current company* ✅ |
| **P**roblem | *closing took 5 days, reports were late* ⚡ |
| **A**ction | *found 3 manual reconciliations, built templates, trained 2 colleagues* ✅ |
| **R**esult | *5 days → 2 days, held for 3 years* ⭐📊 |
| **K**ey point | *the work I'm most proud of* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Two numbers in the result.** ✅ *(5→2 days, held 3 years)* ✅

*(The story-selection rule.)*

> ### 👉 Pick the story that matches the **job you're applying for**. ✅

*(Explain.)* If the new role emphasises **process improvement** → tell the automation story ✅
If it emphasises **team leadership** → tell a story about training or managing ✅
If it emphasises **client handling** → tell a difficult-client story ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Prepare three stories.** ✅ Choose based on the job description. ⭐

---

### 🅣 Block T — TARGET *(15 seconds)*

> ✅ **Why this role, and why now** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"I've reached a point where I've done reconciliation well for several years and I'd like to move into a role with more analysis and reporting. That's exactly what this position looked like from the job description, which is why I applied."* ⭐

*(The rules.)* ✅
1. ⭐ Frame it as **growth**, not escape ✅
2. ✅ Reference something **specific** from the job description ✅
3. ✅ Keep it **positive** ✅

*(⚠️ Never say.)*
- ❌ *"My current company is not good"* ⚠️
- ❌ *"My manager is a problem"* ⚠️
- ❌ *"There is no salary increment"* ⚠️ *(even if true!)*
- ❌ *"I want a change"* ✅ *(too vague)*

*(More on this in Section 4.)* ⚠️

---

### 🎤 FULL MODEL 1 — 8 years, Accounts

*(Read at natural speed.)*

> ✅ *"I'm currently a Senior Accounts Executive at a logistics company in Kochi, and I've been in accounts for about eight years.*
>
> *My core strength is month-end closing and reconciliation — that's what I've done most and what I enjoy. I work daily in Tally and SAP, and I use advanced Excel for reporting. Right now I handle accounts for around 200 clients and report to the Finance Manager.*
>
> *To give you a concrete example — when I joined, month-end closing took five working days and client reports were often late. I went through the process and found three reconciliations being done manually that could be automated in Excel. I built the templates, trained two colleagues, and we brought closing down to two days. It's held for three years now, and it's the work I'm most proud of.*
>
> *I've now done that well for several years, and I'd like to move into something with more analysis and reporting — which is exactly what this role looked like from the description."* ⭐

---

### 🎤 FULL MODEL 2 — 4 years, IT Support

*(A second template at a different level and field.)*

> ✅ *"I'm a Technical Support Engineer at an IT services company in Technopark, and I've been in support for four years.*
>
> *I mainly handle Level 2 tickets — Windows, networking, and Active Directory issues — for a client base of around 800 users across three locations. I'm ITIL-certified, and I've been doing a lot of the knowledge-base documentation for my team.*
>
> *One thing I'd point to — we had a recurring VPN issue that generated maybe 30 tickets a month, and everyone was fixing it individually each time. I traced the actual cause to a client-side config setting, wrote a short guide, and got it added to the onboarding checklist. Those tickets dropped to about three a month. My manager used it as a template for two other recurring issues.*
>
> *I'm looking to move towards a systems or infrastructure role rather than staying in ticket-based support, and this position seemed like the right step for that."* ⭐

*(Point out.)* ⭐ Notice — **30 tickets → 3 tickets** ✅ A specific, memorable number. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (12:30 – 15:30)

### Trap 1: The chronological reflex ⏰

*(The core structural error.)*

*(Explain.)* Why do we default to chronological order? ✅ Because that's how we were taught to answer *"tell me about your education"* in school — **year by year**. ✅

*(The fix — a hard rule.)*

> ### 👉 **Never** start with your first job. Start with **now**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* And mention earlier jobs **only** if they add something: ✅
- ✅ *"I started in a smaller firm, which gave me exposure to the full accounting cycle rather than just one part."* ⭐ *(adds value!)* ✅
- ⚠️ *"In 2016 I did data entry, in 2017 billing..."* ✅ *(adds nothing)* ❌

### Trap 2: Explaining the job change badly ⚠️

*(The highest-risk moment for experienced candidates — handle it thoroughly.)*

*(Explain.)* They **will** ask why you're leaving. ✅ And Malayali candidates often answer with **honest** reasons that damage them: ✅

| ❌ Honest but damaging | ✅ Professional |
|---|---|
| *There's no increment* | **I'm looking for a role with a clearer growth path** ⭐ |
| *My manager is difficult* | **I'm looking for a different working style — more collaborative** ✅ |
| *Too much work pressure* | **I'd like a role where I can go deeper rather than wider** ⭐ |
| *The company is not doing well* | **I'm looking for more long-term stability** ✅ |
| *I want more salary* | **I'm looking for a role that matches my current level of responsibility** ⭐ |
| *No work-life balance* | **I'm looking for something more sustainable long-term** ✅ |
| *I'm bored* | **I've learned what I can in this role and I want a new challenge** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise the principle.)*

> ### 👉 Say what you're moving **TOWARDS**, not what you're running **FROM**. ✅

*(Explain why this matters practically.)* An interviewer hearing complaints about your current employer thinks: *"in two years, they'll say this about us."* ⚠️ ✅

*(Firmly.)* Even if your reasons are **completely valid** — and they often are — the interview is not the place. ✅ Frame forward. ⭐

*(The safe universal answer.)* ⭐
> ✅ *"I've enjoyed my time there and learned a lot, but I've reached a point where I want to move into ___, and that opportunity isn't available in my current role."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* That sentence is **honest**, **positive**, and **complete**. ✅ Memorise the shape. ⭐

### Trap 3: Under-selling — again 🙏 🔗 *(Day 78)*

*(The വിനയം reflex, in its experienced form.)*

*(Explain.)* With experience, under-selling takes a different shape: ✅

| 🙏 Under-sold | ⭐ Accurate |
|---|---|
| I was just doing my job | **That was part of my role, and I improved it by...** ⭐ |
| It was a team effort | **It was a team effort — my part was building the templates** ⭐ |
| Anyone could have done it | **Nobody had looked at it before, so I took it on** ⭐ |
| I only handle small clients | **I handle around 200 accounts** ⭐ |
| Sir, I don't know much about that | **I haven't worked on that directly, but I've done something similar** ⭐ |

*(Highlight row 2!)* ⭐ Note the technique: **acknowledge the team AND name your contribution.** ✅ You can be generous **and** clear. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* Saying *"it was a team effort"* and stopping means the interviewer credits you with **nothing**. ✅

### Trap 4: Talking too long ⏰

*(A practical discipline issue.)*

*(Explain.)* Experienced candidates often talk for **three to five minutes** ✅ And they don't notice, because the interviewer is politely nodding. ✅

*(The rule.)*

> ### 👉 **90 seconds. Then stop and let them ask.** ✅

*(Explain.)* An interview should be a **conversation**, not a monologue. ✅ Ending at 90 seconds invites a follow-up question — which is exactly what you want, because now you're discussing **their** interest, not your script. ⭐

*(The practical technique.)* End with a small handover: ✅
- ✅ *"That's the short version — happy to go deeper on any part of it."* ⭐

*(And practise with a timer!)* ⏱️ 🔗 *(Day 36)* Record yourself. If you're over 2 minutes, cut the earlier jobs. ✂️

### Trap 5: Notice period and salary — mishandled ⚠️

*(Practical and often mishandled.)*

*(Explain.)* Two questions that come up, and Malayali candidates often fumble both: ✅

**Notice period:**
- ❌ *"I can join immediately"* ⚠️ *(if you're employed, this suggests you'd leave irresponsibly)*
- ✅ **"My notice period is 30 days, but I'll try to negotiate for an earlier release."** ⭐

**Current salary:**
- ⚠️ Deflecting entirely can seem evasive ✅
- ✅ **"My current CTC is ₹X. Based on my experience and the responsibilities in this role, I'm looking at around ₹Y."** ⭐

*(Note.)* 🔗 *(Day 81 covers salary in full!)* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Have a **number** ready for both. ✅ Hesitation looks like inexperience. ✅

## 📝 5. HOMEWORK TEST (15:30 – End)

*(Energetic, actionable.)*

**Question 1 — find your numbers.** 📊 In the comments, write **three numbers** from your current job:

> *How many clients / users / files do you handle?*
> *How many people in your team, or how many did you train?*
> *What did you improve, and by how much?*

*(Say why.)* These three numbers are the **backbone** of every interview answer you'll give. ✅ Write them down and keep them. ⭐

**Question 2 — the job-change sentence.** Write **your** reason for leaving, framed **forward** *(towards something, not away from something)*: ⭐

> *"I've enjoyed my time there and learned a lot, but I want to move into ___, and that isn't available in my current role."* ✅

*(Warm close.)*

*(Lean in, sincere.)*

*(Warmly.)* And a word about the salary question, since it sits underneath all of this. 💰

*(Direct.)* Many Malayali professionals are **underpaid** relative to their experience — not because they're less capable, but because they were never taught to **describe** their work in a way that shows its value. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* The person who says *"I handled billing"* and the person who says *"I reduced month-end closing from five days to two, for 200 clients"* ✅ may be doing **exactly the same job**. ✅

*(Firmly.)* But they will not be offered the same salary. 💰

*(Sincere.)* So find your numbers. ✅ Not to boast — to be **accurately** valued. ⭐
