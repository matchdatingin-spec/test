# Day 12 — Bring vs Take: Never Mix Them Up Again

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** *Bring* = towards me. *Take* = away from me. The direction decides the word.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Casual, like a phone call.)*

Your friend calls and says he's coming to your house. You want to say **നാളെ വരുമ്പോൾ ആ ബുക്ക് കൊണ്ടുവരണം.**

And you say:

> ❌ *"Take that book when you come tomorrow."*

*(Confused face.)* Now your friend is thinking: *"Take it? Take it where? Away from your house? But I haven't even come yet!"*

You meant **bring**. You said **take**. And in English, those two words point in **opposite directions**.

Or this classic one, said while leaving the house:

> ❌ *"Bring an umbrella, it may rain."*

*(Smile.)* Wrong again. When you're going out, you **take** an umbrella.

*(Point at camera.)*

Have you mixed these up? Every Malayali does, because in Malayalam the two words look almost identical: **കൊണ്ടുവരുക** and **കൊണ്ടുപോകുക**. Today we fix it with one simple arrow. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Use your hands. Show the direction.)*

Stand in one place. That place is **HERE** — where you are, or where the conversation is happening.

### ⬅️ BRING = movement TOWARDS here

The object comes **closer** to me / to the place I'm talking about.

- ✅ *Bring me a glass of water.* → water comes to me
- ✅ *Bring your certificates to the interview.* → items come to the interview place
- ✅ *He brought sweets to the office.* → sweets came to the office

**BRING = come + carry.** *(കൊണ്ടുവരുക — notice *വരുക* = come!)*

### ➡️ TAKE = movement AWAY from here

The object moves **away** from me / from where I am.

- ✅ *Take this file to the manager.* → file leaves me
- ✅ *Take an umbrella when you go out.* → umbrella leaves the house with you
- ✅ *He took his laptop to Chennai.* → laptop left home

**TAKE = go + carry.** *(കൊണ്ടുപോകുക — notice *പോകുക* = go!)*

### 🎉 The best news of today

*(Big energy.)* Malayalam **already has the answer inside the word**!

| Malayalam | Inside verb | English |
|---|---|---|
| കൊണ്ടു**വരുക** | വരുക = **come** | **bring** |
| കൊണ്ടു**പോകുക** | പോകുക = **go** | **take** |

*(Slogan — repeat 3 times.)*
> **വരുക → bring. പോകുക → take.**
> **Come → bring. Go → take.**

You don't need to memorise anything new. You just need to **listen to the second half of your own Malayalam word.** Beautiful, right?

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** നാളെ വരുമ്പോൾ ആ ബുക്ക് കൊണ്ടുവരണം.

Find the clue: **കൊണ്ടുവരണം** → contains **വരുക** → **BRING** ✅

Now build it in English order. Remember: **Verb → What → Where → When.**

1. **What action?** → bring *(command, so no subject needed — this is the one time English allows it!)*
2. **What?** → that book
3. **When?** → when you come tomorrow

> ✅ **"Bring that book when you come tomorrow."**

Softer, more polite versions:
- ✅ *Could you bring that book tomorrow?*
- ✅ *Don't forget to bring that book.*
- ✅ *Please bring the book along.*

*(Bonus word.)* **"bring along"** = bring with you. Very natural:
- ✅ *Bring your friend along!*
- ✅ *I'll bring my laptop along.*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഈ file മാനേജർക്ക് കൊണ്ടുപോയി കൊടുക്കൂ.

Find the clue: **കൊണ്ടുപോയി** → contains **പോകുക** → **TAKE** ✅

Now, look at the Malayalam order:
- ഈ file → this file *(object first!)*
- മാനേജർക്ക് → to the manager
- കൊണ്ടുപോയി കൊടുക്കൂ → take-and-give *(verb LAST)*

Classic **S-O-V**. Malayalam even stacks **two verbs** at the end (കൊണ്ടുപോയി + കൊടുക്കൂ). English uses **one verb, and it goes first**:

1. **Verb** → Take
2. **What** → this file
3. **Where/To whom** → to the manager

> ✅ **"Take this file to the manager."**

*(Important.)* English doesn't need "and give." **Take ... to ...** already covers the delivery. Adding "give" makes it heavy — and note the missing *the*:
- ❌ *Take this file and give to manager.*
- ✅ **Take this file to the manager.**
- ✅ *(If you really want to stress the handover:)* **Take this file and give it to the manager.** ✅

*(One more pair to feel the direction.)*
- ✅ *Take this file **to** the manager.* → away from me
- ✅ *Bring that file **from** the manager.* → towards me

Feel it? **Take → to. Bring → from.** The prepositions follow the arrows.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| You're leaving the house | **Take an umbrella — it might rain.** | away ➡️ |
| Inviting a friend to your home | **Bring your family too!** | towards ⬅️ |
| Waiter at a restaurant | **Could you bring the bill, please?** | towards ⬅️ |
| Dropping your child at school | **I take my son to school every morning.** | away ➡️ |
| Telling someone what to carry to an interview | **Bring two copies of your resume.** | towards the interview ⬅️ |

*(Stop at row 5 — important nuance.)* Notice: the interview isn't where *you* are standing. So why *bring*?

Because **"here" can be the place you're talking about**, not just your body. The interviewer says *"bring your resume"* because they are imagining themselves **at the interview**. The resume comes **towards** that place.

*(Simple version of this rule.)* Ask: *"Where will the object END UP? At the place I'm focused on, or somewhere else?"*
- Ends up at my focus place → **bring**
- Ends up somewhere else → **take**

*(Useful pair for phone calls.)*
- You're at home, friend is coming → *"**Bring** some snacks."* ⬅️
- You're both going out together → *"**Take** some snacks."* ➡️

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "Take" used for everything

*(Honest observation.)* In Kerala English, *take* has become the all-purpose verb:

- ❌ *Take rest* → ✅ **get some rest**
- ❌ *Take a photo of me... okay this one is fine!* ✅
- ❌ *I will take leave tomorrow* → ✅ **I'll take the day off** / **I'm on leave tomorrow** *(take leave is understood but sounds Indian)*
- ❌ *Take your food* → ✅ **Have your food** / **Go ahead and eat**
- ❌ *Take medicine... also fine!* ✅
- ❌ *I took my degree in 2020* → ✅ **I did my degree in 2020** / **I graduated in 2020**
- ❌ *Take a bath... fine in most English!* ✅

*(Smile.)* So *take* is genuinely useful — just not for **rest**, **food**, or **degrees**.

### Trap 2: Forgetting that "bring" needs a destination in mind

Malayalis say ❌ *"I will bring it"* when they mean *"I'll take it with me."*

Think about where you'll be:
- Talking on the phone, planning to visit your friend → *"I'll **bring** it."* ⬅️ *(coming to you)*
- Talking to your mother at home before leaving → *"I'll **take** it."* ➡️ *(leaving with it)*

**Same object, same day — different word, because your position changed.**

### Trap 3: The cousins — "fetch," "get," "carry"

English has a few more direction words, and knowing them makes you sound natural:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **bring** | carry towards here | *Bring me the file.* |
| **take** | carry away from here | *Take the file to him.* |
| **get** | go and bring back *(informal, very common!)* | *Can you get me some water?* |
| **fetch** | go and bring back *(a bit formal)* | *He fetched a chair.* |
| **carry** | hold and move — no direction | *She's carrying a heavy bag.* |

*(Very useful tip.)* In everyday spoken English, **"get"** replaces *bring* constantly:
- ✅ *I'll get you some tea.*
- ✅ *Can you get my phone from the room?*
- ✅ *Let me get a chair.*

Practise those. They sound extremely natural.

*(And note **carry**.)* Malayalis often reach for *carry* where a native speaker wouldn't:
- ⚠️ *Carry your certificates to the interview* → ✅ **Bring your certificates.**

*Carry* is about the **physical act of holding or transporting** — it says nothing about direction. So it isn't wrong, it's just the wrong focus: the interviewer cares that the documents **arrive**, not how you hold them. Say *"She's carrying a baby"* ✅ but *"Bring your documents"* ✅.
