# Day 55 — Subject–Verb Agreement: 15 Traps Even Good Speakers Miss

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Find the **real** subject — not the nearest noun — and 15 traps disappear.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Quick, testing energy.)*

Fill in the blank:

> *"One of my friends ___ a doctor."* (is / are)

*(Pause.)* Most people say **are** — because *"friends"* is plural, sitting right there. 👥

*(Shake head.)* It's **is**. ✅

*(Confused gesture.)* But... *friends* is plural! 🤔

Now these:

> *"The news ___ good."* → **is** ✅ *(even though it ends in -s!)*
> *"My family ___ big."* → **is** ✅
> *"The police ___ arrived."* → **have** ✅ *(always plural!)*
> *"Ten kilometres ___ a long way."* → **is** ✅

*(Point at camera.)*

Confusing? Only until you learn **one thing**: English doesn't look at the **nearest** noun. It looks at the **real subject**. ✅

*(Lean in.)*

And there's an extra layer for us — **Malayalam verbs don't change for singular or plural at all.** So this whole system is genuinely foreign to your instincts.

Today: one rule, 15 traps. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. This rule solves everything.)*

### First — why this is hard for Malayalam speakers 🪷

*(Important context.)*

Look at Malayalam:

| Malayalam | Verb |
|---|---|
| അവൻ **വരുന്നു** | വരുന്നു |
| അവർ **വരുന്നു** | വരുന്നു ← **same!** |
| ഞാൻ **വരുന്നു** | വരുന്നു ← **same!** |

*(Emphasise.)* **The verb never changes.** One form for everyone. ✅

*(Point out.)* So Malayalam handles this with **zero effort** — and English asks you to change the verb based on the subject. That's a completely new habit. ✅

*(Reassure.)* This is why even **very good** Malayali English speakers slip here. It's not carelessness — it's a structural gap. ✅

### The basic rule ⚖️

| Subject | Verb |
|---|---|
| **One** thing 👤 | verb **+ s** *(works, is, has, does)* |
| **More than one** 👥 | verb **plain** *(work, are, have, do)* |

*(Memory.)*
> **He, she, it — add the S.** ✅
> **I, you, we, they — plain.** ✅

*(The odd one out.)* ⚠️ **You** always takes the **plural** form, even for one person!
- ✅ *You **are** ready.* ✅ *(never "you is")*
- ✅ *You **have** time.* ✅

### 🔑 THE MASTER RULE: find the REAL subject

*(This is the whole lesson.)*

> ### 👉 The verb agrees with the MAIN NOUN, not the nearest noun.

*(Explain with the hook sentence.)*

> *"One of my friends is a doctor."*

*(Ask.)* What is this sentence **about**? 🤔

- Is it about **friends**? No!
- It's about **ONE** ✅ — one person, out of a group.

*(Emphasise.)* **"One"** is the subject. **"of my friends"** is just extra description. ✅ → **is** ✅

*(The technique — delete the middle!)* ✂️

> **Cross out the "of..." phrase, and the answer becomes obvious.** ✅

- *One ~~of my friends~~ **is** a doctor.* ✅
- *The box ~~of chocolates~~ **is** on the table.* ✅
- *The list ~~of names~~ **has** been sent.* ✅
- *One ~~of the students~~ **was** absent.* ✅

*(Point at camera.)* **That crossing-out trick solves half of today's traps.** ✂️ ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Ignore the middle. Find the head noun.** ✂️
> **ആദ്യത്തെ noun നോക്കുക — അടുത്തുള്ളത് അല്ല.** ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എന്റെ സുഹൃത്തുക്കളിൽ ഒരാൾ ഡോക്ടറാണ്.

*(Notice the Malayalam!)* **ഒരാൾ** = "one person" ✅ — and it's clearly **singular** in Malayalam too! ✅

*(Point out.)* So Malayalam actually gives you the answer! **ഒരാൾ = one = singular.** ✅ The confusion only appears because English puts *"friends"* **right next to** the verb. ✅

Build it:

1. **What's the real subject?** → One ✅ *(not friends!)*
2. **Cross out the middle** ✂️ → *One ~~of my friends~~*
3. **One = singular** → **is**
4. **What** → a doctor

> ✅ **"One of my friends is a doctor."**

*(Practise the pattern — say these five.)*
1. *One of my friends **is** getting married.* ✅
2. *One of the students **was** absent.* ✅
3. *One of my colleagues **has** resigned.* ✅
4. *One of these options **is** wrong.* ✅
5. *Each of my brothers **works** abroad.* ✅ 🔗 *(Day 23)*

*(Note the plural noun!)* ⚠️ **"one of my friend**s**"** — the noun **must** be plural! 🔗 *(Day 28)*
- ❌ *one of my friend* ❌
- ✅ **one of my friends** ✅

*(Explain.)* Because you're picking **one out of a group** — and a group needs more than one member. ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് ഒരു നല്ല വാർത്തയുണ്ട്.

Your brain builds: ❌ *"I have a good news."*

*(Two problems!)*

**Problem 1:** *news* is **uncountable** → no *a* ✅ 🔗 *(Day 21)*
**Problem 2:** *news* looks plural *(ends in -s)* but is **singular** ✅

Build it:

> ✅ **"I have some good news."** ⭐
> ✅ **"I have good news for you."** ✅

*(And with a verb:)*
> ✅ **"The news is good."** ✅ *(not "are")*

*(The -s words that are singular!)* ⚠️

| Word | Verb |
|---|---|
| **news** | **is** ✅ |
| **mathematics / physics / economics** | **is** ✅ |
| **politics** | **is** ✅ |
| **athletics** | **is** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ❌ *"The news are..."* ❌ · ✅ **"The news is..."** ✅

*(And the reverse — words that look singular but are plural!)*

| Word | Verb |
|---|---|
| **the police** | **are / have** ✅ |
| **people** | **are** ✅ |
| **children** | **are** ✅ |
| **cattle** | **are** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ❌ *"The police is coming"* → ✅ **"The police are coming."** ✅

*(And "people"!)* ⚠️ **People** is already the plural of *person* — so never *"peoples"* for individuals!
- ✅ *Many people **are** waiting.* ✅
- ❌ *Many peoples are...* ❌

*(Tiny footnote, so nobody catches you out.)* ⭐ *Peoples* **does** exist for **nations or ethnic groups**: ✅ *the indigenous **peoples** of Australia.* ✅ You'll almost never need it — just don't be surprised by it. ⭐

### 🔥 THE 15 TRAPS

*(Fast pace, with the reasoning.)*

#### Trap 1: One of... ✂️
> ✅ *One of my friends **is** a doctor.* → *one* is the subject

#### Trap 2: Each / Every 🔗 *(Day 23)*
> ✅ *Each student **has** a book.* ✅
> ✅ *Every one of them **is** talented.* ✅

#### Trap 3: Everyone / Everybody / Nobody 🔗
> ✅ *Everyone **is** here.* ✅ *(not "are")*
> ✅ *Nobody **knows** the answer.* ✅

#### Trap 4: The news / mathematics
> ✅ *The news **is** good.* ✅
> ✅ *Mathematics **is** difficult.* ✅

#### Trap 5: The police / people
> ✅ *The police **have** arrived.* ✅
> ✅ *People **are** waiting outside.* ✅

#### Trap 6: Uncountable nouns 🔗 *(Day 21)*
> ✅ *The information **is** correct.* ✅
> ✅ *My luggage **is** heavy.* ✅
> ✅ *The furniture **is** new.* ✅

#### Trap 7: Money and distance = one unit 💰
> ✅ *Ten thousand rupees **is** a lot of money.* ✅
> ✅ *Ten kilometres **is** a long way.* ✅
> ✅ *Two hours **is** enough time.* ✅

*(Explain.)* Because you're treating the amount as **one single quantity**, not as separate items ✅

#### Trap 8: Either / Neither
> ✅ *Neither of them **is** coming.* ✅
> ✅ *Either option **works** for me.* ✅

#### Trap 9: A number of vs The number of ⚠️
> ✅ ***A** number of students **are** absent.* ✅ *(= many students)*
> ✅ ***The** number of students **is** increasing.* ✅ *(= the count)*

*(Point out.)* One tiny word — *a* vs *the* — and the verb flips! 🤯

#### Trap 10: There is / There are 🔗 *(Day 52)*
> ✅ *There **is** a problem.* ✅
> ✅ *There **are** two problems.* ✅
> ✅ *There **are** many people here.* ✅

*(Rule.)* **Look at what comes AFTER "there is/are."** ✅

#### Trap 11: Collective nouns 👨‍👩‍👧
> ✅ *My family **is** big.* ✅ *(one unit)*
> ✅ *The team **is** playing well.* ✅
> ✅ *The staff **is** trained.* ✅

*(Note.)* British English sometimes uses plural: ✅ *"The team are playing well"* ✅ Both accepted — but **singular is safer**. ✅

#### Trap 12: Subject separated by a long phrase ✂️
> ✅ *The **box** of chocolates **is** on the table.* ✅
> ✅ *The **list** of names **has** been sent.* ✅
> ✅ *The **quality** of the products **is** excellent.* ✅

*(The trick.)* **Cross out the describing phrase between the subject and the verb.** ✂️ ✅

#### Trap 13: Two subjects joined by "and" ➕
> ✅ *Ravi and Anu **are** coming.* ✅ *(two people = plural)*

*(But careful!)* If it's **one thing** with two names:
> ✅ *Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.* ✅ *(one dish!)*

#### Trap 14: "Or" / "Nor" — the nearest one wins! 🎯
> ✅ *Either Ravi **or** his brothers **are** coming.* ✅ *(brothers is nearest)*
> ✅ *Either his brothers **or** Ravi **is** coming.* ✅ *(Ravi is nearest)*

*(Emphasise.)* With **or/nor**, the verb follows the **nearest** subject! ✅ *(The one exception to today's master rule.)* 😄

#### Trap 15: Percentages and fractions 📊
> ✅ *Fifty percent of the work **is** done.* ✅ *(work = uncountable)*
> ✅ *Fifty percent of the students **are** absent.* ✅ *(students = plural)*

*(Rule.)* **Look at the noun after "of."** ✅

### The 5-second self-check 🎯

*(Give them a tool.)*

1. **Find the real subject** *(cross out the "of..." phrase)* ✂️
2. **Is it one or many?** 👤👥
3. **Special word?** *(news, police, everyone, money)* → check the list ✅
4. **Match the verb.** ✅

*(Drill — I say, you fill in.)*
- *"One of my brothers ___ in Dubai."* → **is / works** ✅
- *"Everyone ___ here."* → **is** ✅
- *"The police ___ investigating."* → **are** ✅
- *"The news ___ shocking."* → **is** ✅
- *"There ___ two options."* → **are** ✅
- *"Five thousand rupees ___ enough."* → **is** ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: The nearest-noun instinct

*(The core error.)*

*(Explain.)* Since Malayalam verbs never change, your brain has **no habit** of tracking the subject. So when it must choose, it grabs the **nearest** noun — because that's the last thing it heard. ✅

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| One of my friends are a doctor | **One of my friends is a doctor** |
| The list of names have been sent | **The list of names has been sent** |
| The quality of these products are good | **The quality of these products is good** |

*(The fix — a physical habit!)*

> ### 👉 When you see "of," draw a mental line through everything after it. ✂️

*(Practise.)* *The **quality** ~~of these products~~ **is** good.* ✅

### Trap 2: Forgetting the -s on he/she/it

*(The most noticed error.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He work in a bank | **He works in a bank** |
| She go to college | **She goes to college** |
| It cost too much | **It costs too much** |
| My brother live in Dubai | **My brother lives in Dubai** |

*(Why it happens.)* Because Malayalam has **no** -s equivalent, and because you're thinking about content, not endings. ✅

*(The fix — build the reflex.)*

*(Drill — say these ten fast.)*
1. He **works**. ✅
2. She **goes**. ✅
3. It **costs**. ✅
4. He **has**. ✅
5. She **does**. ✅
6. He **knows**. ✅
7. She **wants**. ✅
8. It **needs**. ✅
9. He **lives**. ✅
10. She **speaks**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Say that drill every morning for a week. **Ten seconds.** It builds automaticity. ✅

### Trap 3: Double -s with helpers 🔗 *(Day 53)*

*(The reverse over-correction!)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He doesn't likes it | **He doesn't like it** |
| Does she works here? | **Does she work here?** |
| He can drives | **He can drive** |
| He will comes | **He will come** |

*(Rule.)* **When a helper is present, the -s moves to the helper.** ✅ One -s per verb phrase. 🔗

*(The two paths.)*
- ✅ *He **works** here.* ← no helper, -s on verb ✅
- ✅ *He **doesn't work** here.* ← helper takes it ✅
- ✅ *He **can work** here.* ← helper, no -s ✅

### Trap 4: Plural nouns Malayalis pluralise wrongly 🔗 *(Day 21)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| informations | **information** |
| advices | **advice** |
| furnitures | **furniture** |
| luggages | **luggage** |
| equipments | **equipment** |
| staffs | **staff** |
| peoples | **people** |
| childrens | **children** |
| womens | **women** |

*(Note the last three!)* ⚠️ **People, children, women, men** are **already plural**. Adding -s is a double plural. ✅

*(And the verb!)*
- ✅ *The information **is** correct.* ✅ *(singular)*
- ✅ *People **are** waiting.* ✅ *(plural)*
- ✅ *Children **are** playing.* ✅ *(plural)*

### Bonus: the "my family" question 👨‍👩‍👧

*(A genuinely interesting one for Malayalis.)*

*(Explain.)* In Malayalam, **കുടുംബം** is one word, one unit. And in English, *family* is also treated as **one unit** in American English:

- ✅ *My family **is** large.* ✅ *(American — safest)*
- ✅ *My family **are** all doctors.* ✅ *(British — when emphasising individuals)*

*(Advice.)* Use **singular** as your default. ✅ It's accepted everywhere and simpler. *(Plural is normal in British English when you're thinking of the members individually.)*

*(Same for.)* **team, staff, group, committee, class, government, company** → treat as **singular** ✅
