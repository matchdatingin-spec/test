# Day 22 — Some vs Any: The Easy Rule Textbooks Make Complicated

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** *Some* = I know it exists. *Any* = I don't know if it exists. One idea replaces three textbook rules.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Casual, like at a shop.)*

You walk into a shop. You want to know if they have batteries. And you say:

> ⚠️ *"Do you have some batteries?"*

Understandable. But native speakers say:

> ✅ **"Do you have any batteries?"**

*(Then this one.)* Your friend asks if you want tea. You say:

> ⚠️ *"Give me any tea."*

*(Laugh.)* "Any tea"? Now you sound like you don't care what you drink — hot, cold, sweet, salty, whatever! 😄

> ✅ **"I'd like some tea, please."**

*(Point at camera.)*

Your school taught you: *"Some in positive, any in negative and questions."* And that rule is... **mostly right but sometimes wrong**, which makes it useless.

Today I'll give you the **real** logic — one idea, and every case makes sense. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow. This is the key.)*

Forget "positive and negative." Think about what you **expect**.

### ✅ SOME = I'm treating it as real. I'm just not saying how much.

You're **sure** the thing is there.

- ✅ *I have **some** money.* → Money exists in my pocket. Confirmed.
- ✅ *There are **some** people outside.* → I can see them.
- ✅ *Would you like **some** tea?* → I have tea. I'm offering it.

**SOME = it exists ✔️. Malayalam: കുറച്ച്**

### ❓ ANY = I DON'T KNOW if it exists. Zero is possible.

You're **unsure**, or you're saying **zero**.

- ✅ *Do you have **any** money?* → Maybe you have zero. I don't know.
- ✅ *I don't have **any** money.* → Zero. Confirmed empty.
- ✅ *Is there **any** water left?* → Maybe none.

**ANY = existence unknown, or zero ❌. Malayalam: എന്തെങ്കിലും / ഒന്നും**

### Why this explains everything

*(Excited.)*

- **Positive sentences** → you're stating a fact, so you **know** it exists → **some** ✅
- **Negative sentences** → you're saying it's zero → **any** ✅
- **Questions** → you don't know → **any** ✅

*(But now — the exception the textbook rule can't explain!)*

### 🎁 The OFFER exception — when questions take SOME

- ✅ *Would you like **some** tea?* ← a question, but *some*!
- ✅ *Can I get you **some** water?*
- ✅ *Shall I give you **some** advice?*

*(Explain.)* **Why *some*?** Because when I **offer** you tea, I already **have** the tea in my hand! I **know** it exists. So → **some** ✅

*(Beautiful, right?)* The expectation logic explains it. The "positive/negative" rule cannot.

*(One honest line.)* ⚠️ This isn't a law of physics — it's a very reliable **guide**. What *some* really signals is that you're treating the thing as **there**; *any* leaves it genuinely open. ✅

*(Rule to remember.)*
> **Offering or requesting something you expect to exist → SOME.**
> **Genuinely asking if it exists → ANY.**

- ✅ *Could I have **some** water?* ← I know they have water 💧
- ✅ *Do you have **any** vegetarian food?* ← genuinely don't know 🤷

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** നിങ്ങളുടെ കയ്യിൽ ബാറ്ററി ഉണ്ടോ?

*(Notice the Malayalam.)* **ഉണ്ടോ** — that little **-ഓ** is asking *"does it exist?"* Malayalam is literally asking about **existence**!

*(Excited.)* So Malayalam already gives you the answer! **ഉണ്ടോ = existence unknown = ANY** ✅

Build it:

1. **Do I know if batteries exist there?** → No → **any**
2. **Helper** → Do
3. **Who** → you
4. **Plain verb** → have
5. **What** → any batteries

> ✅ **"Do you have any batteries?"**

*(More natural shop English.)*
- ✅ *Do you have any batteries?*
- ✅ *Have you got any batteries?* *(British)*
- ✅ *I'm looking for batteries.* ← very natural! No article needed for plural general

*(Note.)* Also correct and very common: just drop the word completely!
- ✅ *Do you have batteries?* ✅

*(Rule.)* With **plural countable nouns**, you can often say nothing at all. **Any** just adds a little "even one?" feeling.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് കുറച്ച് ചായ വേണം.

*(Find the clue.)* **കുറച്ച്** = a small amount, and it **exists** — the tea is right there in the pot. → **SOME** ✅

Build it:

1. **Does it exist?** → Yes → **some**
2. **Polite request** *(Day 04!)* → I'd like
3. **What** → some tea
4. **Politeness** → please

> ✅ **"I'd like some tea, please."**

*(Other natural versions.)*
- ✅ *Could I have some tea?*
- ✅ *Can I get some tea, please?*
- ✅ *I'll have some tea.*

*(Careful — the "any" version means something else!)*
- ✅ *I'll have **any** tea.* = "I don't care which type" 🤷 *(strange in most situations!)*

*(Show the meaning shift.)* This is the second meaning of *any*:

### 🎲 ANY = "it doesn't matter which one"

*(This is the meaning nobody teaches!)*

- ✅ *Take **any** seat.* = sit wherever you like ✅
- ✅ *Call me **any**time.* = whenever you want ✅
- ✅ ***Any** questions?* = whatever you want to ask ✅
- ✅ *You can pick **any** colour.* = your choice ✅

*(Explain.)* In **positive** sentences, *any* usually means **"your free choice."** That's why *"give me any tea"* sounded odd — you gave away your choice for no reason. 😄

*(Also worth knowing.)* ✅ *Any* turns up after **if** too: ✅ *If you need any help, call me.* ⭐

*(But it's beautiful when used right.)*
- ✅ *Come at any time — I'm free all day.* ✅
- ✅ *Ask me anything.* ✅
- ✅ *Anybody can learn English.* ✅

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | some or any? |
|---|---|---|
| Offering food to a guest | **Would you like some snacks?** | offer → some 🎁 |
| Checking the fridge | **Is there any milk left?** | unknown → any ❓ |
| Explaining you're broke | **I don't have any cash on me.** | zero → any ❌ |
| Telling a friend good news | **I have some good news!** | it exists → some ✅ |
| Being flexible about a meeting | **Any day next week works for me.** | free choice → any 🎲 |

*(Highlight row 4.)* **"I have some good news"** — note: not "a good news"! *News* is uncountable *(Day 21!)* so it takes **some**. ✅

*(Highlight row 5.)* **"Any day works for me."** So natural, so useful for scheduling. Also:
- ✅ *Anytime is fine.*
- ✅ *Any of those options works.*

### The family words

*(Quick expansion — very useful.)*

| some- | any- |
|---|---|
| **someone / somebody** | **anyone / anybody** |
| **something** | **anything** |
| **somewhere** | **anywhere** |
| **sometime** | **anytime** |

Same logic!
- ✅ *There's **someone** at the door.* → I know someone is there ✅
- ✅ *Is there **anyone** at the door?* → I don't know ❓
- ✅ *I didn't see **anyone**.* → zero ❌
- ✅ ***Anyone** can do this.* → free choice 🎲

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "ഒന്നും" and the double negative

*(A big one.)* Malayalam uses **ഒന്നും ഇല്ല** — and the "nothing" idea sits in the noun **and** the verb ending.

**എനിക്ക് ഒന്നും അറിയില്ല** → literally "to me nothing not-know."

So we produce:
- ❌ *I don't know nothing* → ✅ **I don't know anything.** / **I know nothing.**
- ❌ *I didn't see nobody* → ✅ **I didn't see anybody.**
- ❌ *He didn't tell nothing* → ✅ **He didn't tell me anything.**

*(Explain the rule clearly.)* **In standard English, one clause carries only ONE negative word.**

*(Remember Day 03?)* Same economical instinct as the *did* rule — the negative is marked **once** per clause.

*(Precision note.)* ⚠️ A longer sentence **can** hold two negatives when they're in **different** clauses: ✅ *I didn't say she wasn't coming.* ✅ And many English dialects *do* stack negatives — that's a real dialect, not stupidity. But for interviews, exams and international work, use **one negative per clause**. ⭐

So:
- ✅ *I don't have **anything**.* ← negative in "don't," so *any* ✅
- ✅ *I have **nothing**.* ← negative in "nothing," so no "don't" ✅
- ❌ *I don't have nothing.* ← two negatives ❌

*(Practise the pair.)*
- ✅ *I didn't say anything.* ✅
- ✅ *I said nothing.* ✅

Both correct! Pick one. Never both.

### Trap 2: "എന്തെങ്കിലും" always becoming "something"

**എന്തെങ്കിലും** means "something-or-other / anything." And in **questions**, English wants **anything**:

| Malayalam | ⚠️ Not the neutral choice | ✅ Neutral question |
|---|---|---|
| എന്തെങ്കിലും വേണോ? | Do you want something? | **Do you want anything?** |
| എന്തെങ്കിലും പ്രശ്നമുണ്ടോ? | Is there some problem? | **Is there any problem?** |
| ആരെങ്കിലും വന്നോ? | Did somebody come? | **Did anybody come?** |
| എന്തെങ്കിലും ചോദിക്കാനുണ്ടോ? | Some questions? | **Any questions?** |

*(Be accurate here!)* ⚠️ The middle column is **not broken English.** ✅ *"Do you want something?"* and *"Did somebody come?"* are real sentences — they just carry an extra flavour: you're **expecting a yes**, or you already **suspect** it's true. ✅ Use the **any** column when you're asking with a genuinely open mind. ⭐

*(But watch the offer exception again!)*
- ✅ *Would you like something to drink?* ← offering ✅ *some* is correct here!
- ✅ *Can I get you something?* ✅

*(Rule.)* **Asking to know → any. Offering → some.**

### Trap 3: "Some" for singular countable nouns

- ❌ *Give me some pen* → ✅ **Give me a pen.** *(one pen = a)*
- ✅ *I need some help* ← already correct! *(help is uncountable)*
- ⚠️ *There is some problem* → ✅ **There is a problem.** *(one problem = a)*

*(Rule for you to follow.)* For **one** countable thing, use **a/an** — not *some*:
- ✅ some pen**s** ✅ *(plural)*
- ✅ some water ✅ *(uncountable)*
- ❌ some pen ❌ *(singular countable → use "a")*

*(Advanced note — for your ear, not your homework.)* ⭐ Native speakers **do** say *some* + singular when they mean **"a certain one I can't identify"**: ✅ *Some guy called you.* · *There's some problem with the server.* ✅ It's real, but it adds a vague/dismissive tone — so stick with **a/an** until it feels natural. ⭐

*(Connect to Day 20.)* Remember: in an ordinary sentence **a countable singular noun is hardly ever alone** — it needs *a, an, the, my...* not *some*. ✅ *(Fixed phrases like *go to school*, *by bus*, *in bed* are the exceptions.)* 🔗

### Trap 4: "Any" for "every"

- ❌ *Any person has a mobile these days* → ✅ **Everyone has a mobile these days.**
- ⚠️ *Any student must submit the form* → ✅ **Every student must submit the form.**

*(Difference.)*
- **any** = one, your choice 🎲
- **every** = all of them, without exception 💯

- ✅ *Any student can join.* = whoever wants to ✅
- ✅ *Every student must join.* = all, compulsory ✅

### Bonus: sound like a native

*(Nice upgrades.)*
- ✅ *Do you have **any** idea?* → very natural for "do you know?"
- ✅ *Is there **anything** I can do?* → offering help 🤝
- ✅ *Let me know if you need **anything**.* → super polite
- ✅ *I have **some** doubts... no wait!* → ✅ **I have some questions.** *(Day 02! 😄)*
