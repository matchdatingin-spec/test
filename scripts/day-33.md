## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Relatable, slightly funny.)*

You're at a shop. You want the shopkeeper to keep the change. Simple thing. And your brain starts building:

*"Keep the... balance? Change is okay? No problem the change?"* 🤔

*(Pause.)* Meanwhile the shopkeeper is waiting. People behind you are waiting. And you just say... *"Okay okay, fine."* 😅

*(Point at camera.)*

Here's what nobody tells you: **fluent speakers don't build sentences. They REUSE them.** ♻️

When a native speaker wants the change kept, they don't think about grammar. They just say **"Keep the change."** It's stored in their head as **one piece**. Like a single word.

*(Lean in.)*

So today, no grammar. **100 ready-made sentences.** The ones you actually need — at home, at work, in shops, on the phone.

Steal them. Store them. Use them tomorrow. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(This section matters — teach them HOW to use the 100.)*

### Why memorising sentences beats memorising rules

*(Explain with a comparison.)*

Think about how you speak **Malayalam**. When you say **"എന്താ വിശേഷം?"** do you think about grammar? 🤔

**No.** It comes out as **one block**. You never assembled it — you absorbed it. ✅

*(Point out.)* That's how **all** fluent speech works. In any language, about **60% of what people say is pre-made chunks.** Fluency is not building faster. It's **having more chunks ready.** 🧱

*(Slogan.)*
> **Fluency = a big library of ready sentences, not a big book of rules.**

### The 3 rules for using today's list

**Rule 1: Do NOT learn all 100 today.** ❌

*(Firmly.)* If you try, you'll remember zero. Pick **10** that match **your** life. A shopkeeper needs different sentences than an office worker.

**Rule 2: Say each one out loud, 5 times.** 🗣️

Reading is not learning. Your **mouth** must make the shape. Reading with your eyes builds recognition; speaking builds **production**. Only production helps you talk.

**Rule 3: Use each one in real life within 48 hours.** ⏰

*(Emphasise.)* A sentence you've actually **used** is worth far more than a dozen you've only read. Real use is what makes it stick. ✅

### And one thing about pronunciation 🔊

*(Very useful.)* Native speakers **squeeze** these phrases. Learn the squeezed version, not the textbook one:

| Written | ✅ How it actually sounds |
|---|---|
| What do you want? | **"Whaddaya want?"** |
| I am going to | **"I'm gonna"** |
| Do you want to | **"D'you wanna"** |
| I have got to go | **"I gotta go"** |
| Let me see | **"Lemme see"** |
| What is up? | **"What's up?"** |

*(Note carefully.)* **Speak** the squeezed version. **Write** the full version. Never write "gonna" in an email! ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (14:00 – 16:00)

### Trap 1: Learning the sentence but not the SITUATION

*(Important and subtle.)*

Many learners memorise *"How do you do?"* from an old textbook and use it at a tea shop. 😄 Nobody says that anymore!

*(The point.)* A sentence is only useful with its **situation attached**. So when you learn one, store it like this:

> **[Situation] → [Sentence]**
> *Shopkeeper is giving me ₹3 back → "Keep the change."* ✅

*(Advice.)* Don't make a vocabulary list. Make a **situation list.** 📋

### Trap 2: The politeness gap

*(This one really matters for careers.)*

In Malayalam, politeness lives in the **verb ending** — തരാമോ, ചെയ്യാമോ, വരൂ. So when you translate, the politeness **disappears**, and you sound blunt without meaning to:

| Malayalam | ⚠️ Direct | ✅ Polite English |
|---|---|---|
| ഇത് ചെയ്യൂ | Do this | **Could you do this, please?** |
| വെള്ളം താ | Give water | **Could I get some water?** |
| ഇവിടെ വാ | Come here | **Could you come here for a second?** |
| എനിക്ക് ലീവ് വേണം | I want leave | **Would it be possible to take a day off?** |
| ഇത് അയക്ക് | Send this | **Could you send this when you get a chance?** |

*(Key insight — say it strongly.)*

> ### 👉 In Malayalam, politeness is in the VERB TAIL. In English, politeness is in the FIRST WORDS.

*(The three magic openers.)* Memorise these and attach them to anything:
1. **"Could you...?"** ⭐
2. **"Would it be possible to...?"**
3. **"Sorry to bother you, but..."**

*(Emphasise.)* You are **not** rude. You just have no place to put your politeness. Now you do. 🎁

### Trap 3: Over-formal English

*(The opposite problem!)* Some learners go too far the other way:

| ⚠️ Too formal / old-fashioned | ✅ Natural |
|---|---|
| Kindly do the needful | **Please take care of it** |
| I am in receipt of your mail | **Thanks for your email** |
| Please find the attached herewith | **I've attached the file** |
| Awaiting your revert | **Looking forward to hearing from you** |
| It is my humble request | **Could you please...** |
| Respected Sir | **Dear Mr. Nair** / **Hi Ravi** |

*(Explain.)* These come from 1950s letter-writing books that are still used in Kerala schools. They're not **wrong** — they're just **90 years old**. 📜

*(Rule.)* **Modern professional English is SHORT and WARM.** Not long and heavy. ✅

### Trap 4: Word-for-word "thank you" replies

*(Small but noticeable.)*

When someone thanks you, Malayalis often say ❌ *"Mention not"* or ❌ *"It's my pleasure only."*

The natural replies:
- ✅ **You're welcome.**
- ✅ **No worries.** ⭐ *(most common in speech!)*
- ✅ **Anytime.**
- ✅ **Happy to help.** ⭐
- ✅ **No problem at all.**
- ✅ **Sure!**

*(Practise all six out loud.)* Rotate them — using the same one every time sounds robotic. 🤖
