# Day 11 — Been vs Gone: "He Has Been" or "He Has Gone"?

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *Been* = he went and came back. *Gone* = he went and he's still there. One picture, zero confusion.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Playful, like sharing gossip.)*

Someone asks you about your brother. You want to say **എന്റെ സഹോദരൻ ദുബൈയിൽ പോയിട്ടുണ്ട്** — he has visited Dubai, he came back, he has the experience.

And you say:

> ❌ *"My brother has gone to Dubai."*

*(Shocked face.)* The listener now thinks your brother is **sitting in Dubai right now**. He's not in Kerala. He's not coming for dinner. He's gone.

You meant: *"He has visited Dubai."*
You said: *"He is currently in Dubai."*

*(Point at camera.)*

One word. **Been** or **gone**. And the whole meaning flips.

Have you mixed these up? Everyone has. This is a small lesson — but it's one of those tiny fixes that makes people say *"oh, your English is good."* Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Draw it in the air, or use on-screen graphics.)*

Imagine a house and a place — let's say Dubai.

### ✈️🏠 HAS BEEN = went there AND came back

The journey is a **complete circle**. Kerala → Dubai → Kerala. He is **here now**.

- ✅ *He has been to Dubai.* → He visited. He's back. Ask him about it!

**BEEN = experience. The person is here.**

### ✈️➡️ HAS GONE = went there and is STILL there

The journey is a **one-way arrow**. Kerala → Dubai. Full stop. He is **not here**.

- ✅ *He has gone to Dubai.* → He's in Dubai right now. Don't look for him.

**GONE = absence. The person is away.**

### The 2-second test

> **"Is the person in front of me / nearby? Or are they far away?"**

- Here / came back → **has been**
- Away right now → **has gone**

*(Test it.)*
- *Where's Amma?* → *She **has gone** to the temple.* → she's away ✅
- *Have you ever eaten sushi?* → *Yes, I **have been** to a Japanese restaurant.* → I'm back ✅

### Why the same word "been"?

*(Quick logic note.)* **Been** is the third form of **be** — not of *go*! And *be* means "exist / stay."

So *"I have been to Delhi"* literally means *"I have existed in Delhi at some point in my life."* That's why it feels like **experience**.

And *gone* is the third form of **go** — pure movement, one direction.

**Be = staying (then returning). Go = leaving.** That's the root of everything.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ഗോവയിൽ പോയിട്ടുണ്ട്.

*(Point out the Malayalam clue!)* Look at that ending: **പോയിട്ടുണ്ട്.** Malayalam actually **has** this distinction:

- **പോയിട്ടുണ്ട്** = have been (experience, I'm back)
- **പോയി / പോയിരിക്കുന്നു** = has gone (away now)

*(Excited.)* So you already know this idea! Your language marks it too. You just need the right English word.

Build it:

1. **Who** → I
2. **Helper** → have
3. **Which form?** I came back, this is experience → **been**
4. **Where** → to Goa

> ✅ **"I have been to Goa."**

*(Note the tiny word.)* **been TO** a place. Always *to*.
- ❌ *I have been in Goa* → different meaning! *(that means I stayed there for a period)*
- ✅ *I have been to Goa.* → I visited ✅

*(Useful additions.)*
- ✅ *I've been to Goa twice.*
- ✅ *I've never been to Goa.*
- ✅ *Have you ever been to Goa?*

*(Big tip.)* **"Have you ever been to...?"** is one of the best conversation-starter questions in English. Memorise it. Use it with every new person you meet.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** അമ്മ കടയിൽ പോയി. *(and she's still there)*

Your brain: ❌ *"Mother went to shop."* Not wrong exactly — but it doesn't tell the listener she's **still away**. And English wants that information.

Build it:

1. **Who** → Amma / My mother
2. **Helper** *(she = has)* → has
3. **Which form?** She's away right now → **gone**
4. **Where** → to the shop

> ✅ **"My mother has gone to the shop."**

*(Compare all three past forms — this is valuable.)*

| Sentence | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| *She **went** to the shop.* | A past event. We don't know where she is now. |
| *She **has gone** to the shop.* | She's away **right now**. |
| *She **has been** to the shop.* | She's **back**, the work is done. |

*(Practical version.)* Real-life conversation:
- *"Where's Achan?"* → *"He's gone to the bank."* ← still there
- *"Did you get the money?"* → *"Yes, I've been to the bank."* ← finished, I'm back

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Your friend asks about your travel | **I've been to Bangalore many times.** | experience, I'm back |
| Colleague isn't at his desk | **He's gone for lunch.** | away right now |
| Talking about a famous place | **Have you ever been to the Taj Mahal?** | asking about experience |
| Your sister moved abroad | **She has gone to Canada for her studies.** | she's there now |
| Someone knocks, asks for your dad | **Sorry, he's gone out. He'll be back at six.** | away now |

*(Highlight row 5.)* **"He's gone out"** — perfect natural English for *പുറത്ത് പോയി*. Not ❌ *"He went outside"*, not ❌ *"He is out of station."* Say **"He's gone out"** or **"He's not in right now."**

*(Bonus.)* And for a **short absence**, native speakers love this shape:
- ✅ *He's just gone out.*
- ✅ *She's gone to get some water.*
- ✅ *They've gone home already.*

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "Have been" for experience vs "went" for a specific time

Now, a deeper trap. Malayalis often say:

> ❌ *"I have been to Goa last year."*

Feels fine? It's wrong. And here's the logic — beautiful, once you see it.

**"Have been" is about your life so far. It has no date.** It answers *"in your whole life, yes or no?"*

But **"last year" is a specific finished time.** And finished time needs the **simple past**.

- ✅ *I have been to Goa.* ← no date, experience
- ✅ *I went to Goa last year.* ← date given, simple past
- ❌ *I have been to Goa last year.* ← mixing the two

**The rule:** the moment you say a **finished time** — yesterday, last year, in 2019, two days ago — you must switch to **simple past**.

*(Practise the pair.)*
- ✅ *Have you ever been to Delhi?* → ✅ *Yes, I went there in 2022.*

See the pattern? Ask with **have been**, answer the details with **went**. That's exactly how native speakers do it.

### Trap 2: "Been" with no "have"

Because Malayalam packs everything into the verb, we sometimes drop the helper:

- ❌ *I been to Dubai* → ✅ **I have been to Dubai.** / **I've been to Dubai.**
- ❌ *He gone home* → ✅ **He has gone home.** / **He's gone home.**

*(Very important sound tip.)* Native speakers squeeze these:
- *I have been* → **"I've been"**
- *He has gone* → **"He's gone"**
- *They have gone* → **"They've gone"**

Practise those short forms out loud. If you say the full *"I have been"* every time, you'll always sound like a textbook. Say **"I've been."**

### Trap 3: "Gone" vs "went" — never together

- ❌ *I have went to Kochi* → ✅ **I have been to Kochi.** / **I went to Kochi.**
- ❌ *He has go home* → ✅ **He has gone home.**

*(Remember Day 03's logic.)* After *have/has*, the verb must be the **third form** — go, went, **gone**. Never the second form. *Have* + *went* is a broken sentence.

*(Quick drill — say these with me.)*
1. I've been to Chennai. ✅
2. She's gone to class. ✅
3. Have you ever been on a flight? ✅
4. They've gone home. ✅

### One extra meaning of "been" you'll love

**"Been"** also works with time periods, for things still continuing:

- ✅ *I have been in this company for three years.* ← still working here
- ✅ *She has been sick since Monday.* ← still sick
- ✅ *We have been friends for ten years.* ← still friends

*(Note.)* Here it's **been IN / been sick / been friends** — no travel meaning. It just means "existed in that state." Same root logic: **be = stay.**
