# Day 06 — Do vs Make: 30 Expressions Most Learners Get Wrong

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** *Do* = perform an activity. *Make* = create a result. One picture, 30 expressions fixed.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Fast and fun.)*

Quick. Which one?

> *"I did a mistake."*
> *"I made a mistake."*

*(Pause.)*

If you said the first one — congratulations, you just **made** a mistake. 😄

And it's not your fault. Because in Malayalam, there is one hero word: **ചെയ്യുക.**

- Homework ചെയ്യുക
- Mistake ചെയ്യുക
- Food ചെയ്യുക... okay, ഉണ്ടാക്കുക for that one
- Phone call ചെയ്യുക

Your brain has learned: **ചെയ്യുക = do.** So *do* becomes your default. And you say:

> ❌ *"I did a phone call."*
> ❌ *"She did a cake."*
> ❌ *"Don't do noise."*
> ❌ *"He did a lot of money."*

Have you said any of these? Today we fix all of them — with one picture you will never forget, plus 30 real expressions you use every week. Let's go.

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow. This is the heart.)*

Here is the difference. Only this.

### 🔨 MAKE = something NEW appears

When you **make** something, at the end there is a **new object, a new result, a new thing in the world** that wasn't there before.

- *Make tea* → before: no tea. After: **tea exists.** ✅
- *Make a mistake* → before: no mistake. After: **a mistake exists.** ✅
- *Make money* → money appears ✅
- *Make noise* → noise appears ✅
- *Make a decision* → a decision appears ✅

**MAKE = create. Something is born.**

### 🧹 DO = an activity happens, nothing new is born

When you **do** something, you spend effort on a **task**, a **job**, an **activity**. Nothing new is created — you just perform it.

- *Do homework* → the homework already existed; you just performed it ✅
- *Do the dishes* → the dishes already existed; you washed them ✅
- *Do exercise* → an activity, no product ✅
- *Do a job* → work performed ✅

**DO = perform. An action is completed.**

### The 3-second test

Before choosing, ask yourself:

> **"At the end, is there a NEW THING? Or was it just an ACTIVITY?"**

- New thing → **MAKE**
- Just activity → **DO**

Test it:
- A cake → new thing → **make a cake** ✅
- Washing → activity → **do the washing** ✅
- A plan → new thing → **make a plan** ✅
- Yoga → activity → **do yoga** ✅

*(Bonus signpost.)* One more shortcut: **DO** loves the words *work, task, job, chore, duty*. **MAKE** loves *food, money, sounds, decisions, and relationships*.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ഒരു തെറ്റ് ചെയ്തു.

Watch your brain:
- ഞാൻ → I
- ഒരു തെറ്റ് → one mistake
- ചെയ്തു → did

Out comes: ❌ *"I did one mistake."* Two problems — *did*, and *one*.

Now apply the test. **Before** I acted, did the mistake exist? No. **After** I acted, does it exist? Yes. A brand-new mistake was born. So → **MAKE**.

Build it in English order:

1. **Who?** → I
2. **Verb** → made
3. **What?** → a mistake *(ഒരു = a, not one)*

> ✅ **"I made a mistake."**

*(Useful extras.)*
- ✅ *I made a small mistake.*
- ✅ *Sorry, that was my mistake.*
- ✅ *I made the same mistake again.*

*(Sidebar.)* And please never say ❌ *"mistake happened from me"* (എന്നിൽ നിന്ന് തെറ്റ് സംഭവിച്ചു). In English, you take ownership: **"I made a mistake."** Short, honest, professional. Interviewers love it.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ അവനെ ഒന്ന് വിളിക്കാം. / ഞാൻ ഒരു call ചെയ്യാം.

Your brain: ❌ *"I will do a call."*

Apply the test. Is a call an activity, or is a new thing created? Hmm — tricky! Feels like an activity.

But English says: a phone call is a **thing you produce**. You bring a conversation into existence. → **MAKE**.

> ✅ **"I'll make a call."**

Even better, native speakers usually skip the noun completely:

> ✅ **"I'll call him."** ← simplest and best

*(Honest teaching moment.)* And here's the truth: not every expression follows the logic perfectly. Some are just **fixed phrases** — set combinations that English decided long ago. *Make a call, make a decision, do a favour.* You don't calculate these. You **swallow them whole**, like vocabulary.

So today's strategy: **use the logic for 80%, and memorise the special 20%.** Here is that list.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

Here's the real trap, and it goes deeper than do/make.

### Malayalam builds verbs by adding ചെയ്യുക to English nouns

We speak like this every day:

- *Study ചെയ്യുക*
- *Call ചെയ്യുക*
- *Complete ചെയ്യുക*
- *Try ചെയ്യുക*
- *Fight ചെയ്യുക*
- *Enjoy ചെയ്യുക*

This is a brilliant Malayalam shortcut — take any English word, glue ചെയ്യുക on the end, done. But when you speak English, that same habit produces this:

- ❌ *I will do study* → ✅ **I will study.**
- ❌ *I did try* → ✅ **I tried.**
- ❌ *He did complete it* → ✅ **He completed it.**
- ❌ *We did enjoy* → ✅ **We enjoyed it.**
- ❌ *Don't do fight* → ✅ **Don't fight.**

*(Key insight — say it strongly.)* In English, **the word is already a verb.** *Study* is a verb. *Call* is a verb. *Try* is a verb. They don't need a helper. Adding *do* is like putting a second engine on a car that already runs.

**The fix — one question:** *"Can this word stand alone as an action?"* If yes, drop *do* and use it directly.

| Malayalam habit | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| Study ചെയ്യണം | I have to do study | **I have to study.** |
| Call ചെയ്തോ? | Did you do call? | **Did you call?** |
| Rest ചെയ്യണം | I want to do rest | **I want to rest.** |
| Book ചെയ്തു | I did book | **I booked it.** |
| Wait ചെയ്യൂ | Do wait | **Please wait.** |

### One exception — the *emphasis* do

There **is** a time when English adds *do* before a normal verb — for **strong emphasis**:

- ✅ *I **do** understand your problem.* (= I really do)
- ✅ *She **did** call you — check your phone!* (= she really did)
- ✅ *Please **do** come.* (= warm invitation)

But careful — this is only for **arguing or insisting**. Not for normal sentences. And notice: when you use emphasis-*do*, the main verb goes plain (remember Day 03's boss rule? ✅ *She **did call***, never *did called*).

### And the second half: ഉണ്ടാക്കുക

**ഉണ്ടാക്കുക** = make. That one is easy. But careful — ഉണ്ടാക്കുക is not always *make*:

- ചായ ഉണ്ടാക്കി → ✅ **made tea**
- പ്രശ്നം ഉണ്ടാക്കി → ✅ **caused a problem** *(not "made a problem")*
- ഒരു കഥ ഉണ്ടാക്കി → ✅ **made up a story**

*(Smile.)* See how English keeps splitting? That's why you learn **expressions**, not words. From today: collect phrases, not vocabulary.
