# Day 51 — If I Was or If I Were? All Conditionals Made Simple

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Four "if" types on one ladder — from *always true* to *too late*. And the reason English uses a past tense for an imaginary present.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confident, curious.)*

Two sentences. Which is correct?

> *"If I **was** rich, I would buy a house."*
> *"If I **were** rich, I would buy a house."*

*(Pause.)* The second one is the **safe, exam-correct** choice. ✅ **"If I were."** Even though *I was* is normally correct English! *(And yes — you'll hear "If I was" from native speakers too. More on that later.)*

*(Confused gesture.)* And it gets stranger. Look:

> ✅ *"If I **had** money, I **would** buy it."*

*(Point out.)* **"Had"** is past tense. But I'm talking about **today**! I don't have money **right now**. So why past?! 🤔

*(Lean in, confident.)*

Because English does something beautiful and strange: it uses the **past tense** to mean **"not real."** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Not past **time**. Past **reality**. Once you see that, every confusing "if" sentence in English suddenly makes sense.

Today: four types, one ladder. Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. This is the conceptual heart.)*

### 🔑 The secret: past tense = DISTANCE FROM REALITY

*(Explain this properly — it unlocks everything.)*

In English, moving a verb **one step back in time** doesn't always mean the past. Sometimes it means **"this isn't true."** ✅

*(Show the pattern.)*

| Sentence | Verb form | Actually about |
|---|---|---|
| *If I **have** money...* | present | real, possible ✅ |
| *If I **had** money...* | past | **imaginary now** 💭 |
| *If I **had had** money...* | past perfect | **impossible, too late** ⛔ |

*(Point out.)* One step back = imaginary. Two steps back = impossible. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Past tense = far from reality, not far in time.**
> **ഭൂതകാലം = യാഥാർത്ഥ്യത്തിൽ നിന്ന് ദൂരം.**

*(Connect.)* And this is the **same** logic behind *would*, *could*, *should* from Day 07! 🔗 They're all past forms doing "imaginary" work. ✅

### 🪜 THE LADDER — four types of "if"

*(Write it big. This table is the whole lesson.)*

| Type | Situation | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Always true 🔁 | if + present … **present** | *If you heat water, it boils.* |
| **1** | Real, possible ✅ | if + present … **will** | *If it rains, I'll stay home.* |
| **2** | Imaginary now 💭 | if + **past** … **would** | *If I had money, I'd buy a car.* |
| **3** | Too late ⛔ | if + **had + V3** … **would have + V3** | *If I had studied, I would have passed.* |

*(Say it as a rhythm.)*
> **Present + present. Present + will. Past + would. Had + would have.** ✅

*(Emphasise the matching!)* The two halves must **match**:
- ✅ present … will ✅
- ✅ past … would ✅
- ✅ had … would have ✅
- ❌ past … will ❌ ← the classic Malayali error
- ❌ had … would ❌

*(Rule.)* **Pick a level and stay on it.** ✅ *(These three are your safe templates. Advanced speakers do mix them — but master the three first.)*

### The Malayalam clue 🪷

*(Very useful — Malayalam marks these too!)*

| Malayalam ending | Type | English |
|---|---|---|
| -എങ്കിൽ ... ചെയ്യും | Type 1 ✅ | if + present … will |
| -ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ... ചെയ്യുമായിരുന്നു | Type 2 💭 | if + past … would |
| -ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ... ചെയ്തിരുന്നേനെ / ചെയ്യുമായിരുന്നു | Type 3 ⛔ | if + had … would have |

*(Point out.)* **-ഉമായിരുന്നു** is your "imaginary" marker! ✅ Whenever your Malayalam sentence has that long tail, you need **would** in English. 🎯

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece *(Type 1 — real)*

**Malayalam:** നാളെ മഴ പെയ്താൽ ഞാൻ വരില്ല.

*(Find the clue.)* **പെയ്താൽ** — the **-ആൽ** ending = a **real** condition ✅ *(it might genuinely rain tomorrow)*

→ **Type 1** ✅

Build it:

**Half 1 — the condition:**
1. **If** → If
2. **What** → it
3. **PRESENT tense** *(not will!)* → rains
4. **When** → tomorrow

**Half 2 — the result:**
5. **Who** → I
6. **will + not** → won't
7. **Plain verb** → come

> ✅ **"If it rains tomorrow, I won't come."**

*(The critical rule — emphasise heavily!)*

> ### 👉 Don't put "will" in the "if" half.

- ❌ *If it **will** rain tomorrow...* ❌
- ✅ *If it **rains** tomorrow...* ✅

*(Explain why.)* Because **if** already signals the future. Adding *will* is doubling. 🔗 *(Same principle as Day 26 and Day 49!)* ✅

*(Practise Type 1 — say these five.)*
1. *If you study, you'll pass.* ✅
2. *If he calls, I'll tell him.* ✅
3. *If I get time, I'll help you.* ✅
4. *If it's cheap, I'll buy it.* ✅
5. *If you need anything, let me know.* ⭐ *(imperative result — also fine!)*

*(Note #5.)* The result half can also be a **command** or a **modal**:
- ✅ *If you're tired, **take** a break.* ✅
- ✅ *If it rains, we **might** cancel.* ✅
- ✅ *If you finish early, you **can** leave.* ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece *(Type 2 — imaginary)*

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് പണം ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ ഒരു കാർ വാങ്ങുമായിരുന്നു.

*(Find the clue.)* **വാങ്ങുമായിരുന്നു** — the imaginary tail! 💭 *(I don't have money — this is a daydream)*

→ **Type 2** ✅

Build it:

**Half 1:**
1. **If** → If
2. **Who** → I
3. **PAST form** *(= the "unreal" costume)* → had
4. **What** → money

**Half 2:**
5. **Who** → I
6. **would** → would *('d)*
7. **Plain verb** → buy
8. **What** → a car

> ✅ **"If I had money, I would buy a car."**
> ✅ **"If I had money, I'd buy a car."** ⭐ *(natural contraction)*

*(Emphasise the logic again.)* **"Had"** here does **not** mean the past! It means *"I don't have it."* ✅

*(Practise Type 2 — the daydream sentences.)*
1. *If I were you, I'd take the job.* ⭐
2. *If I had more time, I'd learn guitar.* ✅
3. *If she spoke English, she'd get that job.* ✅
4. *If I lived in Bangalore, I'd visit you often.* ✅
5. *If I knew his number, I'd call him.* ✅

*(Highlight #1 — the most useful conditional in English!)*

> ### ⭐ "If I were you, I would..."

*(Explain.)* This is **the** phrase for giving advice politely in English. Use it constantly:
- ✅ *If I were you, I'd apply for that job.* ✅
- ✅ *If I were you, I'd talk to him directly.* ✅
- ✅ *If I were you, I wouldn't worry about it.* ✅

*(Say it firmly.)* Memorise it exactly: **"If I were you, I'd..."** ⭐ It's warmer and less pushy than *"You should..."* ✅

---

### 🤔 Now — "If I was" or "If I were"?

*(Answer the hook properly.)*

*(Explain.)* In Type 2 sentences, English traditionally uses **were** for **all** subjects:

| ✅ Traditional | Note |
|---|---|
| If **I were** you... | ⭐ standard |
| If **he were** here... | ✅ |
| If **she were** taller... | ✅ |
| If **it were** cheaper... | ✅ |

*(This is called the "subjunctive" — but forget the name.)* 📚 Just know: **imaginary → were.** ✅

*(But be honest.)* In **casual spoken** English, *"If I was..."* is extremely common and nobody minds:
- ✅ *"If I was you..."* ✅ *(casual, accepted)*
- ✅ *"If I were you..."* ⭐ *(correct, better)*

*(Practical advice.)*

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| IELTS writing ⭐ | **were** |
| Job interview ⭐ | **were** |
| Formal email | **were** |
| Casual chat with friends | either ✅ |

*(Rule.)* **Learn "were." Use "were." You'll never be wrong.** ✅

*(One exception!)* When it's a **real** past situation, use **was**:
- ✅ *If I **was** rude yesterday, I'm sorry.* ✅ *(maybe I really was — this is real, not imaginary!)*

### Sentence 3 — Type 3 *(too late)*

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ നന്നായി പഠിച്ചിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ ജയിച്ചിരുന്നേനെ.

*(This is regret — Day 17's territory!)* 🔗 The exam is over. Nothing can change.

Build it:

> ✅ **"If I had studied, I would have passed."**

*(The structure.)*
- **if + had + V3** ⛔ ... **would have + V3** ✅

*(The classic error.)*
- ❌ *If I **would have** studied...* ❌ ← *would* never goes in the if-half!
- ✅ *If I **had** studied...* ✅

*(Practise Type 3.)*
1. *If I had known, I would have come.* ✅
2. *If she had called me, I would have waited.* ✅
3. *If we had left earlier, we wouldn't have missed the train.* ✅
4. *If he had asked, I would have helped him.* ✅

*(Note the contractions!)* 🔊
- *If I'd known, I'd have come.* ⭐ *(both 'd's — the first is "had," the second is "would")*

*(Point out.)* In fast speech, natives say: **"If I'd known, I'd've come."** 🗜️ *(Day 43!)* Don't worry about producing that — just recognise it. ✅

---

### Bonus: the mixed conditional 🔀

*(A useful advanced one.)*

Sometimes the **past** affects the **present**:

> ✅ *"If I had studied engineering, I would be an engineer now."* ⭐

*(Break it down.)*
- **if + had + V3** ⛔ → the past *(I didn't study engineering)*
- **would + plain verb** 💭 → the present *(I'm not one now)*

*(More examples.)*
- ✅ *If I had saved money, I'd have a house now.* ✅
- ✅ *If she had learned English earlier, she'd be working abroad today.* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Very useful for talking about **life regrets** and **paths not taken**. And genuinely impressive in IELTS speaking. ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: "will" in the if-half

*(The #1 conditional error.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| If it will rain, I won't come | **If it rains, I won't come** |
| If he will call, tell him | **If he calls, tell him** |
| If I will get time, I'll do it | **If I get time, I'll do it** |

*(Why it happens.)* Because Malayalam **-ആൽ** attaches to a future-feeling verb: **പെയ്താൽ, വിളിച്ചാൽ**. And your brain reads that as "future," so it adds *will*. ✅

*(The fix — a self-check.)*

> ### 👉 One "will" per if-sentence. And it belongs in the RESULT half.

*(Emphasise.)* If you hear two *wills*, one is wrong. ✅

### Trap 2: Mixing Type 1 and Type 2 🔀

*(The most common failure.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| If I had money, I **will** buy a car | **If I had money, I would buy a car** |
| If I **have** money, I would buy a car | **If I have money, I will buy a car** |
| If she **studied**, she **will** pass | **If she studies, she will pass** ✅ |

*(The self-check — one second.)*

> **Look at the if-half. Is the verb PRESENT or PAST?**
> **Present → will.** ✅
> **Past → would.** ✅

*(Practise the matching — say these pairs.)*
- *If I **get** the job, I **will** move to Kochi.* ✅ *(real)*
- *If I **got** the job, I **would** move to Kochi.* ✅ *(imaginary)*

*(Point out.)* Both correct! But they mean different things:
- **get/will** → I've applied, it's possible ✅
- **got/would** → daydreaming, unlikely 💭

### Trap 3: "would" in the if-half

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| If I would have money... | **If I had money...** |
| If he would come, we would start | **If he came, we would start** |
| If I would know, I would tell you | **If I knew, I would tell you** |

*(Rule — say it firmly.)*

> ### 👉 "Would" NEVER goes after "if."

*(Emphasise.)* Never. Not in any of the four types. **"Would" lives only in the result half.** ✅

### Trap 4: The Malayalam word-order flip

*(A structural note.)*

Malayalam almost always puts the **condition first**:
- **മഴ പെയ്താൽ ഞാൻ വരില്ല** ← condition, then result ✅

English allows **both orders**! ✅

- ✅ *If it rains, I won't come.* ← condition first *(comma needed!)* ✅
- ✅ *I won't come if it rains.* ← result first *(no comma!)* ✅

*(Note the comma rule.)*
> **If-half first → use a comma.** ✅
> **If-half second → no comma.** ✅

*(Practical tip for speaking.)* Putting the **result first** is often **easier** for a Malayali mouth, because you get your person + verb out immediately! 🔗 *(Day 39's habit)*

- ✅ *"I'll call you if I get time."* ⭐ ← easier to say ✅
- ✅ *"If I get time, I'll call you."* ✅ ← also fine

*(Emphasise.)* **Try the result-first version when you're speaking.** It reduces the freeze risk. ✅

### Bonus: other words that mean "if" 🔗

*(A useful expansion.)*

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **unless** | if not ❓ *(Day 26!)* | *Unless you hurry, you'll be late.* |
| **as long as** | on the condition that ✅ | *You can come as long as you're on time.* |
| **in case** | in preparation for ✅ | *Take an umbrella in case it rains.* ⭐ |
| **provided that** | formal *if* 📜 | *Provided that you agree, we'll proceed.* |
| **otherwise** | if not, then ⚠️ | *Hurry, otherwise we'll be late.* ⭐ |

*(Highlight "in case.")* **"in case"** ⭐ is genuinely useful and Malayalis rarely use it:
- ✅ *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* ✅ *(it might not rain, but be prepared)*
- ✅ *Save my number **in case** you need help.* ✅

*(Difference from "if.")*
- *Take an umbrella **if** it rains.* → only take it when rain starts ✅
- *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* → take it now, as preparation ⭐
