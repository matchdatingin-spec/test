# Day 04 — Would, Should, or Could? ഇനി Confusion വേണ്ട | English Malayalam

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Three words, three jobs — *could* = possible, *should* = advice, *would* = imagine. Never guess again.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confessional tone, like sharing a secret.)*

Let me describe your brain when you speak English.

You want to sound polite. So you reach for a soft word. And your hand grabs whichever one comes first:

> ❌ *"Should you help me?"*
> ❌ *"I would like to know, would you free tomorrow?"*
> ❌ *"If I would study more, I could be a doctor."*

We all do this. We know these three words are the "polite words," so we sprinkle them like salt and hope for the best.

But listen — *"Should you help me?"* does not mean "please help me." It means **"Is it your duty to help me? Are you morally required to?"** You just questioned someone's obligations. 😄

*(Lean in.)*

Have you mixed these up? Everybody has. Today we end it. One job for each word. Simple, clean, permanent. Let's go.

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

Forget grammar books. Here are three pictures.

### 🔓 COULD = the door is open

*Could* is about **possibility and ability**. Is this thing possible? Is it available?

- *I could finish it today.* → It's possible.
- *Could you help me?* → Is helping available to you?
- *He could swim when he was five.* → He had the ability.

Think: **COULD = "possible?"**

### 👉 SHOULD = the finger pointing the right way

*Should* is about **advice and the right thing**. It's your mother, your doctor, your conscience.

- *You should sleep early.* → That's the good choice.
- *I should call her.* → It's the right thing to do.
- *We shouldn't be late.* → That's wrong behaviour.

Think: **SHOULD = "good idea / right thing."**

### 💭 WOULD = the imagination machine

*Would* is about **an unreal world**. Something imagined, dreamed, or conditional. Not real yet.

- *I would buy a car if I had money.* → I don't have money. Imaginary.
- *That would be great!* → In that imagined situation, great.
- *Would you like some tea?* → Imagining you accepting. Very polite.

Think: **WOULD = "in my imagination."**

### The one-line summary

| Word | Question it answers | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| **Could** | Is it possible? | ability, option |
| **Should** | Is it right? | advice, duty |
| **Would** | What if? | imagination, politeness |

*(Repeat with energy.)* Could = possible. Should = right. Would = imagine.

Say it with me three times. This is the spine of today's class.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Situation:** You want your colleague to send you a file. Politely.

**Malayalam:** ആ file എനിക്ക് അയച്ചു തരാമോ?

Look at that Malayalam ending again — **തരാമോ**. Politeness is baked into the verb tail. English has no verb tail, so English puts the politeness **at the very front**, in the helper word.

Let's build:

1. **Which helper?** Ask: am I checking possibility, giving advice, or imagining? → I'm checking if it's possible for them → **Could**
2. **Person** → you
3. **Plain verb** (helper is present, so plain) → send
4. **What** → me the file

> ✅ **"Could you send me the file?"**

Now compare the family:

| Sentence | What it really means |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Can** you send me the file? | Normal, friendly. Fine with friends. |
| ✅ **Could** you send me the file? | Polite, safe with anyone. **Use this.** |
| ✅ **Would** you send me the file? | Polite, slightly formal, a bit distant. |
| ❌ **Should** you send me the file? | *"Is it your duty to send it?"* 😳 Wrong. |

**Golden rule for requests:** *Could* is your default. Learn one and use it everywhere: **"Could you...?"**

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Situation:** Your friend has a bad fever and is going to work.

**Malayalam:** നിനക്ക് പനിയുണ്ട്, നീ ഡോക്ടറെ കാണണം.

The word **കാണണം** — that *-ണം* ending means "must / should." Necessity is inside the verb again.

Build in English:

1. **Which helper?** Am I giving advice? Yes → **should**
2. **Person** → You
3. **Plain verb** → see
4. **Whom** → a doctor

> ✅ **"You should see a doctor."**

Now feel the strength levels. This is where students get lost — so let's make it a staircase:

| Sentence | Strength | Situation |
|---|---|---|
| You **could** see a doctor. | just an option | Casual suggestion |
| You **should** see a doctor. | recommendation | Normal advice |
| You **must** see a doctor. | strong necessity | Serious concern |
| You **have to** see a doctor. | necessity from outside | Rule / circumstances |

*(Say this out loud.)* ⚠️ These are **directions on a dial, not fixed amounts.** ✅ There's no real percentage attached to a modal — tone, relationship and situation change the force completely. A gentle *"You must try this!"* is warm, and a cold *"You should leave"* can be an order. So feel the **order**, don't memorise numbers. ⭐

*(Also honest.)* **must** and **have to** are close, and they often swap freely. The usual nuance: *must* frequently carries **the speaker's own** urgency, while *have to* often points to a **rule or circumstance** outside the speaker. In everyday conversation, *have to* is the more common of the two. ✅

*(Point at camera.)* Notice: **could** is the softest, not the strongest. Many learners think *could* is the polite version of *should*. It is not. *Could* removes the pressure completely — it's just "here's an option."

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct sentence | Why this word |
|---|---|---|
| You want tea offered to a guest | **Would you like some tea?** | imagining them accepting — most polite offer |
| Your phone bill is high | **You should switch to a cheaper plan.** | advice |
| Talking about your childhood | **I could run very fast when I was young.** | past ability |
| Dreaming about money | **If I won the lottery, I would buy a house in Munnar.** | imaginary world |
| Guessing where your friend is | **He could be stuck in traffic.** | possibility / guess |

*(Highlight row 1.)* **"Would you like...?"** — memorise this exact phrase. It is the most polite way to offer anything in English. Not *"Do you want tea?"* — that's blunt. Not *"You want tea?"* — that's very Malayali. Say **"Would you like some tea?"**

*(Highlight row 5.)* And note: for an **open guess**, English uses *could*, *might*, or *may*. ✅ *"He could be stuck in traffic"* = one possibility among several.

*(Careful — useful nuance.)* ⚠️ *should* **can** also be used for guessing, but it's a **confident expectation**, not an open possibility: ✅ *"He should be home by now"* = based on what I know, I expect he is. ⭐ So the contrast is:

- ✅ *He **might** be home.* → possible, I don't know
- ✅ *He **should** be home.* → I expect it, based on the time he left
- ✅ *He **must** be home.* → I'm almost certain

*(Emphasise.)* So don't say *should* never guesses. Say: **could/might/may = open possibility; should = expectation; must = strong conclusion.** ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

Two traps today. Both come from Malayalam structure.

### Trap 1: The disappearing verb — "Would you free tomorrow?"

Malayalis constantly say:

> ❌ *"Would you free tomorrow?"*
> ❌ *"Could you happy with this?"*
> ❌ *"Should I late today?"*

Why? Because in Malayalam, **you don't need a verb for "be."**

**നാളെ നിനക്ക് സമയമുണ്ടോ?** / **നീ നാളെ free ആണോ?** — there's no separate "am/is/are" word floating around; the ആണ് merges in, and in many sentences it vanishes completely.

But in English, **every sentence needs a real verb**. And *would*, *could*, *should* are only **helpers** — they cannot work alone. A helper must always hold hands with a main verb.

So after would/could/should, you must supply the verb — and very often, the missing verb is **be**:

- ❌ Would you free tomorrow? → ✅ **Would you be free tomorrow?**
- ❌ Could you happy with this? → ✅ **Would you be happy with this?**
- ❌ Should I late? → ✅ **Should I be late?**
- ❌ That would nice → ✅ **That would be nice.**
- ❌ It could difficult → ✅ **It could be difficult.**

*(Strong.)* Remember this: **would / could / should + be.** When there is no action, insert **be**. Say it: would be, could be, should be.

### Trap 2: "I would like" vs "I like"

Malayalis often say ❌ *"I am liking to join your class"* or ❌ *"I like to know the fees."*

The difference is small but important:

- **I like coffee.** = general truth, always, forever.
- **I would like a coffee.** = right now, please give me one. *(This is a polite request!)*

**വേണം** = would like. **ഇഷ്ടമാണ്** = like.

- എനിക്ക് ഒരു ചായ വേണം → ✅ **I would like a tea.** / **I'd like a tea, please.**
- എനിക്ക് ചായ ഇഷ്ടമാണ് → ✅ **I like tea.**

*(Practical tip.)* In a shop, a restaurant, an office — always use **"I'd like..."** It is the softest way to ask for something in English.

- ✅ *I'd like to book a room.*
- ✅ *I'd like to know the fees.*
- ✅ *I'd like two coffees, please.*

That little **'d** is short for *would*. Practise the sound: **I'd like.** Not "I would like" slowly — native speakers squeeze it: *"I'd like."*
