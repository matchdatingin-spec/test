# Day 63 — Supposed To, Meant To, or Expected To? What Is the Difference?

**Target length:** 12–13 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** Three phrases for talking about **rules, plans, and expectations** — and the most useful way in English to say *"that's not my job."*

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Workplace scene energy.)*

Your manager asks why the report isn't ready. The truth is: **nobody told you it was your job.**

And you say:

> ❌ *"Sir, that is not my work."*

*(Wince.)* True. But it lands hard — like you're refusing. 😬

Now watch the English version:

> ✅ **"I wasn't supposed to do that one — I thought Anu was handling it."** ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* **Same meaning. Zero conflict.** ✅

*(Point at camera.)*

That phrase — **"supposed to"** — is one of the most useful things in workplace English. And most Malayalis never use it. 😔

*(Explain why.)* Because Malayalam covers **all** of this with **-ണം** and **-ആയിരുന്നു**:

- **ഞാൻ ഇന്ന് തീർക്കണം** → I'm supposed to finish today ✅
- **ഇത് എളുപ്പമാകേണ്ടതായിരുന്നു** → This was meant to be easy ✅
- **നിങ്ങൾ വരണം** → You're expected to attend ✅

*(Lean in.)*

Three English phrases, one Malayalam ending. Today we split them. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. Three clean meanings.)*

### 1️⃣ SUPPOSED TO = the rule / the arrangement 📋

*(The most useful of the three.)*

> **"Supposed to" = this is what was arranged, agreed, or expected.** ✅

- ✅ *I'm **supposed to** finish this today.* → that's the plan/rule ✅
- ✅ *You're **supposed to** wear a helmet.* → that's the law ✅
- ✅ *The meeting was **supposed to** start at 3.* → it was arranged ⭐

*(The hidden power!)* ⭐ **"Supposed to" often implies it DIDN'T happen:**
- ✅ *The meeting was supposed to start at 3.* → *(but it didn't!)* 😅
- ✅ *He was supposed to call me.* → *(he didn't)* ✅
- ✅ *It was supposed to rain today.* → *(but it's sunny)* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* That gap between **plan** and **reality** is exactly what makes this phrase so useful. ✅

**Malayalam:** **-ണം / -ണമായിരുന്നു** ✅

### 2️⃣ MEANT TO = the intention / the purpose 🎯

> **"Meant to" = this was the intention or design.** ✅

- ✅ *This button is **meant to** turn it off.* → its purpose ✅
- ✅ *It was **meant to** be a surprise!* → the intention ⭐
- ✅ *I was **meant to** call you, but I forgot.* → I intended to ✅

*(Note.)* **"meant to"** and **"supposed to"** overlap a lot ✅ — often interchangeable. ✅

*(The difference.)*
- **supposed to** → **external** rule or arrangement 📋
- **meant to** → **intention** or **design** 🎯

*(Test it.)*
- ✅ *You're **supposed to** submit it by Friday.* ← a rule ✅
- ✅ *This form is **meant to** be filled in blue ink.* ← its design ✅

**Malayalam:** **ഉദ്ദേശിച്ചത് / ആകേണ്ടതായിരുന്നു** ✅

### 3️⃣ EXPECTED TO = someone requires it of you 👔

> **"Expected to" = someone or something is counting on it happening.** ✅ *(Often a rule or a boss — sometimes just a prediction: "Rain is expected to arrive tonight.")*

- ✅ *Employees are **expected to** arrive by 9.* → company requirement ✅
- ✅ *You're **expected to** attend the meeting.* → not optional ✅
- ✅ *I'm **expected to** manage the whole team now.* → responsibility ✅

*(Note.)* **"Expected to"** is more **formal** and carries more **weight** than *supposed to*. ✅

**Malayalam:** **പ്രതീക്ഷിക്കുന്നു / വേണമെന്ന് ആഗ്രഹിക്കുന്നു** ✅

### The comparison 📊

| Phrase | Source of the obligation | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **supposed to** 📋 | an arrangement or rule | neutral, everyday ⭐ |
| **meant to** 🎯 | intention or design | softer |
| **expected to** 👔 | an authority | formal, heavier |

*(Slogan.)*
> **Supposed to = the plan 📋. Meant to = the intention 🎯. Expected to = the requirement 👔.** ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 10:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ഇത് ഇന്ന് തീർക്കണം.

*(Notice.)* **-ണം** again ✅ 🔗 *(Day 10!)*

*(Ask.)* Which meaning? Is this:
- advice? → *should* ✅
- necessity? → *have to* ✅
- **an arrangement or plan?** → **supposed to** ⭐

*(In an office context, it's usually the arrangement.)* ✅

Build it:

1. **Who** → I
2. **be verb** *(needed!)* 🔗 *(Day 52)* → am
3. **supposed to** → supposed to
4. **Plain verb** → finish
5. **What / When** → this today

> ✅ **"I'm supposed to finish this today."** ⭐

*(Compare all four options — feel the difference.)*

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I **should** finish this today.* | good idea, my judgement ✅ |
| *I **have to** finish this today.* | necessity, no choice ✅ |
| *I'm **supposed to** finish this today.* | that's the **arrangement** ⭐ |
| *I'm **expected to** finish this today.* | someone **requires** it 👔 |

*(Emphasise.)* **"Supposed to"** is the most **neutral** ✅ It reports the plan without claiming it's your choice or your burden. ⭐

*(Practise — say these five.)*
1. *I'm supposed to be there by 9.* ✅
2. *You're supposed to sign at the bottom.* ✅
3. *We're supposed to submit it online.* ✅
4. *Aren't you supposed to be at work?* ⭐
5. *What am I supposed to do?* ⭐

*(Highlight #5!)* ⭐ **"What am I supposed to do?"** — genuinely useful when you're stuck or frustrated ✅
- ✅ *"The system is down. What am I supposed to do?"* ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece *(the past — "it didn't happen")*

**Malayalam:** മീറ്റിംഗ് 3 മണിക്ക് തുടങ്ങേണ്ടതായിരുന്നു.

*(Find the clue.)* **-ണ്ടതായിരുന്നു** → the plan existed in the **past**, and *(implied)* it **didn't** happen ✅

Build it:

1. **What** → The meeting
2. **be verb, past** → was
3. **supposed to** → supposed to
4. **Plain verb** → start
5. **When** → at 3

> ✅ **"The meeting was supposed to start at 3."** ⭐

*(Emphasise the implication!)* ✅ You've now told your listener **two** things:
1. The plan was 3 o'clock ✅
2. It probably **didn't** start at 3 ✅ *(without saying so!)*

*(Point out.)* That's elegant. English packs a complaint into a neutral-sounding sentence. ✅

*(Tiny precision note.)* ⭐ It's a strong **hint**, not a guarantee — you can also say ✅ *"It was supposed to start at 3, and it did."* ✅ But on its own, listeners will assume something went wrong. ⭐

*(Practise the past form — very useful for complaints and excuses!)*
1. *He was supposed to call me yesterday.* ✅ *(he didn't)*
2. *The delivery was supposed to arrive on Monday.* ✅ *(it didn't)*
3. *I was supposed to be on leave today.* ✅ *(but I'm working)*
4. *It wasn't supposed to be this difficult.* ⭐
5. *This was supposed to be easy!* ⭐

*(Highlight #5!)* ⭐ *"This was supposed to be easy!"* — perfect natural frustration ✅ 😅

### 🌟 The workplace phrases these unlock

*(This is the practical value — spend time here.)*

#### "That's not my job" — the polite versions ⭐

| ❌ Blunt | ✅ Professional |
|---|---|
| That is not my work | **I don't think I was supposed to handle that one** ⭐ |
| I don't have to do it | **That wasn't part of my task list — should I take it on?** ⭐ |
| Not my responsibility | **I think Anu was supposed to cover that** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* 🔗 *(Day 42)* All three **decline** — but they sound **collaborative**, not defensive. ✅

#### Clarifying your role ✅

- ✅ *Just to confirm — am I supposed to send this to the client, or to you?* ⭐
- ✅ *Was I supposed to attend that meeting?* ✅
- ✅ *Who's supposed to approve this?* ⭐

*(Point out.)* **"Who's supposed to...?"** ⭐ is the perfect question when responsibilities are unclear — and it doesn't blame anyone. ✅

#### Explaining a delay ✅

- ✅ *The file was supposed to come from accounts, but I haven't received it yet.* ⭐
- ✅ *We were expected to get approval first, so I'm waiting on that.* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Notice — you explain the delay **without** blaming a person. ✅ Very professional. 💼

---

### 🎁 Bonus: the pronunciation ⚠️

*(Important and often missed.)*

*(Explain.)* **"supposed to"** is **not** pronounced as written! 🔊

- Written: *supposed to* ✅
- Spoken: **"suh-POST-tuh"** ⭐ *(the "d" disappears!)*

*(Same for others.)* 🔗 *(Day 43)*
- *used to* → **"yoostu"** ✅
- *meant to* → **"men-tuh"** ✅
- *expected to* → **"expec-tid-tuh"** ✅

*(Practise.)* **"I'm suh-POST-tuh finish it today."** ✅ Say it three times. 🗣️

*(Note.)* And the **spelling** matters — always **"supposed"** with a **-d** ✅ Never *"suppose to."* ⚠️

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (10:00 – 12:00)

### Trap 1: Using "should" or "have to" for everything

*(The core gap.)*

*(Explain.)* Because **-ണം** covers all of it, Malayalis default to *should* or *have to*:

| Malayalam | ⚠️ Limited | ✅ Better |
|---|---|---|
| ഞാൻ ഇന്ന് തീർക്കണം | I should finish today | **I'm supposed to finish it today** ⭐ |
| Meeting 3-ന് തുടങ്ങേണ്ടതായിരുന്നു | Meeting should start at 3 ⚠️ | **The meeting was supposed to start at 3** ⭐ |
| നിങ്ങൾ helmet ഇടണം | You should wear helmet | **You're supposed to wear a helmet** ✅ |
| അവൻ വിളിക്കേണ്ടതായിരുന്നു | He should call me | **He was supposed to call me** ⭐ |

*(Point out the difference.)* ⚠️ *"Meeting should start at 3"* sounds like **your opinion** ✅ *"The meeting **was supposed to** start at 3"* reports the **actual plan** — and implies it didn't. ⭐

### Trap 2: Dropping the "be" verb 🔗 *(Day 52)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I supposed to finish today | **I'm supposed to finish today** ✅ |
| He supposed to call | **He was supposed to call** ✅ |
| You expected to attend | **You're expected to attend** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* In **this** pattern — *supposed to, meant to, expected to* — you **always** need am/is/are/was/were ✅ These are **descriptions**, not stand-alone verbs. ✅

*(Small note.)* ⭐ The words *mean* and *expect* **are** ordinary verbs elsewhere: ✅ *I **meant** to call you.* · *I **expect** to finish today.* ✅ It's the *-ed to* pattern that needs *be*. ⭐

*(The forms.)*
| Time | Form |
|---|---|
| Present | **am/is/are supposed to** ✅ |
| Past | **was/were supposed to** ✅ |
| Negative | **am/is not supposed to** ✅ |
| Question | **Am/Is ... supposed to?** ✅ |

### Trap 3: "suppose to" — the missing -d ⚠️

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I'm suppose to go | **I'm supposed to go** ✅ |
| He was suppose to call | **He was supposed to call** ✅ |

*(Explain.)* Because the spoken form sounds like *"supposto,"* people write *"suppose to."* ✅ But in **writing**, always **supposed** with a **-d**. ⚠️

### Trap 4: "not supposed to" = forbidden ⚠️

*(An important nuance.)*

*(Explain.)* **"not supposed to"** usually means **it's against the rules** ✅ *(and sometimes just "that's not how it's meant to work": ✅ "This button isn't supposed to do that.")*

- ✅ *You're **not supposed to** park here.* → it's prohibited 🚫
- ✅ *We're **not supposed to** use phones in the meeting.* ✅
- ✅ *I'm **not supposed to** tell you this, but...* ⭐ 🤫

*(Compare.)* 🔗 *(Day 10)*
| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *You **mustn't** park here.* | forbidden — strong 🚫 |
| *You're **not supposed to** park here.* | against the rules — softer ⭐ |
| *You **don't have to** park here.* | it's optional ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* **"Not supposed to"** is a gentler way to point out a rule ✅ — perfect for correcting someone politely. ⭐

- ✅ *"Sorry, I don't think we're supposed to sit here."* ⭐ *(so polite!)* ✅

### Bonus: "meant to be" — the fate phrase 💫

*(A lovely extra.)*

- ✅ *It was **meant to be**.* ✅ = it was destiny 💫
- ✅ *I guess we weren't **meant to be** friends.* ✅
- ✅ *This job was **meant for** me.* ✅

*(Note.)* **Malayalam:** **വിധിയായിരുന്നു / ഉദ്ദേശിച്ചതായിരുന്നു** ✅

### The 3-second self-check 🎯

1. **Is it an arrangement or a rule?** → **supposed to** ⭐
2. **Is it an intention or a design?** → **meant to** ✅
3. **Does an authority require it?** → **expected to** ✅
4. **Did I include am/is/are/was?** ✅ *(essential!)*

*(Drill — I say Malayalam-style, you fix it.)*
- *"I should finish today"* *(it's the plan)* → **I'm supposed to finish it today** ✅
- *"He should call me yesterday"* → **He was supposed to call me yesterday** ✅
- *"You should wear ID card"* *(office rule)* → **You're supposed to wear your ID card** ✅
- *"That is not my work"* → **I wasn't supposed to handle that** ⭐
