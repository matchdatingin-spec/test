# Day 02 — "I Have a Doubt" Is Wrong? 20 Malayalam-to-English Mistakes

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** You will learn the difference between a *doubt* and a *question* — and 19 other fixes that make you sound educated instantly.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Start with a small role-play. Play both characters.)*

Imagine this. You are in a class. Or a meeting. The teacher finishes talking. You raise your hand and you say:

> ❌ *"Sir, I have a doubt."*

Every single one of us has said this. In school, in college, in tuition. It's the most famous English sentence in Kerala.

And it is wrong.

Not "slightly informal." Not "old-fashioned." **The meaning is different.** When you say "I have a doubt" to a native English speaker, they don't hear *"I want to understand."*

They hear: *"I don't believe you."*

*(Pause. Let it land.)*

Yes. You just told your boss you don't trust him. And you were smiling while doing it.

Have you said this? Of course you have. Today we fix it forever — plus 19 more sentences we all get wrong. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

Here is the whole problem in one word: **സംശയം.**

In Malayalam, സംശയം does **two** jobs.

1. **എനിക്ക് ഒരു സംശയം ഉണ്ട്** — I don't understand something. I need help.
2. **അവനെക്കുറിച്ച് എനിക്ക് സംശയം ഉണ്ട്** — I suspect him. I don't trust him.

Same word. Two totally different feelings. Malayalam is comfortable with that.

But English splits these into **two different words**:

- **Question** = I don't have the information. Please give it to me. *(Empty box → please fill it.)*
- **Doubt** = I have the information, but I don't believe it. *(Full box → but I think it's fake.)*

So a **question** points at *yourself* — "my knowledge has a gap."
A **doubt** points at *the other person* — "your statement may be false."

That is why "Sir, I have a doubt" sounds so strange to them. You are pointing your finger at the teacher.

Real examples of correct *doubt*:

- ✅ *I doubt he will come on time.* (= I don't think he will)
- ✅ *There is no doubt about it.* (= it is 100% certain)
- ✅ *The police doubted his story.* (= they didn't believe him)

See? Doubt always carries suspicion. Question carries curiosity.

**So what should you say instead?** You have four beautiful options:

1. ✅ **"Sir, I have a question."** — simple, safe, perfect.
2. ✅ **"I have a quick question about..."** — sounds polite and professional.
3. ✅ **"I'm a little confused about step three."** — best when you *understood* but not fully.
4. ✅ **"Could you explain that part again?"** — best in a meeting.

*(Big energy.)* Number 3 is my favourite. Because most of the time, you don't have a doubt or a question — you have **confusion**. And "I'm confused about..." is exactly the sentence for that.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് ഈ topic-ൽ ഒരു സംശയം ഉണ്ട്.

Your brain's version: ❌ *"I have one doubt in this topic."*

Three problems here. Let's fix them one at a time.

1. **ഒരു → "one"?** No. ഒരു here means *a*. → *a doubt*
2. **സംശയം → "doubt"?** No. You want information. → *question*
3. **ഈ topic-ൽ → "in this topic"?** No. In English, a question is *about* something, not *in* something. → *about this topic*

Now stack it in English order — **Who → Verb → What → About what:**

> ✅ **"I have a question about this topic."**

*(Say it three times with the audience.)* I have a question about this topic. Beautiful. Professional. Correct.

Want to sound even better? Add a soft opener:

> ✅ **"Sorry to interrupt — I have a quick question about this topic."**

That sentence works in a Kochi office, in a London office, anywhere.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** അവൻ വരുമോ എന്ന് എനിക്ക് സംശയം ഉണ്ട്.

Here സംശയം really *does* mean doubt. So now "doubt" is correct — but the structure is a trap.

Look at the Malayalam order:
- അവൻ വരുമോ എന്ന് → "he will come whether"
- എനിക്ക് സംശയം ഉണ്ട് → "to me doubt is there"

Direct translation: ❌ *"He will come or not, to me doubt is there."*

English refuses this. In English, **the opinion comes first, the detail comes second.** Malayalam builds the detail first and drops the opinion at the end. English drops the opinion immediately, then explains.

1. **Who?** → I
2. **Feeling as a verb** → doubt *(yes! "doubt" can be a verb — that's the trick)*
3. **The detail** → he will come

> ✅ **"I doubt he will come."**

Four words. Compare to your ten-word version. **English rewards short.**

More of the same shape:
- ✅ *I doubt it will rain today.*
- ✅ *I doubt she got my message.*
- ✅ *I doubt we'll finish on time.*

*(Teaching moment.)* Notice: **"I doubt" = "I don't think."** They are twins. If "I doubt..." feels hard, just say **"I don't think he will come."** Also perfect.

### The 20 mistakes — rapid fire

*(On screen: ❌ then ✅. Keep it snappy, 8–10 seconds each.)*

| # | ❌ Malayali English | ✅ Correct English | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I have a doubt | **I have a question** | doubt = suspicion |
| 2 | Clear my doubt | **Could you clarify this?** | *clarify* is the real word |
| 3 | What is your good name? | **May I know your name?** | "good name" is a Hindi/Malayalam habit |
| 4 | Myself Arun | **I'm Arun** | *myself* is not a name-word |
| 5 | I am having a headache | **I have a headache** | owning ≠ -ing |
| 6 | He is my cousin brother | **He's my cousin** | *cousin* has no gender |
| 7 | I passed out in 2020 | **I graduated in 2020** | *passed out* = fainted! |
| 8 | Do one thing... | **Here's what you can do...** | direct translation of ഒരു കാര്യം ചെയ്യ് |
| 9 | I am from Kerala only | **I'm from Kerala** | *only* is തന്നെ, English doesn't need it |
| 10 | Where you are staying? | **Where are you staying?** | question needs the flip |
| 11 | Tell me na? | **Please tell me** | *na / no?* is Malayalam glue |
| 12 | Give me one water | **Could I have a glass of water?** | water can't be counted |
| 13 | I will do it na | **I'll definitely do it** | show promise with *definitely* |
| 14 | Today itself I'll send | **I'll send it today** | *itself* is തന്നെ again |
| 15 | Prepone the meeting | **Move the meeting earlier** | *prepone* is Indian English — outsiders won't know it |
| 16 | Kindly do the needful | **Please take care of it** | 100-year-old office English |
| 17 | Revert back to me | **Get back to me** | *revert* = go back to old state |
| 18 | I know to swim | **I know how to swim** | skills need *how* |
| 19 | She is knowing the answer | **She knows the answer** | knowing is not an action |
| 20 | Off the fan | **Turn off the fan** | *off* alone is not a verb |

*(Slow down on 7.)* Number 7 is dangerous. If you tell an interviewer *"I passed out in 2020,"* they will imagine you lying on the floor unconscious. Say **"I graduated in 2020."** Please. For your career.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

The deepest trap today is not any single word. It is the tiny Malayalam word **തന്നെ.**

**തന്നെ** is beautiful in Malayalam. It adds emphasis, certainty, warmth:

- ഞാൻ കേരളത്തിൽ നിന്ന് **തന്നെ** ആണ്
- ഇന്ന് **തന്നെ** ഞാൻ അയക്കും
- അത് അവൻ **തന്നെ** ചെയ്തു

Your brain looks for an English തന്നെ. And it finds two candidates: **"only"** and **"itself."** So out comes:

> ❌ *"I am from Kerala only."*
> ❌ *"Today itself I will send."*
> ❌ *"He himself only did it."*

Why does this fail? Because in English, **"only" means "nothing else."** It is a limiting word, not an emphasis word.

So *"I am from Kerala only"* actually means: *"I am from Kerala and from nowhere else — I have never been anywhere else."* Which is a strange thing to announce.

English does emphasis in a completely different way. Not with an extra word at the end — with **stress in the voice**, or with a **strong adverb**.

| Malayalam | ❌ Word-for-word | ✅ Real English |
|---|---|---|
| ഞാൻ കേരളത്തിൽ നിന്ന് തന്നെ | I'm from Kerala only | **I'm from Kerala.** *(stress "Kerala")* |
| ഇന്ന് തന്നെ അയക്കും | Today itself I'll send | **I'll send it today itself → I'll send it TODAY.** |
| ഞാൻ തന്നെ ചെയ്തു | I only did it | **I did it myself.** |
| അത് ശരി തന്നെ | That is correct only | **That's absolutely correct.** |
| ഇപ്പോൾ തന്നെ വരണം | Now itself you must come | **You need to come right now.** |

**The rule to remember:** Malayalam adds emphasis with an extra word. English adds emphasis with **volume** or with words like *absolutely, definitely, right now, myself, exactly*.

*(Practice with me.)* Don't say "Now itself." Say **"right now."** Don't say "same to same." Say **"exactly the same."**
