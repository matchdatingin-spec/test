# Day 35 — Ask Any Question in English Using These 5 Patterns

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** Five patterns. Any question. Because in English, a question is not a tone change — it's a **word swap**.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Observational, insightful.)*

Here's something I've noticed about Malayali English speakers.

You can **answer** questions reasonably well. But when it's **your** turn to ask? Silence. 🤐

Or this comes out:

> ❌ *"Where you are working?"*
> ⚠️ *"You are coming tomorrow?"* *(only works as a "confirm this for me" question)*
> ⚠️ *"What is your name?"* *(grammatical — just very direct)* ... ❌ *"What you are doing?"*

*(Point at camera.)*

And there's a very specific reason. In Malayalam, asking a question is the **easiest thing in the world**:

- **അവൻ വന്നു** → statement
- **അവൻ വന്നോ?** → question

*(Snap fingers.)* Just add **-ഓ**. Nothing moves. Nothing changes position. One sound at the end. ✨

But English? English **rearranges the whole sentence.** The words physically **swap places**. 🔄

*(Lean in.)*

And this matters, because in conversation, **the person asking questions is the person in control.** If you can't ask, you can't lead a conversation.

Today: **5 patterns. Every question you'll ever need.** Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow. This is the structural heart.)*

### The one thing English does that Malayalam doesn't: THE SWAP 🔄

Watch this carefully:

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| You **are** happy. | **Are** you happy? |
| He **is** coming. | **Is** he coming? |
| They **can** swim. | **Can** they swim? |
| She **will** come. | **Will** she come? |
| You **have** eaten. | **Have** you eaten? |

*(Point it out.)* See what happened? **The helper word jumped over the person.** 🐸

`You are` → `Are you`
`He is` → `Is he`

*(Slogan.)*
> ### 👉 In English, a question = the HELPER jumps to the FRONT.

### But what if there's no helper?

*(This is the key problem.)*

Look: *"You **like** tea."* There's no helper — just *like*. So what jumps?

*(Answer.)* English **hires** a helper: **do / does / did.** ✅

- You like tea → **Do** you like tea? ✅
- He likes tea → **Does** he like tea? ✅
- You liked it → **Did** you like it? ✅

*(Connect to Day 03.)* And remember the boss rule — when *do/does/did* arrives, the main verb goes **plain**! 🔗
- ✅ *Does he **like**...?* *(not "likes")*
- ✅ *Did you **go**...?* *(not "went")*

### The 5 patterns

*(Write them big. This is the takeaway.)*

> **Pattern 1: [Helper] + person + verb?** → yes/no questions
> **Pattern 2: [Question word] + helper + person + verb?** → information questions
> **Pattern 3: [Question word] + verb?** → asking about the subject *(no helper!)*
> **Pattern 4: [Polite opener] + normal sentence?** → soft/polite questions
> **Pattern 5: Statement + tag?** → checking questions

*(Reassure.)* Five shapes. These cover almost every question you'll need to **build** from scratch. Let's do each one. ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece *(Pattern 2)*

**Malayalam:** നിങ്ങൾ എവിടെ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു?

*(Watch the Malayalam order.)*
- നിങ്ങൾ → you
- എവിടെ → where
- ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു → work *(verb at the END)*

Direct translation → ❌ *"You where working?"* or the common version ❌ *"Where you are working?"*

*(Explain the specific error.)* Look at *"Where you are working?"* — you got the question word right, you got the words right. But **you forgot to swap.** *You are* stayed together instead of becoming *are you*. ✅

Now build it properly. **Pattern 2: Question word + helper + person + verb.**

1. **Question word** → Where
2. **Helper** *(no helper visible in "work," so hire one — you → **do**)* → do
3. **Person** → you
4. **Plain verb** → work

> ✅ **"Where do you work?"**

*(Show the family.)*
- ✅ *Where do you **work**?* ← your job, generally
- ✅ *Where are you **working** now?* ← at this moment/currently
- ✅ *Which company do you work **for**?* ⭐ *(most natural in interviews!)*

*(Practise Pattern 2 — say these with me!)*
| Question word | + helper + person + verb |
|---|---|
| **What** | do you do? ⭐ *(= what's your job)* |
| **Where** | do you live? |
| **When** | did you join? |
| **Why** | did he leave? |
| **How** | do you go to work? |
| **Which** | one do you want? |
| **How much** | does it cost? |
| **How long** | have you been here? |

*(Highlight "What do you do?")* ⭐ That's the standard English way to ask someone's profession. Not ⚠️ *"What is your job?"* *(a bit blunt)* and definitely not ⚠️ *"What you are doing?"* *(that asks what they're doing right now!)* ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece *(Pattern 4 — the polite one)*

**Malayalam:** നിങ്ങളുടെ പേര് പറയാമോ?

*(Notice.)* **പറയാമോ** — polite verb tail again. And remember Day 33's lesson: Malayalam politeness is in the **tail**, English politeness is at the **front**. 🎁

*(The problem with the direct question.)* ⚠️ *"What is your name?"* is grammatically perfect — but to a stranger, an officer, or an interviewer, it can sound like an **interrogation**. 👮

**Pattern 4: Polite opener + normal word order.**

*(This is the magic of Pattern 4!)* When you add a polite opener, the sentence **stops swapping**. It goes back to normal order! 🎉

1. **Polite opener** → Could you tell me
2. **Then normal statement order** → your name

> ✅ **"Could you tell me your name?"**
> ✅ **"May I know your name?"** ⭐ *(Day 09!)*

*(Now show the beautiful part — this is genuinely useful.)*

Compare these two shapes:

| ❓ Direct question *(swapped)* | 🎁 Polite question *(NOT swapped)* |
|---|---|
| *Where **is** the station?* | *Could you tell me where **the station is**?* |
| *What **does** it cost?* | *Do you know what **it costs**?* |
| *When **does** the bus come?* | *Could you tell me when **the bus comes**?* |
| *Why **did** he leave?* | *Do you know why **he left**?* |

*(Emphasise heavily.)*

> ### 👉 Inside these polite openers, DO NOT swap. Use normal statement order.

*(Small clarification.)* ⚠️ This applies when the question sits **inside** a bigger sentence — *Could you tell me...* / *Do you know...* ✅ A short polite word in front changes nothing: ✅ *"Excuse me, where **is** the station?"* stays swapped. ⭐

*(Explain why this is good news for you.)* Because **normal order is easier for a Malayali brain!** You don't have to flip anything. Just say the polite opener, then say the sentence straight. ✅

*(Common error — the double question.)*
- ❌ *Could you tell me where **is** the station?* ← swapped by mistake ❌
- ✅ **Could you tell me where the station is?** ✅

*(Practise the four openers.)*
1. **"Could you tell me...?"** ⭐
2. **"Do you know...?"** ⭐
3. **"May I know...?"**
4. **"I was wondering if..."** *(very polite)*

*(Demo.)* *"Do you know **what time the shop opens**?"* ✅ Normal order after "know." ✅

### The 5 patterns in full

#### 🅰️ Pattern 1 — Yes/No questions: [Helper] + person + verb?

| ✅ Example | Note |
|---|---|
| **Are** you free tomorrow? | *be* verb jumps |
| **Do** you have a minute? | hired helper |
| **Did** you get my message? | past → *did* |
| **Can** you help me? | *can* jumps |
| **Have** you eaten? | *have* jumps |
| **Is** he coming? | |
| **Would** you like some tea? | *(Day 04)* |

*(Malayali warning.)* ⚠️ Don't **rely** on just raising your tone: *"You are coming tomorrow?"* Build the real question instead — ✅ **"Are you coming tomorrow?"** ✅

*(Accuracy note, so you're not confused later.)* ⭐ Native speakers **do** say *"You're coming tomorrow?"* — with a rising tone it means *"I thought so — confirm it for me?"* ✅ It's real English, but it's a **confirming** question, not the neutral one. ⚠️ Because Malayalam's **-ഓ** habit pushes you into it **every** time, train the **swap** until it's automatic. ⭐

#### 🅱️ Pattern 2 — Information questions: [Q-word] + helper + person + verb?

| ✅ Example |
|---|
| **What did** you say? |
| **Where does** he live? |
| **When are** you leaving? |
| **Why is** she upset? |
| **How do** you know him? |
| **Which one is** better? |
| **Whose bag is** this? |

*(The 7 question words — say them with me!)* **What · Where · When · Why · Who · Which · How** ✅

#### 🅲 Pattern 3 — Subject questions: [Q-word] + verb? *(NO helper!)*

*(This is the special one from Day 03.)*

When the question word **is** the subject, no helper is needed:

| ✅ Example | ❌ Wrong |
|---|---|
| **Who called** you? | *Who did called you?* |
| **What happened?** | *What did happened?* |
| **Who told** you that? | *Who did tell...* |
| **Which bus goes** to Kaloor? | *Which bus does go...* |
| **How many people came?** | *How many people did come?* |

*(The test.)* Ask: *"Is the question word DOING the action?"*
- Yes → **no helper** ✅
- No → **helper needed** ✅

*(Compare the pair.)*
- **Who** called you? → asking about the **caller** → no helper ✅
- **Who did** you call? → asking about the **receiver** → helper ✅

#### 🅳 Pattern 4 — Polite questions: [Opener] + normal order

| ✅ Example |
|---|
| **Could you tell me** where the bank is? |
| **Do you know** what time it starts? |
| **May I know** your good... *(no!)* → **May I know your name?** |
| **I was wondering if** you could help me. |
| **Would you mind** telling me the price? |
| **Do you happen to know** when he's coming? |

*(Note.)* **"Would you mind + verb-ing"** ✅ *(Day 07!)* — *Would you mind **waiting**?* ✅ Not "to wait." ✅

#### 🅴 Pattern 5 — Tag questions: Statement + tag?

*(Very natural, very underused by Malayalis!)*

| ✅ Example | Malayalam feeling |
|---|---|
| You're coming, **aren't you**? | വരുമല്ലോ, അല്ലേ? |
| He's not here, **is he**? | |
| You know him, **don't you**? | |
| It's hot today, **isn't it**? | |
| She can drive, **can't she**? | |

*(The rule.)* **Positive statement → negative tag. Negative statement → positive tag.** 🔄

*(The Malayali shortcut!)* And honestly? In casual speech, you can use **"right?"** for everything:
- ✅ *You're coming, **right**?* ✅
- ✅ *He's the manager, **right**?* ✅
- ✅ *This is the bus to Kochi, **right**?* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **"Right?"** is your safe universal tag. Use it while you build confidence with the proper tags. ✅

*(But note!)* ❌ *"You are coming, **isn't it**?"* ❌ — This is the classic Indian English tag. It's understood in India but wrong internationally. Say **"aren't you?"** or **"right?"** ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: The missing swap — the #1 question error

*(The root cause, clearly.)*

Malayalam questions **never** change word order. Only the ending changes:

| Malayalam statement | Malayalam question | Order changed? |
|---|---|---|
| അവൻ വന്നു | അവൻ വന്ന**ോ**? | ❌ No |
| നീ പോകുന്നു | നീ പോകുന്നു**ണ്ടോ**? | ❌ No |
| അത് ശരിയാണ് | അത് ശരിയാണ**ോ**? | ❌ No |

*(Point out.)* **Zero movement.** Three times. Your brain has literally **never** practised moving words for a question. 🧠

*(So the fix is physical, not intellectual.)* You need to **drill the swap** until it's automatic.

*(Do this drill together — fast, ten times!)*

I say the statement, you say the question:

| I say | You say |
|---|---|
| You are ready. | **Are you ready?** |
| He is here. | **Is he here?** |
| They are coming. | **Are they coming?** |
| You have time. | **Do you have time?** |
| She likes it. | **Does she like it?** |
| You went there. | **Did you go there?** |
| He can drive. | **Can he drive?** |
| You will come. | **Will you come?** |
| It is possible. | **Is it possible?** |
| They have finished. | **Have they finished?** |

*(Say firmly.)* Do that drill **every morning for one week.** Ten pairs, thirty seconds. That's all it takes to build the swap reflex. 💪

### Trap 2: Wrong helper — "is" for actions

*(Very common.)*

- ❌ *Is you have a pen?* → ✅ **Do you have a pen?**
- ❌ *Is he came yesterday?* → ✅ **Did he come yesterday?**
- ❌ *Are you like coffee?* → ✅ **Do you like coffee?**

*(The rule.)* Which helper? Look at the **statement** form:

| Statement contains | Question helper |
|---|---|
| **am / is / are / was / were** | use **is/are/was/were** ✅ |
| **can / will / should** | use that same word ✅ |
| **have** as a *helper* — *they **have** finished* | use **have/has** ✅ → *Have they finished?* |
| **have** meaning *own/possess* — *you **have** time* | hire **do** ✅ → *Do you have time?* |
| **nothing** *(just a plain verb)* | hire **do / does / did** ✅ |

*(Say this clearly — it trips everyone up.)* ⭐ **Have** has two jobs. ✅ Helper *have* + third form flips: *Have you eaten?* ✅ Possession *have* takes *do*: *Do you have a pen?* ⭐

*(Test.)*
- *You **are** busy* → *Are you busy?* ✅ *(be verb present)*
- *You **work** here* → *Do you work here?* ✅ *(no helper → hire do)*

### Trap 3: The forgotten "you" / missing subject

*(Malayalam drops subjects — Day 08's problem again.)*

- ❌ *Can help me?* → ✅ **Can you help me?**
- ❌ *Coming tomorrow?* → ✅ **Are you coming tomorrow?**
- ❌ *Where going?* → ✅ **Where are you going?**

*(Rule.)* **A full English question needs a subject.** When you're building a proper question, put the person in. ✅

*(Two real exceptions, so you're not confused.)* ✅ Short casual questions genuinely drop it: *"Coming?"* · *"Ready?"* — fine with friends, not in an interview. ✅ And **subject questions** don't need one at all, because the question word **is** the subject: ✅ *"Who called?"* ⭐

### Trap 4: Only asking, never following up

*(A conversation skill, not grammar — and it's valuable.)*

Malayalis often ask one question, get an answer, and then... silence. 🤐

*(The fix — the 3-question chain.)* After every answer, ask **one more** connected question:

*(Demo the pattern.)*
> **You:** Where do you work? ❓
> **Them:** At Infosys.
> **You:** Oh nice — **how long have you been there?** ❓ *(follow-up 1)*
> **Them:** About three years.
> **You:** **And what do you do there?** ❓ *(follow-up 2)*

*(Point out.)* **Three questions = a conversation.** One question = an interview. 🎯

*(The four magic follow-ups.)* Memorise these — they work after **any** answer:
1. **"Really? Tell me more."**
2. **"How long have you been doing that?"**
3. **"How did you get into that?"**
4. **"What's that like?"** ⭐

*(Highlight #4.)* **"What's that like?"** ⭐ You can ask it after literally any answer. *"I work in Dubai."* → *"Oh, what's that like?"* ✅ Instant conversation. ✅
