# Day 13 — Borrow vs Lend: ഈ രണ്ട് വാക്കുകൾ ഇനി തെറ്റില്ല

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** *Borrow* = I take. *Lend* = I give. Just find out who is holding the thing at the end.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Friendly, slightly teasing.)*

You need a pen. You turn to your friend and say:

> ❌ *"Can you borrow me your pen?"*

*(Laugh.)* Do you know what you just asked? You asked your friend: **"Can you TAKE your own pen from me?"**

*(Confused gesture.)* It's his pen! He already has it! Why would he borrow it from you?

The correct sentences are:
> ✅ **"Can I borrow your pen?"** *(I take)*
> ✅ **"Could you lend me your pen?"** *(you give)*

*(Point at camera.)*

I promise you — this exact mistake happens in Kerala offices, colleges, and interviews every single day. And it's such an easy fix.

Why do we get it wrong? Because in Malayalam, both are just **വാങ്ങുക / കടം കൊടുക്കുക** and the direction lives in the sentence ending, not in a separate word. Today, we fix it forever. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Use your two hands. Left hand = me. Right hand = the other person.)*

Same as yesterday's *bring/take* — it's all about **direction**. But this time, direction of **ownership**, not movement.

### ⬅️ BORROW = I TAKE (temporarily)

**I** am the one receiving. The thing comes to **me**. And I will return it later.

- ✅ *Can I borrow your pen?* → the pen comes to me
- ✅ *I borrowed ₹500 from my brother.* → money came to me
- ✅ *She borrowed a book from the library.* → book came to her

**BORROW = the subject RECEIVES. The person who supplies it is marked with FROM.**

*(Note.)* ✅ You can also drop the source completely when it's obvious: ✅ *Can I borrow a pen?* ⭐

### ➡️ LEND = I GIVE (temporarily)

**I** am the one giving. The thing leaves **me** and goes to someone else. They'll return it.

- ✅ *Could you lend me your pen?* → the pen leaves you, comes to me
- ✅ *I lent him ₹500.* → money left me
- ✅ *The bank lends money.* → money leaves the bank

**LEND = the subject SUPPLIES. The receiver goes right after the verb, or after TO.**

*(Note.)* ✅ The receiver can be left out too: ✅ *Banks lend money.* ⭐

### The 2-second test

*(This is the whole lesson. Get this one question right and you can never mix them up.)*

> **"Look at the SUBJECT of the sentence. Is that person GIVING or RECEIVING?"**

- The subject **receives** → **borrow**
- The subject **supplies** → **lend**

*(Point at camera — this is the trap.)* ⚠️ Do **not** ask "who ends up with the object?" That question gives you the wrong answer! Look:

- ✅ *Could you **lend** me your pen?* → I end up with the pen... but the **subject is *you***, and *you* are the one **giving**. So: **lend**. ✅
- ✅ *Could I **borrow** your pen?* → I still end up with the pen — but now the **subject is *I***, and *I* am **receiving**. So: **borrow**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Same pen, same direction — different subject, different verb.** ✅ It's never about who holds it at the end. It's about **the job the subject is doing.** ⭐

### The magic word pair

*(Say it strongly, three times.)*

> **BORROW = FROM.**
> **LEND = TO.**

- ✅ *I borrowed it **from** him.*
- ✅ *I lent it **to** him.*

If you can just remember **borrow-from** and **lend-to**, you will never say *"borrow me"* again.

*(Why is "borrow me" broken? Be precise.)* ⚠️ Not because *from* is missing — ✅ *"Can I borrow a pen?"* has no *from* and is perfectly fine. It's broken because of **what follows the verb**: ✅ *borrow* takes **the thing** *(borrow a pen)*, so a person there makes no sense. ✅ *lend* is the verb that takes **a person + the thing** *(lend me a pen)*. ⭐

*(Dialect note.)* Some English dialects do say *"borrow me a pen."* It exists — but it's regional, so keep *lend* for interviews, exams and writing. ✅

*(Extra help.)* Notice: **borrow** and **from** both have the "o... o" sound of taking. **Lend** and **to**... okay, that one you just memorise. 😄 But *"lend me"* is a fixed phrase — practise it: **"Lend me your pen."**

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് നിന്റെ പെൻ ഒന്ന് തരാമോ?

*(Notice the Malayalam.)* **തരാമോ** — from **തരുക** = "to give (to me)." So Malayalam is telling you: the other person **gives**.

Give → **LEND** ✅

Build it:

1. **Politeness helper** *(Day 08!)* → Could
2. **Who gives?** → you
3. **Plain verb** → lend
4. **To whom** → me
5. **What** → your pen

> ✅ **"Could you lend me your pen?"**

*(Structure note.)* Look at the shape: **lend + person + thing.** Just like *tell* from Day 05!
- ✅ *lend me your pen*
- ✅ *lend him some money*
- ✅ *lend us your car*

Or the other shape with **to**:
- ✅ *Could you lend your pen **to** me?* *(correct, but longer — the first shape is more natural)*

**Now the same request from the other direction:**

If you make **yourself** the doer, the word flips:

> ✅ **"Can I borrow your pen?"**

*(Emphasise.)* Both sentences mean exactly the same thing in real life! The only difference is **who you put in the driver's seat**:

| Sentence | Who is the subject? | Which verb |
|---|---|---|
| **Can I borrow your pen?** | I (I take) | borrow |
| **Could you lend me your pen?** | you (you give) | lend |

*(Practical tip.)* In everyday speech, **"Can I borrow...?"** is the most common. Learn that one first. It works for everything:
- ✅ *Can I borrow your charger?*
- ✅ *Can I borrow this book for two days?*
- ✅ *Can I borrow your bike tomorrow?*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ എന്റെ സഹോദരനിൽ നിന്ന് 500 രൂപ വാങ്ങി.

Look at the Malayalam marker: **-ൽ നിന്ന്** = "from." A **from** is present!

From → **BORROW** ✅

Build it:

1. **Who** → I
2. **Verb, past** → borrowed
3. **What** → ₹500
4. **From whom** → from my brother

> ✅ **"I borrowed ₹500 from my brother."**

*(Word-order note.)* Compare the orders:

- **Malayalam:** I — from my brother — 500 rupees — borrowed *(S-O-V, verb last)*
- **English:** I — borrowed — 500 rupees — from my brother *(S-V-O, verb second)*

*(Emphasise the pattern.)* See how the **"from my brother"** part travels all the way to the **end** in English? In Malayalam it sits in the middle. This is the S-O-V vs S-V-O switch in action. **English: person → action → thing → details.**

*(Careful with a common error.)* Some Malayalis say ⚠️ *"I took ₹500 from my brother."* That's correct English, but *took* says nothing about returning it — so it can sound like a gift, or even like you didn't ask. Use **borrowed** to make the **loan** clear.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| You need a charger at work | **Can I borrow your charger for ten minutes?** | I take ⬅️ |
| A friend needs money | **I lent him ₹2000 last week.** | I give ➡️ |
| Explaining a bank loan | **I took a loan from the bank.** | *(loans use "take"!)* |
| Returning something | **Thanks for lending me your notes.** | they gave ➡️ |
| Library rule | **You can borrow up to three books.** | you take ⬅️ |

*(Highlight row 3 — important exception.)* For **loans** from banks, English says **take a loan** or **get a loan**, not "borrow a loan":
- ✅ *I took a home loan.*
- ✅ *I got a loan from SBI.*
- ✅ *The bank lent me ₹10 lakhs.* *(bank gives = lend ✅)*

*(Highlight row 4.)* **"Thanks for lending me..."** — such a natural, polite phrase. Notice: after *for*, the verb takes **-ing** → *lending*. Not "thanks for lend me."

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: The direction lives in the verb ending in Malayalam

*(This is the root cause. Explain it slowly.)*

Malayalam has a beautiful system. Look:

- **തരുക** = give **to me** *(towards the speaker)*
- **കൊടുക്കുക** = give **to someone else** *(away from the speaker)*

*(Amazed.)* Two different verbs for "give," depending on direction! Malayalam is very precise about this.

- എനിക്ക് **തരൂ** → give it to me
- അവന് **കൊടുക്കൂ** → give it to him

So your brain already tracks direction — it just tracks it **inside the verb**. English tracks it with a **completely different word**:

| Malayalam | Direction | English |
|---|---|---|
| വാങ്ങുക / കടം വാങ്ങുക | towards me ⬅️ | **borrow** |
| കൊടുക്കുക / കടം കൊടുക്കുക | away from me ➡️ | **lend** |
| തരുക | towards me ⬅️ | **give me** |

*(Key message.)* So the fix isn't memorising — it's **asking one question before you speak**: *"Am I the receiver or the giver?"*

### Trap 2: "Give" used for everything

Because **തരുക/കൊടുക്കുക** are so flexible, we over-use **give**:

- ❌ *Give me your pen for two days* → ✅ **Could you lend me your pen for two days?**
- ❌ *He gave me money, I will return* → ✅ **He lent me some money.**

*(Explain the difference clearly.)*
- **give** = hand it over, with **no** built-in promise to return it 🎁
- **lend** = hand it over, and you **will** return it ↩️

*(Careful.)* ⚠️ *Give* doesn't always mean "forever" — ✅ *Give me the pen for a second* is normal. ✅ The point is that **lend** *states* the return; **give** doesn't. ⭐

So if you say *"Give me ₹500,"* you are asking for a **gift**. If you say *"Could you lend me ₹500?"* you're asking for a **loan**. Big difference in a friendship! 😄

### Trap 3: "Borrow" with no "from"

- ❌ *Can you borrow me your bike?* → ✅ **Can I borrow your bike?**
- ❌ *He borrowed me his laptop* → ✅ **He lent me his laptop.**
- ❌ *I borrowed him ₹100* → ✅ **I lent him ₹100.**

*(Simple check.)* **After "borrow," the next word must be a THING, never a person.**
- ✅ borrow **a pen**, borrow **money**, borrow **your car**
- ❌ borrow **me**, borrow **him**, borrow **her**

**After "lend," the next word IS usually a person.**
- ✅ lend **me**, lend **him**, lend **us**

*(Drill — say these with me, fast.)*
1. Can I borrow your pen? ✅
2. Could you lend me your pen? ✅
3. I borrowed it from him. ✅
4. I lent it to him. ✅

### Bonus: the third word — "rent"

*(Quick upgrade.)*
- **borrow** = take temporarily, and **give it back** 🔄
- **rent** = pay to use something temporarily 💰
- **lend** = give temporarily, expecting it back 🔄
- **rent out** = let someone use your thing, for money

- ✅ *I borrowed my friend's car.* → usually a favour, no payment
- ✅ *I rented a car for the trip.* → I paid a rental company
- ✅ *He rents out his flat.* → he receives money

*(Precise note.)* ⚠️ The real difference is **"do I return it?"**, not "is it free?" ✅ Banks *lend* money and charge **interest** — so borrowing isn't automatically free. ✅ *Rent* is specifically about **paying to use** something for a period. ⭐
