# Day 05 — Say, Tell, Speak, or Talk? One Simple Rule for Each

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** Four English words, one Malayalam word (പറയുക). After today, you will pick the right one automatically.

All four wrong. All four extremely common. Why?

Because in Malayalam you have **one** beautiful word — **പറയുക** — and English rudely splits it into **four**: say, tell, speak, talk.

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

Here is the whole lesson in one idea:

### 👉 The difference is not the meaning. The difference is WHO or WHAT comes next.

Each of these four words has a rule about the word that follows it. Learn the *shape*, not the meaning.

### 1️⃣ SAY → focuses on the WORDS

*Say* wants to know **what** was spoken. It does **not** want a person right after it.

- ✅ *He said the truth.*
- ✅ *She said "hello".*
- ✅ *He said that he is busy.*
- ❌ *He said me...* ← **never** a person directly after *say*

If you must mention the person, you need the word **to**:
- ✅ *He said something **to** me.*

**SAY + words. SAY TO + person.**

### 2️⃣ TELL → focuses on the PERSON

*Tell* is the opposite. *Tell* is hungry for a person. It needs one immediately.

- ✅ *He told **me** the truth.*
- ✅ *Tell **him** to wait.*
- ✅ *She told **us** a story.*
- ❌ *He told that he is coming.* ← who? *Tell* is asking "tell WHOM?"

**TELL + person + message.**

*(Trick.)* SAY = words. TELL = people. **Say the word, tell the person.** Repeat that: *Say the word, tell the person.*

### 3️⃣ SPEAK → languages and formal / one-direction talking

- ✅ *I speak Malayalam and English.* ← languages always take *speak*
- ✅ *May I speak to Mr. Kumar?* ← telephone English
- ✅ *The manager spoke for ten minutes.* ← one person, others listening

**SPEAK + a language, or SPEAK TO someone (formal).**

### 4️⃣ TALK → a two-way, friendly conversation

- ✅ *We talked for two hours.*
- ✅ *I need to talk to you.*
- ✅ *They're talking about cricket.*

**TALK = chatting. Two people, both making noise.**

*(Summary on screen.)*

| Word | What comes next | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **say** | the message | say something |
| **tell** | the person | tell me |
| **speak** | a language / formally | speak English |
| **talk** | a conversation | talk to a friend |

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** അവൻ എന്നോട് സത്യം പറഞ്ഞു.

Look at the Malayalam pieces:
- അവൻ → he
- **എന്നോട്** → to me ← **a person is present!**
- സത്യം → the truth
- പറഞ്ഞു → said/told

Now the decision. A **person** is in the sentence, sitting right there. Which word loves people?

**TELL.** ✅

Build in English order — **Who → Verb → Person → Message:**

1. **Who?** → He
2. **Verb** → told
3. **Person** → me
4. **Message** → the truth

> ✅ **"He told me the truth."**

And if you really want to use *say*, you must add **to** and push the person to the back:

> ✅ **"He said the truth to me."** *(grammatically fine, but nobody talks like this — use tell)*

*(Key insight.)* Notice the Malayalam marker **-ഓട്** (എന്നോട്, അവനോട്, അവളോട്). Whenever you see **-ഓട്** in your Malayalam sentence, a **person** is involved. And person = **TELL**.

*(Make it a slogan.)* **-ഓട് കണ്ടാൽ TELL.** If you see -ഓട്, use TELL.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് നന്നായി ഇംഗ്ലീഷ് സംസാരിക്കണം.

The dream sentence. Everyone watching wants to say this. And everyone says it wrong:

> ❌ *"I want to talk English fluently."*

Why is *talk* wrong? Because **talk** means *have a conversation*. But **English** is not a conversation — English is a **language**. And languages belong to **speak**.

Build it:

1. **Who?** → I
2. **Verb** → want
3. **Second verb (with *to*)** → to speak
4. **What** → English
5. **How** → fluently

> ✅ **"I want to speak English fluently."**

*(Extra polish.)* Even better versions:
- ✅ *I want to become fluent in English.*
- ✅ *I want to speak English confidently.*

*(Warning.)* Never say ❌ *"I want to talk in English fluently"* — drop the *in*. It's simply **speak English**. No preposition.

But — ✅ *"Let's talk in English"* is fine! Because there, *talk* = conversation, and *in English* = the medium. Feel the difference:
- **Speak English** = have the skill.
- **Talk in English** = right now, let's converse using English.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | ❌ Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| On the phone, asking for someone | **May I speak to Rahul?** | *May I talk Rahul?* |
| Asking someone's name politely | **Could you tell me your name?** | *Can you say me your name?* |
| Reporting a message | **She said she would come.** | *She told that she will come.* |
| Needing a private conversation | **I need to talk to you for a minute.** | *I need to speak you.* |
| Giving instructions | **Tell him to call me back.** | *Say him to call me.* |

*(Stop at row 3.)* This one is gold. Look at these two shapes:

- ✅ **She said (that) she would come.** ← *say* + message
- ✅ **She told me (that) she would come.** ← *tell* + person + message

Both correct! The only difference is the person. **No person → say. Person → tell.** ❌ *"She told that..."* is wrong because *tell* is standing there with empty hands, waiting for a person you never gave it.

*(Stop at row 5.)* And the instruction pattern: **tell + person + to + verb.**
- ✅ *Tell him to wait.*
- ✅ *Tell her to send it.*
- ✅ *Tell them not to worry.*

Never *"say him to..."*. Say cannot give orders to people.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

The core trap: **Malayalam packs the listener into the verb, so you forget the listener is a separate word in English.**

In Malayalam you can say just **പറഞ്ഞു** and everyone understands who was told, from context. The sentence feels complete.

- **ഞാൻ പറഞ്ഞു** — I said/told. Complete. Nobody asks "to whom?"

English is not so relaxed. English keeps checking: *"Told whom? Said what?"* If you use *tell* without a person, English feels a hole in the sentence.

So this is the mental switch:

> **Before you speak, ask yourself: "Am I focusing on the words, or on the human?"**

- Focusing on words → **say**
- Focusing on the human → **tell**

### The second half of the trap: വിളിച്ചു പറയുക, ചോദിക്കുക

Malayalis also stretch *say* over jobs that belong to **ask**:

- ❌ *He said me a question* → ✅ **He asked me a question.**
- ❌ *Say me the time* → ✅ **Could you tell me the time?**
- ❌ *He said to come tomorrow* → ✅ **He asked me to come tomorrow.** / **He told me to come tomorrow.**

**Remember:** requests and questions = **ask**. Information = **tell**. Words = **say**.

### The four fixed phrases to memorise today

*(Tell the audience: don't learn rules for these — just swallow them whole.)*

1. ✅ **"May I speak to ___ ?"** — every phone call
2. ✅ **"Could you tell me ___ ?"** — every polite question
3. ✅ **"I need to talk to you."** — every serious conversation
4. ✅ **"He said that ___ ."** — every time you report news

Four sentences. They cover about 80% of real situations. Say them out loud with me now — three times each.
