# Day 58 — Who, Which, That, or Whose? Join Sentences Like a Pro

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** These four words let you join two sentences into one — the single biggest upgrade available to an intermediate speaker.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confident, building.)*

Listen to these two answers to *"Tell me about your friend."*

**Answer A:** *"I have a friend. He is a doctor. He works in Kochi. He studied with me."*

**Answer B:** *"I have a friend **who** is a doctor in Kochi — we studied together."*

*(Pause.)*

**Answer A: four short sentences.** ✅ Correct. But it sounds like a **list**. 📋
**Answer B: one flowing sentence.** ⭐ Sounds like a **person talking**.

*(Point at camera.)*

The difference is **one word**: **who**. ✅

*(Lean in.)*

And here's the Malayalam problem. In Malayalam, you join sentences by putting a **description before** the noun:

**എന്റെ ഡോക്ടറായ ഒരു സുഹൃത്ത്** → literally *"my doctor-being one friend"* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* The description comes **first**, then the noun. English does the **opposite** — noun first, then the description trails behind. 🔄

Today: four words, one clean rule. Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. The join-point idea is the whole lesson.)*

### 🔑 The rule: repeat the noun? Replace it with a joining word.

*(Explain with the simplest possible example.)*

Two sentences:
> *"I have a friend. **He** is a doctor."*

*(Point out.)* The word **"He"** is just repeating *"a friend."* ✅ So English says: **don't repeat it — replace it with a joining word.** 🔗

> ✅ *"I have a friend **who** is a doctor."* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **The joining word takes the place of the repeated word.** ✅ That's it.

*(Practise the process.)*
- *I bought a phone. **It** is very fast.* → *I bought a phone **which** is very fast.* ✅
- *That's the man. **He** helped me.* → *That's the man **who** helped me.* ✅
- *This is the house. I grew up **there**.* → *This is the house **where** I grew up.* ✅

### Which word to use? 📋

*(The simple table.)*

| Joining word | Use for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | 👤 people | *the man **who** called* |
| **which** | 📦 things, animals | *the phone **which** broke* |
| **that** | 👤📦 **both!** *(informal)* | *the man **that** called* ✅ |
| **whose** | 🔑 possession *(of anyone)* | *the man **whose** car broke* |
| **where** | 📍 places | *the shop **where** I bought it* |
| **when** | 🕐 times | *the day **when** we met* |

*(Emphasise.)* **who = people. which = things. that = either.** ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Repeating a noun? Replace it with a joining word.** 🔗
> **ആളാണോ → who. വസ്തുവാണോ → which. രണ്ടും → that.** ✅

### 🎉 The great news: "that" works for almost everything

*(This genuinely simplifies things.)*

*(Explain.)* In everyday spoken English, **"that"** can replace both *who* and *which*:

- ✅ *the man **that** called* ✅ *(= who)*
- ✅ *the phone **that** broke* ✅ *(= which)*

*(Emphasise.)* **So if you're unsure — say "that."** ⭐ It's correct, natural, and very common in speech. ✅

*(But note.)* In **formal writing** and **IELTS**, prefer:
- **who** for people ✅
- **which** for things ✅

*(Advice.)* **Speak "that." Write "who/which."** ✅

### 🎁 The even better news: you can often DELETE it!

*(This is a genuinely valuable shortcut.)*

*(Explain.)* When the joining word is the **object** *(not the doer)*, you can **remove** it completely:

- ✅ *The book **that** I bought* → ✅ *The book I bought* ⭐
- ✅ *The man **who** I met* → ✅ *The man I met* ⭐
- ✅ *The film **that** we watched* → ✅ *The film we watched* ⭐

*(But NOT when it's the doer!)*
- ✅ *The man **who** called me* ← must keep *who*! ✅
- ❌ *The man called me* ❌ *(different meaning!)*

*(The test.)* **Is there another subject right after?**
- *The book **I** bought* → yes, "I" ✅ → can delete ⭐
- *The man **who called*** → no other subject → must keep ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Native speakers **delete** it constantly. ✅ *"The film we watched was great."* ⭐ Practise that shape! 🗣️

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് ഡോക്ടറായ ഒരു സുഹൃത്തുണ്ട്.

*(Notice the Malayalam structure!)* Look at the order:
- **ഡോക്ടറായ** → "doctor-being" ← **the description comes FIRST** ✅
- **ഒരു സുഹൃത്ത്** → "a friend" ← then the noun

*(Emphasise.)* Malayalam **front-loads** the description. English **back-loads** it. 🔄

*(That's why we produce.)* ❌ *"I have a doctor friend"* ✅ *(understandable, but limited)* or ❌ *"I have one friend, he is doctor"* ✅

Build it the English way:

1. **Main sentence first** → I have a friend
2. **Is the extra info about a person?** → Yes → **who**
3. **The description** → is a doctor

> ✅ **"I have a friend who is a doctor."** ⭐

*(Add more layers!)* 🔗 *(Day 39)*
> ✅ *"I have a friend who is a doctor **in Kochi**."* ✅
> ✅ *"I have a friend who works as a doctor in Kochi."* ✅

*(Practise the "who" pattern — say these five.)*
1. *I have a colleague **who** speaks four languages.* ✅
2. *The man **who** called you is my uncle.* ✅
3. *She's the teacher **who** taught me English.* ✅
4. *Do you know anyone **who** can fix a laptop?* ⭐
5. *I'm looking for someone **who** has experience in sales.* ⭐

*(Highlight #4 and #5!)* ⭐ These are genuinely useful patterns:
- ✅ *"Do you know anyone who...?"* ⭐ — for asking for help
- ✅ *"I'm looking for someone who..."* ⭐ — for hiring or requests

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ വാങ്ങിയ ഫോൺ പണിമുടക്കി.

*(Notice again!)* **ഞാൻ വാങ്ങിയ** = "I-bought" ← description **before** the noun ✅

*(So we produce.)* ❌ *"I bought phone is not working"* ✅ or ❌ *"The phone which I bought it broke"* ⚠️ *(extra "it"!)*

Build it:

1. **Main sentence** → The phone broke
2. **Which phone?** → the one I bought
3. **Joining word for a thing** → which / that
4. **Or delete it!** ⭐ → *(object position, so deletable)*

> ✅ **"The phone which I bought broke."** ✅
> ✅ **"The phone that I bought broke."** ✅
> ✅ **"The phone I bought broke."** ⭐ *(most natural!)*

*(Emphasise.)* All three correct! ✅ And the **third** one — with nothing — is what native speakers actually say. ⭐

*(⚠️ The critical error — don't repeat the object!)*

- ❌ *The phone which I bought **it** broke.* ❌
- ✅ **The phone which I bought broke.** ✅

*(Explain.)* Because **"which"** already **is** the phone! ✅ Adding *"it"* means saying it twice. 🔁

*(This is a very common Malayali error.)* Practise removing the extra pronoun:
- ❌ *The man who I met **him*** → ✅ *The man I met* ✅
- ❌ *The book that I read **it*** → ✅ *The book I read* ✅
- ❌ *The place where I went **there*** → ✅ *The place where I went* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **One reference per noun.** ✅ 🔗 *(Same economy as one-past, one-negative!)*

---

### The four words in action 📋

#### 👤 WHO — for people

- ✅ *The girl **who** won the prize is my cousin.* ✅
- ✅ *Anyone **who** wants to join can come.* ✅
- ✅ *He's the kind of person **who** always helps.* ⭐

#### 📦 WHICH — for things

- ✅ *The bus **which** goes to Kaloor is delayed.* ✅
- ✅ *This is the app **which** I use for tickets.* ✅
- ✅ *He was late, **which** annoyed the manager.* ⭐ *(commenting on the whole idea!)*

*(Highlight that last one!)* ⭐ **"which"** can refer to the **whole previous sentence**:
- ✅ *She got the job, **which** is great news!* ✅
- ✅ *The meeting was cancelled, **which** saved me two hours.* ✅

*(Very useful and Malayalis rarely use it.)* ⭐

#### 🔑 WHOSE — for possession

- ✅ *The man **whose** car was stolen called the police.* ✅
- ✅ *I met a woman **whose** son studies with me.* ✅
- ✅ *That's the company **whose** ad we saw.* ✅

*(Note.)* **Whose** works for **people AND things**! ✅ Not just people. ✅

*(Common confusion.)* ⚠️ **whose ≠ who's**
- **whose** = belonging to ✅ *(the man whose car...)*
- **who's** = who is ✅ *(the man who's coming...)*

#### 📍 WHERE and 🕐 WHEN

- ✅ *That's the shop **where** I bought it.* ✅
- ✅ *This is the office **where** I work.* ✅
- ✅ *I remember the day **when** we first met.* ✅
- ✅ *Monday is the day **when** I'm busiest.* ✅

*(Note.)* You can often replace *where* with **"that... in"**:
- ✅ *the shop **where** I bought it* ✅
- ✅ *the shop **that** I bought it **in*** ✅ *(both fine)*

---

### 🎯 The two types — with commas and without

*(Important for writing, especially IELTS.)*

**Type 1: NO commas = essential information** ✅
*(It tells you WHICH one)*

> ✅ *"My friend **who lives in Dubai** is coming."* ✅
> → I have several friends. This is the **Dubai** one. ✅

**Type 2: WITH commas = extra information** ✅
*(You already know which one)*

> ✅ *"My friend, **who lives in Dubai**, is coming."* ✅
> → I have one friend *(you know who)*, and by the way, he lives in Dubai. ✅

*(Emphasise the difference!)*
- *My brother **who lives in Kochi*** → I have **more than one** brother ✅
- *My brother, **who lives in Kochi**,* → I have **one** brother ✅

*(Practical note.)* ⚠️ With **commas**, you **cannot** use *that*:
- ✅ *My friend, **who** lives in Dubai, is coming.* ✅
- ❌ *My friend, **that** lives in Dubai, is coming.* ❌

*(Simple advice for speaking.)* Don't worry about commas when **speaking**. ✅ Just know it for **writing** and IELTS. ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: Front-loading the description *(Malayalam order)*

*(The core structural issue.)*

Malayalam puts the description **before** the noun, and English puts it **after**:

| Malayalam | ❌ Direct | ✅ English |
|---|---|---|
| ഞാൻ വാങ്ങിയ ഫോൺ | I bought phone | **the phone I bought** ⭐ |
| അവൻ പറഞ്ഞ കാര്യം | he said matter | **the thing he said** ⭐ |
| നമ്മൾ പോയ സ്ഥലം | we went place | **the place we went** ⭐ |
| എനിക്ക് അറിയാവുന്ന ഒരാൾ | I know one person | **someone I know** ⭐ |
| ഞാൻ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്ന കമ്പനി | I work company | **the company I work for** ⭐ |

*(The fix — a physical habit!)*

> ### 👉 Say the NOUN first. Then describe it. ✅

*(Practise the flip — I say Malayalam-order, you say English-order.)*
- *"I bought phone"* → **"the phone I bought"** ✅
- *"he told thing"* → **"the thing he told me"** ✅
- *"I studied college"* → **"the college I studied at"** ✅
- *"we ate restaurant"* → **"the restaurant we ate at"** ✅

*(Note the preposition at the end!)* ⚠️ *the company I work **for*** ✅ · *the college I studied **at*** ✅ · *the person I spoke **to*** ✅

*(Explain.)* In English, that little preposition often lands at the **very end**. ✅ It feels strange but it's completely correct — and very natural. ⭐

### Trap 2: The extra pronoun

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| The man who I met him | **The man I met** |
| The book which I read it | **The book I read** |
| The place where I went there | **The place I went to** |
| The person whom I called her | **The person I called** |

*(Rule.)* **The joining word already IS the noun. Don't say it twice.** ✅

### Trap 3: "Which" for people

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I have a friend which is a doctor | **a friend who is a doctor** |
| The man which called | **the man who called** |
| Anyone which wants to join | **anyone who wants to join** |

*(Rule.)* **People → who** *(or that)*. **Never "which."** ✅

*(Memory aid.)* **WHO has a "h" like "human."** 👤 ✅

### Trap 4: Chaining too many

*(A specifically Indian-English pattern.)* 🔗 *(Day 39)*

❌ *"I have a friend who is a doctor who works in a hospital which is in Kochi which is near my house."* 😄

*(The fix.)* **Maximum ONE joining word per sentence.** ✅ Then use a **full stop**.

✅ *"I have a friend who is a doctor. He works at a hospital in Kochi, near my house."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* **One join, then stop.** Two joins means you should have used a full stop. ✅

### Bonus: "whom" — do I need it? 🤔

*(A fair question.)*

*(Answer honestly.)* **Almost never.** ✅

*(Explain.)* **Whom** is the object form of *who*:
- ✅ *The man **whom** I met* ✅ *(formal, correct)*
- ✅ *The man **who** I met* ✅ *(accepted in modern English)*
- ✅ *The man I met* ⭐ *(best — just delete it!)*

*(Advice.)* **Skip "whom" entirely in speech.** ✅ Use it only in very formal writing, and even then — you can usually delete the word instead. ✅

*(One exception where it's needed.)* After a **preposition**:
- ✅ *To **whom** it may concern* ✅ *(a formal letter opening)* 📧
- ✅ *The person **to whom** I spoke* ✅ *(formal)*
- ⭐ Better: *The person I spoke to* ✅
