## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:30)

*(Set up the diagnostic lens.)*

### 🔍 The four things that go wrong in work emails

*(Give viewers a checklist to watch for.)*

*(Explain.)* Across all four emails today, the same four problems appear: ✅

#### ❌ Problem 1: No clear ask 🎯

*(Explain.)* The email describes a **situation** but never states **what the reader should do**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* This is the **biggest** one. ⚠️ And it's why emails get ignored — the reader doesn't know they're required to act. ✅

#### ❌ Problem 2: The point is buried ⛏️ 🔗 *(Day 83)*

*(Explain.)* Context first, request last. ✅ The Malayalam politeness order. ✅

*(Firmly.)* Busy readers scan the **first two lines** and decide whether to read on. ⚠️

#### ❌ Problem 3: Colonial phrases 📜

*(Explain.)* *"Do the needful," "revert," "herewith," "the same," "till date."* ✅

#### ❌ Problem 4: Missing specifics 📊

*(Explain.)* No amounts, no dates, no names, no deadlines. ✅ Vagueness forces the reader to reply and **ask**. ⚠️

### 🔑 The four fixes

| ❌ Problem | ✅ Fix |
|---|---|
| No clear ask 🎯 | add **one sentence** starting *"Could you..."* ⭐ |
| Buried point ⛏️ | move the **conclusion** to line 1 ⭐ |
| Colonial phrases 📜 | use the **Day 83 table** ✅ |
| Missing specifics 📊 | add **numbers, dates, names** ⭐ |

*(Slogan.)*
> **Point first. One clear ask. Numbers always.** ⭐
> **കാര്യം ആദ്യം. ഒരു വ്യക്തമായ ആവശ്യം.** ✅

### 📏 And the length test

*(A useful rule of thumb.)*

> ### 👉 If a routine email runs past **120 words**, check it — something is often **repeated** or **unnecessary**. ✂️

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Most everyday work emails land at **40–90 words**. ✅ *(Genuinely complex or legal messages can be longer — that's fine.)* ✅ But a long email about a **simple** request usually means you haven't decided what you actually want. ⚠️

### 📧 MAKEOVER 1 — Chasing a payment *(the hook email, in full)*

#### ❌ BEFORE — 88 words

> **Subject:** *Regarding*
>
> *Respected Sir, Hope you are doing well. With reference to the above cited subject, I would like to bring to your kind notice that the payment for the month of February has not been received from the client till date. #### 🔧 THE DIAGNOSIS

| Problem | Where |
|---|---|
| Subject says nothing ⚠️ | *"Regarding"* |
| No client **name** or **amount** 📊 | throughout |
| No clear **ask** 🎯 | *"do the needful"* |
| Colonial phrases 📜 | *above cited, kind notice, in this connection, oblige* |
| *"till date"* ⚠️ | Indian English |

#### ✅ AFTER — 50 words

> **Subject:** *Alpha Traders — February payment overdue (₹2.4L)*
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *Alpha Traders' February payment of ₹2.4 lakhs is now 22 days overdue. I've sent two reminders with no response.*
>
> *Could you call their finance head? I think it needs someone at your level now.*
>
> *Happy to send the invoice history if useful.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Anu* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Note the line **"I think it needs someone at your level now."** ✅

*(Explain.)* That's a compliment **and** a justification for escalating. ✅ It makes the ask easy to accept. ⭐

### 📧 MAKEOVER 2 — Asking for a deadline extension

#### ❌ BEFORE — 81 words

> **Subject:** *Report*
>
> *Dear Sir,*
>
> *As you are aware, this month has been extremely busy for our team and there have been many client queries also. Additionally, two of my colleagues are on leave since last week and I am handling their work also. Due to all these reasons, I am unable to complete the reconciliation report by tomorrow as scheduled. I am extremely sorry for the inconvenience caused. Kindly grant me some more time and do the needful.*
>
> *Thanking you,*
> *Vinod*

#### 🔧 THE DIAGNOSIS

| Problem | Where |
|---|---|
| **Excuses first**, request last ⛏️ | whole structure |
| Never says **how much** extra time 📊 | ⚠️ |
| Over-apologises 🙏 🔗 *(Day 84)* | *"extremely sorry for the inconvenience caused"* |
| *"are on leave since last week"* ⚠️ 🔗 *(Day 19)* | the **tense** is wrong, not *since*: **"have been on leave since last week"** ✅ |
| No **new date** offered ⚠️ | ⚠️ |

#### ✅ AFTER — 62 words

> **Subject:** *Reconciliation report — requesting 2 extra days*
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *Could I have until Thursday for the reconciliation report, instead of tomorrow?*
>
> *Two colleagues have been on leave for a week and I've been covering their client queries alongside my own. The report is about 60% done — Thursday would let me finish it properly rather than rushing it.*
>
> *Let me know if that's a problem.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Vinod* ⭐

*(Emphasise three things.)* ⭐
1. ✅ **The ask is line one** — *"Could I have until Thursday?"* ⭐
2. ✅ **A specific new date**, not "some more time" ⭐
3. ⭐ **"about 60% done"** — a number that proves he's working, not stalling ✅

*(Firmly.)* And notice — **zero apologies.** ✅ Asking for a realistic extension is professional, not a failure. ⭐

### 📧 MAKEOVER 3 — Reporting your own mistake

*(The hardest email to write well.)*

#### ❌ BEFORE — 82 words

> **Subject:** *Sorry sir*
>
> *Respected Sir,*
>
> *I am extremely sorry to inform you that there was a mistake in the invoice which was sent to Beta Logistics last week. Actually the amount was entered wrongly due to some confusion in the system. It was not intentional and I am very sorry for this error. I take full responsibility and I promise it will not happen again. Kindly forgive me for this mistake. Please advise what I should do.*
>
> *Extremely sorry once again,*
> *Meera*

#### 🔧 THE DIAGNOSIS

| Problem | Where |
|---|---|
| **Four** apologies 🙏 ⚠️ | throughout |
| Passive voice hides the facts 🔗 *(Day 59)* | *"was entered wrongly"* |
| Blames "the system" ⚠️ | *"due to some confusion in the system"* |
| **No amount**, no invoice number 📊 | ⚠️ |
| **Asks the manager** what to do ⚠️ | *"please advise what I should do"* |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ That last one is the real weakness. ✅ She reported a problem and handed it upward **without a solution**. ⚠️

#### ✅ AFTER — 68 words

> **Subject:** *Invoice #4471 (Beta Logistics) — error, corrected version attached*
>
> *Hi Rajesh,*
>
> *I made an error on invoice #4471 to Beta Logistics last week — I entered ₹84,000 instead of ₹48,000.*
>
> *I've prepared a corrected invoice and a credit note for the original *(both attached)*. I've also called their accounts contact to explain, and she's fine with the revision.*
>
> *Unless you'd rather review it first, I'll send both to them this afternoon.*
>
> *Thanks,*
> *Meera* ⭐

*(Emphasise strongly.)* ⭐ Look at the difference: ✅

| ❌ Before | ✅ After |
|---|---|
| four apologies | **one clear ownership sentence** ⭐ |
| *"was entered wrongly"* | ***I* entered ₹84,000"** ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 59!)* |
| blames the system | takes responsibility ✅ |
| asks what to do | **presents the solution** ⭐ |

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **"I made an error... I've prepared a corrected invoice... I've also called the client."** ✅

*(Sincere.)* That email makes Meera look **more** capable, not less. ✅ Because a person who fixes their own mistakes is exactly who managers want. ⭐

### 📧 MAKEOVER 4 — Emailing a client *(highest stakes)*

#### ❌ BEFORE — 99 words

> **Subject:** *Regarding the requirement*
>
> *Respected Sir,*
>
> *Greetings from our company! Hope this mail finds you in the best of health and spirits.*
>
> *With reference to our telephonic conversation held yesterday, I am pleased to inform you that we are in a position to provide the services as per your esteemed requirement. Please find attached herewith our quotation for the same. Kindly go through it and revert back to us with your valuable feedback at your earliest convenience so that we may proceed further in this regard.*
>
> *Thanking you and assuring you our best services always,*
> *Yours faithfully,*
> *Suresh Kumar*

#### 🔧 THE DIAGNOSIS

| Problem | Where |
|---|---|
| Flowery opening 📜 | *"best of health and spirits"* |
| *"esteemed," "valuable," "assuring you our best services"* 📜 ⚠️ | throughout |
| *"revert back"* 🔗 | ⚠️ |
| No **deadline**, no **next step** ⚠️ | *"at your earliest convenience"* |
| Never says what the quotation **is for** or **how much** 📊 | ⚠️ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⚠️ This style is common in Indian B2B email ✅ — and to an international client it reads as **unclear** and possibly **inexperienced**. ⚠️

#### ✅ AFTER — 72 words

> **Subject:** *Quotation — annual maintenance contract (₹4.2L)*
>
> *Dear Mr. Menon,*
>
> *Thanks for your time on the call yesterday. I've attached our quotation for the annual maintenance contract — ₹4.2 lakhs, covering quarterly servicing and unlimited breakdown callouts.*
>
> *Two things worth noting: the price holds for 30 days, and we can start within two weeks of confirmation.*
>
> *Could you let me know by the 25th if you'd like to proceed? Happy to walk through any of it on a call.*
>
> *Best regards,*
> *Suresh Kumar* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Note what got **added**, not just removed: ✅
- ✅ The **amount** in the subject line ⭐
- ✅ **What** the quotation covers ✅
- ✅ The **validity period** ⭐
- ✅ The **start timeline** ✅
- ✅ A **specific date** for the reply ⭐

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **The professional version has MORE information in FEWER words.** ✅ That's what "concise" actually means. ⭐

### 🔑 The five principles you can apply today

#### 1️⃣ Put the ask in the first two lines 🎯

*(Explain.)* Before you write anything else, finish this sentence: ✅
> ✅ *"I want the reader to ___."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* Then make **that** your opening line. ✅

#### 2️⃣ Put a number in the subject line 📊

- ✅ *"February payment overdue (₹2.4L)"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Quotation — AMC (₹4.2L)"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Requesting 2 extra days"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ A number in the subject lets a busy reader **triage** your email instantly. ✅

#### 3️⃣ One apology maximum — and only if warranted 🙏

| Situation | Apology? |
|---|---|
| Your genuine error ✅ | **one** sentence ⭐ |
| Short notice ✅ | **one** brief line ✅ |
| Asking a normal question | **none** ✅ |
| Requesting leave 🔗 | **none** ✅ |
| Asking for an extension | **none** ✅ |

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ 🔗 *(Days 82, 84)* Apologising for normal professional requests reduces your standing. ⚠️

#### 4️⃣ Report problems WITH solutions 🔧

*(The highest-value habit.)*

> ### 👉 Never send a problem without at least **one** proposed action. ✅

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"Please advise what to do"* ⚠️ | *"I've prepared a corrected version — unless you'd rather review it first, I'll send it today."* ⭐ |
| *"There's an error, kindly guide"* ⚠️ | *"There's an error. I think the fix is X — shall I proceed?"* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ This single habit changes how senior people see you. ✅ Problem-bringers get managed. **Solution-bringers get promoted.** ⭐

#### 5️⃣ Delete these fifteen phrases forever ✂️ 🔗 *(Day 83)*

*(Rapid-fire on screen.)*

> ❌ *do the needful · revert back · herewith · the same · above cited · kind notice · in this connection · till date · at the earliest · oblige · esteemed · valuable feedback · Respected Sir · Thanking you · assuring you our best services* 📜

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ Ctrl+F your sent folder. ✅ You'll find most of them. ⭐

### 💙 One honest note about the "before" emails

*(Close the section warmly — this matters.)*

*(Sincere.)* I want to be clear about something. ✅

*(Explain.)* Every "before" email in this video was written by someone **competent** at their job. ✅ Anu **had** chased the client twice. Meera **had** already fixed the invoice. Suresh **had** prepared a real quotation. ✅

*(Firmly.)* All the good work was there. ✅ The email just **hid** it. ⚠️

*(Emphasise, warmly.)* And that's the pattern across this whole makeover block — Day 82's interview, today's emails, tomorrow's essay. ⭐

> ### 👉 The problem is almost never the work. It's the **description** of the work. ✅

*(Sincere.)* Which is genuinely good news — because descriptions are much easier to fix than skills. 💪
