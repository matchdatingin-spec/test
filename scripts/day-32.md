# Day 32 — Translate Long Malayalam Sentences into English with This Formula

**Target length:** 14–16 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** A 5-step formula that breaks any long Malayalam sentence into English — even a 25-word one with the verb hiding at the end.

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Building tension.)*

Short sentences are easy now. **എനിക്ക് വിശക്കുന്നു** → *"I'm hungry."* Done. ✅

But then real life happens. Someone asks you a question, and you need to say **this**:

> **ഞാൻ ഇന്നലെ ഓഫീസിൽ പോയപ്പോൾ മാനേജർ പറഞ്ഞു അടുത്ത ആഴ്ച ഒരു മീറ്റിംഗ് ഉണ്ടെന്ന്, അതുകൊണ്ട് എനിക്ക് റിപ്പോർട്ട് വേഗം തീർക്കണം.**

*(Pause. Wide eyes.)*

**Twenty-five words. Four ideas. Three verbs.** All the verbs at the end of their clauses.

And what happens? Your mouth opens and... *"Yesterday I office went, manager said, next week meeting is, so report fast finish..."* 😰

*(Point at camera, confident.)*

**Freezing on long sentences is the #1 reason people say "I know grammar but I can't speak."**

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow. This is the most important section of the series so far.)*

### Why long sentences break your brain

Here's the real problem. In Malayalam, a long sentence is built like a **train with the engine at the back** 🚂 — and there can be **several** engines, each hidden at the end of its own carriage.

Look at our monster sentence's shape:

```
[ഞാൻ ഇന്നലെ ഓഫീസിൽ പോയപ്പോൾ]  ← verb at end
[മാനേജർ പറഞ്ഞു]                 ← verb at end
[അടുത്ത ആഴ്ച ഒരു മീറ്റിംഗ് ഉണ്ടെന്ന്]  ← verb at end
[എനിക്ക് റിപ്പോർട്ട് വേഗം തീർക്കണം]   ← verb at end
```

*(Point out.)* **Four verbs, all at the end of their piece.** Your brain has to hold four incomplete thoughts in memory at once. That's why it crashes. 💥

*(Reassuring.)* It's not a language problem. It's a **memory** problem. And the solution is not to remember better — it's to **break the sentence into pieces**.

### 🔑 THE FORMULA — 5 steps

*(Write it big. Repeat each step.)*

> ### Step 1: CUT the Malayalam into small pieces (one idea each)
> ### Step 2: In each piece, find the VERB (it's at the end)
> ### Step 3: In each piece, find WHO does it
> ### Step 4: Build each piece separately as a mini English sentence
> ### Step 5: JOIN the pieces with a connector word

*(Slogan.)*
> **മുറിക്കുക → verb കണ്ടെത്തുക → ആള് കണ്ടെത്തുക → ചെറിയ വാക്യം → ചേർക്കുക**
> **Cut. Verb. Who. Build. Join.**

*(Say it three times with the audience.)* **Cut — Verb — Who — Build — Join.** ✂️

### The one habit that changes everything

*(Very important — emphasise strongly.)*

> ### 👉 You do NOT have to say one long sentence. Say THREE short ones.

*(Pause.)* Read that again.

Malayalam **loves** long connected sentences. It's beautiful that way. But **English prefers short sentences!** Native speakers speak in short bursts.

So this is completely acceptable, natural, professional English:

> ✅ *"Yesterday I went to the office. The manager said there's a meeting next week. So I need to finish the report quickly."*

**Three short sentences. Zero mistakes. Perfect English.** ✅

*(Firmly.)* Nobody will think you're a beginner. They'll think you're **clear**. Clear is better than complicated. 🎯

*(This is permission.)* You don't need long sentences to sound fluent. **Stop trying to match Malayalam's length.** ✂️

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 9:00)

### Sentence 1 — the monster, built step by step

**Malayalam:**
> **ഞാൻ ഇന്നലെ ഓഫീസിൽ പോയപ്പോൾ മാനേജർ പറഞ്ഞു അടുത്ത ആഴ്ച ഒരു മീറ്റിംഗ് ഉണ്ടെന്ന്, അതുകൊണ്ട് എനിക്ക് റിപ്പോർട്ട് വേഗം തീർക്കണം.**

*(Work through it slowly on screen. This is the centrepiece of the video.)*

---

#### ✂️ STEP 1 — CUT into pieces

Look for the **cutting points**: `-പ്പോൾ`, `-എന്ന്`, `അതുകൊണ്ട്`, and commas. These are Malayalam's joints. 🦴

| Piece | Malayalam |
|---|---|
| A | ഞാൻ ഇന്നലെ ഓഫീസിൽ പോയപ്പോൾ |
| B | മാനേജർ പറഞ്ഞു |
| C | അടുത്ത ആഴ്ച ഒരു മീറ്റിംഗ് ഉണ്ട് |
| D | എനിക്ക് റിപ്പോർട്ട് വേഗം തീർക്കണം |

*(Relief.)* Four small pieces. **Suddenly it's easy.** ✅

---

#### 🔍 STEP 2 & 3 — Find the VERB and the WHO in each piece

| Piece | Verb *(at the end!)* | Who |
|---|---|---|
| A | പോയി → **went** | ഞാൻ → **I** |
| B | പറഞ്ഞു → **said** | മാനേജർ → **the manager** |
| C | ഉണ്ട് → **is / there is** | മീറ്റിംഗ് → **a meeting** |
| D | തീർക്കണം → **need to finish** | എനിക്ക് → **I** |

---

#### 🔨 STEP 4 — Build each piece as a mini English sentence

*(Verb second, remember!)*

- **A:** I went to the office yesterday. ✅
- **B:** The manager said... ✅
- **C:** There's a meeting next week. ✅
- **D:** I need to finish the report quickly. ✅

*(Point out piece C!)* **ഉണ്ട് → "there is / there are."** ⭐

This is a **hugely** useful pattern that Malayalis underuse:
- ✅ *There **is** a meeting.* ← ഒരു മീറ്റിംഗ് ഉണ്ട്
- ✅ *There **are** two options.* ← രണ്ട് വഴികൾ ഉണ്ട്
- ✅ *There **was** a problem.* ← ഒരു പ്രശ്നം ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നു
- ✅ *There **isn't** any water.* ← വെള്ളമില്ല

⚠️ Not *"A meeting is there."* ⚠️ Not *"Meeting is having."*

*(Rule.)* **Whenever Malayalam says ഉണ്ട് / ഇല്ല about existence → use "There is / There isn't."** ✅

---

#### 🔗 STEP 5 — JOIN with connectors

Now pick your connectors:
- പോയപ്പോൾ → **when**
- പറഞ്ഞു ...എന്ന് → **said that**
- അതുകൊണ്ട് → **so**

**Version A — three short sentences (recommended!):**

> ✅ **"Yesterday I went to the office. The manager said there's a meeting next week. So I need to finish the report quickly."**

**Version B — one long sentence (advanced):**

> ✅ **"When I went to the office yesterday, the manager said that there's a meeting next week, so I need to finish the report quickly."**

*(Compare them honestly.)* Both correct. **Version A is easier to say and easier to understand.** Version B sounds slightly more polished in writing.

*(Advice.)* **Speak Version A. Write Version B.** 🗣️✍️

---

### Sentence 2 — a second worked example

**Malayalam:**
> **എന്റെ സഹോദരൻ ദുബൈയിൽ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നുണ്ട്, അവൻ അടുത്ത മാസം നാട്ടിൽ വരുമെന്ന് പറഞ്ഞു, അപ്പോൾ ഞങ്ങൾ ഒരുമിച്ച് ഒരു യാത്ര പോകും.**

*(Now do it faster — let the audience follow along.)*

#### ✂️ Step 1 — Cut *(look for the commas and -എന്ന്)*

| Piece | Malayalam |
|---|---|
| A | എന്റെ സഹോദരൻ ദുബൈയിൽ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നുണ്ട് |
| B | അവൻ അടുത്ത മാസം നാട്ടിൽ വരും *(എന്ന് പറഞ്ഞു)* |
| C | ഞങ്ങൾ ഒരുമിച്ച് ഒരു യാത്ര പോകും |

#### 🔍 Steps 2–3 — Verb + Who

| Piece | Verb | Who |
|---|---|---|
| A | ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു → **works** | സഹോദരൻ → **my brother** |
| B | വരും → **will come** + പറഞ്ഞു → **said** | അവൻ → **he** |
| C | പോകും → **will go** | ഞങ്ങൾ → **we** |

#### 🔨 Step 4 — Build

- **A:** My brother works in Dubai. ✅
- **B:** He said he **would** come home next month. ✅ ← *(Day 07! "said" → "would," not "will")*
- **C:** Then we'll go on a trip together. ✅

#### 🔗 Step 5 — Join

> ✅ **"My brother works in Dubai. He said he would come home next month, and then we'll go on a trip together."**

*(Highlight two things.)*

**1. "said he would"** — remember Day 07? When you report in the **past**, *will* becomes **would**. ✅

**2. "come home"** — not ❌ *"come to home"*! Remember Day 18? *Home* takes no preposition. 🏠

---

### The connector toolbox 🧰

*(This is the practical takeaway — spend time here.)*

Step 5 is where most people get stuck, because they only know **"and"** and **"but."** Here's your full toolbox:

| Malayalam | ✅ English connector | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -പ്പോൾ / -അപ്പോൾ | **when** | *When I arrived, he had left.* |
| അതുകൊണ്ട് | **so** | *It was raining, so I stayed home.* |
| കാരണം / എന്തുകൊണ്ടെന്നാൽ | **because** | *I'm late because of the traffic.* |
| പക്ഷേ / എങ്കിലും | **but** / **although** | *Although it rained, we went.* |
| -എങ്കിൽ | **if** | *If it rains, we'll cancel.* |
| -എന്ന് (പറഞ്ഞു) | **that** | *He said that he'd come.* |
| -ശേഷം / കഴിഞ്ഞ് | **after** | *After I finish, I'll call you.* |
| -മുൻപ് | **before** | *Before you go, sign this.* |
| -വരെ | **until** | *Wait until I come.* |
| കൂടാതെ / അതുപോലെ | **also** / **and** | *He speaks Hindi and Tamil too.* |
| എന്നിരുന്നാലും | **however** | *However, I disagree.* |
| അതിനാൽ | **therefore** | *Therefore, we should wait.* |

*(Highlight "although" — Malayalis underuse it!)*
- ✅ *Although I was tired, I finished the work.* ⭐
- ✅ *Although it's expensive, it's worth it.*

*(Warning about a common error.)* ❌ *"Although I was tired, **but** I finished"* — you can't use *although* and *but* together! Pick **one**. ✅

*(Same for because.)* ❌ *"Because it rained, **so** we stayed"* → ✅ **"Because it rained, we stayed."** or ✅ **"It rained, so we stayed."** ✅

*(Rule.)* **One connector per joint.** Not two. *(Same economy principle as one-past-per-sentence! 🔗)*

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (9:00 – 11:00)

### Trap 1: Trying to keep the Malayalam LENGTH

*(The deepest trap.)*

Malayalam sentences are naturally long because the language **glues clauses together with verb endings**. One Malayalam sentence can carry four ideas comfortably.

But when you force English into that shape, you get sentence chains like:

❌ *"Yesterday I went office and manager said meeting is there next week and so I have to finish report fast and I told him okay and..."*

*(Wince.)* That's **and-and-and** English. Everyone understands it. Nobody enjoys it.

*(The fix — permission again.)* **FULL STOP is your friend.** 🛑

*(Practical instruction.)* When you speak English, **stop every 8–10 words.** Take a breath. Start a new sentence.

*(Demonstrate — say it both ways.)*

⚠️ *"I went to the office and the manager told me about a meeting and I have to prepare a report and it is due on Friday."* *(one breath, exhausting)*

✅ *"I went to the office. The manager told me about a meeting. I have to prepare a report — it's due on Friday."* *(three breaths, clear and confident)* ⭐

*(Point out.)* Which one sounds more fluent? **The short one.** Because short sentences sound **controlled**. Long chains sound **panicked**. 😌

### Trap 2: The verb-last leak

*(When your brain runs out of processing time, Malayalam order leaks through.)*

- ❌ *I to Kochi tomorrow going* → ✅ **I'm going to Kochi tomorrow.**
- ❌ *He me the money gave* → ✅ **He gave me the money.**
- ❌ *Yesterday I my friend met* → ✅ **I met my friend yesterday.**

*(The fix — a physical habit!)* Train yourself to **say the person + verb together, immediately, as one unit**:

**"I went..." "He said..." "She works..." "They came..." "We need..."**

*(Emphasise.)* Get those **two words** out of your mouth first, and the rest of the sentence has nowhere to go but the correct place. ✅

*(Drill — say these ten pairs fast with me!)*
I went · He said · She works · They came · We need · I have · He wants · She told · They gave · We saw ✅

*(That's it.)* **Person + verb, immediately.** That single habit fixes word order permanently. 🔑

### Trap 3: Time words at the front (every time)

Malayalam almost always puts time **first**: ഇന്നലെ, നാളെ, അടുത്ത ആഴ്ച.

English allows time at the **front or the end** — but the **end is more natural** in speech:

- ✅ *I went to the office **yesterday**.* ⭐ *(natural)*
- ✅ ***Yesterday** I went to the office.* ✅ *(also fine — adds emphasis)*
- ❌ *I yesterday went to the office.* ❌ *(never in the middle!)*

*(Rule.)* **Put time expressions at the FRONT or the END — not between the person and the verb.** ✅ *(Short frequency words are the exception: ✅ "I **often** go," "I **never** eat there.")*

*(And remember Day 18!)* When you stack time words, English goes **small → big**:
- ✅ *at 6 o'clock on Monday in January* ✅
*(Malayalam does the opposite: ജനുവരിയിൽ തിങ്കളാഴ്ച ആറു മണിക്ക്)* 🔄

### Trap 4: Translating "-എന്ന്" as a separate word

**-എന്ന്** is Malayalam's reporting marker. It becomes **that** — and here's the good news:

*(Happy fact.)* **In spoken English, you can just DROP it!** ✅

- ✅ *He said **that** he would come.* ✅
- ✅ *He said he would come.* ✅ ← **more natural!**
- ✅ *I think **that** it's fine.* ✅
- ✅ *I think it's fine.* ✅ ← **more natural!**

*(Emphasise.)* **Dropping "that" makes you sound MORE native, not less.** One less word to worry about. 🎉
