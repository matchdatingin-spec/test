# Day 15 — Already, Still, or Yet? Learn the Difference in 10 Minutes

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *Already* = earlier than expected. *Still* = continuing. *Yet* = not until now. And each one has its own seat in the sentence.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Energetic, a bit rushed — like real conversation.)*

Your friend calls at 9 PM. He asks if you've eaten. You want to say **ഞാൻ ഇതുവരെ കഴിച്ചിട്ടില്ല.**

And you say:

> ❌ *"I didn't eat yet."*
> ❌ *"I not yet eaten."*
> ❌ *"Still I didn't eat."*

*(Shake head, smiling.)* All three understood. All three wrong.

The natural sentences are:
> ✅ **"I haven't eaten yet."**
> ✅ **"I still haven't eaten."**

*(Lean in.)*

Notice the problem? It's not the **word** — you know *yet*, you know *still*. The problem is **WHERE you put it.**

Malayalam puts these words in the middle: *ഞാൻ **ഇതുവരെ** കഴിച്ചിട്ടില്ല.* English has very strict seats for them. Sit in the wrong seat, and the sentence sounds broken.

Today: three words, three seats, permanent clarity. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Draw a timeline in the air.)*

Think of a **timeline** with NOW in the middle.

### ✅ ALREADY = it happened, and EARLIER than expected

There's a feeling of **surprise** — sooner than you thought.

- ✅ *I have already eaten.* → *(Don't cook for me!)*
- ✅ *He has already left.* → *(So early?)*
- ✅ *The movie has already started.* → *(We're late!)*

**ALREADY = done, and sooner than expected.** Malayalam: **കഴിഞ്ഞു / നേരത്തെ തന്നെ**

### ⏳ STILL = it was happening before, and it's CONTINUING now

There's a feeling of **surprise that it hasn't stopped**.

- ✅ *He is still sleeping.* → *(At 11 AM? Really?)*
- ✅ *I still live in Kochi.* → nothing changed
- ✅ *Are you still working there?* → continuing

**STILL = continuing, longer than expected.** Malayalam: **ഇപ്പോഴും / ഇനിയും**

### ❌ YET = it has NOT happened up to now (but we expect it)

Used mostly in **negatives and questions**.

- ✅ *I haven't eaten yet.* → but I will
- ✅ *Has he come yet?* → I'm expecting him
- ✅ *The bus hasn't arrived yet.*

**YET = not until now, but expected.** Malayalam: **ഇതുവരെ**

### 🪑 Now — the SEATS. This is the real lesson.

*(Slow down. Very important.)*

| Word | Where it sits | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **already** | **middle** — after the helper, before the main verb | I have **already** eaten. |
| **still** | **middle** — but *before* the helper in negatives! | He is **still** working. / I **still** haven't eaten. |
| **yet** | **END** of the sentence | I haven't eaten **yet**. |

*(Say the slogan.)*
> **Already → middle. Still → middle. Yet → END.**

**Yet** always goes to the back of the bus. Always. 🚌

---

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ഇതുവരെ ഭക്ഷണം കഴിച്ചിട്ടില്ല.

Let's see the Malayalam order:
- ഞാൻ → I
- **ഇതുവരെ** → until now *(sitting in the MIDDLE)*
- ഭക്ഷണം → food
- കഴിച്ചിട്ടില്ല → have not eaten *(verb LAST, negative inside)*

Your brain produces: ❌ *"I yet food not eaten."* Or the cleaner-but-still-wrong: ❌ *"I didn't eat yet."*

Now build it the English way:

1. **Who** → I
2. **Helper + not** *(this is a "up to now" situation → present perfect)* → have not / haven't
3. **Third form of the verb** → eaten
4. **Yet goes to the END** → yet

> ✅ **"I haven't eaten yet."**

*(Two important points.)*

**Point 1 — why "haven't eaten," not "didn't eat"?**
Because *yet* means "up to **now**." It connects the past to the present. And the tense that connects past to present is **have/has + third form**.

- ❌ *I didn't eat yet.* → *didn't* is a closed, finished box. It doesn't reach "now."
- ✅ *I haven't eaten yet.* → reaches all the way to now ✅

**Point 2 — the alternative with *still*:**

> ✅ **"I still haven't eaten."**

Same meaning! But feel the difference in emotion:
- *I haven't eaten **yet**.* → neutral, just information
- *I **still** haven't eaten.* → slight frustration! *(It's 9 PM! Why hasn't anyone fed me?!)*

*(Note the position.)* In a negative, **still comes BEFORE the helper**: *I **still** haven't...* Not ❌ *"I haven't still eaten."*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ഭക്ഷണം കഴിഞ്ഞു. / ഞാൻ കഴിച്ചു കഴിഞ്ഞു.

You want to say: *"Don't cook for me — I ate already."*

Build it:

1. **Who** → I
2. **Helper** → have
3. **Already sits in the MIDDLE** → already
4. **Third form** → eaten

> ✅ **"I have already eaten."**

*(Casual spoken version.)*
> ✅ **"I've already eaten."**
> ✅ **"I already ate."** ← very common in everyday speech, especially American

*(Position check.)* Where does *already* go?

- ✅ *I have **already** eaten.* → between helper and verb ✅ **best**
- ✅ *I have eaten **already**.* → at the end ✅ also correct, adds emphasis
- ❌ *Already I have eaten.* → wrong position *(this is the Malayalam order!)*
- ❌ *I already have eaten.* → wrong, before the helper

*(Simple memory.)* **Already loves to sandwich itself between the two verbs.** *have* — **already** — *eaten*.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Word & feeling |
|---|---|---|
| It's 11 AM, brother is in bed | **He's still sleeping!** | continuing, surprise |
| You arrive at the cinema late | **The movie has already started.** | earlier than expected |
| Waiting for a friend | **Has he reached yet?** | expecting, question |
| Someone offers you tea | **No thanks, I've already had one.** | done earlier |
| Job application, no reply | **They haven't called me yet.** | not up to now |

*(Highlight row 3.)* Notice: in **questions**, *yet* also goes to the **end**:
- ✅ *Have you finished **yet**?*
- ✅ *Has the bus come **yet**?*
- ✅ *Are we there **yet**?*

*(Highlight row 1.)* And **still** in a positive sentence goes **after** be/helper:
- ✅ *He **is** still sleeping.*
- ✅ *I **am** still waiting.*
- ✅ *She **can** still come.*

But with a **plain verb** (no helper), *still* goes **before the verb**:
- ✅ *I **still** live in Kochi.*
- ✅ *He **still** works there.*

*(Summary of still's seat.)* **Still goes right before the "main content" of the sentence** — before the plain verb, after be/can/will, and before "haven't/don't" in negatives.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "Still" used where English wants "yet"

*(This is huge in Kerala English.)*

We say:
> ❌ *"He didn't come still."*
> ❌ *"Still he didn't call me."*
> ❌ *"Food is not ready still."*

Why? Because **ഇപ്പോഴും** and **ഇതുവരെ** feel like the same idea in Malayalam, and both often land at the end of the clause.

The English versions:
- ✅ *He hasn't come yet.* / *He still hasn't come.*
- ✅ *He hasn't called me yet.*
- ✅ *The food isn't ready yet.*

*(Rule to remember.)* **In negatives, English prefers YET at the end.** *Still* is possible, but it must move to the **front of the negative**:

| ❌ Malayali English | ✅ Version A (yet) | ✅ Version B (still) |
|---|---|---|
| He didn't come still | He hasn't come **yet**. | He **still** hasn't come. |
| I didn't get salary still | I haven't got my salary **yet**. | I **still** haven't got my salary. |
| Bus not came still | The bus hasn't arrived **yet**. | The bus **still** hasn't arrived. |

*(Practise the shapes out loud.)*
- **"...hasn't ___ yet."**
- **"...still hasn't ___."**

### Trap 2: "Already" at the beginning

Malayalam puts time words early. So we say:

- ❌ *Already I told you* → ✅ **I already told you.** / **I've already told you.**
- ❌ *Already he went* → ✅ **He has already left.**
- ❌ *Already 5 o'clock* → ✅ **It's already 5 o'clock!** ✅ *(this one works!)*

*(Note.)* Starting a sentence with *already* is only okay in a few dramatic cases. As a habit, keep it in the **middle**.

### Trap 3: "Itself" instead of "already"

*(This is a very Malayali one!)*

**ഇന്ന് തന്നെ / ഇപ്പോൾ തന്നെ / അപ്പോൾ തന്നെ** → we translate തന്നെ as *itself*:

- ❌ *I sent it yesterday itself* → ✅ **I sent it yesterday.** / **I already sent it yesterday.**
- ❌ *He came morning itself* → ✅ **He came in the morning itself → He came as early as this morning.** / **He already came this morning.**
- ❌ *Now itself I'll do it* → ✅ **I'll do it right now.**

*(Rule.)* When Malayalam uses **തന്നെ** for "as early as that," English uses **already** or restructures completely. **"Itself" is never a time word in English.**

### Trap 4: Yet ≠ but... except when it is!

*(Fun advanced note.)* **Yet** has a second, formal meaning: **but**.

- ✅ *He is very rich, yet he lives simply.* = but ✅
- ✅ *It's a small car, yet very powerful.* ✅

Don't worry about this for speaking — just don't get confused when you see it in writing.

### The full comparison — memorise this table

| Situation | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Done, sooner than expected | **already** | I've **already** paid. |
| Not done, but expected | **yet** | I haven't paid **yet**. |
| Continuing from before | **still** | I'm **still** paying the loan. |
| No longer happening | **anymore** | I don't work there **anymore**. |

*(Bonus.)* That last one — **"anymore"** — is the opposite of *still*! And Malayalis rarely use it:
- ✅ *He doesn't live here anymore.* *(ഇനി ഇവിടെ താമസിക്കുന്നില്ല)*
- ✅ *I don't smoke anymore.*

*(Note the position.)* **Anymore goes at the END**, like *yet*.
