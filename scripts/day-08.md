# Day 08 — Can vs Could: Ability, Permission, and Polite Requests

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** *Can* and *could* do three jobs each. Learn which job you're doing, and the word picks itself.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Direct, friendly.)*

You are standing at a shop counter. You want to ask for a bag. And you say:

> ❌ *"Can you give one bag?"*

The shopkeeper understands you. But something feels rough. Like you ordered him.

Or you're in an interview, and the manager asks *"Can you handle pressure?"* and you answer:

> ⚠️ *"Yes sir, I could."*

*(Wince.)* Ooh. You just said **"maybe, in some imaginary situation."** In an interview! When you needed confidence!

*(Point at camera.)*

This is the danger of can vs could. It's not just grammar — it changes how **confident** you sound, how **polite** you sound. And most Malayalis use *could* thinking it's always "more respectful."

It isn't. Sometimes *could* makes you sound weak. Today, you'll know exactly when to use each. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

Both words do **three jobs**. Same three jobs. The difference is the *strength*.

### Job 1: ABILITY (കഴിവ്)

- **can** = I have this ability **now**.
  ✅ *I can drive.* ✅ *She can speak three languages.*
- **could** = I had this ability **in the past**.
  ✅ *I could run fast when I was young.* ✅ *He could swim at age four.*

**For ability: can = now. Could = past.** Simple.

### Job 2: PERMISSION (അനുവാദം)

- **can** = normal, friendly asking.
  ✅ *Can I sit here?*
- **could** = more polite, more careful.
  ✅ *Could I sit here?*
- **may** = most formal.
  ✅ *May I sit here?*

**For permission: can → could → may.** Politeness increases left to right.

### Job 3: POSSIBILITY (സാധ്യത)

- **can** = generally possible, a fact about the world.
  ✅ *Kerala can get heavy rain in June.* (it's a known fact)
- **could** = maybe, a guess about this specific case.
  ✅ *It could rain today.* (I'm guessing)

**For possibility: can = a general truth. Could = a specific guess.**

### The master idea

*(Say slowly.)* **CAN is strong and certain. COULD is soft and uncertain.**

So now you understand the interview problem. *"Can you handle pressure?"* → **"Yes, I can."** Strong. Certain. Confident.

If you say *"I could"*, you sound like *"maybe... under some conditions... who knows."* Never do that in an interview.

But at a shop counter, softness is a **good** thing. So there, *could* wins.

> **Rule of thumb: about YOURSELF → can (be confident). ASKING others → could (be polite).**

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Situation:** Politely asking a shopkeeper for a bag.

**Malayalam:** ഒരു ബാഗ് തരാമോ?

*(Notice.)* The Malayalam sentence has **no subject at all**! No ഞാൻ, no നിങ്ങൾ. Just the verb with a polite tail: **തരാമോ**.

That's why Malayalis produce subject-less English: ❌ *"Give one bag?"* ❌ *"Can give a bag?"* English can't do that here. **In a question or a statement, English needs the person.**

*(One exception you already use.)* ✅ **Commands** drop the subject naturally: ✅ *"Give me a bag."* · *"Sit down."* ✅ That's fine — it's questions and statements that need the person. ⭐

Build it:

1. **Which job?** → I'm asking someone to do something → request → politeness matters → **Could**
2. **Who does the action?** → you *(must be stated!)*
3. **Plain verb** → give
4. **To whom** → me
5. **What** → a bag *(ഒരു = a, not one)*
6. **Add warmth** → please

> ✅ **"Could you give me a bag, please?"**

Even more natural in a shop:
> ✅ **"Could I get a bag, please?"**
> ✅ **"Could you pass me a bag?"**

*(Compare the whole family.)*

| Sentence | How it sounds |
|---|---|
| *Give me a bag.* | An order. Rude to a stranger. |
| *Can you give me a bag?* | Fine with friends and family. |
| **Could you give me a bag?** | **Polite. Safe everywhere. Use this.** |
| *Would you mind giving me a bag?* | Very polite, slightly formal. |

*(Slogan.)* **"Could you...?" is your universal key.** It opens every door in English — shops, offices, emails, strangers.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Situation:** An interview. "Are you able to work night shifts?"

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് night shift ചെയ്യാൻ കഴിയും.

Look at **കഴിയും** — that's the ability word. And notice the Malayalam starts with **എനിക്ക്** = "to me." That's why we hear ❌ *"To me possible to do night shift."*

Build it:

1. **Which job?** → my own ability, and I want to sound sure → **can**
2. **Who?** → I
3. **can** → can
4. **Plain verb** → work *(not "do work")*
5. **What** → night shifts

> ✅ **"Yes, I can work night shifts."**

*(Upgrade for interviews.)* Even stronger answers:
- ✅ *Yes, I'm comfortable working night shifts.*
- ✅ *Yes, I've done night shifts before, so it's not a problem.*

*(Warning.)* Never answer an interview ability question with *could*, *may be*, or *I think so*. Those three kill your confidence. Say **"Yes, I can."**

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Asking a stranger for directions | **Excuse me, could you tell me where the bus stand is?** | *could* + *tell me* |
| Talking about your skills on a resume call | **I can handle customer support and billing.** | *can* = confident |
| Childhood memory | **I could climb trees when I was ten.** | past ability |
| Guessing about a late friend | **He could be stuck in traffic.** | guess = *could be* |
| Asking your boss for leave | **Could I take a day off tomorrow?** | polite permission |

*(Stop at row 3.)* Careful — for **one specific past success**, English prefers *was able to* or *managed to*, not *could*:

- ❌ *Yesterday I could finish the report.*
- ✅ **Yesterday I was able to finish the report.**
- ✅ **Yesterday I managed to finish the report.**

Why? Because *could* means "general ability," not "I actually did it once." For a single completed success, use **was able to**.

But in the **negative**, *couldn't* is perfectly fine for one event:
- ✅ *I couldn't finish the report yesterday.* ✅

*(Smile.)* Strange, isn't it? English allows *couldn't* for one event but not *could*. Just accept it — negatives are relaxed, positives are strict.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "കഴിയും / അറിയാം" → can... but which one?

Malayalam has two ability words and we translate both as *can*:

- **കഴിയും** = am able to → **can** ✅
- **അറിയാം** = know how to → **know how to** ✅

So look at this:

- എനിക്ക് നീന്താൻ അറിയാം → ❌ *I know to swim* → ✅ **I know how to swim.** / **I can swim.**
- എനിക്ക് ഇംഗ്ലീഷ് അറിയാം → ❌ *I know English speaking* → ✅ **I know English.** / **I can speak English.**
- എനിക്ക് വരാൻ കഴിയില്ല → ❌ *To me not possible to come* → ✅ **I can't come.**

*(Key point.)* When *അറിയാം* is about a **skill**, English needs **how**: *know **how to** swim, know **how to** drive, know **how to** cook.* Never ❌ "know to swim."

### Trap 2: The subject disappears

This is the big structural one. Malayalam politeness deletes the subject:

| Malayalam | ⚠️ Direct translation | ✅ English |
|---|---|---|
| ഒന്ന് സഹായിക്കാമോ? | Can help? | **Could you help me?** |
| ഇവിടെ ഇരിക്കാമോ? | Can sit here? | **Could I sit here?** |
| ഒന്ന് കാണിക്കാമോ? | Can show? | **Could you show me?** |
| നാളെ വരാമോ? | Can come tomorrow? | **Could you come tomorrow?** |

*(Point out the danger.)* Notice something scary: *"Can help?"* could mean **either** "Can *I* help?" **or** "Can *you* help?" — completely opposite meanings! Without the subject, English becomes confusing.

**So always ask yourself: WHO is doing the action?**
- *You* are doing it → **Could you...?** (request)
- *I* am doing it → **Could I...?** (permission)

*(Practise the pair out loud.)*
- Could **you** open the window? → *I want you to open it.*
- Could **I** open the window? → *I'm asking permission.*

### Trap 3: "Can" for the future

Malayalis say ⚠️ *"I can come tomorrow"* when they mean *"I will come tomorrow."* *(The sentence is correct — it just states availability, not a promise.)*

- **Can** = ability/possibility exists.
- **Will** = it's actually going to happen.

So:
- ✅ *I can come tomorrow.* = It's possible for me. (But I haven't promised.)
- ✅ *I will come tomorrow.* = I promise, I'm coming.

If your boss asks "Will you finish this today?" and you say "I can," you have not committed to anything. Say **"Yes, I'll finish it today."**
