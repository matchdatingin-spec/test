## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 6:00)

*(Explain the task and criteria precisely.)*

### 🏥 What OET Writing actually asks

*(Set the frame.)*

| Detail | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Time** | 45 minutes *(5 reading + 40 writing)* ⏰ |
| **Length** | **180–200 words** ⭐ |
| **Task** | usually a **referral letter** ✅ |
| **Given** | case notes + a specific reader ✅ |
| **Profession-specific** | nurses get nursing notes, doctors medical, etc. ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ You are writing to **one specific reader** for **one specific purpose**. ✅ Everything is judged against that. ⭐

### 📊 The six criteria

*(Show the table — candidates need to see this.)*

*(Explain.)* OET Writing is scored on **six** separate criteria. ✅ Most candidates have never seen them, which is why they lose marks without knowing why. ⭐

| Criterion | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **1. Purpose** 🎯 | is the reason for writing clear immediately? ⭐ |
| **2. Content** 📋 | did you include the **relevant** information, accurately? ✅ |
| **3. Conciseness & Clarity** ✂️ | did you **leave out** the irrelevant? ⭐ |
| **4. Genre & Style** 👔 | correct register for a professional reader ✅ |
| **5. Organisation & Layout** 📄 | logical paragraphs, correct letter format ✅ |
| **6. Language** ✍️ | grammar, vocabulary, spelling ✅ |

*(Emphasise criterion 3!)* ⭐

> ### 👉 **"Conciseness"** means you are marked on what you OMIT. ✅

*(Explain.)* This is genuinely unusual in an English exam. ✅ Most exams reward **more**. OET rewards **judgement**. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* Including a patient's 1998 appendectomy in a letter about a fall risk **loses** you marks. ⚠️ Not because it's wrong — because it's **irrelevant to this reader's purpose**. ✅

### 🔑 The selection question

*(The most important idea in the video.)*

*(Explain.)* Before including **any** case note, ask: ✅

> ### 👉 **"Does this reader need this, for what they're about to do?"** ✅

*(Show it.)*

*(Scenario.)* You're referring a patient to a **community nurse** for wound care after discharge. ✅

| Case note | Include? |
|---|---|
| Current wound size and dressing type | ✅ **yes** — that's their job ⭐ |
| Diabetes, poorly controlled | ✅ **yes** — affects healing ⭐ |
| Lives alone, no family nearby | ✅ **yes** — affects care plan ⭐ |
| Appendectomy in 1998 | ❌ **no** — irrelevant ✅ |
| Allergic to penicillin | ✅ **yes** — safety ⭐ |
| Was a schoolteacher before retirement | ❌ **no** ✅ |
| Daughter is a nurse in Dubai | ❌ **no** *(unless relevant to support)* ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Same notes, different reader, different selection.** ✅ If you were referring to a **dietitian**, the diabetes detail becomes central and the wound size becomes secondary. ⭐

*(Slogan.)*
> **Select for the reader's purpose. You're marked on what you leave out.** ✂️
> **വായനക്കാരന് വേണ്ടത് മാത്രം.** ⭐

### 📄 The letter structure

*(The formula.)*

> ### **1. Address + date + salutation** ✅
> ### **2. Purpose paragraph** — why you're writing *(1–2 sentences)* ⭐
> ### **3. Relevant background** — condensed history ✅
> ### **4. Current status** — what's happening now ✅
> ### **5. Request** — exactly what you want the reader to do ⭐
> ### **6. Closing** ✅

*(Emphasise paragraph 2!)* ⭐ **The purpose must be clear immediately.** ✅ This is where Criterion 1 is won or lost — though the whole letter is judged, not just the opening. ⭐

> **Diagnosis:** Cellulitis, right lower leg
>
> **Medical history:**
> • Type 2 diabetes, diagnosed 2009, poorly controlled *(HbA1c 9.2%)*

> • Appendectomy 1998
> • Allergic to penicillin *(rash)*
>

> • Retired bank clerk
> • Independent with personal care before admission
>
> **Treatment during admission:**
> • IV clindamycin ×5 days, then oral clindamycin
> • Wound: 4 cm × 3 cm, right shin; daily dressing
> • Blood glucose monitored; insulin dose adjusted by diabetes team
>
> **On discharge:**
> • Wound reduced to 2 cm × 2 cm, healing, no discharge
> • Continue oral clindamycin, 3 more days
> • Requires **daily wound dressing** for 10 days

### 🅐 Step 1 — Selection *(5 minutes)*

*(Do this on screen, colour-coded.)*

*(Ask.)* What does a **community nurse** doing home visits need? ✅

#### ✅ INCLUDE

| Note | Why |
|---|---|
| Cellulitis diagnosis, discharge date ✅ | context |
| Wound size, healing status ⭐ | her core task |
| Daily dressing for 10 days ⭐ | the request |
| Oral clindamycin, 3 days remaining ✅ | continuing treatment |
| **Penicillin allergy** ⭐ | safety — she may administer or advise on medication |
| Diabetes, poorly controlled *(HbA1c 9.2%)* ⭐ | affects healing |
| Needs diabetes education ⭐ | second request |
| Lives alone, first floor, no lift ⭐ | access and safety |
| Struggles with stairs ✅ | mobility risk |

#### ❌ OMIT

| Note | Why |
|---|---|
| Appendectomy 1998 ❌ | irrelevant ✅ |
| Retired bank clerk ❌ | irrelevant ✅ |
| Hypertension, well controlled ❌ | stable, not relevant to this care ✅ |
| IV clindamycin details ❌ | completed in hospital ✅ |
| Daughter's location ⚠️ | borderline — include **only** as support context ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Nine included. Four omitted.** ✅ That judgement **is** the exam. ⭐

---

### 🅑 Step 2 — Layout and salutation

**Model:**
> ✅ *Ms. Priya Menon*
> *Community Nurse*
> *Ernakulam Community Health Centre*
>
> *20 March 2026*
>
> *Dear Ms. Menon,* ⭐

*(Notes.)* ✅
- ✅ Reader's **name and title** ✅
- ✅ **Date** ✅
- ✅ **"Dear Ms. Menon,"** ⭐ *(use the name given!)*
- ⚠️ If no name is given → **"Dear Sir/Madam,"** ✅

---

### 🅒 Step 3 — The purpose paragraph ⭐

> ### **Formula: Re: patient + why you're writing** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"**Re: Mrs. Sarah Thomas, 68 years**
>
> I am writing to refer Mrs. Thomas, who is being discharged today following treatment for cellulitis of the right lower leg. She requires daily wound dressing for the next ten days and would benefit from diabetes education, and I would be grateful if you could take over her care at home."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ In **two sentences**, the reader knows: ✅ who, why, what's needed, and what's being asked. ✅ That's the strongest possible start on Criterion 1 — now keep it up through the rest of the letter. ⭐

*(The purpose phrases.)*
- ✅ *"I am writing to refer ___, who ___"* ⭐
- ✅ *"I am writing to request your assistance with ___"* ✅
- ✅ *"I would be grateful if you could ___"* ⭐

---

### 🅓 Step 4 — Relevant background

**Model:**
> ✅ *"Mrs. Thomas was admitted on 12 March with cellulitis and treated with intravenous clindamycin, followed by oral therapy. Please note she is **allergic to penicillin**, which produces a rash.*
>
> *She has a history of type 2 diabetes, diagnosed in 2009 and currently poorly controlled, with a recent HbA1c of 9.2%. Her insulin regimen was adjusted during admission by the diabetes team."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Note **"Please note she is allergic to penicillin"** ✅ — flagging safety information explicitly is exactly what a professional reader expects. ⭐

*(Note the condensing.)* ✅ Eight case-note lines became **four** sentences. ⭐

---

### 🅔 Step 5 — Current status

**Model:**
> ✅ *"The wound has responded well and has reduced from 4 cm × 3 cm to 2 cm × 2 cm, with no discharge at present. She will complete three further days of oral clindamycin.*
>
> *Mrs. Thomas lives alone in a first-floor flat with no lift and is currently mobilising slowly, finding stairs difficult. Her daughter lives in Bangalore and visits monthly, so daily support is not available at home."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice how the **social** information is presented with its **clinical implication** ✅ — *"so daily support is not available."* ⭐

*(Firmly.)* That's not just reporting. That's **clinical reasoning** in English — and it scores well. ⭐

---

### 🅕 Step 6 — The request

**Model:**
> ✅ *"I would therefore appreciate it if you could arrange daily wound dressing for the next ten days and review her mobility, given the stairs. It would also be helpful if you could provide education on dietary management and blood glucose monitoring, as her understanding in these areas is limited.*
>
> *Please contact me if you require any further information."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Specific, numbered requests.** ✅ Not *"please do the needful."* ⚠️ 🔗 *(Day 83!)*

---

### 🅖 Step 7 — Closing

> ✅ *"Yours sincerely,*
>
> *Charge Nurse*
> *Medical Ward, City Hospital"* ⭐

*(Notes.)* ✅
- ✅ **"Yours sincerely"** when you used the reader's **name** ⭐
- ✅ **"Yours faithfully"** when you wrote *"Dear Sir/Madam"* ✅
- ⚠️ Do **not** sign your own name — OET requires anonymity ✅ Use your **role**. ⭐

>

>

>

>
> *Charge Nurse* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **196 words. Five paragraphs. Purpose in sentence one.** ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (15:00 – 19:00)

### Trap 1: Including everything 📋

*(The core error — and it comes from a good place.)*

*(Explain warmly.)* This is one of the **most common** reasons strong Malayali candidates get Grade C instead of B. ✅

*(Explain why it happens.)* Three genuine reasons: ✅
1. **Clinical training** teaches completeness — omission can harm ✅
2. It feels **disrespectful** to the patient to leave things out ✅
3. Indian exam culture rewards **more** content ✅

*(Firmly, kindly.)* All three are understandable. ✅ And all three lose you marks here. ⚠️

*(The reframe.)*

> ### 👉 In OET, **omitting** irrelevant detail shows you understand your reader's needs. ✅ That selection skill is a big part of what's being marked. ⭐

*(The practical test.)* Before each note, ask: ✅
- ✅ *"Would this change what the reader does?"* ⭐
- **No** → **cut it** ✂️

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ A padded 250-word letter stuffed with the 1998 appendectomy will usually score **lower** on selection than a tight 190-word letter that leaves it out. ✅

### Trap 2: Copying case notes word-for-word ⚠️

*(A Language and Clarity error.)*

*(Explain.)* Case notes are written in **abbreviated** style. Letters must be in **full sentences**. ✅

| ⚠️ Copied note style | ✅ Letter style |
|---|---|
| *Pt c/o pain* ⚠️ | **The patient complained of pain** ✅ |
| *Wound 4×3 cm, R shin, daily dressing* ⚠️ | **The wound, measuring 4 cm × 3 cm on the right shin, requires daily dressing** ⭐ |
| *DM2 dx 2009, poorly controlled* ⚠️ | **She has type 2 diabetes, diagnosed in 2009 and currently poorly controlled** ⭐ |
| *Lives alone, 1st flr, no lift* ⚠️ | **She lives alone in a first-floor flat with no lift** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⚠️ **Don't** carry case-note shorthand like *c/o, pt, dx, hx, NAD* into the letter. ✅ Write them out in full sentences. ⭐

*(The exception.)* ✅ **Standard clinical terms and units** your reader uses every day are fine: *HbA1c, IV, BP, mg* ✅

*(The principle.)* ⭐ Ask: **would this professional reader use this form in a letter?** ✅ If yes, keep it. If it's note-taking shorthand, expand it. ⭐

### Trap 3: Wrong register 👔

*(A Genre and Style error.)*

*(Explain.)* The reader is a **fellow professional** ✅ — so the tone is **collegial**, not deferential and not casual. ✅

| ⚠️ Too formal/old 📜 | ⚠️ Too casual | ✅ Right |
|---|---|---|
| *I humbly request your kind assistance* ⚠️ | *Can you check her wound?* ⚠️ | **I would be grateful if you could review her wound** ⭐ |
| *Kindly do the needful* 📜 🔗 | *Please sort it out* ⚠️ | **I would appreciate it if you could arrange daily dressing** ⭐ |
| *Awaiting your kind revert* 📜 | *Let me know* ⚠️ | **Please contact me if you require further information** ⭐ |
| *Respected Madam* 📜 | *Hi Priya* ⚠️ | **Dear Ms. Menon** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ 🔗 Day 83's clear-out applies here directly. ✅ *"Do the needful"* in an OET letter costs you marks on **two** criteria — Style and Clarity. ⚠️

### Trap 4: Vague requests ⚠️

*(A Purpose error.)*

| ⚠️ Vague | ✅ Specific |
|---|---|
| *Please take care of the patient* ⚠️ | **Please arrange daily wound dressing for ten days** ⭐ |
| *Kindly follow up* ⚠️ | **Please review her mobility and assess her ability to manage stairs** ⭐ |
| *Please do the necessary* 📜 | **Please provide education on dietary management** ⭐ |

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Name the task. Give the duration. Be specific.** ✅

### Trap 5: Language to fix ⚠️ *(some are errors, some are register)*

*(Practical checklist.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *The patient is having diabetes* 🔗 *(Day 54!)* | **The patient has diabetes** ✅ |
| *She is suffering from since 2009* 🔗 *(Day 19)* | **She has had diabetes since 2009** ✅ |
| *Advices were given* 🔗 *(Day 56)* | **Advice was given** ✅ |
| *She was admitted in 12 March* 🔗 *(Day 18)* | **admitted on 12 March** ✅ |
| *Discharge is on today* | **is being discharged today** ✅ |
| *Informations regarding* 🔗 | **information regarding** ✅ |
| *She is not able to walk properly* | **Her mobility is limited** ⭐ |
| *Please do dressing daily* | **Please carry out daily dressing** ✅ |
| *Patient's condition is good* | **Her condition has improved** ⭐ |
| *Kindly note the same* 📜 | **Please note** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Days 18, 19, 54, and 56 all show up in OET marking. ✅ The foundation work pays here. 🔗

### ⏰ The 45-minute discipline

*(Practical timing.)*

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| **0–5 min** | Read notes, **identify the reader's purpose** 🎯 |
| **5–10 min** | **Select** — tick what's in, cross what's out ✂️ ⭐ |
| **10–38 min** | Write ✍️ |
| **38–45 min** | Check — word count, allergies included, requests specific 🔍 |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Ten minutes before you write a word.** ✅ Selection is where most marks are won or lost; the writing is the delivery. ⭐

### ✅ The OET checklist

| ✅ | Check |
|---|---|
| ⬜ | Purpose clear in the **first sentence** ⭐ |
| ⬜ | **Allergies** included *(whenever your reader may give or advise on medication)* ⚠️ |
| ⬜ | Irrelevant history **omitted** ✂️ ⭐ |
| ⬜ | No case-note **abbreviations** *(c/o, pt, dx)* ✅ |
| ⬜ | Requests are **specific** with duration ⭐ |
| ⬜ | **180–200** words ✅ |
| ⬜ | *Yours sincerely* with name · *faithfully* with Sir/Madam ✅ |
| ⬜ | Signed with **role**, not your name ⚠️ |
| ⬜ | Zero *"do the needful," "kindly," "humbly"* 📜 |
