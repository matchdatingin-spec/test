## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confident, myth-busting.)*

In school, they gave you a **table**: 📋

> *am/is → was · will → would · today → that day · here → there · this → that · come → go...*

*(Flat expression.)* You memorised it for the exam. You scored well. And a week later — gone. 🕳️

*(Point at camera.)*

Meanwhile, in real life, you say:

> ❌ *"He told that he is coming tomorrow."*
> ❌ *"She said me she will call."*
> ❌ *"He asked me where I am going."*

*(Lean in, confident.)*

Here's what nobody explained: **that table isn't a list of rules. It's a list of consequences.** ✅

There's **one** idea underneath all of it. And once you have that idea, you don't need the table — you can **work out** every shift yourself.

Today: one idea, and reported speech becomes logical. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. This idea is the whole video.)*

### 🔑 THE ONE IDEA: the moment has moved

*(Explain with a picture.)*

*(Set the scene.)* Yesterday at 10 a.m., Ravi stood in front of you and said:

> 💬 *"I **am** busy **today**."*

*(Point out.)* When Ravi said it, **"today"** meant **yesterday** *(from where you stand now)*, and **"am"** was true **at that moment**.

*(Now.)* You're reporting it **today**. You've **moved** — in time, and in place. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* So any word that pointed at **Ravi's moment** may need re-pointing at **your moment**. 🔄

> ✅ *"He said he **was** busy **that day**."* ✅

*(The insight.)*

> ### 👉 Reported speech isn't about grammar rules. It's about **re-pointing** words from THEIR moment to YOUR moment.

*(Slogan.)*
> **The speaker's "now" is your "then." So everything steps back one place.** ⏮️
> **അവരുടെ ഇപ്പോൾ = നിങ്ങളുടെ അപ്പോൾ.** ✅

### What steps back? Everything that points ⏮️

*(Now the table makes sense — derive it, don't memorise it.)*

**1. Verbs step back one tense** ⏮️

| Their words | Your report |
|---|---|
| *"I **am** busy."* | he **was** busy |
| *"I **work** here."* | he **worked** there |
| *"I **went** home."* | he **had gone** home |
| *"I **will** call."* | he **would** call |
| *"I **can** help."* | he **could** help |
| *"I **have** finished."* | he **had** finished |

*(Point out the pattern.)* Present → past ✅ · Past → past perfect ✅ · will → would ✅ · can → could ✅

*(Connect to Day 51!)* 🔗 Remember: **past form = distance.** Here it's distance in **time** rather than reality — same mechanism. ✅

**2. Time words step back** ⏮️

| Their words | Your report |
|---|---|
| today | **that day** |
| yesterday | **the day before** |
| tomorrow | **the next day** |
| now | **then** |
| last week | **the week before** |
| next year | **the following year** |

**3. Place and pointing words step back** ⏮️

| Their words | Your report |
|---|---|
| here | **there** |
| this | **that** |
| these | **those** |
| come | **go** *(only if the destination isn't where you are now)* |

*(Careful with the last one!)* ⚠️ *Come/go* follows **where you are**, not a fixed rule: ✅ if he said *"I'll come to your house"* and you're reporting it, ✅ *"He said he'd **come** to my house"* is correct — because it's still **towards** you. ⭐

**4. People change to match YOUR view** 👤

| Their words | Your report |
|---|---|
| *"**I** am busy."* | **he/she** was busy |
| *"**You** are late."* *(said to me)* | **I** was late |
| *"**My** phone."* | **his/her** phone |

*(The real rule.)* ⚠️ Don't memorise *you → I*. ✅ Ask **who was actually being spoken to.** ✅ If Ravi said *"You are late"* to **Anu**, the report is *"He said **she** was late."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* Four categories. And they're all doing the **same** thing — re-pointing from their moment to yours. ✅

### 🎉 And now the great news: often, you DON'T shift!

*(This genuinely simplifies real speech.)*

*(Explain.)* If the thing is **still true**, or you're reporting **very soon after**, native speakers often **keep** the original tense:

- ✅ *He said he **is** coming tomorrow.* ✅ *(he's still coming — it's still tomorrow!)* ⭐
- ✅ *She said she **works** at Infosys.* ✅ *(she still does)* ⭐
- ✅ *He said the meeting **is** at 3.* ✅ *(it still is)*

*(Emphasise.)* **Both versions are correct!** ✅
- *He said he **was** coming.* ✅ *(formal, safe)*
- *He said he **is** coming.* ✅ *(natural, if still true)* ⭐

*(Practical advice.)* In **writing** and **exams**, shift the tense. ✅ In **speech**, don't stress — if it's still true, keeping the present is completely natural. ⭐

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Their words:** 💬 *"I am coming tomorrow."*
**Malayalam:** അവൻ പറഞ്ഞു അവൻ നാളെ വരുമെന്ന്.

*(Notice the Malayalam!)* **-എന്ന്** ✅ — that's Malayalam's reporting marker! It becomes **"that"** in English ✅ 🔗 *(Day 32)*

*(And notice.)* Malayalam says **വരുമെന്ന്** — a **future** form, not shifted back! ✅

*(Point out.)* **Malayalam doesn't shift tenses in reported speech.** ✅ That's exactly why *"He said he will come"* comes out of our mouths automatically — and why **would** needs deliberate practice. ⭐

Build it:

1. **Who reported?** → He
2. **Reporting verb, past** → said
3. **that** *(optional!)* → (that)
4. **Who** → he
5. **will → would** ⏮️ → would
6. **Plain verb** → come
7. **tomorrow → the next day** ⏮️ → the next day

> ✅ **"He said (that) he would come the next day."** ✅

*(And the natural spoken version — if it's still tomorrow!)*
> ✅ **"He said he's coming tomorrow."** ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* Both correct! ✅ The second is what you'd actually say to a colleague. ⭐

*(⚠️ The critical error — said vs told!)* 🔗 *(Day 05)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He told that he would come | **He said that he would come** ✅ |
| He told that... | **He told me that...** ✅ |
| He said me that... | **He said to me** ✅ / **He told me** ✅ |

*(Explain.)* **TELL needs a person immediately.** **SAY doesn't take one.** ✅
- ✅ *He **said** (that) he would come.* ✅ *(no person)*
- ✅ *He **told me** (that) he would come.* ✅ *(person!)*
- ❌ *He **told** that he would come.* ❌ *(told whom?)*

*(Say it.)* **Say the words. Tell the person.** ✅ Same rule as Day 05. 🔗

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece *(reported QUESTIONS)*

**Their words:** 💬 *"Where are you going?"*
**Malayalam:** അവൻ എന്നോട് ചോദിച്ചു ഞാൻ എവിടെ പോകുന്നു എന്ന്.

*(This is where everyone struggles.)*

Build it:

1. **Reporting verb for questions** → asked ✅ *(not "said"!)*
2. **Person** → me
3. **Question word** → where
4. **⚠️ NOW — the word order goes back to NORMAL!** ✅
5. **Person + verb, shifted** → I was going

> ✅ **"He asked me where I was going."** ✅

*(The critical rule — emphasise heavily!)*

> ### 👉 In reported questions, the SWAP disappears. Use normal statement order. ✅

*(Show it.)*

| 💬 Direct question *(swapped)* | ✅ Reported *(normal order)* |
|---|---|
| *"Where **are you** going?"* | *He asked where **I was** going.* |
| *"What **did you** say?"* | *She asked what **I had** said.* |
| *"**Are you** ready?"* | *He asked **if I was** ready.* |
| *"**Can you** help?"* | *She asked **if I could** help.* |

*(Point out the common error.)*
- ❌ *He asked me where **was I** going.* ❌ *(kept the swap!)*
- ✅ **He asked me where I was going.** ✅

*(Connect to Day 35!)* 🔗 This is the **same** pattern as polite questions: *"Could you tell me where the station **is**?"* ✅ After an opener, **no swap**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **One rule, two places.** ✅

*(And yes/no questions need IF or WHETHER!)* ⚠️

*(Explain.)* If the original question had **no question word**, you must add **if** or **whether**:

| 💬 Direct | ✅ Reported |
|---|---|
| *"Are you coming?"* | *He asked **if** I was coming.* ✅ |
| *"Did you eat?"* | *She asked **if** I had eaten.* ✅ |
| *"Do you know him?"* | *He asked **whether** I knew him.* ✅ |

*(Malayali note.)* 🔗 *(Day 41)* ❌ *"He asked me I am coming or not"* → ✅ **"He asked if I was coming."** ✅ No "or not" needed! ✅

---

### The reporting verbs 📋

*(Very useful — Malayalis over-use "said.")*

| Verb | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **said** | said (that)... | *He said he was busy.* |
| **told** | told **+ person** (that)... | *He told me he was busy.* |
| **asked** | asked (person) if/wh-... | *He asked if I was free.* |
| **told (order)** | told + person + **to** + verb | *He told me **to wait**.* ⭐ |
| **asked (request)** | asked + person + **to** + verb | *She asked me **to help**.* ⭐ |
| **advised** | advised + person + to... | *He advised me to apply.* ✅ |
| **suggested** | suggested + **-ing** ⚠️ | *He suggested **going** early.* ✅ |
| **explained** | explained (that)... | *She explained that it was urgent.* ✅ |
| **promised** | promised **to**... | *He promised to call.* ✅ |
| **complained** | complained (that)... | *He complained that it was too loud.* ✅ |

*(Highlight orders and requests!)* ⭐

> ### ✅ Commands and requests → **verb + person + TO + plain verb**

- 💬 *"Wait here."* → ✅ *He told me **to wait** there.* ✅
- 💬 *"Please help me."* → ✅ *She asked me **to help** her.* ✅
- 💬 *"Don't be late."* → ✅ *He told me **not to be** late.* ✅

*(Note the negative!)* ⚠️ **"not to"** ✅ — *He told me **not to** worry.* ✅

*(Highlight "suggested"!)* ⚠️ It takes **-ing** 🔗 *(Day 57)*
- ✅ *He suggested **going** early.* ✅
- ❌ *He suggested to go early.* ❌
- ✅ *He suggested **that we go** early.* ✅ *(also correct)*

*(Emphasise.)* Using varied reporting verbs — *explained, advised, complained, promised* — makes your English sound much more advanced. ⭐ Especially in IELTS writing. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: "told that" without a person

*(The #1 error.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He told that he is busy | **He said (that) he was busy** ✅ |
| She told that she will come | **She told me she would come** ✅ |
| They told that the office is closed | **They said the office was closed** ✅ |

*(The fix — one question.)*

> **Is there a person right after?** ✅ → **told**
> **No person?** ✅ → **said**

*(Practise the pair.)*
- ✅ *He **said** he was late.* ✅
- ✅ *He **told me** he was late.* ✅

### Trap 2: No tense shift *(Malayalam habit)*

| ⚠️ Unshifted | ✅ Shifted — the safe default |
|---|---|
| He said he will come | **He said he would come** ✅ |
| She said she is busy | **She said she was busy** ✅ |
| He said he can help | **He said he could help** ✅ |
| They said they have finished | **They said they had finished** ✅ |

*(Remember the good news from earlier!)* ⭐ The left column isn't always wrong — it's fine when the thing is **still true right now**. ✅ But because Malayalam **never** shifts, your mouth will always pick the left column. ⚠️ So drill the **right** column until *would/was/could/had* come out automatically. ⭐

*(Why it happens.)* Malayalam **-എന്ന്** attaches to the **original** tense: **വരുമെന്ന്** *(will come)*, **തിരക്കാണെന്ന്** *(is busy)* ✅ No shifting. ✅

*(The four essential shifts — memorise these.)*
| Their word | Your report |
|---|---|
| **is/am/are** → | **was/were** ✅ |
| **will** → | **would** ✅ |
| **can** → | **could** ✅ |
| **have** → | **had** ✅ |

*(Say them three times.)* 🗣️

*(But remember the exception!)* ✅ If it's **still true**, keeping the present is natural in speech. ⭐

### Trap 3: Keeping the question swap

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He asked where was I going | **He asked where I was going** ✅ |
| She asked what is my name | **She asked what my name was** ✅ |
| He asked when will I come | **He asked when I would come** ✅ |
| She asked me am I ready | **She asked if I was ready** ✅ |

*(Rule.)* **Reported question = normal statement order.** ✅ No helper at the front. ✅

*(Practise the flip.)*
- 💬 *"What is your name?"* → ✅ *He asked what my name **was**.* ✅
- 💬 *"Where do you live?"* → ✅ *She asked where I **lived**.* ✅
- 💬 *"When did you join?"* → ✅ *He asked when I **had joined**.* ✅

### Trap 4: Forgetting the pronoun change

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He said "I am busy" → He said I was busy ⚠️ | **He said he was busy** ✅ |
| She said "You are late" → She said you were late ⚠️ | **She said I was late** ✅ |
| He said "My phone is dead" → He said my phone... ⚠️ | **He said his phone was dead** ✅ |

*(Explain.)* **"I"** in their mouth becomes **"he/she"** in yours ✅ · **"you"** becomes **"I"** ✅ · **"my"** becomes **"his/her"** ✅

*(Test it — a fun one!)* Ravi says to you: 💬 *"I will call you tomorrow."*

You report it: ✅ *"He said **he** would call **me** the next day."* ✅

*(Point out.)* **Three** changes: *I → he* ✅ · *will → would* ✅ · *you → me* ✅ · *tomorrow → the next day* ✅ — four, actually! ✅

### The realistic advice for speaking 😌

*(An honest, freeing conclusion.)*

*(Direct.)* Here's how natives actually report speech in casual conversation:

- ✅ *"He said he's coming tomorrow."* ⭐ *(no shift — still true)*
- ✅ *"She was like, 'I can't make it.'"* ⭐ *(very casual — direct quote!)*
- ✅ *"He told me to wait."* ⭐ *(the "to" pattern — very common)*

*(Emphasise.)* **The full shifting system matters most in WRITING.** ✅ In speech, natives are relaxed. ⭐

*(The priority list.)*

| Situation | How careful? |
|---|---|
| IELTS writing ⭐ | very — shift everything ✅ |
| Formal email | shift properly ✅ |
| Work conversation | shift if it's clearly past ✅ |
| Casual chat | relax — keep the present if still true ✅ |

*(Firmly.)* But **always** fix these two, everywhere:
1. ✅ **"told me"** not "told that" ✅
2. ✅ **normal word order** in reported questions ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Those two are noticed. The tense shifts are forgiven. ✅
