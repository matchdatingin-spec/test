# Day 42 — 15 Polite English Phrases for Requests, Disagreement, and Saying No

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Malayalam hides politeness in the verb tail. English puts it in **added words**. Here are the exact words.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Warm, slightly serious.)*

Your manager asks you to work on Saturday. You genuinely can't — it's your sister's wedding. And you say:

> ❌ *"No sir, I can't come. I have a function."*

*(Pause.)* You were **honest**. You were **correct**. And somehow... it landed **badly**. The manager's face changed. 😐

*(Point at camera.)*

Here's what happened. In Malayalam, you'd say **"സാർ, ശനിയാഴ്ച ഒരു ചടങ്ങുണ്ട്, വരാൻ പറ്റുമോ എന്നറിയില്ല..."** — and all the softness lives in the **verb endings** and your **tone**. 🎵

But English has **no verb tails**. So when you translate, **all the softness disappears** — and what's left is a bare, hard "no." ❌

*(Lean in.)*

The natural version:

> ✅ *"I'd really like to help, but Saturday is my sister's wedding. Would Sunday work instead?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* **Same answer. Same honesty. Completely different feeling.** ✅

Today: **15 phrases** that put the politeness back. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. This principle explains everything.)*

### Where politeness LIVES in each language 🏠

*(This is the key structural insight.)*

**Malayalam** puts politeness in **three places**:
1. **Verb endings** → തരാമോ, ചെയ്യാമോ, വരൂ
2. **Respect words** → സാർ, ചേട്ടൻ, ചേച്ചി, താങ്കൾ
3. **Tone and face** → the softening happens in your voice 🎵

**English** puts politeness in **three completely different places**:
1. **Extra words at the FRONT** → *Could you...*, *Would you mind...*
2. **Softening words in the MIDDLE** → *maybe, a bit, actually, might*
3. **A reason or alternative at the END** → *...because...*, *...but would X work?*

*(Emphasise.)*

> ### 👉 Malayalam softens with ENDINGS. English softens with EXTRA WORDS.

*(Explain.)* So being polite in English costs you **more words**, not different words. If you translate word-for-word, you naturally produce the **shortest** version — which sounds like an **order**. ⚡

*(Reassuring.)* You are **not** rude. You just have **nowhere to put your politeness**. Today we give you the places. 🎁

### The 3-part politeness formula 🔑

*(Write it big — this works for everything.)*

> **1. SOFTEN the opening** → *"Would it be possible to..."*
> **2. GIVE a reason** → *"...because I have a class that day"*
> **3. OFFER an alternative** → *"...but would Friday work?"*

*(Point out.)* Part **3** is the one Malayalis skip most — and it's the **most powerful**. ⭐

*(Explain why.)* Because an alternative changes the whole message. Without it, you're **blocking**. With it, you're **cooperating**:
- ❌ *"I can't come Saturday."* → blocking 🚫
- ✅ *"I can't come Saturday, but I could do Sunday."* → cooperating 🤝

*(Emphasise.)* **Same "no." Completely different relationship.** ✅

### The politeness ladder 🪜

*(Practical reference.)*

| Level | Phrase | Use with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — direct | *Send me the file.* | very close friends |
| 2 — normal | *Can you send me the file?* | colleagues, friends |
| 3 — polite ⭐ | *Could you send me the file?* | **anyone, always safe** |
| 4 — very polite | *Would you mind sending me the file?* | seniors, strangers |
| 5 — formal | *Would it be possible to send me the file?* | clients, officials |

*(Advice.)* **Live at Level 3.** ⭐ *"Could you...?"* works with your boss, your colleague, a shopkeeper, and a stranger. One phrase, universal. ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece *(saying NO)*

**The situation:** Your manager asks you to work Saturday. You can't.

**Malayalam:** സാർ, ശനിയാഴ്ച എന്റെ സഹോദരിയുടെ കല്യാണമാണ്, അതുകൊണ്ട് വരാൻ പറ്റില്ല.

*(Notice.)* The Malayalam already has a **reason** *(കല്യാണമാണ്)* and a soft ending *(പറ്റില്ല)*. But when translated bluntly, only the **refusal** survives. ❌

Let's build it with all three parts.

**Part 1 — SOFTEN the opening** *(show willingness first!)*

*(This is the crucial move.)* Before saying no, show that you **want** to say yes:

> ✅ *"I'd really like to help..."*
> ✅ *"I wish I could..."*
> ✅ *"Normally I would, but..."*

*(Explain.)* This tells the listener *"the problem is the situation, not you."* ✅ It protects the relationship. 🤝

**Part 2 — GIVE the reason** *(short and specific)*

> ✅ *"...but Saturday is my sister's wedding."*

*(Note.)* **Short.** ✅ One clause. Long explanations sound like excuses. 🚫

**Part 3 — OFFER an alternative**

> ✅ *"Would Sunday work instead?"* ⭐
> ✅ *"I could come in early on Monday if that helps."* ⭐

**Put it together:**

> ✅ **"I'd really like to help, but Saturday is my sister's wedding. Would Sunday work instead?"** ⭐

*(Compare.)*
- ❌ *"No sir, I can't come."* → 6 words, feels like a wall 🧱
- ⭐ *The full version* → 20 words, feels like teamwork 🤝

*(Emphasise.)* **Fourteen extra words saved the relationship.** That's the whole lesson. ✅

*(The 5 ways to say NO politely — memorise these!)*

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| **"I'd love to, but..."** ⭐ | social invitations |
| **"I wish I could, but..."** | genuine regret |
| **"Unfortunately, I won't be able to..."** | formal/work |
| **"That's a bit difficult for me because..."** ⭐ | soft refusal |
| **"Can I get back to you on that?"** ⭐ | when you need time! |

*(Highlight the last one.)* **"Can I get back to you on that?"** ⭐ This is your **escape hatch**. When you're put on the spot and don't want to say yes — buy time. Then refuse later, by message, calmly. ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece *(DISAGREEING)*

**The situation:** In a meeting, your colleague suggests something you think won't work.

**Malayalam:** അത് ശരിയാകില്ല എന്ന് എനിക്ക് തോന്നുന്നു.

Your brain builds: ❌ *"That is wrong. It will not work."*

*(Wince.)* In a meeting, that lands like a **slap**. 😬 Even though your Malayalam was gentle!

Let's build it properly. English disagreement has **three moves**:

**Move 1 — ACKNOWLEDGE their point first** ✅

> ✅ *"I see what you mean..."*
> ✅ *"That's a fair point..."*
> ✅ *"I understand where you're coming from..."* ⭐

*(Explain.)* This is **not** dishonesty. You're saying *"I heard you"* — which is different from *"I agree."* ✅

**Move 2 — SOFTEN your disagreement**

> ✅ *"...but I'm not sure it'll work in this case."*
> ✅ *"...though I see it a bit differently."*

*(Note the hedges.)* **"not sure," "a bit," "might," "perhaps"** — these are your softening tools. 🛡️

**Move 3 — GIVE your reason or alternative**

> ✅ *"...because we only have two weeks. What if we tried a smaller version first?"* ⭐

**Put it together:**

> ✅ **"I see what you mean, but I'm not sure it'll work in this case — we only have two weeks. What if we tried a smaller version first?"** ⭐

*(Point out.)* You **completely** disagreed. And nobody felt attacked. ✅

*(The 5 ways to disagree politely.)*

| Phrase | Strength |
|---|---|
| **"I'm not sure I agree."** ⭐ | soft |
| **"I see your point, but..."** ⭐ | soft, professional |
| **"I'd look at it slightly differently."** | very soft |
| **"That could work, but have we considered...?"** ⭐ | collaborative |
| **"I have to disagree there."** | firm but acceptable |

*(Highlight #4.)* **"Have we considered...?"** ⭐ — this is brilliant meeting English. It disagrees by **adding an idea** instead of rejecting one. Managers love it. 💼

### 🔥 THE 15 ESSENTIAL POLITENESS PHRASES

*(Grouped. Say each twice.)*

#### 🙏 Making requests (1–5)

| # | ✅ Phrase | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Could you...?"** ⭐ | universal, use daily |
| 2 | **"Would you mind + -ing?"** | very polite *(note the -ing!)* |
| 3 | **"Would it be possible to...?"** | formal/email |
| 4 | **"When you get a chance, could you...?"** ⭐ | considerate |
| 5 | **"Sorry to bother you, but..."** ⭐ | perfect opener |

*(Warning on #2.)* ✅ *Would you mind **waiting**?* ✅ Not "to wait"! 🔗 *(Day 07)*

*(And the funny reply trap.)* When someone says *"Would you mind waiting?"* — the agreeing answer is **"Not at all!"** ✅ *(because you don't mind)*. Saying *"Yes"* means *"yes, I do mind"*! 😄

#### 🚫 Saying no (6–10)

| # | ✅ Phrase |
|---|---|
| 6 | **"I'd love to, but..."** ⭐ |
| 7 | **"Unfortunately, I won't be able to..."** |
| 8 | **"Can I get back to you on that?"** ⭐ |
| 9 | **"That's a bit difficult for me right now."** |
| 10 | **"I'm afraid I can't."** ⭐ |

*(Note on #10.)* **"I'm afraid..."** ⭐ has nothing to do with fear! It's a **softener** for bad news:
- ✅ *I'm afraid he's not in right now.* ✅
- ✅ *I'm afraid we can't do that.* ✅

#### 💬 Disagreeing (11–15)

| # | ✅ Phrase |
|---|---|
| 11 | **"I see your point, but..."** ⭐ |
| 12 | **"I'm not sure I agree."** ⭐ |
| 13 | **"Have we considered...?"** ⭐ |
| 14 | **"Actually, I think..."** |
| 15 | **"That's true, but on the other hand..."** |

*(Note on #14.)* **"Actually"** is a gentle correction word:
- ✅ *"Actually, the meeting is at 3, not 4."* ⭐ — corrects without embarrassing anyone ✅

### Bonus: the 4 magic softener words 🪄

*(These four words can be dropped into any sentence.)*

| Word | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **maybe / perhaps** | reduces certainty | *Maybe we could try...* |
| **a bit / slightly** | reduces force | *That's a bit difficult.* |
| **just** | reduces size | *I just wanted to ask...* ⭐ |
| **actually** | softens correction | *Actually, I think...* |

*(Demo the transformation.)*
- ❌ *"This is wrong."*
- ✅ *"**Maybe** this is **a bit** off?"* → same message, zero offence ✅

*(Highlight "just.")* **"I just wanted to ask..."** ⭐ — the word *just* makes your request feel **small and easy to grant**. Very powerful. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: Saying YES when you mean NO

*(The deepest cultural trap. Handle with care.)*

*(Observation.)* In Malayalam culture, a direct **"ഇല്ല"** to a senior can feel disrespectful. So we soften it into vagueness: **"നോക്കാം," "ശ്രമിക്കാം," "പറ്റുമെങ്കിൽ."** ✅ And everyone understands that this **means no**. 🙏

*(The problem.)* Translate that into English and you get:
- ❌ *"I will try."*
- ❌ *"Let me see."*
- ❌ *"I will manage somehow."*

*(Point out the danger.)* An English speaker hears these as **YES**! 😱 They will plan around you. And when you don't come, **you** look unreliable — even though you said no in your own language.

*(The fix.)* In English, **"no" must contain a clear negative word**:
- ✅ *"I **won't** be able to."* ⭐
- ✅ *"I **can't** make it."* ⭐
- ✅ *"That **won't** work for me, unfortunately."* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* Then **soften around it** — but the negative word must be **there**. 🎯

*(Rule.)* **Soft wrapping, clear content.** 🎁 Not vague content.

### Trap 2: Over-apologising

*(The opposite problem.)*

| ❌ Too much | ✅ Right amount |
|---|---|
| *Sorry sorry, very sorry, I am extremely sorry for the inconvenience caused...* | **"Sorry about that — I'll fix it right away."** ⭐ |
| *I humbly apologise for my mistake and request your kind forgiveness* | **"That was my mistake. I'll sort it out."** ⭐ |

*(Explain.)* In English, **excessive apology reduces your authority.** It signals *"I am beneath you."* 😔 In a workplace, that has real career costs. 💼

*(The professional formula.)*

> ### 👉 Apologise ONCE + state the fix. Then stop.

- ✅ *"Sorry, that was my mistake. I'll have it corrected by evening."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* **One sorry. One solution.** That's what confidence sounds like. ✅

### Trap 3: Missing the alternative

*(The high-value fix.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayalam refusals often stop at the reason: **"എനിക്ക് ക്ലാസ്സുണ്ട്."** ✅ And the listener is expected to figure out the rest.

English expects **you** to solve it:

| ❌ Incomplete | ✅ Complete |
|---|---|
| *I can't come Friday.* | *I can't come Friday, **but I'm free Saturday**.* ⭐ |
| *I don't have time today.* | *I can't do it today, **but I'll finish it first thing tomorrow**.* ⭐ |
| *I don't know that software.* | *I haven't used that software, **but I can learn it this week**.* ⭐ |

*(Point out.)* Look at that third one — it's an **interview answer**! ⭐ Turning a "no" into a "not yet, but here's my plan" is one of the most valuable career skills in English. 💼

### Trap 4: Using "please" instead of a soft structure

*(A small but useful correction.)*

*(Observation.)* Malayalis often add **"please"** to a command and think it's now polite:

- ❌ *"Please send the file."* ← still an order, just with please 🚫
- ✅ *"Could you send the file?"* ⭐ ← genuinely polite ✅

*(Explain.)* **"Please" doesn't change the structure — it just decorates it.** Real politeness in English comes from turning the **order into a question**. ❓

*(The rule.)*

> ### 👉 Politeness = turn the command into a QUESTION.

- ❌ *Come here, please.* → ✅ **Could you come here for a second?** ✅
- ❌ *Wait, please.* → ✅ **Could you wait a moment?** ✅
- ❌ *Explain, please.* → ✅ **Could you explain that again?** ✅

*(Note.)* **"Please" is still good** — add it to the question for extra warmth! ✅ *"Could you send the file, please?"* ⭐ Best of both. ✅
