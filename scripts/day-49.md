# Day 49 — Will vs Going To: Which Future Form Should You Use?

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *Will* = decided **right now**. *Going to* = decided **before**. And *-ing* = already **arranged**.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Quick, curious.)*

Your phone rings. It's on the table. You say:

> ✅ *"I'll get it!"* 📞

Now — could you say *"I'm going to get it"*? 🤔

*(Shake head.)* Technically yes... but it would sound odd, because you decided **this second**. And English has a natural form for decisions made in the moment: **will**. ✅

Now the opposite. Your friend asks about your evening:

> ⚠️ *"I will watch a movie tonight."* *(correct — but it doesn't sound like a plan you already had)*
> ✅ *"I'm going to watch a movie tonight."* ✅

*(Point at camera.)*

Why? Because you'd **already planned** it. The plan existed **before** the question. ✅

*(Lean in.)*

In Malayalam, all of this is just **ചെയ്യും**. One form for every future. So how do you choose in English?

**One question: When did you decide?** ⏰ Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:30)

*(Slow. This one question does everything.)*

### 🔑 THE QUESTION: When did you decide?

*(Write it big.)*

> **Deciding RIGHT NOW, as I speak** → **WILL** ⚡
> **Decided BEFORE this conversation** → **GOING TO** 📋
> **Already ARRANGED / booked** → **-ING** 📅

*(Test it.)*

- Phone rings → *"I'**ll** get it!"* ← decided this second ⚡ ✅
- Friend asks your evening plan → *"I'**m going to** watch a movie."* ← planned earlier 📋 ✅
- Ticket booked → *"I'**m flying** to Delhi tomorrow."* ← arranged 📅 ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **Same future. Three forms. The difference is WHEN the decision happened.** ✅

### ⚡ WILL — the 4 jobs

**Job 1: Instant decisions** ⚡
- ✅ *I'll have a coffee.* *(ordering — deciding now)*
- ✅ *I'll help you.*
- ✅ *I'll take this one.*

**Job 2: Promises** 🤝
- ✅ *I'll call you tonight.*
- ✅ *I'll finish it by Friday.*

**Job 3: Predictions based on opinion** 💭
- ✅ *I think it'll rain.*
- ✅ *He'll be late, I'm sure.*

**Job 4: Offers** 🎁
- ✅ *I'll carry that for you.*
- ✅ *Shall I help?* / *I'll do it.*

### 📋 GOING TO — the 2 jobs

**Job 1: Plans and intentions decided earlier** 📋
- ✅ *I'm going to start a business.*
- ✅ *We're going to buy a house next year.*

**Job 2: Predictions based on EVIDENCE you can see** 👁️
- ✅ *Look at those clouds — it's going to rain!* ☁️ *(evidence!)*
- ✅ *He's driving too fast. He's going to crash!* 🚗

*(Point out the difference — this is beautiful.)*
- ✅ *I think it **will** rain.* → my **opinion** 💭
- ✅ *Look, it's **going to** rain.* → I can **see** the evidence 👁️

*(Emphasise.)* **Will = from my head. Going to = from my eyes.** ✅

### 📅 -ING — one job

**Fixed arrangements** *(booked, scheduled, in the diary)* 📅
- ✅ *I'm meeting the client at 4.*
- ✅ *We're getting married in December.* 💍
- ✅ *I'm flying to Delhi on Monday.* ✈️

*(Slogan.)*
> **Will = now ⚡. Going to = before 📋. -ing = booked 📅.**

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:30 – 10:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Situation:** Your colleague's hands are full and she drops a file. You react.

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ എടുക്കാം. / ഞാൻ സഹായിക്കാം.

*(Notice the Malayalam!)* **-ആം** ending *(എടുക്കാം, സഹായിക്കാം)* — that's Malayalam's **offer/instant-decision** form! ✅

*(Excited.)* So Malayalam **does** mark this! **-ആം = will** ⚡ ✅

Build it:

1. **Decided when?** → this second ⚡ → **will**
2. **Who** → I
3. **will** → will *('ll)*
4. **Plain verb** → get / help
5. **What** → it

> ✅ **"I'll get it."** / **"I'll help you."**

*(Very important — the contraction!)* 🔊

*(Emphasise.)* Native speakers **almost never** say the full *"I will."* They say **"I'll."** ⭐

- ✅ *I'**ll** get it.* · *He'**ll** come.* · *We'**ll** see.* · *They'**ll** call.* · *You'**ll** like it.*

*(Explain when to use full "will".)* The full form is for **emphasis** or **insistence**:
- ✅ *"I **will** finish it, I promise!"* ← strong ✅
- ✅ *"I **will** not accept that."* ← firm ✅

*(Practise the contractions.)* **I'll · you'll · he'll · she'll · we'll · they'll · it'll** ✅ Say each three times. 🗣️

*(The -ആം family — all the offers!)*
| Malayalam | ✅ English |
|---|---|
| ഞാൻ ചെയ്യാം | **I'll do it.** |
| ഞാൻ വിളിക്കാം | **I'll call you.** |
| ഞാൻ നോക്കാം | **I'll check.** ⭐ |
| ഞാൻ അയക്കാം | **I'll send it.** |
| നമുക്ക് പോകാം | **Let's go.** ⭐ *(different — this is "let's"!)* |

*(Highlight the last one.)* ⚠️ **നമുക്ക് ...ആം** = **"Let's..."** ✅ Not "we will"!
- ✅ *നമുക്ക് പോകാം* → **"Let's go."** ✅
- ⚠️ *"We will go."* ⚠️ *(that's a plan, not a suggestion)*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Situation:** Your friend asks about your career plans.

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ അടുത്ത വർഷം ഒരു business തുടങ്ങാൻ പോകുന്നു.

*(Find the clue!)* **തുടങ്ങാൻ പോകുന്നു** — literally *"going to start"*! ✅

*(Excited.)* Malayalam has this **exact** structure! **-ആൻ പോകുന്നു = going to** 📋 ✅

Build it:

1. **Decided when?** → earlier, it's a plan 📋 → **going to**
2. **Who** → I
3. **am going to** → am going to
4. **Plain verb** → start
5. **What** → a business
6. **When** → next year

> ✅ **"I'm going to start a business next year."**

*(The spoken form!)* 🔊 In relaxed speech, *"going to"* usually squeezes:
- ✅ *"I'm **gonna** start a business."* ⭐ *(Day 43!)* 🔗

*(Reminder.)* **Say "gonna." Write "going to."** ✅ Never write *gonna* in an email! 📧

*(More plan examples.)*
- ✅ *I'm going to look for a new job.*
- ✅ *We're going to move to Bangalore.*
- ✅ *She's going to study abroad.*
- ✅ *I'm not going to accept that offer.*

---

### The three forms side by side 📊

*(This comparison makes everything click.)*

**Same future event, three different feelings:**

| Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *I'**ll** meet him.* ⚡ | I just decided, this second |
| *I'**m going to** meet him.* 📋 | I planned this earlier |
| *I'**m meeting** him at 4.* 📅 | It's arranged — time fixed, he knows |

*(Point out.)* Notice the **certainty increases** left to right:
- **will** → a decision ⚡
- **going to** → an intention 📋
- **-ing** → an arrangement 📅 *(most certain!)*

*(Practical advice for real life.)*
- **Work meetings, appointments** → use **-ing** ✅ *"I'm meeting the client at 4."* ⭐
- **Future plans, ambitions** → use **going to** ✅
- **Offers, promises, instant replies** → use **will** ✅

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Form & why |
|---|---|---|
| Waiter takes your order | **I'll have the chicken biryani.** | deciding now ⚡ |
| Friend asks weekend plans | **I'm going to visit my parents.** | planned 📋 |
| Someone asks about Monday | **I'm meeting my manager at 10.** | arranged 📅 |
| Dark clouds appear | **It's going to rain.** | evidence 👁️ |
| Promising your boss | **I'll send it by evening.** | promise 🤝 |

*(Highlight row 1.)* **"I'll have..."** ⭐ — the standard way to order food in English. Not ❌ *"I want..."* *(too blunt)* and not ❌ *"Give me..."* ✅

*(Practise.)* *"I'll have a masala dosa and a tea, please."* ✅ Say it three times. ☕

*(Highlight row 5.)* **"I'll send it by evening."** ⭐ Perfect work English. **Will** for promises makes you sound reliable. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (10:00 – 12:30)

### Trap 1: "Will" for everything

*(The main trap.)*

Because **ചെയ്യും** is one form, Malayalis default to *will*:

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I will go to Kochi tomorrow, ticket is booked | **I'm going to Kochi tomorrow.** 📅 |
| I will meet the client at 4 *(fixed)* | **I'm meeting the client at 4.** 📅 |
| Next year I will buy a house *(planned)* | **I'm going to buy a house next year.** 📋 |
| Look, it will rain! *(clouds visible)* | **Look, it's going to rain!** 👁️ |

*(Explain the impression.)* Using *will* for a **booked** plan sounds oddly **uncertain** — like you haven't really committed. 🤔

*(Emphasise.)* Say ✅ *"I'm meeting the client at 4"* ✅ and you sound **organised**. Say ⚠️ *"I will meet the client at 4"* ⚠️ and it sounds like you **just** thought of it. ✅

### Trap 2: "Will" after time words

*(A structural error — Day 26!)* 🔗

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I'll call you when I **will** get there | **I'll call you when I get there.** |
| Wait until he **will** come | **Wait until he comes.** |
| After I **will** finish, I'll leave | **After I finish, I'll leave.** |
| As soon as she **will** arrive | **As soon as she arrives** |
| If it **will** rain, we'll cancel | **If it rains, we'll cancel.** |

*(Rule — say it firmly.)*

> ### 👉 After when / until / after / before / as soon as / if → use PRESENT tense.

*(Explain.)* The time word **already** signals the future. Adding *will* is doubling — and English hates doubling. 🔗 *(Same principle as one-past-per-sentence!)*

*(Practise these five.)*
1. *I'll tell you when I **know**.* ✅
2. *Call me as soon as you **land**.* ✅
3. *Wait until I **come** back.* ✅
4. *If it **rains**, we'll stay home.* ✅
5. *I'll leave after I **finish**.* ✅

### Trap 3: "I will come now" 🔗

*(The Day 29 classic — worth repeating.)*

- ⚠️ *"I will come now."* → sounds like you're **arriving** 🤔
- ✅ **"I'll be right back."** ⭐ = ഞാൻ ഇപ്പോൾ വരാം *(leaving briefly)*
- ✅ **"I'm coming!"** ⭐ = ഞാൻ വരുന്നു *(on my way to you)*

*(And a third.)*
- ✅ **"I'm on my way."** ⭐ = travelling towards you right now ✅

*(Practise all three.)* They're completely different situations. ✅

### Trap 4: "Shall" — is it wrong?

*(A fair question — Malayalis learned this in school.)*

*(Explain honestly.)* **Shall** is not wrong, but modern English uses it in only **two** places:

**Use 1: Offers and suggestions** *(with I / we)* ✅
- ✅ *Shall I help you?* ⭐ *(very natural!)*
- ✅ *Shall we go?* ⭐
- ✅ *Shall I open the window?* ✅

**Use 2: Formal/legal writing** 📜
- ✅ *The tenant shall pay by the 5th of each month.* ✅

*(But.)* ⚠️ *"I shall go to Kochi tomorrow"* — very rare in everyday speech now, and it can sound like a 1940s novel. 📚 Say ✅ **"I'll go"** or **"I'm going."** ✅ *(Some British speakers do still use it — it isn't wrong, just formal.)*

*(Emphasise.)* Learn **"Shall I...?"** and **"Shall we...?"** ⭐ — those two are genuinely useful and polite. Drop the rest. ✅

### Bonus: "about to" — the immediate future ⏰

*(A useful extra.)*

**"about to"** = happening in the next few **seconds or minutes** ⚡

- ✅ *I'm about to leave.* ⭐ = leaving right now
- ✅ *The movie is about to start.* 🎬
- ✅ *I was about to call you!* ⭐ *(what a coincidence!)*

*(Point out.)* That last one is such a natural phrase: ✅ *"I was just about to call you!"* ⭐ Perfect when someone calls you first. 📞

*(Malayalam.)* **-ആൻ പോകുന്നു *(ഉടനെ)*** → *about to* ✅

## 📝 5. HOMEWORK TEST (12:30 – End)

*(Energetic close.)*

**Question 1 — translate these three.** Same Malayalam future, three different English forms:
1. **ഞാൻ നോക്കാം.** *(you're offering, right now)*
2. **ഞാൻ അടുത്ത മാസം ബൈക്ക് വാങ്ങാൻ പോകുന്നു.** *(a plan)*
3. **ഞാൻ നാളെ 10 മണിക്ക് doctor-നെ കാണുന്നു.** *(appointment booked!)*

1. The phone's ringing! — *"I ___ get it."*
2. Look at those clouds — *"It ___ rain."*
3. I ___ meet my manager at 3. *(it's scheduled)*
4. I'll call you when I ___ home. *(get / will get)*
5. ___ I help you with that? *(Will / Shall)*

*(Warm close.)*

*(Lean in.)*

And the one habit to build this week:

> ### 🎯 Practise the contraction: **"I'll"** — not "I will."

*(Say why.)* Because *"I will"* said fully, every time, is one of the clearest markers of a learner. And **"I'll"** is one of the easiest fixes in all of English pronunciation. 🔊

*(Practise ten.)* **I'll go. I'll check. I'll call you. I'll send it. I'll be there. I'll help. I'll try. I'll let you know. I'll get back to you. I'll see.** ✅
