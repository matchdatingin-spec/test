# Day 87 — Office Meeting English: Present, Interrupt, and Disagree Politely

**Target length:** 15–17 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** How to enter a conversation that has no gaps, disagree with someone senior, and admit you don't know — without losing credibility.

**📌 Format note:** Closes the **career block**. Section 3 groups phrases by meeting function. Section 4 addresses the specific reasons Malayali professionals stay silent in meetings.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Recognisable, slightly painful.)*

You're in a meeting. Someone proposes something you know **won't work** — you've seen it fail before. ✅

*(Explain.)* You have the point ready. You're waiting for a gap. ⏳

*(Pause.)* And the conversation moves on. ⏳ And then it moves on again. ⏳

*(Firmly.)* Forty minutes later, the meeting ends. ✅ You never spoke. ✅ And the decision was made without the one piece of information that mattered. ⚠️

*(Point at camera.)*

*(Explain.)* Here's the thing — **you weren't waiting for a gap. You were waiting for permission.** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* And in an English-speaking meeting, permission never arrives. ✅ You have to **take** the turn. ⭐

*(Lean in, warm.)*

*(Explain.)* But taking a turn feels **rude** when you were raised to wait for elders to finish. ✅ That's genuine, and it's not a flaw. ✅

*(Confident.)* So today: the **exact phrases** that let you interrupt politely, disagree safely, and say *"I don't know"* professionally. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:30)

*(Explain the cultural mechanics — this is the real content.)*

### 🗣️ Why meetings are harder than one-to-one conversation

*(Explain.)*

*(Explain.)* Three reasons, and only one of them is about English: ✅

| Reason | Type |
|---|---|
| 1️⃣ **No gaps** — people talk continuously | structural ✅ |
| 2️⃣ **Multiple accents** at speed | listening 👂 |
| 3️⃣ **You have to claim the turn yourself** | cultural ⚠️ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Number 3 is the real barrier. ✅ Your English is good enough. **Turn-taking** is the skill you're missing. ✅

### 🔑 The turn-taking truth

*(The central insight.)*

*(Explain.)* In English-speaking meetings, people **do not** leave gaps for others. ✅ Silence of more than about two seconds feels awkward, so someone always fills it immediately. ✅

*(Firmly.)*

> ### 👉 Waiting for a polite gap means **never speaking.** ⚠️
> ### You must **enter** the conversation, using a signal phrase. ✅

*(Explain.)* And here's the reassuring part: **English has built-in phrases for exactly this.** ✅ Interrupting with a signal phrase is **not** rude — it's **expected**. ⭐

*(The signal phrases.)*
- ✅ *"Can I just jump in here?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Sorry to interrupt —"* ✅
- ✅ *"Can I add something to that?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ These exist **because** interrupting is normal in English meetings. ✅ Using them makes you look **engaged**, not impolite. ⭐

*(Slogan.)*
> **Don't wait for a gap. Signal and enter.** 🚪
> **വിടവ് കാത്തിരിക്കരുത് — സൂചന നൽകി കയറുക.** ⭐

### 🎯 The 3-part meeting contribution

*(A structure that works every time.)*

> ### **1. Signal** — claim the turn ✅
> ### **2. Point** — one clear sentence ⭐
> ### **3. Hand back** — return the floor ✅

*(Show it.)*

> ✅ *"Can I just jump in here?"* **(signal)** *"I think we might hit a problem with the timeline — the audit is in the same week."* **(point)** *"But maybe I'm missing something on the scheduling side?"* **(hand back)** ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Part 3 is what makes it feel **collaborative** rather than confrontational. ✅ It invites a response instead of demanding agreement. ⭐

*(The hand-back phrases.)*
- ✅ *"...but I might be missing something."* ⭐
- ✅ *"...does that make sense?"* ✅
- ✅ *"...what do you think?"* ✅
- ✅ *"...happy to be corrected on that."* ⭐

## 🔍 3. THE PHRASES BY FUNCTION (4:30 – 13:00)

---

### 1️⃣ 🚪 INTERRUPTING / entering the conversation

*(The most important set.)*

| Phrase | Feel |
|---|---|
| **"Can I just jump in here?"** ⭐ | natural, confident ✅ |
| **"Sorry to interrupt, but —"** ✅ | polite, safe ✅ |
| **"Can I add something to that?"** ⭐ | collaborative ✅ |
| **"Just quickly on that point —"** ⭐ | signals you'll be brief ✅ |
| **"If I could come in on that —"** ✅ | slightly formal ✅ |
| **"Before we move on, can I check one thing?"** ⭐ | stops the agenda moving ✅ |

*(Highlight the last one!)* ⭐ **"Before we move on..."** ✅ is genuinely powerful — it's how you stop a decision being made too fast. ✅

*(Practise the top three.)* 🗣️ **"Can I just jump in here?"** · **"Sorry to interrupt —"** · **"Can I add something?"** ✅

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *"Excuse me sir, may I say something please?"* ⚠️ *(too much permission-seeking)*
- ❌ *"Sorry sorry, one small thing sir"* ⚠️ 🔗 *(Day 82's shrinking!)*

---

### 2️⃣ 💬 GIVING YOUR OPINION

| Phrase | Strength |
|---|---|
| **"I think —"** ✅ | neutral, safe ✅ |
| **"From my side, —"** ⭐ | very useful for team context ✅ |
| **"In my experience, —"** ⭐ | adds authority ✅ |
| **"The way I see it, —"** ✅ | confident ✅ |
| **"I'd suggest —"** ⭐ | proposing action ✅ |
| **"What if we —"** ⭐ | collaborative proposal ✅ |

*(Highlight #3!)* ⭐ **"In my experience..."** ✅ — this phrase carries genuine weight, and it's completely honest. ✅ Use it when you've actually seen something before. ⭐

*(Highlight #6!)* ⭐ **"What if we tried...?"** ✅ — proposes an idea **without** claiming it's the answer. ✅ Very safe in front of seniors. ⭐

---

### 3️⃣ ⚠️ DISAGREEING — especially with someone senior

*(The highest-stakes function. Uses Day 42's structure.)* 🔗

*(The 3-move structure.)*

> ### **Acknowledge** → **Soften** → **Give your reason** ✅

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| **"I see your point, but —"** ⭐ | standard, safe ✅ |
| **"That makes sense. My only concern is —"** ⭐ | very safe with seniors ✅ |
| **"I might be looking at it differently —"** ✅ | softest ✅ |
| **"Have we considered —?"** ⭐ | disagrees by **adding**, not rejecting ⭐ |
| **"That could work. The risk I see is —"** ⭐ | balanced ✅ |
| **"I'm not sure I agree, and here's why —"** ✅ | firmer, still fine ✅ |

*(Highlight #2 and #4!)* ⭐ These two are the safest ways to disagree with a manager: ✅

- ✅ *"That makes sense. My only concern is that the audit is in the same week."* ⭐
- ✅ *"Have we considered doing the migration after month-end instead?"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice — neither one says *"you're wrong."* ✅ They add **information** and let the senior person reach the conclusion themselves. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* That's not weakness. ✅ That's how professionals disagree upwards. ⭐

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *"No, that is wrong."* ⚠️
- ❌ *"That won't work."* ⚠️ *(too blunt without a reason)*
- ❌ *"Sir, actually you are mistaken."* ⚠️

---

### 4️⃣ 👂 WHEN YOU DIDN'T CATCH IT

*(Everyone needs this. Nobody teaches it.)*

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| **"Sorry, I didn't catch that."** ⭐ | didn't hear ✅ |
| **"Could you say that again?"** ✅ | simple ✅ |
| **"Sorry, could you repeat the last part?"** ⭐ | specific — better ✅ |
| **"Just to make sure I understood — you're saying ___?"** ⭐ | checks meaning ⭐ |
| **"Sorry, what was the figure again?"** ✅ | for numbers ✅ |
| **"Could you spell that?"** ✅ | for names ✅ |

*(Emphasise #4!)* ⭐ **"Just to make sure I understood — you're saying ___?"** ✅

*(Explain why it's brilliant.)* Three reasons: ✅
1. It **confirms** you understood ✅
2. It shows you were **listening carefully** ⭐
3. It's what **senior** people do — checking understanding is a leadership habit ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Asking for clarification makes you look careful, not slow.** ✅ Guessing and getting it wrong is what looks bad. ⚠️

---

### 5️⃣ 🤔 SAYING "I DON'T KNOW" professionally

*(A genuinely valuable skill.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *"I don't know."* | **"I'm not sure — let me check and get back to you."** ⭐ |
| *"Sorry sir, no idea."* | **"That's outside my area, but I can find out."** ✅ |
| *(silence)* ⚠️ | **"I'd need to look at the data before I answer that."** ⭐ |
| *(guessing)* ⚠️ | **"I don't want to guess on that — can I confirm and get back to you by this evening?"** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)*

> ### 👉 **"I'm not sure — let me check"** is a professional answer, not a failure. ✅

*(Explain.)* Guessing wrong in a meeting damages you far more than admitting you'll check. ✅ Senior people say *"let me come back to you"* constantly. ⭐

*(And the follow-through matters!)* ⚠️ If you say you'll check — **actually check, and actually come back.** ✅ That's what builds the reputation. ⭐

---

### 6️⃣ 📊 PRESENTING / giving an update

*(A quick structure.)*

> ### **Headline** → **Detail** → **Next step** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"Quick update on receivables. The headline is that we're down to ₹6 lakhs outstanding over 90 days, from ₹18 lakhs in March.*
>
> *Two things drove that — the weekly follow-up tracker, and moving from email to phone calls for anything over 60 days.*
>
> *Next step is applying the same approach to the 30-to-60 day bucket. I'll have a plan by Friday."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Headline first.** ✅ Same principle as email 🔗 *(Day 83)* — busy people need the conclusion before the detail. ⭐

*(The presentation openers.)*
- ✅ *"Quick update on ___."* ⭐
- ✅ *"The headline is ___."* ⭐
- ✅ *"Three things to cover — ___, ___, and ___."* ⭐
- ✅ *"I'll keep this short."* ✅

---

### 7️⃣ ✅ WRAPPING UP and confirming actions

*(Underused and valuable.)*

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| **"So just to confirm the actions —"** ⭐ | summarising ✅ |
| **"Who's picking that up?"** ⭐ | assigning clearly ✅ |
| **"What's the deadline on that?"** ✅ | getting specifics ✅ |
| **"I'll take that one."** ⭐ | volunteering ✅ |
| **"Shall we park that for now?"** ⭐ | postponing a side topic ✅ |
| **"Let's follow up on that separately."** ✅ | 🔗 *(Day 76)* |

*(Highlight #1!)* ⭐ **"Just to confirm the actions..."** ✅ — the person who summarises the actions at the end of a meeting looks like the most **organised** person in the room. ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ Do this **once** and your manager will notice. ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (13:00 – 16:00)

### Trap 1: Waiting for permission 🙏

*(The core cultural barrier.)*

*(Explain warmly.)* In Malayalam conversation — and especially with elders or seniors — you **wait** until the other person has finished. ✅ Interrupting is genuinely disrespectful. 🙏 That's a good value. ✅

*(But.)* In an English meeting, continuous speech is the norm, and no gap arrives. ✅ So the polite instinct produces **silence**. ⚠️

*(Firmly.)* And silence in a meeting is read as: *"has nothing to contribute."* ⚠️ Not as respect. ✅

*(The reframe.)*

> ### 👉 In an English meeting, **speaking up is participation**, not rudeness. ✅

*(Practical technique — the 10-minute rule.)* ⭐

> **Say something in the first 10 minutes.** ✅

*(Explain.)* Anything — even a question or an agreement: ✅
- ✅ *"Just to confirm — we're talking about Q3, not Q2?"* ✅
- ✅ *"That matches what I've seen on my side."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Once you've spoken **once**, speaking again is much easier. ✅ The hard part is the **first** time. ⭐

### Trap 2: "Sir" in meetings 🙏 🔗 *(Days 80, 82, 85)*

*(Brief but worth repeating.)*

*(Explain.)* In a meeting with international colleagues, repeated *"sir"* marks you as **junior** — even when you're the most experienced person in the room. ⚠️

*(The fix.)* Use the person's **name**: ✅
- ✅ *"I see your point, Rajesh, but —"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Using a senior person's **name** is normal and respectful in professional English. ✅ It signals you see yourself as a **participant**, not a subordinate. ⭐

### Trap 3: Over-agreeing 👍

*(A subtle credibility problem.)*

*(Explain.)* When we're unsure or uncomfortable, we agree: ✅
- ⚠️ *"Yes sir." "Correct sir." "Noted sir." "Yes yes."* ✅

*(Firmly.)* If you agree with **everything**, your agreement stops carrying information. ✅ And when you eventually **do** disagree, it lands harder — because nobody expected it. ⚠️

*(The fix — the middle options.)* ⭐

*(Explain.)* English has phrases for **partial** agreement. Malayalis rarely use them: ✅

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **"I see what you mean."** ⭐ | I heard you *(not "I agree")* ✅ |
| **"That's fair."** ✅ | reasonable point ✅ |
| **"Possibly, yes."** ✅ | partial ✅ |
| **"I'd need to think about that."** ⭐ | honest neutrality ✅ |
| **"That's one way to look at it."** ✅ | polite non-agreement ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **"I see what you mean"** ✅ is the most useful phrase here — it's warm, it acknowledges them, and it commits you to **nothing**. ⭐

### Trap 4: Not asking when you didn't understand 🤐

*(A confidence issue with real consequences.)*

*(Explain.)* Many of us nod through something we didn't catch — because asking feels like admitting weakness. ✅ Or because the accent was difficult and we're embarrassed. 😔

*(Firmly.)* But the cost is real: you act on the wrong information, and **that** damages you. ⚠️

*(The reframe.)*

> ### 👉 Asking for clarification is a **senior** behaviour, not a junior one. ✅

*(Explain.)* Watch experienced managers in meetings. ✅ They ask *"sorry, can you clarify that?"* constantly. ✅ Because they'd rather be clear than look clever. ⭐

*(The safe phrase to memorise.)*
> ✅ **"Just to make sure I've got this right — you're saying ___?"** ⭐

*(Practise it five times.)* 🗣️

### Trap 5: The long contribution ⏰

*(A practical discipline issue.)*

*(Explain.)* When you finally **do** speak, there's a temptation to say **everything** — because you waited so long. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* ⚠️ Long contributions lose the room. ✅ 🔗 *(Day 39's 15-word rule!)*

*(The rule.)*

> ### 👉 **One point. Two sentences. Then stop.** ✅

*(Explain.)* If they want more, they'll ask ✅ — and then you're in a **conversation**, which is much stronger than a monologue. ⭐

*(The technique.)* End with the hand-back: ✅
- ✅ *"...but I might be missing something."* ⭐

### 💙 And one thing about accents in meetings

*(A genuinely useful closing point.)*

*(Warmly.)* If you're on a call with an American manager, an Australian client, and a Filipino colleague — **everyone** is struggling a little. ✅ Including them, with your accent. ✅

*(Firmly.)* And that's completely normal in international work. ⭐

*(The professional move.)* ✅ Say it openly when needed:
- ✅ *"Sorry, the line's not great — could you repeat that?"* ⭐
- ✅ *"Would you mind slowing down slightly? I want to get the numbers right."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ That second one is a **completely** professional request. ✅ Nobody will think less of you — and most people will appreciate the honesty. ⭐

*(Sincere.)* You are not the only person in the meeting who missed something. ✅ You're just the one willing to ask. 💪

## 🔑 PRESENTER ANSWER KEY — *do not read on camera*

*(For the pinned comment, the video description, and replying in the comments. Several items have **more than one correct answer** — accept the alternatives listed.)*

**Q1 — one interrupting, one disagreeing, one clarification phrase**
**What a good answer looks like:** three **different** functions, not three interrupting phrases · each a complete usable sentence · the interrupting one signals before speaking · the clarification one names what was missed, not just *"pardon?"*
Example: *"Sorry, can I jump in on that? · I see it slightly differently — may I explain why? · Sorry, could you repeat the second point?"*

**Q2 — disagreeing with your manager**
**What a good answer looks like:** acknowledges the pressure first · softens with a hedge · gives **one** concrete risk · offers an alternative with a time.
Example: *"I understand we're behind, and I want to get it out today. I'm a bit concerned that if the figures are wrong we'll spend longer explaining it. Could I take 40 minutes to check the totals and send it by five?"*

> **⚠️ Marking rule:** `❌` only for genuinely ungrammatical English. If a learner's sentence is well-formed but **regional, dated, or means something else**, say so warmly and give the international version — never call it wrong. 🔗 *(symbol legend in the scripts README)*
