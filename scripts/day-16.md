# Day 16 — Used To, Be Used To, or Get Used To? One Lesson Is Enough

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Three phrases that look like twins but mean *past habit*, *comfortable now*, and *becoming comfortable*.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Curious tone.)*

Read these three sentences. They look almost the same:

1. *I **used to** drink coffee.*
2. *I **am used to** drinking coffee.*
3. *I **am getting used to** drinking coffee.*

*(Pause.)* Do they mean the same thing?

**No.** They mean three completely different things:

1. = I drank coffee **before, but not now.** ❌ Stopped.
2. = Coffee is **normal for me.** It doesn't affect me. ✅ Comfortable.
3. = It was strange, and I'm **slowly becoming comfortable.** 🔄 In progress.

*(Point at camera.)*

Now here's the Malayali problem. You want to say **ഞാൻ പഴയകാലത്ത് സിഗരറ്റ് വലിച്ചിരുന്നു** — I smoked in the past, not now.

And you say: ❌ *"I am used to smoke."*

*(Wince.)* That sentence is broken. Three things wrong with it.

Today, one clean lesson. Three phrases, three shapes, three meanings. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Slow. Focus on SHAPE, not meaning.)*

The secret is: **look at the word that comes AFTER, and whether there's a "be" verb before.**

### 1️⃣ USED TO + plain verb = past habit, finished

**No "am/is/are" in front.** Just *used to*.

- ✅ *I **used to** play cricket.* → I don't anymore
- ✅ *She **used to** live in Delhi.* → she moved
- ✅ *We **used to** be neighbours.* → not now

**Shape: [person] + used to + PLAIN VERB**
**Meaning: before, but not now.** Malayalam: **-ഇരുന്നു** (കളിച്ചിരുന്നു, താമസിച്ചിരുന്നു)

*(Big note.)* This is **only for the past**. There is no "use to" in the present! If you do something now, just say *"I play cricket."*

### 2️⃣ BE USED TO + noun / -ing = it feels normal to me

**There IS an "am/is/are/was" in front.**

- ✅ *I **am used to** the heat.* → it doesn't bother me
- ✅ *He **is used to** working late.* → normal for him
- ✅ *She **isn't used to** spicy food.* → it's difficult for her

**Shape: [person] + am/is/are + used to + NOUN or VERB+ing**
**Meaning: I'm comfortable, it's familiar.** Malayalam: **ശീലമായി / പരിചയമായി**

### 3️⃣ GET USED TO + noun / -ing = becoming comfortable (a process)

- ✅ *I'm **getting used to** the new office.* → in progress
- ✅ *You'll **get used to** it.* → it will become normal
- ✅ *It took me a month to **get used to** the traffic.*

**Shape: [person] + get/am getting/will get + used to + NOUN or VERB+ing**
**Meaning: the process of becoming familiar.** Malayalam: **ശീലമാകുന്നു**

### The 3-second decision

*(Show on screen.)*

> **Is there a "be" verb (am/is/are)?**
> **NO** → *used to + plain verb* → **past habit**
> **YES** → *be used to + -ing* → **comfortable now**
> **"get" instead** → **becoming comfortable**

*(Slogan.)* **No "am" → past. With "am" → normal. With "getting" → in progress.**

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ പഴയകാലത്ത് സിഗരറ്റ് വലിച്ചിരുന്നു. *(now I don't)*

*(Find the Malayalam clue.)* Look at that ending: **വലിച്ചിരുന്നു.** That **-ഇരുന്നു** tail is Malayalam's "used to" — a habit that existed in the past.

*(Excited.)* So Malayalam has this too! **-ഇരുന്നു = used to.** Beautiful.

Build it:

1. **Is it a finished past habit?** → Yes → **used to** *(no "am"!)*
2. **Who** → I
3. **used to** → used to
4. **Plain verb** → smoke *(not smoking, not smoked — PLAIN)*

> ✅ **"I used to smoke."**

*(Add detail.)*
- ✅ *I used to smoke, but I quit two years ago.*
- ✅ *I used to smoke a lot in college.*

*(The three common errors — show each.)*

| ❌ Wrong | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| *I am used to smoke.* | "am" changed the meaning + needs -ing |
| *I used to smoking.* | after *used to*, verb must be PLAIN |
| *I use to smoke.* | must be *used* with a **d** — it's past! |

*(Pronunciation tip — important!)* Native speakers say **"used to"** as one squeezed word: **"yoostu."** Not "yoozd... to." Practise: *I **yoostu** smoke.* That sound is the mark of a fluent speaker.

*(Question and negative forms.)*
- ✅ *Did you **use to** smoke?* ← question drops the *d* *(remember Day 03 — "did" carries the past!)*
- ✅ *I **didn't use to** smoke.* ← negative also drops the *d*

*(Explain why.)* Because *did* is already carrying the past! The boss rule from Day 03. Beautiful, right? Everything connects.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് രാത്രി ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നത് ശീലമായി.

*(Find the clue.)* **ശീലമായി** = "has become a habit / I'm accustomed." That's **be used to** ✅

Build it:

1. **Comfortable now?** → Yes → **be used to**
2. **Who** → I
3. **be verb** → am
4. **used to** → used to
5. **Verb + ING** *(because "to" here is a preposition, not an infinitive!)* → working
6. **When** → at night

> ✅ **"I am used to working at night."**

*(Explain the -ing — this is the deepest point of the lesson.)*

*(Ask the audience.)* Why *working* and not *work*?

Because in **"be used to,"** the word *to* is a **preposition** — like *in, on, at*. And after a preposition, English **always** uses **-ing**:

- ✅ *interested **in** learn**ing***
- ✅ *good **at** cook**ing***
- ✅ *before **going***
- ✅ *used **to** work**ing***

But in **"used to smoke,"** the *to* is part of the infinitive **"to smoke"** — so the verb stays plain.

*(Simple version.)* **Two different "to"s. One takes -ing, one doesn't.**
- *used to* + **plain** → past habit
- *be used to* + **-ing** → comfortable

*(Practise the pair out loud.)*
- I **used to work** at night. *(before, not now)*
- I **am used to working** at night. *(it's normal for me)*

Feel the difference? Say both three times.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Which one |
|---|---|---|
| Childhood memory | **We used to play in the rain every June.** | past habit |
| A Malayali in Delhi winter | **I'm not used to this cold.** | not comfortable |
| New job, second week | **I'm still getting used to the new system.** | in progress |
| Reassuring a friend | **Don't worry, you'll get used to it.** | future comfort |
| Explaining your work life | **I'm used to handling pressure.** | comfortable ✅ *interview gold!* |

*(Highlight row 5 — interview tip!)* **"I'm used to..."** is a powerful interview phrase. It shows experience without bragging:
- ✅ *I'm used to working in a team.*
- ✅ *I'm used to meeting tight deadlines.*
- ✅ *I'm used to handling difficult customers.*

Write those down. Use them in your next interview.

*(Highlight row 4.)* **"You'll get used to it."** — the kindest sentence in English. Use it to comfort anyone starting something new.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: Using "used to" for the present

Malayalis often say:

> ❌ *"I used to go to gym daily."* *(meaning: I go daily, now!)*

*(Serious.)* This is a **meaning disaster**. Your listener hears: *"I went to the gym before, but I stopped."* You said the opposite of what you meant!

**"Used to" is ALWAYS the past. Always.** For present habits, use **simple present**:

| Meaning | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| I go now, daily | **I go to the gym every day.** |
| I went before, stopped | **I used to go to the gym.** |
| It's normal for me | **I'm used to going to the gym early.** |

*(Rule.)* **Present habit → simple present + every day / usually / always.**
- ✅ *I usually wake up at six.*
- ✅ *I always drink tea in the morning.*
- ✅ *He works from home.*

### Trap 2: "-ഇരുന്നു" is not always "used to"

*(Important nuance.)* Malayalam **-ഇരുന്നു** has two meanings:

1. **Repeated habit** → *used to* ✅
   - ഞാൻ ദിവസവും നടക്കാൻ **പോയിരുന്നു** → *I used to go for a walk every day.*
2. **One continuous past action** → *was + -ing*
   - ഞാൻ അപ്പോൾ **പഠിച്ചിരുന്നു** → *I was studying at that time.*

*(How to tell?)* Ask: **"Did it happen many times, or once?"**
- Many times, regularly → **used to**
- One time, continuing → **was/were + -ing**

*(Examples.)*
- ✅ *I used to visit my grandmother every Sunday.* ← many times
- ✅ *I was visiting my grandmother when you called.* ← one time

### Trap 3: Forgetting the "be" verb

Same old Malayalam habit — no separate "be" verb:

- ❌ *I used to this weather* → ✅ **I'm used to this weather.**
- ❌ *He not used to spicy food* → ✅ **He isn't used to spicy food.**
- ❌ *Are you used to it?* ✅ *(this one's correct!)*

*(Reminder.)* If your meaning is "comfortable," you **must** have am/is/are/was/were. No "be" = no comfort meaning.

### Trap 4: The "would" alternative — a free upgrade

*(Bonus.)* For past habits, English has a second option: **would**.

- ✅ *Every summer, we **would** go to my uncle's house.*
- ✅ *My father **would** wake up at 5 every morning.*

*(When to use which?)*
- **used to** → works for **states and actions** ✅ safest
- **would** → only for **repeated actions**, not states

So:
- ✅ *I used to live in Kochi.* ✅
- ❌ *I would live in Kochi.* ← wrong! *live* is a state, not an action

*(Simple advice.)* Stick with **used to**. Use **would** only when telling a story about the past — it sounds beautiful and literary there.
