## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. This framework is the whole video.)*

### Forget 12 tenses. There are only 3 TIMES and 4 SHAPES.

*(This is the key simplification — spend time here.)*

Every tense in English is just **two decisions**:

**Decision 1 — WHEN?** *(3 options)* 🕐
- **Past** ⬅️
- **Present** ⏺️
- **Future** ➡️

**Decision 2 — WHAT SHAPE?** *(4 options)* 🔷

| Shape | Meaning | Signal word |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | a fact, a complete event | *(nothing)* |
| **Continuous** | in progress, unfinished | **-ing** |
| **Perfect** | connected to a later point | **have / had** |
| **Perfect Continuous** | in progress AND connected | **have been + -ing** |

*(Do the maths.)* **3 times × 4 shapes = 12 tenses.** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* That's it! You don't memorise twelve things. You memorise **three** and **four**, and then **combine** them. 🧩

### The two magic words 🔑

*(Simplify further — this is genuinely useful.)*

Look at the four shapes. They're built from just **two** ideas:

> **-ING = it's happening / unfinished** 🔄
> **HAVE / HAD = it connects to a later moment** 🔗

*(Test it.)*
- *I work* → no -ing, no have → **simple fact** ✅
- *I am working* → -ing → **in progress** 🔄
- *I have worked* → have → **connects to now** 🔗
- *I have been working* → have **+** -ing → **in progress AND connects** 🔗🔄

*(Emphasise.)* **Two little words control the entire tense system.** 🎯

*(Slogan.)*
> **-ing = happening. have = connecting.**
> **-ing = നടക്കുന്നു. have = ബന്ധിപ്പിക്കുന്നു.**

### Why Malayalam makes this hard 🪷

*(Important context.)*

Malayalam has roughly **three** main time buckets:

| Malayalam | Rough meaning |
|---|---|
| ചെയ്യുന്നു | present-ish *(covers 3–4 English tenses!)* |
| ചെയ്തു | past-ish *(covers 3–4 English tenses!)* |
| ചെയ്യും | future-ish |

*(Point out.)* So **one** Malayalam form must be split into **four** English ones. That's the whole difficulty. ✅

*(Reassure.)* And this is why you can't translate tenses. You have to ask the **English question**: *"Is it happening? Does it connect?"* 🎯

---

### ⏺️ PART 1 — PRESENT (the 4 present tenses)

---

#### 1️⃣ Simple Present — *"Ravi drinks tea every morning."*

**Shape:** plain verb *(add -s for he/she/it)*
**Meaning:** a **habit**, a **routine**, a **general truth** ✅
**Malayalam:** ദിവസവും ചായ കുടിക്കുന്നു

> ✅ *"Ravi **drinks** tea every morning."*
> ✅ *"He **works** at a bank."*
> ✅ *"Water **boils** at 100 degrees."*

*(Signal words.)* **every day, usually, always, never, generally** ⏰

---

#### 2️⃣ Present Continuous — *"Right now, he is drinking tea."*

**Shape:** am/is/are **+ -ing** 🔄
**Meaning:** happening **at this moment**
**Malayalam:** ഇപ്പോൾ കുടിക്കുന്നു

> ✅ *"Right now, he **is drinking** tea."*
> ✅ *"Look — he **is reading** the newspaper too."*

*(Signal words.)* **right now, at the moment, currently** ⏺️

*(Important comparison — this is Day 47's whole topic.)*
- *He **drinks** tea.* = habit ✅
- *He **is drinking** tea.* = right now ✅

---

#### 3️⃣ Present Perfect — *"He has finished his tea."*

**Shape:** have/has **+ V3** 🔗
**Meaning:** finished, but **connected to now** — the **result** matters
**Malayalam:** കുടിച്ചു കഴിഞ്ഞു

> ✅ *"He **has finished** his tea."* → the cup is empty **now** ✅
> ✅ *"He **has already read** the newspaper."*
> ✅ *"He **hasn't eaten** breakfast yet."* *(Day 15!)* 🔗

*(The key idea.)* **No specific time!** ❌ You cannot say *"He has finished his tea at 8 o'clock."*
- Time given → **simple past** ✅ *"He finished at 8."*
- No time, result matters → **present perfect** ✅

---

#### 4️⃣ Present Perfect Continuous — *"He has been drinking tea for 20 years."*

**Shape:** have/has been **+ -ing** 🔗🔄
**Meaning:** started in the past, and it **reaches up to now** *(still going, or just stopped)*
**Malayalam:** 20 വർഷമായി കുടിക്കുന്നു

> ✅ *"He **has been drinking** tea for twenty years."* ← still does ✅
> ✅ *"He **has been working** at that bank since 2015."*

*(The interview tense!)* ⭐ *(Day 19 and Day 27)* 🔗 This is **the** tense for describing your current job:
> ✅ *"I **have been working** in accounts for three years."* ⭐

---

### ⬅️ PART 2 — PAST (the 4 past tenses)

*(Now the story moves. Yesterday.)*

**Yesterday**, Ravi had a bad day. ☔

---

#### 5️⃣ Simple Past — *"He drank tea at 7 a.m."*

**Shape:** V2 *(second form)*
**Meaning:** finished, at a **specific time** ✅
**Malayalam:** കുടിച്ചു

> ✅ *"He **drank** tea at seven."*
> ✅ *"He **went** to the office by bus."*
> ✅ *"He **didn't finish** the report."* *(Day 03 — the boss rule!)* 🔗

*(Signal words.)* **yesterday, last week, in 2019, two days ago, at 7 a.m.** 📅

*(Emphasise.)* **The moment you name a finished time → simple past.** ✅ *(This direction is reliable — a finished time blocks the present perfect.)* ⭐

---

#### 6️⃣ Past Continuous — *"He was drinking tea when the phone rang."*

**Shape:** was/were **+ -ing** 🔄
**Meaning:** in progress **in the past** — usually **interrupted** by something
**Malayalam:** കുടിച്ചുകൊണ്ടിരിക്കുമ്പോൾ

> ✅ *"He **was drinking** tea **when** the phone **rang**."* ⚡

*(The classic pattern — teach this properly!)*

> ### 👉 Long action *(was + -ing)* + interrupted by short action *(simple past)*

- ✅ *I **was walking** home when it **started** to rain.* ☔
- ✅ *She **was cooking** when the power **went** off.* 💡
- ✅ *We **were watching** the match when he **called**.* 📞

*(Emphasise.)* **The -ing is the background. The simple past is the event.** 🎬

---

#### 7️⃣ Past Perfect — *"He had already left when I arrived."*

**Shape:** had **+ V3** 🔗
**Meaning:** the **earlier** of two past events ⛔
**Malayalam:** പോയിരുന്നു / കഴിഞ്ഞിരുന്നു

> ✅ *"When I reached the office, Ravi **had** already **left**."*

*(Explain the order.)*
1. Ravi left ← **earlier** → *had left* ✅
2. I reached ← **later** → *reached* ✅

*(Rule.)* **Two past events, and you want to mark which came first? The earlier one can take HAD.** ✅ *(Often optional — Day 50 goes deep on this!)*

---

#### 8️⃣ Past Perfect Continuous — *"He had been waiting for an hour before I came."*

**Shape:** had been **+ -ing** 🔗🔄
**Meaning:** in progress **before** another past moment ⛔
**Malayalam:** ഒരു മണിക്കൂറായി കാത്തിരുന്നു

> ✅ *"He **had been waiting** for an hour **before** I **came**."*

*(Note — Day 27!)* 🔗 This one is mostly for **writing** and **storytelling**. In speech, people often simplify: ✅ *"He'd been waiting an hour when I came."* ✅

*(Careful with the shortcut.)* ⚠️ *"He was waiting for an hour"* changes the picture slightly — it doesn't clearly say the hour was **already finished** when you arrived. ✅ If the sequence matters, keep **had been**. ⭐

---

### ➡️ PART 3 — FUTURE (the 4 future tenses)

*(The story looks forward. Tomorrow.)*

**Tomorrow** is Ravi's big presentation. 📊

---

#### 9️⃣ Simple Future — *"He will give the presentation."*

**Shape:** will **+ plain verb**
**Meaning:** a **decision**, a **promise**, a **prediction**
**Malayalam:** ചെയ്യും

> ✅ *"He **will give** the presentation tomorrow."*
> ✅ *"I **will help** him with the slides."*

*(Note — Day 49's topic!)* *Will* vs *going to* — coming up in three days. 🔗

---

#### 🔟 Future Continuous — *"At 10 a.m., he will be presenting."*

**Shape:** will be **+ -ing** 🔄
**Meaning:** in progress **at a future moment**
**Malayalam:** അപ്പോൾ ചെയ്തുകൊണ്ടിരിക്കും

> ✅ *"At ten tomorrow, he **will be presenting** to the client."*
> ✅ *"Don't call me at three — I **will be driving**."* 🚗

*(Very useful phrase!)* ⭐ *"I'll be travelling tomorrow, so I might not answer."* ✅

---

#### 1️⃣1️⃣ Future Perfect — *"By 5 p.m., he will have finished."*

**Shape:** will have **+ V3** 🔗
**Meaning:** it will be **complete before** a future point ✅
**Malayalam:** അതിനുമുൻപ് കഴിഞ്ഞിരിക്കും

> ✅ *"**By** five o'clock, he **will have finished** the meeting."*
> ✅ *"**By** next year, I **will have completed** my course."*

*(Signal word.)* **BY** + a future time ⏰ ✅ That's your clue for this tense.

---

#### 1️⃣2️⃣ Future Perfect Continuous — *"By next month, he will have been working there for 10 years."*

**Shape:** will have been **+ -ing** 🔗🔄
**Meaning:** duration **up to** a future point

> ✅ *"**By** next month, he **will have been working** at that bank **for** ten years."*

*(Honest note.)* This tense is **rare** — you'll use it maybe once a year. ✅ Know it, don't stress about it. 😊

---

### 🎬 THE FULL STORY — all 12 in one flow

*(Read this as one continuous story. Put the tense name on screen as each appears.)*

> *"Ravi **drinks** tea every morning. ①
> Right now, he **is drinking** his second cup. ②
> He **has finished** the first one already. ③
> Actually, he **has been drinking** tea like this for twenty years. ④
>
> Yesterday, he **drank** his tea at seven. ⑤
> He **was drinking** it when his manager **called**. ⑥
> By the time he reached the office, the meeting **had** already **started**. ⑦
> The team **had been waiting** for him for twenty minutes. ⑧
>
> Tomorrow, he **will give** the presentation. ⑨
> At ten o'clock, he **will be presenting** to the client. ⑩
> By five, he **will have finished** everything. ⑪
> And by next month, he **will have been working** there for ten years." ⑫

*(Pause. Warm.)* **Twelve tenses. One story. One man. One cup of tea.** ☕✅

*(Point out.)* And notice — **you understood every single sentence.** No confusion. Because in a story, tenses feel **natural** — they're just the way time moves. ✅

### 📊 The cheat sheet

*(Show as a clean graphic they can screenshot.)*

| | **Simple** | **Continuous** 🔄 | **Perfect** 🔗 | **Perfect Continuous** 🔗🔄 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | drinks | is drinking | has drunk | has been drinking |
| **Past** | drank | was drinking | had drunk | had been drinking |
| **Future** | will drink | will be drinking | will have drunk | will have been drinking |

*(Point out the beauty.)* Look at the **columns**:
- Column 2 always has **-ing** ✅
- Column 3 always has **have/had** ✅
- Column 4 has **both** ✅

*(And the rows.)* Row 2 always has a **past** helper *(was, had)*. Row 3 always has **will**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **It's a system, not a list.** Once you see the pattern, you can build any tense yourself. 🧩

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (13:00 – 16:00)

### Trap 1: "ചെയ്യുന്നു" → always "am doing"

*(The biggest tense trap for Malayalis.)*

*(Explain.)* Malayalam **ചെയ്യുന്നു** covers **four** English tenses. Watch:

| Malayalam sentence | ✅ Correct English | Which tense |
|---|---|---|
| ഞാൻ ദിവസവും ചായ കുടിക്കുന്നു | I **drink** tea every day | simple present ① |
| ഞാൻ ഇപ്പോൾ ചായ കുടിക്കുന്നു | I **am drinking** tea now | present continuous ② |
| ഞാൻ 3 വർഷമായി ഇവിടെ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു | I **have been working** here for 3 years | present perfect cont. ④ |
| ഞാൻ നാളെ പോകുന്നു | I **am going** tomorrow | present cont. for future ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* **Same Malayalam form. Four different English tenses.** 🤯

*(The fix — three questions.)*

> **1. Is it a habit?** → simple present ✅
> **2. Is it right now?** → am/is/are + -ing ✅
> **3. Does it have a duration?** *(for 3 years / since 2020)* → **have been + -ing** ✅

*(Practise the three.)* Say them with me:
- *I **drink** tea every day.* ✅
- *I **am drinking** tea now.* ✅
- *I **have been drinking** tea for 20 years.* ✅

### Trap 2: "ചെയ്തു" → always "did"

*(The past-side version of the same trap.)*

| Malayalam | ✅ Correct | Tense |
|---|---|---|
| ഞാൻ ഇന്നലെ പോയി | I **went** yesterday | simple past ⑤ |
| ഞാൻ പോയിട്ടുണ്ട് | I **have been** there | present perfect ③ |
| ഞാൻ പോയിരുന്നു *(before something)* | I **had gone** | past perfect ⑦ |
| ഞാൻ പോകുകയായിരുന്നു | I **was going** | past continuous ⑥ |

*(The deciding question.)*

> **Is a finished TIME mentioned?** *(yesterday, in 2019, last week)*
> **YES → simple past** ✅ *(this direction is safe)*
> **NO, and the result matters now → present perfect** ✅

*(One honest caution.)* ⚠️ "No time mentioned" does **not** force the present perfect. ✅ *I saw him.* · *She called.* ✅ are perfect English with no time word — because we're telling a **past story**. ✅ Ask the second question too: **does the result matter right now?** ⭐

*(Practise the pair — very important.)*
- ✅ *I **have been** to Goa.* ← experience, no date ✅
- ✅ *I **went** to Goa **last year**.* ← date given ✅
- ❌ *I have been to Goa last year.* ❌ *(Day 11!)* 🔗

### Trap 3: -ing on state verbs

*(We've met this on Days 01, 19, 27 — reinforce it.)* 🔗

Some verbs describe **states**, not actions. In their state meaning, they normally **don't** take *-ing*:

**know · have (own) · understand · believe · like · love · want · need · belong · seem · mean**

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I am having a car | **I have a car** |
| I am knowing him | **I know him** |
| I am not understanding | **I don't understand** |
| I am wanting tea | **I want tea** |
| She is having long hair | **She has long hair** |

*(The test.)* **Is it something you DO, or something that just IS?** ⚙️
- *drinking* → something you do ✅ → *-ing* fine
- *knowing* → a state you're in ❌ → no *-ing*

*(Advanced note — don't let it confuse you.)* ⭐ Some of these verbs **change meaning** and then allow *-ing*: ✅ *I'm **having** lunch* *(= eating)* · *I'm **thinking** about it* · *You're **being** rude.* ✅ The rule is about the **state meaning**, not the word itself. ⭐

### Trap 4: Trying to use all 12 in conversation

*(An honest, freeing correction.)*

*(Direct.)* Here's a fact that will relieve you: in normal spoken English, a **small handful** of tenses carries the vast majority of what you say. ✅

**The essential ones:**
1. **Simple present** → *I work here.* ①
2. **Simple past** → *I went yesterday.* ⑤
3. **Present continuous** → *I'm working now.* ②
4. **Present perfect** → *I've finished.* ③
5. **Present perfect continuous** → *I've been working here for 3 years.* ④

*(Emphasise.)* **Master those five completely.** The rest will come naturally, over years, from reading and listening. ✅

*(Firmly.)* Don't try to force *"Future Perfect Continuous"* into a conversation to sound advanced. Native speakers barely use it. **Simple and correct beats complex and shaky.** ✅
