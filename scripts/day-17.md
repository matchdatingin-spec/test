# Day 17 — Should Have, Could Have, Would Have: Past Modals Made Easy

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** One shape — *[helper] + have + third form* — lets you express regret, missed chances, and "what if" about the past.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Regretful tone, then energetic.)*

You reach the station. The train has left. You feel that familiar pain and you want to say:

**ഞാൻ നേരത്തെ വരേണ്ടിയിരുന്നു.**

And you say:

> ⚠️ *"I should come early."*

*(Shake head.)* That means "I should come early **tomorrow**." It's about the future! Your regret disappeared.

Or:
> ❌ *"I should came early."*
> ❌ *"I should have came early."*

*(Smile.)* So close! The correct sentence is:

> ✅ **"I should have come early."**

*(Point at camera.)*

Now listen — this pattern is the **emotional heart** of English. Regret, blame, missed chances, imagining a different life. Without it, you can only talk about facts. With it, you can talk about **feelings**.

And it's just one shape. One. Let's learn it forever. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Write the formula big.)*

### 👉 THE SHAPE: [should / could / would] + HAVE + third form

That's it. That's the whole grammar. **Three parts, never changing.**

- should + have + **gone**
- could + have + **done**
- would + have + **helped**

*(Emphasise.)* The **have** never changes to *has* or *had*. And the verb is always the **third form** (V3): gone, done, seen, come, eaten, written.

Now — what does each one **mean**?

### 😔 SHOULD HAVE = regret. It was the right thing, but I didn't do it.

- ✅ *I should have studied more.* → I didn't. I regret it.
- ✅ *You should have told me!* → You didn't. I'm upset.
- ✅ *He shouldn't have said that.* → He said it. It was wrong.

**SHOULD HAVE = "അത് ചെയ്യേണ്ടിയിരുന്നു" — regret / blame.**

### 😐 COULD HAVE = the chance existed, but it didn't happen.

- ✅ *I could have gone to the party.* → It was possible, but I didn't go.
- ✅ *You could have called me.* → You had the option. *(mild complaint)*
- ✅ *He could have been a doctor.* → The ability was there.

**COULD HAVE = "ചെയ്യാൻ കഴിയുമായിരുന്നു" — missed possibility.**

### 💭 WOULD HAVE = an imaginary result. In a different world, this would be true.

- ✅ *I would have helped you, but I was out of town.* → I didn't help.
- ✅ *If I had known, I would have come.* → I didn't know, so I didn't come.

**WOULD HAVE = "ചെയ്യുമായിരുന്നു" — imaginary past result.**

### The one-line summary

*(Say it three times.)*

| Word | Feeling |
|---|---|
| **should have** | I regret it 😔 |
| **could have** | it was possible, but no ⚪ |
| **would have** | in another world, yes 💭 |

*(Big note.)* And here's what they share **in this lesson**: none of these examples actually happened. In these three meanings, we're describing a past that **did not come true.**

*(Honest flag — we come back to this later!)* ⚠️ Careful: *could have* and *might have* have a **second** job, which we'll meet at the end of the class — **guessing** about the past: ✅ *He could have missed the bus.* ✅ There, it may well have happened. So "didn't happen" is true for the **regret** meaning, not for every use. ⭐

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ നേരത്തെ വരേണ്ടിയിരുന്നു.

*(Look at the Malayalam.)* **വരേണ്ടിയിരുന്നു** — see how long that ending is? Malayalam packs three ideas into that tail:
- വരുക = come
- -ണ്ടി = should / necessary
- -ഇരുന്നു = past

*(Amazed.)* **Three meanings, one word!** That's why Malayalis struggle — English needs **three separate words** for what Malayalam does in one ending.

Build it:

1. **Who** → I
2. **Which feeling?** Regret about the right thing → **should**
3. **have** *(never changes)* → have
4. **Third form of "come"** → come *(come–came–**come**)*
5. **How** → early

> ✅ **"I should have come early."**

*(The V3 trap!)* Look at *come*. Its three forms are **come – came – come**. So the third form is *come*, the same as the first! That's why ❌ *"should have came"* is such a common error — people grab the second form.

*(Show a mini V3 chart.)*

| First | Second | **Third (use this!)** |
|---|---|---|
| come | came | **come** |
| go | went | **gone** |
| do | did | **done** |
| see | saw | **seen** |
| eat | ate | **eaten** |
| take | took | **taken** |
| write | wrote | **written** |
| tell | told | **told** |

*(Rule.)* **After "have," always the THIRD form.** Never the second.
- ❌ should have went → ✅ should have **gone**
- ❌ should have did → ✅ should have **done**
- ❌ should have saw → ✅ should have **seen**

*(Pronunciation gold — this is how you sound native.)* Nobody says "should have" fully. Native speakers say:
- *should have* → **"shoulda"** *(shudda)*
- *could have* → **"coulda"** *(cudda)*
- *would have* → **"woulda"** *(wudda)*

Practise: *"I **shoulda** come early."* *(In writing, always write the full form. In speech, squeeze it.)*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് അറിഞ്ഞിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ ഞാൻ വരുമായിരുന്നു.

Now the full "impossible past" sentence. Two halves.

**Half 1 — the condition:**
1. **If** → If
2. **Who** → I
3. **had + V3** *(this is the "unreal past" marker)* → had known

→ *If I had known...*

**Half 2 — the result:**
4. **Who** → I
5. **would have** → would have
6. **Third form** → come

> ✅ **"If I had known, I would have come."**

*(Connect to Day 07.)* Remember the three levels of "if"? Here they all are:

| Level | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Real / possible** | if + present … **will** | If I **know**, I **will** come. |
| **Imaginary now** | if + past … **would** | If I **knew**, I **would** come. |
| **Too late / past** | if + **had** + V3 … **would have** + V3 | If I **had known**, I **would have come**. |

*(Emphasise.)* Level 3 is for **regret about the past**. It's already over. Nothing can change it.

*(The classic error.)* ❌ *"If I would have known, I would have come."* — very common, but wrong. In these three patterns, **don't put "would" in the if-half.** Say: **"If I had known..."**

*(Practise the shape.)*
- ✅ *If I had studied, I would have passed.*
- ✅ *If she had called, I would have waited.*
- ✅ *If we had left early, we wouldn't have missed the train.*

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| Friend didn't invite you | **You should have told me about the party!** | complaint 😤 |
| You skipped an easy exam question | **I could have answered that easily.** | missed chance |
| Explaining why you didn't help | **I would have helped, but I was in a meeting.** | polite excuse |
| A colleague made a rude joke | **He shouldn't have said that.** | disapproval |
| Guessing about someone's delay | **She must have missed the bus.** | logical guess ⭐ |

*(Highlight row 5 — bonus pattern!)* **must have + V3** = "I'm almost certain this happened":
- ✅ *He must have forgotten.*
- ✅ *They must have left already.*
- ✅ *You must have been very tired.*

And the opposite:
- ✅ *He **can't have** forgotten — I reminded him twice.* = impossible

*(Complete guessing scale for the past.)*
- **must have** = 95% sure it happened
- **might / could have** = maybe it happened
- **can't have** = surely it didn't happen

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: Regret expressed with the present

*(The biggest one.)*

Because **-ണ്ടിയിരുന്നു** is a single ending, Malayalis often drop the "have" completely:

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| ഞാൻ പഠിക്കേണ്ടിയിരുന്നു | I should study | **I should have studied.** |
| നീ പറയേണ്ടിയിരുന്നു | You should tell me | **You should have told me.** |
| ഞാൻ പോകേണ്ടിയിരുന്നില്ല | I should not go | **I shouldn't have gone.** |

*(Explain the danger clearly.)*

- *I should study.* → **future**. I will study tonight. ✅ no regret
- *I should have studied.* → **past**. The exam is over. I failed. 😔 regret

**The word "have" is what makes it past.** Never drop it.

*(Memory trick.)* **have = the time machine.** Add *have*, and the sentence travels to the past. 🕰️

### Trap 2: "should have" + second form

- ❌ *should have went* → ✅ should have **gone**
- ❌ *could have took* → ✅ could have **taken**
- ❌ *would have came* → ✅ would have **come**

*(Why does this happen?)* Because Malayalam has **only one past form**. പോയി is പോയി — there's no "second form / third form" system at all. So your brain has no slot for V3.

*(Practical advice.)* Learn the **20 most common V3 forms** and this problem disappears:
**been, done, gone, come, seen, taken, given, made, said, told, got, found, thought, known, written, eaten, spoken, brought, forgotten, left.**

Say them out loud, right now, with me. That list will serve you for the rest of your English life.

### Trap 3: "would have" in the if-half

- ❌ *If I would have money, I would buy...* → ✅ **If I had money, I would buy...**
- ❌ *If he would have come, we would have started* → ✅ **If he had come, we would have started.**

*(Rule.)* **In these patterns, "would" belongs in the RESULT half, not the "if" half.**

*(Simple check.)* One *would* per conditional sentence. If you hear two, one is almost certainly wrong.

### Trap 4: Missing the "not" position

- ⚠️ *I should have not gone* → ✅ **I shouldn't have gone.** / *I should not have gone.* ✅
- ⚠️ *I would have not helped* → ✅ **I wouldn't have helped.**

*(Rule.)* **Put the "not" on the FIRST helper.** should**n't** have, could**n't** have, would**n't** have.

*(Small honesty note.)* ✅ *"I should have not gone"* isn't broken English — it just puts heavy stress on *not*, which is rare. ✅ Make **shouldn't have** your default. ⭐

*(Drill — say these six with me, fast.)*
1. I should have called. ✅
2. I shouldn't have said that. ✅
3. You could have told me. ✅
4. I would have come. ✅
5. He must have forgotten. ✅
6. If I had known, I would have helped. ✅
