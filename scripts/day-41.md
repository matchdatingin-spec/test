## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:30)

*(Explain the underlying principles — this is what makes the list stick.)*

There are **four habits** behind almost all "learner English." Fix the habits, and hundreds of sentences improve.

### 🅰️ Habit 1: Long words instead of short ones

*(The counter-intuitive one.)*

School taught you that **big words sound educated**. In real English, the opposite is true — **native speakers prefer short, simple words** in speech:

| 📚 Learner | ⭐ Natural |
|---|---|
| commence | **start** |
| purchase | **buy** |
| utilise | **use** |
| inform | **tell** |
| assist | **help** |
| require | **need** |
| reside | **live** |

*(Explain.)* The long words aren't wrong — they're just **formal**. Using them in casual speech is like wearing a suit to a tea shop. ☕👔

*(Rule.)* **Short words for speaking. Long words for legal documents.** ✅

### 🅱️ Habit 2: Adding words English doesn't need

*(The Malayalam-influence one.)*

| ⚠️ Extra word | ✅ Natural |
|---|---|
| discuss **about** | discuss |
| return **back** | return |
| repeat **again** | repeat |
| **actually** speaking | actually |
| **as per** my opinion | in my opinion |
| the **same** *(= it)* | it |

*(Explain.)* Malayalam case endings and emphasis particles create these extras. English is **economical** — one word, one job. ✂️

### 🅲 Habit 3: Missing the small linking words

*(The opposite problem!)*

| ⚠️ Missing or not needed | ✅ Natural |
|---|---|
| I'm going home *(fine!)* ✅ | ✅ |
| Explain me | explain **to** me |
| Listen the song | listen **to** the song |
| Married **with** | married **to** |

*(Explain.)* Some verbs need a small word; some refuse one. There's no logic — only **memory**. So learn them as **phrases**. ✅

### 🅳 Habit 4: Formal politeness instead of warm politeness

*(The most impactful one.)*

| 📚 Cold-formal | ⭐ Warm-natural |
|---|---|
| Kindly do the needful | **Could you take care of this?** |
| Awaiting your reply | **Looking forward to hearing from you** |
| Respected Sir | **Hi Ravi** / **Dear Mr. Nair** |
| It is requested that | **Could you please...** |

*(Emphasise.)* Modern professional English is **short and warm**, not long and stiff. Warmth is the modern marker of professionalism. ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Shorter. Warmer. Fewer extra words.**

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:30 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**The situation:** You're messaging a colleague about a meeting.

**Malayalam:** ആ മീറ്റിംഗ് നടക്കുന്നുണ്ടോ എന്ന് അറിയാൻ ആഗ്രഹിക്കുന്നു.

Your brain builds: ❌ *"I would like to know whether the meeting is happening or not."*

*(Nod.)* Grammatically **correct**. But let's fix four things.

**Fix 1 — "I would like to know"** → too formal for a colleague
→ ✅ **"Do you know..."** or just ask directly ✅

**Fix 2 — "whether... or not"** → textbook structure
→ ✅ Just ask the question ✅

**Fix 3 — "is happening"** → English has a lovely short word for this
→ ✅ **"is... still on"** ⭐

**Fix 4 — the whole shape** → make it a direct question

> ✅ **"Is the meeting still on?"** ⭐

*(Compare out loud.)*
- 📚 *"I would like to know whether the meeting is happening or not."* → **13 words**
- ⭐ *"Is the meeting still on?"* → **6 words** ✅

*(Point out.)* **Half the words. Twice the confidence.** ✅

*(Teach the "on" family — very useful!)*
- ✅ *Is the meeting **on**?* = is it happening?
- ✅ *The match is **on**.* = it's happening
- ✅ *Is it still **on** for Friday?* ⭐
- ✅ *Something came **up**.* = a problem arose *(perfect excuse phrase!)* ⭐

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**The situation:** Asking a colleague to send a file.

**Malayalam:** ആ file ഒന്ന് അയച്ചു തരാമോ? എനിക്ക് അത് ഇന്ന് തന്നെ വേണം.

Your brain builds: ❌ *"Kindly send me the same file. I require it today itself."*

*(Wince.)* Three problems:

**Fix 1 — "Kindly"** → very Indian-English. Native speakers use **"Could you"** or **"Please"**
→ ✅ **"Could you send..."** ⭐

**Fix 2 — "the same"** → this is business-letter English for *"it"*
→ ✅ **"it"** or **"that file"** ✅

**Fix 3 — "I require it today itself"** → two problems! *Require* is too formal, and *itself* is തന്നെ *(Day 02!)* 🔗
→ ✅ **"I need it today."** ✅

> ✅ **"Could you send me that file? I need it today."** ⭐

*(Even warmer for a friendly colleague.)*
> ✅ **"Hey, could you send me that file when you get a chance? I need it by today."** ⭐

*(Point out.)* **"when you get a chance"** ⭐ — this phrase makes a request sound considerate instead of demanding. Learn it. ✅

### 🔥 THE 30 UPGRADES

*(Fast pace. 📚 learner → ⭐ natural.)*

#### Group A — Everyday speech (1–10)

| # | 📚 Learner English | ⭐ Natural English |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I am having a doubt | **I have a quick question** |
| 2 | Kindly do the needful | **Could you take care of this?** |
| 3 | I require this | **I need this** |
| 4 | Please intimate me | **Please let me know** ⭐ |
| 5 | Regarding the same | **About that** |
| 6 | I would like to know | **Do you know...?** |
| 7 | It is happening or not? | **Is it still on?** ⭐ |
| 8 | I will do it today itself | **I'll do it today** |
| 9 | Please repeat again | **Sorry, could you repeat that?** |
| 10 | What is the matter? | **What's up?** / **What's going on?** |

#### Group B — Making plans (11–18)

| # | 📚 | ⭐ |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Shall we go? | **Ready to go?** / **Shall we?** ⭐ |
| 12 | I am coming | **I'm on my way** ⭐ |
| 13 | I will reach at 5 | **I'll be there by 5** |
| 14 | Some work came | **Something came up** ⭐ |
| 15 | I am not free | **I'm tied up** / **I've got something on** |
| 16 | Let us meet sometime | **Let's catch up sometime** ⭐ |
| 17 | Postpone it | **Push it back** |
| 18 | Is it convenient for you? | **Does that work for you?** ⭐ |

*(Highlight #14.)* **"Something came up."** ⭐ The perfect polite excuse — vague enough to need no explanation, honest enough to be true. Use it. ✅

#### Group C — Reacting and responding (19–26)

| # | 📚 | ⭐ |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | It is okay | **No worries** / **That's fine** |
| 20 | I understood | **Got it** ⭐ |
| 21 | Is it? | **Really?** / **Oh really?** |
| 22 | Very nice | **That's great!** / **Nice one!** |
| 23 | I am not knowing | **I'm not sure** |
| 24 | What to do | **What can you do?** / **That's how it is** |
| 25 | Do one thing | **Here's an idea** / **How about this —** ⭐ |
| 26 | Like that only | **Something like that** ⭐ |

*(Highlight #24.)* ❌ *"What to do"* is a direct translation of **എന്ത് ചെയ്യാൻ** — very Malayali! ✅ Say **"What can you do?"** or **"That's just how it is."** ✅

#### Group D — Work and email (27–30)

| # | 📚 | ⭐ |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | Please find attached herewith | **I've attached the file** ⭐ |
| 28 | Awaiting your revert | **Looking forward to your reply** ⭐ |
| 29 | As discussed telephonically | **As we discussed on the call** |
| 30 | Thanking you in advance | **Thanks in advance** ✅ |

*(Note on #30.)* **"Thanks in advance"** is fine! ✅ But ❌ *"Thanking you"* as a closing line is 1950s letter English. Drop the *-ing*. ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: "Only" and "itself" — the തന്നെ ghost 👻

*(We met this on Day 02. It's worth killing properly.)*

**തന്നെ** is emphasis. English has **no single word** for it. So it comes out as *only* or *itself* — and both are wrong:

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| ഇന്ന് തന്നെ | today itself | **today** *(stress it)* / **as early as today** |
| ഇപ്പോൾ തന്നെ | now itself | **right now** ⭐ |
| ഞാൻ തന്നെ ചെയ്തു | I only did | **I did it myself** |
| അവിടെ തന്നെ | there itself | **right there** ⭐ |
| ഇവിടെ തന്നെ ഇരിക്ക് | Sit here itself | **Stay right here** ⭐ |

*(The key discovery!)* Notice the pattern — **തന്നെ often becomes "right"** in English! 🎯

- ✅ *right now* · *right here* · *right there* · *right away* ⭐

*(Say them with me.)* **Right now. Right here. Right there. Right away.** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **"Right away"** is beautiful for **ഉടനെ തന്നെ**: ✅ *"I'll send it right away."* ⭐

### Trap 2: The double question — "or not"

*(Very common.)*

Malayalam questions often carry an explicit alternative: **വരുന്നുണ്ടോ ഇല്ലയോ?**

| ⚠️ Sounds impatient | ✅ Neutral |
|---|---|
| Are you coming or not? | **Are you coming?** |
| Is it correct or not? | **Is it correct?** |
| I don't know he will come or not | **I don't know if he'll come** ⭐ |
| Tell me you want or not | **Let me know if you want it** |

*(Explain the tone problem!)* ⚠️ **"Are you coming or not?"** sounds **angry** in English! 😠 Like you're losing patience.

*(Emphasise.)* Just ask: ✅ **"Are you coming?"** ✅ The "or not" is already built into the question. ✅

*(And the reported version.)* Use **if** or **whether**:
- ✅ *I don't know **if** he'll come.* ⭐ *(natural)*
- ✅ *I don't know **whether** he'll come.* ✅ *(slightly formal)*
- ❌ *I don't know he will come or not.* ❌

### Trap 3: "Like that" and "in that" — the അങ്ങനെ habit

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| അങ്ങനെ അല്ല | Not like that | **That's not it** / **Not quite** |
| അതുപോലെ | Like that only | **Something like that** ⭐ |
| അങ്ങനെ ഒന്നും ഇല്ല | Nothing like that | **It's nothing like that** ✅ |
| ഇങ്ങനെ ചെയ്യ് | Do like this | **Do it this way** ⭐ |

*(Rule.)* **"like this / like that"** ✅ exists — but English prefers **"this way / that way"** for instructions. ✅

### Trap 4: Over-politeness that becomes distance

*(A subtle and genuinely important point.)*

*(Explain.)* In Malayalam, formality shows **respect** — സാർ, ചേട്ടൻ, താങ്കൾ. It's warm. 🙏

But in English, **too much formality creates distance**. It can accidentally signal *"I don't feel comfortable with you"* or *"I'm below you."* 😔

*(Show the difference.)*

| Situation | ⚠️ Over-formal | ✅ Warm-professional |
|---|---|---|
| Email to a colleague | *Respected Sir, I humbly request...* | **Hi Ravi, could you...** ⭐ |
| Thanking your boss | *I am extremely grateful for your kindness* | **Thanks a lot — I really appreciate it** ⭐ |
| Asking a question | *May I have the permission to ask a doubt* | **Can I ask something?** ✅ |
| Declining | *I regret to inform you of my inability* | **Sorry, I won't be able to make it** ✅ |

*(Key insight.)*

> ### 👉 In English, respect is shown through WARMTH, not through formality.

*(Explain the career impact.)* This genuinely matters. In international workplaces, over-formal English can make you seem **less confident** — like you don't consider yourself an equal. And confidence affects promotions. 💼

*(Practical rule.)* Match the other person's level:
- They write *"Hi"* → you write **"Hi"** ✅
- They write *"Dear Mr. Nair"* → you write **"Dear Mr. Nair"** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **Mirror them.** That's the safest rule in professional English. 🪞
