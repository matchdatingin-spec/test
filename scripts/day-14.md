# Day 14 — Me Too, So Do I, or Neither Do I? Agree Correctly in English

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** Three simple patterns that turn you from a silent listener into a real conversation partner.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Warm, social energy.)*

Someone says: *"I don't like coffee."*

You also don't like coffee. So you say:

> ❌ *"Me too."*

*(Wince.)* Uh oh. You just said **"I DO like coffee."** You agreed with the opposite!

The correct answer is:
> ✅ **"Me neither."** or ✅ **"Neither do I."**

*(Lean in, friendly.)*

And here's the bigger problem. Most Malayalis know only **two** replies in English: *"Me too"* and *"Yes."* That's it. So every conversation sounds like this:

*"I love biryani."* → "Me too."
*"I went to Bangalore."* → "Me too."
*"I'm tired."* → "Me too."

*(Smile.)* It works... but it's flat. Like a one-note song.

Today I'll give you **five natural ways** to agree — including the beautiful **"So do I"** and **"Neither do I"** patterns that instantly make you sound fluent. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

There are just **two situations**. Positive and negative.

### ➕ They said something POSITIVE → agree with SO

| Their sentence | Your reply |
|---|---|
| *I like tea.* | **So do I.** / *Me too.* |
| *I am tired.* | **So am I.** / *Me too.* |
| *I went to Kochi.* | **So did I.** / *Me too.* |
| *I can swim.* | **So can I.** / *Me too.* |
| *I have a bike.* | **So do I.** / *Me too.* |

### ➖ They said something NEGATIVE → agree with NEITHER

| Their sentence | Your reply |
|---|---|
| *I don't like coffee.* | **Neither do I.** / *Me neither.* |
| *I'm not tired.* | **Neither am I.** / *Me neither.* |
| *I didn't go.* | **Neither did I.** / *Me neither.* |
| *I can't swim.* | **Neither can I.** / *Me neither.* |
| *I haven't eaten.* | **Neither have I.** / *Me neither.* |

*(Point at camera.)* Do you see the pattern? Look at the **helper word** in their sentence — *do, am, did, can, have* — and use **the same one** in your reply!

> ### 👉 So / Neither + [same helper] + I

That's the whole formula. Copy their helper. That's it.

### The two easy shortcuts

*(Relief energy.)* If the formula feels hard right now, use these:

- Positive agreement → **"Me too."** ✅
- Negative agreement → **"Me neither."** ✅

Two phrases. Always safe. **Just never mix them up** — that was our hook mistake.

*(Slogan.)* **Positive → me too. Negative → me neither.**

### Why "So do I" instead of "So I do"?

*(Quick logic.)* Because in English, when *so* or *neither* comes first, the sentence **flips** — helper before person, like a question.

- ✅ *So **do I**.* — flipped ✅
- ❌ *So I do.* — this means something different ("yes, that's true")

Feel it: **So do I. Neither do I.** The helper jumps in front. Practise that jump.

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Their sentence:** *"I don't like coffee."*
**You want to say:** എനിക്കും ഇഷ്ടമല്ല.

*(Look at the Malayalam.)* **എനിക്കും** — that little **-ഉം** means "also / too." And notice: Malayalam adds **-ഉം** whether the sentence is positive or negative!

- എനിക്കും ഇഷ്ടമാണ് → I like it too ➕
- എനിക്കും ഇഷ്ടമല്ല → I don't like it either ➖

*(Key insight.)* **Same -ഉം for both!** The negative is handled by the verb ending (ഇഷ്ടമല്ല). That's exactly why Malayalis say "me too" for negatives — your language uses the same "too" word both times.

But English has **two different words**: *too* for positive, *either/neither* for negative.

Build the reply:

1. **Is their sentence positive or negative?** → Negative *(don't)*
2. **So which agreement word?** → Neither
3. **What helper did they use?** → do
4. **Who?** → I

> ✅ **"Neither do I."**

Or the short, casual version:
> ✅ **"Me neither."**

Or a third natural option:
> ✅ **"I don't either."**

*(Show all three on screen.)* Three correct replies. Pick your favourite and use it tonight.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Their sentence:** *"I went to Munnar last month."*
**You want to say:** ഞാനും പോയി.

Build it:

1. **Positive or negative?** → Positive
2. **Agreement word** → So
3. **What helper?** *(Careful! They said "went" — there's no visible helper. What's the hidden helper for simple past?)* → **did** *(remember Day 03 — the boss!)*
4. **Who?** → I

> ✅ **"So did I."**

*(Emphasise this.)* This is the tricky part. When their sentence has **no helper**, you must **find the hidden one**:

| Their sentence | Hidden helper | Your reply |
|---|---|---|
| *I **like** tea.* | do | **So do I.** |
| *He **likes** tea.* | does | **So does he.** |
| *I **went** there.* | did | **So did I.** |
| *I **am** happy.* | am *(visible!)* | **So am I.** |
| *I **can** drive.* | can *(visible!)* | **So can I.** |
| *I **have** finished.* | have *(visible!)* | **So have I.** |
| *I **will** come.* | will *(visible!)* | **So will I.** |

*(Simple rule.)* **Present simple → do/does. Past simple → did. Everything else → copy what you see.**

### Five more real-life exchanges

*(Act these out — play both people!)*

| They say | ✅ You say |
|---|---|
| *"I'm really tired today."* | **So am I.** / *Same here!* |
| *"I haven't had lunch yet."* | **Neither have I.** I'm starving! |
| *"I can't come tomorrow."* | **Neither can I** — I have work. |
| *"I love Malayalam movies."* | **So do I!** Which one's your favourite? |
| *"I'll be there by six."* | **So will I.** See you then! |

*(Highlight row 4.)* Notice what I added — **a follow-up question**! That's the secret to real conversation:

> **Agreement + question = conversation continues.**

- *"So do I! Which one's your favourite?"*
- *"Me too! When did you go?"*
- *"Neither do I. Why don't you like it?"*

*(Strong tip.)* Malayalis often stop after "me too" and then there's silence. **Always add a question.** That single habit will double your speaking confidence.

### Bonus: other natural ways to agree

*(These are gold for small talk.)*

| Phrase | When to use |
|---|---|
| **Same here.** | works for positive AND negative! very safe 😌 |
| **Exactly!** | strong agreement with an opinion |
| **I know, right?** | very natural, casual |
| **True.** / **That's true.** | agreeing with a fact |
| **I feel the same way.** | agreeing with a feeling |
| **Absolutely.** | strong, polite agreement |

*(Point at "Same here.")* If you learn only **one** thing today, learn **"Same here."** It works everywhere, positive or negative, and it sounds completely natural.

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "-ഉം" becomes "also" and lands in the wrong place

Malayalis love the word **also**, because **-ഉം = also**. But we put it in Malayalam position:

- ⚠️ *I also want* → ✅ **I want it too.** / **I also want it.** *(the fix is the missing "it" — "also" itself is fine here!)*
- ❌ *Me also* → ✅ **Me too.**
- ⚠️ *He also came* → ✅ **He came too.** / **He also came.** ✅ *(both correct — "also" sits before the verb)*

*(To be clear.)* ⭐ **"Also" before the verb is correct English.** ✅ The genuine error is ❌ *"Me also"* — that one should be **"Me too."** ⭐

*(Explain the position rule.)*
- **also** goes **before the main verb**: *I **also** like it.* ✅
- **too** goes at the **end** of the sentence: *I like it **too**.* ✅
- ❌ Never *"Me also."* That's a direct translation of ഞാനും.

*(Practise.)* Say: **"Me too."** Not "me also." **"Me too."**

### Trap 2: "Me too" as an answer to everything

*(This is the deeper problem.)* In Malayalam, **എനിക്കും / ഞാനും** covers dozens of situations. So we translate all of them as "me too."

But watch — sometimes "me too" is not even the right shape:

| They say | ❌ Me too | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|---|
| *"I'm going to Kochi."* | Me too | **Me too.** ✅ *(fine!)* |
| *"My name is Arun."* | Me too | **Oh, mine's Arun too!** |
| *"I have two kids."* | Me too | **So do I.** / **Me too.** ✅ |
| *"I don't smoke."* | Me too ❌ | **Neither do I.** |
| *"I'm not from Kerala."* | Me too ❌ | **Neither am I.** |
| *"Nice to meet you."* | Me too ❌ | **Nice to meet you too!** |

*(Highlight the last row — very common!)* When someone says *"Nice to meet you,"* please don't say "me too" — it sounds odd. Say **"Nice to meet you too!"** or **"You too!"**

Same with:
- *"Have a nice day!"* → ✅ **"You too!"** *(not "me too")*
- *"Happy Onam!"* → ✅ **"Happy Onam to you too!"**
- *"Take care."* → ✅ **"You too."**

*(Big tip.)* **When they wish YOU something, reply "You too."** When they describe **themselves**, reply "Me too." That's the split.

### Trap 3: Disagreeing — nobody teaches this!

*(Valuable addition.)* What if you **don't** agree? Malayalis often just go silent, or say "no no." Here are the natural patterns:

| They say | You disagree politely |
|---|---|
| *"I love coffee."* | **Oh, I don't.** / **Really? I don't like it much.** |
| *"I don't like cricket."* | **Oh, I do!** / **Really? I love it.** |
| *"I can't swim."* | **I can, actually.** |
| *"That movie was boring."* | **You think so? I actually enjoyed it.** |

*(Point out the beauty.)* Look how short they are! **"Oh, I don't."** **"Oh, I do!"** Just the helper + a flip. Very English. Very natural.

*(And the softeners.)* To disagree politely, start with:
- ✅ *Really?*
- ✅ *Actually...*
- ✅ *Hmm, I'm not sure about that.*
- ✅ *I see your point, but...*

Never just ❌ *"No, wrong."* That's a direct translation of അല്ല and it sounds harsh in English.
