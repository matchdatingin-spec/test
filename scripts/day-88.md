## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 6:00)

*(This section is essential — most candidates have never seen the criteria.)*

### 📊 The four criteria — what you're actually scored on

*(Show the table. This is the most important information in the video.)*

*(Explain.)* Task 2 is scored on **four** things, each worth **25%**: ✅

| Criterion | What it measures | Band 7 needs |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Task Response** 🎯 | did you answer the question fully? | a **clear position** throughout + all parts covered + ideas **developed** ✅ |
| **2. Coherence & Cohesion** 🔗 | is it organised and logical? | clear **paragraphing**, each paragraph with one clear central topic ✅ |
| **3. Lexical Resource** 📚 | vocabulary range and accuracy | some **less common** words, used correctly ✅ |
| **4. Grammatical Range** ✍️ | sentence variety and accuracy | a variety of complex structures with **frequent error-free** sentences ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice — **Coherence & Cohesion** is a full **25%** of your mark, and it's purely about **organisation**, not vocabulary. ✅ And weak organisation drags **Task Response** down too, because undeveloped ideas score lower. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* So a candidate with **excellent** English and **poor** structure often lands at Band 6. ✅ And a candidate with **good** English and **excellent** structure can reach Band 7. ⭐

### ⭐ The single biggest Band 6 → 7 fix

*(The key practical insight.)*

> ### 👉 **One paragraph = one idea, stated in the first sentence.** ✅

*(Explain.)* This is called a **topic sentence**. ✅ Every body paragraph must begin with a sentence that states its **single** idea. ✅

*(Show the difference.)*

❌ **Band 6 paragraph:**
> *"There are many advantages. Technology helps students learn faster and they can access information. Also, online classes are cheaper. But sometimes students get distracted by mobile phones and social media which is a problem. Teachers also need training."* ⚠️

*(Point out.)* ⚠️ Four different ideas in one paragraph. ✅ Advantages, cost, distraction, teacher training. ✅ The examiner can't follow a line of argument. ⚠️

✅ **Band 7 paragraph:**
> *"The most significant advantage of online learning is accessibility. Students in rural areas, who previously had to travel long distances to reach a good institution, can now attend the same lectures as students in cities. In Kerala, for example, several universities began streaming classes during the pandemic, and enrolment from smaller districts rose noticeably. This suggests that the barrier for many students was never ability, but distance."* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **One idea — accessibility.** ✅ Stated first, explained, exemplified, concluded. ⭐

*(Important note about that example.)* ⚠️ In IELTS Task 2, examiners assess your **argument and language** — they do **not** fact-check your examples, and a plausible illustration like the Kerala one is completely acceptable. ✅ But notice the hedging: *"rose noticeably,"* not *"rose by 34%."* ⭐ **Never invent a precise statistic.** ✅ A specific fake number reads as false and adds nothing — and outside the exam, in a report or an email at work, an invented figure is a serious problem. ⛔

*(That's the whole difference.)* ✅

### ⏰ The 40-minute plan

*(Practical timing — candidates lose marks by mismanaging this.)*

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| **0–5 min** | **Read and plan** 📝 ⭐ |
| **5–33 min** | **Write** ✍️ |
| **33–40 min** | **Check** 🔍 |

*(Emphasise the planning!)* ⭐ **Five minutes of planning saves you the essay.** ✅ Candidates who start writing immediately produce disorganised essays and lose Coherence marks. ⚠️

*(What to write in the 5 minutes.)*
- ✅ Underline **all parts** of the question ✅
- ✅ Decide your **position** *(agree / disagree / partly)* ⭐
- ✅ Write **two** body-paragraph ideas ✅
- ✅ Note **one example** for each ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Four lines of notes.** ✅ Then write. ⭐

### 📏 And the word count

*(Practical rules.)*

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Official minimum** | 250 words ✅ — going under costs you, because you can't develop enough ⚠️ |
| **Practical target** | **270–290** ⭐ *(our recommendation, not an IELTS rule)* |
| **Watch out past** | ~320 — more words usually means more errors and less control ⚠️ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Longer is not better.** ✅ A tight 280-word essay scores higher than a rambling 400-word one. ⭐

*(Slogan.)*
> **One paragraph, one clear idea, stated first.** 🔗
> **Organisation is a quarter of your mark — and it lifts Task Response too.** ⭐

## 🔍 3. BUILDING THE ESSAY (6:00 – 15:00)

*(Build it paragraph by paragraph, live.)*

---

### 📋 The question

> **"Some people believe that online learning will eventually replace traditional classrooms. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"**
>
> *Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.*
>
> *Write at least 250 words.*

---

### 🅐 Step 1 — Analyse the question *(2 minutes)*

*(Do this on screen.)*

**Underline the key parts:** ✅
- **Topic:** online learning replacing traditional classrooms ✅
- **Task:** *to what extent do you agree or disagree* ⭐
- **Required:** reasons + examples ✅

*(The critical decision.)* ⭐

> ### 👉 *"To what extent"* means you must state **how much** you agree. ✅

*(Explain.)* You have three options: ✅
1. **Fully agree** ✅
2. **Fully disagree** ✅
3. **Partly agree** ⭐ *(usually the easiest to argue well)*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ And whichever you choose — **state it clearly and keep it consistent** for the whole essay. ✅ An unclear or shifting position pulls **Task Response** down badly. ⚠️

*(Our choice.)* **Partly agree** — online learning will replace **some** classroom functions but not all. ⭐

---

### 🅑 Step 2 — Plan *(3 minutes)*

*(Write the four-line plan on screen.)*

> **Position:** Partly agree — will replace some functions, not the classroom entirely ✅
> **Body 1:** Online learning **will** replace lecture-based teaching *(accessibility, cost)* — example: Kerala university streaming ✅
> **Body 2:** But it **cannot** replace practical and social learning — example: lab work, medical training ✅
> **Conclusion:** Blended model, not replacement ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Four lines.** ✅ Now the essay writes itself. ⭐

---

### 🅒 Step 3 — The introduction *(2 sentences + position)*

*(Build it.)*

> ### **Formula: Paraphrase the question + State your position** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"It is often argued that digital instruction will ultimately make physical classrooms unnecessary. While I accept that online delivery is likely to replace certain kinds of teaching, particularly large lectures, I do not believe it can replace the classroom entirely, especially where practical skills are involved."* ⭐

*(Break it down.)*
| Piece | Why |
|---|---|
| *It is often argued that...* | paraphrases the question ✅ 🔗 *(Day 59's passive!)* |
| *digital instruction... physical classrooms* | **different words** from the question ⭐ |
| *While I accept that... I do not believe...* | **clear position**, and it's a **partly agree** ⭐ |

*(⚠️ Critical.)* ❌ **Never copy the question wording.** ✅ Copied wording shows the examiner **nothing** about your own English, so it can't earn you Lexical Resource marks — and it wastes space you need for real content. ⚠️

*(Paraphrasing techniques.)*
| Question word | ✅ Paraphrase |
|---|---|
| online learning | **digital instruction / remote learning** ✅ |
| traditional classrooms | **physical classrooms / face-to-face teaching** ✅ |
| replace | **make unnecessary / supersede / take the place of** ✅ |
| some people believe | **it is often argued / many maintain** ⭐ |

---

### 🅓 Step 4 — Body paragraph 1

*(Show the four-part paragraph structure.)*

> ### **Topic sentence → Explanation → Example → Link back** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"**There are certainly areas where online delivery is likely to prevail**, and the clearest of these is lecture-based teaching. *(topic)* A lecture involves one person transmitting information to a large group, and a recording can do this just as effectively while removing the barriers of travel and cost. *(explanation)* In Kerala, for instance, several universities began streaming lectures during the pandemic, and enrolment from smaller districts increased noticeably — suggesting that distance, rather than ability, had been the real obstacle for many students. *(example)* For this type of instruction, the physical classroom adds little that a screen cannot. *(link back)*"* ⭐

*(Emphasise the structure!)* ⭐ Four moves, in order. ✅ Every body paragraph should follow this shape. ⭐

*(Note the language.)*
- ✅ *"is likely to prevail"* ✅ *(less common phrasing → Lexical Resource)* ⭐
- ✅ *"suggesting that... rather than..."* ⭐ *(complex structure → Grammatical Range)* ✅
- ✅ *"for instance"* ✅ *(cohesion)* ✅

---

### 🅔 Step 5 — Body paragraph 2

*(The counterbalance.)*

**Model:**
> ✅ *"**However, a great deal of education involves more than the transfer of information**, and this is where remote learning falls short. *(topic)* Skills that require physical practice — laboratory work, clinical training, engineering workshops — cannot be acquired through a screen, no matter how well the content is designed. *(explanation)* A nursing student, for example, may watch a procedure a hundred times online, but competence only develops through supervised practice on real patients. *(example)* There is also the social dimension: much of what students gain from a classroom comes from unplanned interaction with peers, which structured video calls do not reproduce. *(extension)*"* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice **"However"** at the start ✅ — it signals the shift in argument clearly. ✅ Examiners look for exactly this. ⭐

*(Note.)* ⭐ This paragraph adds an **extension** *(the social dimension)* instead of a link-back. ✅ Both are acceptable. ✅

---

### 🅕 Step 6 — The conclusion

> ### **Formula: Restate your position in different words + one forward-looking sentence** ✅

**Model:**
> ✅ *"In conclusion, while I agree that online learning will take over much of the lecturing that currently happens in classrooms, I do not think it will replace them altogether. The more probable outcome is a blended model, in which information is delivered remotely and the physical classroom is reserved for the practical and collaborative work that genuinely requires it."* ⭐

*(⚠️ Rules for the conclusion.)*
- ✅ **No new arguments** ✅
- ✅ **Restate**, don't repeat word-for-word ⭐
- ✅ 2–3 sentences is enough ✅
- ✅ Must **match** your introduction's position ⚠️

---

### 📄 The full essay *(295 words)*

*(Show it complete on screen.)*

> ✅ *It is often argued that digital instruction will ultimately make physical classrooms unnecessary. While I accept that online delivery is likely to replace certain kinds of teaching, particularly large lectures, I do not believe it can replace the classroom entirely, especially where practical skills are involved.*
>
> *There are certainly areas where online delivery is likely to prevail, and the clearest of these is lecture-based teaching. A lecture involves one person transmitting information to a large group, and a recording can do this just as effectively while removing the barriers of travel and cost. In Kerala, for instance, several universities began streaming lectures during the pandemic, and enrolment from smaller districts increased noticeably — suggesting that distance, rather than ability, had been the real obstacle for many students. For this type of instruction, the physical classroom adds little that a screen cannot.*
>
> *However, a great deal of education involves more than the transfer of information, and this is where remote learning falls short. Skills that require physical practice — laboratory work, clinical training, engineering workshops — cannot be acquired through a screen, no matter how well the content is designed. A nursing student, for example, may watch a procedure a hundred times online, but competence only develops through supervised practice on real patients. There is also the social dimension: much of what students gain from a classroom comes from unplanned interaction with peers, which structured video calls do not reproduce.*
>
> *In conclusion, while I agree that online learning will take over much of the lecturing that currently happens in classrooms, I do not think it will replace them altogether. The more probable outcome is a blended model, in which information is delivered remotely and the physical classroom is reserved for the practical and collaborative work that genuinely requires it.* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Four paragraphs. 295 words. One clear position held throughout.** ✅

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (15:00 – 19:00)

### Trap 1: The memorised essay 🧊

*(The most damaging exam habit.)*

*(Explain.)* Many coaching centres teach **template essays** to memorise — with fixed phrases and pre-written paragraphs. ✅

*(Firmly.)* Examiners are **trained** to spot this. ⚠️ A memorised essay that doesn't precisely answer the question scores badly on Task Response — no matter how good the English is. ✅ And wholly memorised material can be penalised much more seriously than that. ⚠️

*(The specific danger.)* ⚠️ If the question asks *"to what extent do you agree"* and you produce a memorised *"advantages and disadvantages"* essay, you've failed the task. ✅

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 Memorise **structures**, not **essays**. ✅

*(What's safe to memorise.)*
- ✅ The four-part **paragraph** shape ⭐
- ✅ 10–15 **linking phrases** ✅
- ✅ The **introduction formula** ✅
- ✅ 5–6 **complex sentence patterns** ⭐

*(What's not.)* ❌ Whole paragraphs ✅ ❌ Fixed examples for every topic ⚠️

### Trap 2: Not answering all parts of the question ⚠️

*(A Task Response killer.)*

*(Explain.)* IELTS questions often have **two** parts: ✅

> *"Some people think X. Others think Y. **Discuss both views and give your own opinion.**"*

*(Emphasise.)* ⚠️ That requires **three** things: view X, view Y, **and your opinion**. ✅ Miss the opinion, and you cannot get above Band 6 on Task Response. ⚠️

*(The question types and what each demands.)*

| Question type | You must |
|---|---|
| *To what extent do you agree?* | state **how much** you agree, clearly ⭐ |
| *Discuss both views and give your opinion* | cover **both** + **your** view ⭐ |
| *What are the causes and solutions?* | **causes** AND **solutions** ✅ |
| *Advantages and disadvantages* | **both** — and if it asks which **outweighs**, answer that too ✅ |
| *Do you think this is positive or negative?* | take a **clear** stand — one side, or a well-argued "mostly X" ✅ |

*(The golden rule.)* ⭐ Answer **the exact words on the paper**. ✅ Don't apply a memorised question-type formula. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ In your 5 minutes of planning, **underline every part of the question** and tick each one when you've covered it. ✅

### Trap 3: Formal-word overload 📚

*(A specifically Indian coaching habit.)*

*(Explain.)* Candidates are often taught to insert "high-level" vocabulary — and it backfires: ✅

| ❌ Overloaded | ✅ Natural academic |
|---|---|
| *In this contemporary era of globalisation* ⚠️ | **Today** ✅ / **In recent decades** ✅ |
| *It is a highly debatable topic since time immemorial* ⚠️ | **This is a contested issue** ✅ |
| *Last but not least* ⚠️ | **Finally** ✅ |
| *In a nutshell* ⚠️ | **In conclusion** ✅ |
| *Each and every coin has two sides* ⚠️ | *(delete entirely)* ✅ |
| *plethora / myriad of* ⚠️ *(overused)* | **many** ✅ / **a range of** ✅ |

*(Firmly.)* ⚠️ These phrases appear in so many essays that examiners recognise them as **coaching-centre language**. ✅ They do **not** raise your Lexical Resource band. ⚠️

*(What DOES raise it.)* ⭐ **Precise, topic-specific** vocabulary used correctly: ✅
- ✅ *enrolment · curriculum · pedagogy · retention · accessibility* ✅ *(for education)*
- ✅ *emissions · congestion · infrastructure · commuters* ✅ *(for transport)*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Topic words, not decoration words.** ✅

### Trap 4: One long paragraph 📄

*(A Coherence killer, and purely structural.)*

*(Explain.)* Some candidates write **250 words in two paragraphs** — or worse, one. ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⚠️ This badly damages Coherence & Cohesion. ✅ Not because of the English — because the examiner **cannot see** your organisation. ⚠️

*(The required shape.)*

> ### 👉 **Use four paragraphs.** Introduction, Body 1, Body 2, Conclusion. ✅
> *(Or five, if you have three body ideas.)*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Leave a clear blank line between paragraphs.** ✅ Make the structure **visible**. ✅

*(Accuracy note.)* ⚠️ IELTS doesn't **require** a specific number of paragraphs — what's marked is whether your organisation is clear and logical. ✅ Four is simply the **most reliable shape** under exam pressure. ⭐

### Trap 5: Malayalam-influenced structures ⚠️

*(Language-specific errors that appear in Indian IELTS essays.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *Nowadays, in this modern world, today* ⚠️ *(all three!)* | **Today** ✅ |
| *Not only... but also...* *(overused)* | vary it ✅ |
| *As per my opinion* 🔗 | **In my opinion** ✅ |
| *Discuss about this issue* 🔗 | **discuss this issue** ✅ |
| *Since 10 years* 🔗 | **for ten years** ✅ |
| *Informations / advices* 🔗 | **information / advice** ✅ |
| *Peoples* 🔗 | **people** ✅ |
| *Due to this reason* | **For this reason** ✅ |
| *In my point of view* | **From my point of view** ✅ / **In my view** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Days 19, 56, and 57's fixes all appear in IELTS marking. ✅ Everything connects. 🔗

### 🎯 The Band 7 checklist

*(A practical tool — screenshot this.)*

| ✅ | Check |
|---|---|
| ⬜ | Position stated in the **introduction** ⭐ |
| ⬜ | Same position in the **conclusion** ✅ |
| ⬜ | **All parts** of the question answered ⭐ |
| ⬜ | **Four** paragraphs, clearly separated ✅ |
| ⬜ | Each body paragraph has a **topic sentence** ⭐ |
| ⬜ | Each body paragraph is **developed** — example, evidence or explanation ✅ |
| ⬜ | Comfortably over **250** words *(around 270–290 is a good target)* ✅ |
| ⬜ | Question **not copied** word-for-word ⚠️ |
| ⬜ | Mix of **simple and complex** sentences ✅ |
| ⬜ | No *"nowadays in this modern era"* ⚠️ |
