## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Explain how to use this video — and why corrections work.)*

### 🔍 Why correcting OTHER people's sentences works

*(A genuinely useful learning principle.)*

*(Explain.)* Spotting a mistake in **someone else's** sentence is easier than spotting it in your own. ✅ Your brain auto-corrects your own writing — you see what you **meant**, not what you **wrote**. ✅

*(Emphasise.)* But the **skill transfers**. ⭐ Once you can spot *"I am having a car"* in someone else's comment, you start catching it in your own speech. ✅

*(Firmly.)* That's why correction practice is one of the most efficient learning activities there is. ⭐

### 📊 The six error families

*(Give them a lens — this makes the 25 memorable.)*

*(Explain.)* Almost every mistake in this video belongs to one of **six** families: ✅

| # | Family | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1️⃣ | **The invisible "be"** 🔤 | *I am agree* / *I working* 🔗 *(Day 52)* |
| 2️⃣ | **Helper verb errors** 🔧 | *He don't* / *Did you went* 🔗 *(Day 53)* |
| 3️⃣ | **Small words** *(in/on/at, since/for)* 🔍 | *since 3 years* 🔗 *(Days 18, 19)* |
| 4️⃣ | **Word order** 🔄 | *Where you are going?* 🔗 *(Day 35)* |
| 5️⃣ | **Countability** 📦 | *informations* 🔗 *(Day 56)* |
| 6️⃣ | **Direct translation** 🪷 | *Sleep is coming* 🔗 *(Day 01)* |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Six families.** ✅ Learn which one you fall into most, and you know exactly what to fix. 🎯

### 🎯 How to score yourself

*(Practical instruction.)*

1. ✅ **Write** your correction before I say it ✍️
2. ✅ **One mark** per fully correct sentence ✅
3. ⭐ **Note the family** of every mistake you miss 📊
4. ⭐ At the end, find your **most common** family — that's your revision target ✅

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **The pattern matters more than the score.** ✅ 🔗 *(Days 30, 65)*

## 🔍 3. THE 25 CORRECTIONS (4:00 – 15:00)

*(3-second pause after each. Then correction + reason + day number.)*

---

### 🔤 Family 1 — The invisible "be" *(1–5)*

**1.** ❌ *"I am agree with your opinion sir."*
⏱️ *3, 2, 1...*
✅ **"I agree with your opinion."** — *agree* is a **verb**; it takes no *am* 🔗 **Day 28, 52**
*(Also: drop the "sir" in writing.)* 🔗 *Day 80*

**2.** ❌ *"I working in a private company since 2019."*
✅ **"I have been working in a private company since 2019."** — *-ing* needs a **be** verb, and *since* + still true needs **have been** 🔗 **Days 19, 52**

**3.** ❌ *"That would nice if you make a video about tenses."*
✅ **"That would be nice if you made a video about tenses."** — *would* + **be**; and *if* + past for the imaginary 🔗 **Days 51, 52**

**4.** ❌ *"I am not understanding this lesson properly."*
✅ **"I don't understand this lesson properly."** — *understand* is a **state**; no *-ing* 🔗 **Days 01, 47**

**5.** ❌ *"She is having two children and one house."*
✅ **"She has two children and a house."** — owning isn't an action; and *ഒരു* = **a**, not one 🔗 **Days 01, 20, 54**

---

### 🔧 Family 2 — Helper verbs *(6–10)*

**6.** ❌ *"My friend don't know English well."*
✅ **"My friend doesn't know English well."** — he/she/it → **doesn't** 🔗 **Day 53**

**7.** ❌ *"Did you went to the class yesterday?"*
✅ **"Did you go to the class yesterday?"** — *did* carries the past; the verb goes plain 🔗 **Day 03**

**8.** ❌ *"Does she works in Bangalore?"*
✅ **"Does she work in Bangalore?"** — one **-s** per sentence, and it's on *does* 🔗 **Days 53, 55**

**9.** ❌ *"I should came earlier to the meeting."*
✅ **"I should have come earlier to the meeting."** — regret needs **have + third form** 🔗 **Day 17**

**10.** ❌ *"He have finished the work already."*
✅ **"He has finished the work already."** — he → **has** 🔗 **Days 54, 55**

---

### 🔍 Family 3 — Small words *(11–15)*

**11.** ❌ *"I am learning English since 6 months."*
✅ **"I have been learning English for six months."** — *six months* is a **length** → **for**; and duration + still true → **have been** 🔗 **Days 19, 27**

**12.** ❌ *"I will reach in office at 9 o'clock on Monday."*
✅ **"I'll reach the office at 9 o'clock on Monday."** — *reach* takes **no preposition** 🔗 **Days 18, 28**

**13.** ❌ *"I am in the bus now, I will call you after 10 minutes."*
✅ **"I'm on the bus now — I'll call you in ten minutes."** — public transport → **on**; and future time → **in**, not *after* 🔗 **Day 18**

**14.** ❌ *"He didn't come still, I am waiting since morning."*
✅ **"He hasn't come yet — I've been waiting since morning."** — *yet* goes at the **end**; and *since* needs **have been** 🔗 **Days 15, 19**

**15.** ❌ *"Please discuss about this topic in your next video."*
✅ **"Please discuss this topic in your next video."** — *discuss* already contains "about" 🔗 **Days 18, 28**

---

### 🔄 Family 4 — Word order *(16–19)*

**16.** ❌ *"Sir, where you are staying now?"*
✅ **"Where are you staying now?"** — questions need the **swap** 🔗 **Day 35**

**17.** ❌ *"Can you tell me what is the meaning of this word?"*
✅ **"Can you tell me what this word means?"** — after a **polite opener**, no swap 🔗 **Days 35, 60**

**18.** ❌ *"He told that he will come tomorrow."*
✅ **"He said he would come tomorrow."** — *tell* needs a **person**; and *said* → **would** 🔗 **Days 05, 60**

**19.** ❌ *"I very much liked your yesterday's video."*
✅ **"I really liked yesterday's video."** — *very much* goes at the **end**, and *really* is safer 🔗 **Days 34, 74**

---

### 📦 Family 5 — Countability *(20–22)*

**20.** ❌ *"Please give me some informations about IELTS."*
✅ **"Please give me some information about IELTS."** — *information* is **uncountable** 🔗 **Days 21, 56**

**21.** ❌ *"Sir I need your advices, I have 3 years experiences."*
✅ **"I'd like your advice — I have three years of experience."** — both **uncountable**; no -s 🔗 **Day 56**

**22.** ❌ *"There is many peoples in my office."*
✅ **"There are many people in my office."** — *people* is already **plural** 🔗 **Days 55, 56**

---

### 🪷 Family 6 — Direct translation *(23–25)*

**23.** ❌ *"Sir I have a doubt, sleep is coming while studying."*
✅ **"I have a question — I get sleepy while studying."** — *doubt* → **question**; and feelings: you **become** them 🔗 **Days 01, 02**

**24.** ❌ *"Yesterday night I didn't get the bus, so I reached late."*
✅ **"Last night I missed the bus, so I arrived late."** — *last night*; and *missed the bus* 🔗 **Days 01, 31**

**25.** ❌ *"Myself Rahul, I passed out in 2022 and I am fresher."*
✅ **"I'm Rahul. I graduated in 2022 and I'm a recent graduate."** — three fixes: *myself*, *passed out*, *fresher* 🔗 **Days 02, 78**

*(Emphasise on #25.)* ⭐ That one sentence contains **three** of the most damaging interview errors. ✅ And it's the sentence most freshers open with. ⚠️

### 🧠 The self-correction habit

*(A practical closing technique.)*

*(Explain.)* Here's how to catch your own errors, since your brain hides them: ✅

**Technique 1: Write, wait, reread** ⏰
Write something. Wait **an hour**. Reread it. ✅ The delay lets you see it as a stranger would. ⭐

**Technique 2: Read it aloud** 🗣️
Your **ear** catches what your **eye** misses. ✅ Especially missing *be* verbs and wrong helpers. ⭐

**Technique 3: The four-item check** ✅
After writing anything important, check **only** these four: ✅
1. ⬜ *since* vs *for* 🔗
2. ⬜ Is there a **be** verb where needed? 🔤
3. ⬜ Does *he/she/it* have its **-s**? ✅
4. ⬜ Any **-s** on uncountables? 📦

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Four checks. Thirty seconds.** ✅ And they catch about 70% of your errors. ⭐
