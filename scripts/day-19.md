# Day 19 — Since vs For: Fix This Common Mistake Forever

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes
**Level:** B1
**One-line promise:** *Since* = the starting point. *For* = the length. One question — "when did it start?" or "how long?" — decides everything.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Confident, like revealing a secret.)*

This is the sentence you say in **every interview**. And most people get it wrong.

You want to say: **ഞാൻ മൂന്ന് വർഷമായി ഇവിടെ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു.**

And you say:

> ❌ *"I am working here since three years."*

*(Shake head.)* Two mistakes in one short sentence. And the interviewer notices both.

The correct version:
> ✅ **"I have been working here for three years."**

*(Point at camera.)*

*Since* became **for**. *Am working* became **have been working**.

Why? Because in Malayalam, one little word covers both ideas: **-ആയി.**

- മൂന്ന് വർഷമായി → **for** three years *(a length)*
- 2020 മുതൽ → **since** 2020 *(a starting point)*

*(Lean in.)* Today, one simple question fixes this forever. And it's the sentence that will improve your interview English more than any other. Let's go.

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 3:00)

*(Draw a line in the air. Left = past, right = now.)*

Imagine a **line** from a point in the past to **NOW**.

### 📍 SINCE = the STARTING POINT (a date, a time, an event)

*Since* answers: **"When did it begin?"**

- ✅ *since 2020*
- ✅ *since Monday*
- ✅ *since this morning*
- ✅ *since I was a child*
- ✅ *since last week*

*(Dialect note — important.)* ⚠️ Bare *"since morning"* / *"since evening"* is common **Indian English**. ✅ Broader international English adds the determiner: **since *this* morning**, **since *yesterday* evening**. ⭐ Both are understood; the determiner version travels further.

**SINCE = the dot where the line starts.** Malayalam: **മുതൽ**

### 📏 FOR = the LENGTH of the line (a duration)

*For* answers: **"How long?"**

- ✅ *for three years*
- ✅ *for two hours*
- ✅ *for a long time*
- ✅ *for ten minutes*

**FOR = the size of the line.** Malayalam: **-ആയി / നേരം**

### The 2-second test

*(Big and clear.)*

> **Can you point to it on a calendar or clock?** → **SINCE**
> **Is it a NUMBER of hours/days/years?** → **FOR**

*(Test it.)*
- *2020* → a year on the calendar 📅 → **since 2020** ✅
- *three years* → a quantity of time 📏 → **for three years** ✅
- *Monday* → a day on the calendar 📅 → **since Monday** ✅
- *two days* → a quantity 📏 → **for two days** ✅

*(Beautiful pair — show this!)*
- ✅ *I've lived here **since** 2020.* ← starting point
- ✅ *I've lived here **for** six years.* ← length

**Same meaning. Two different ways to say it.** Both correct!

### And the second half of the fix: the TENSE

*(Important.)* When you use *since* or *for* about something **still continuing**, this is the tense to reach for:

> ### 👉 have/has been + verb-ing

- ✅ *I **have been working** here for three years.*
- ✅ *She **has been waiting** since this morning.*
- ✅ *They **have been living** in Dubai since 2019.*

*(Explain the logic.)* Why this long tense? Because you need to show **two things at once**:
1. It **started in the past** *(so you need "have," the past-connector)*
2. It is **still happening now** *(so you need "-ing," the continuing marker)*

**have = the bridge back. -ing = still going. Together = started before and still going.** 🎯

*(That's it.)* **Since/for + have been + -ing.** That's the interview sentence.

*(Also correct — don't panic if you hear it.)* ⭐ The shorter **have/has + V3** works too: ✅ *"I've **worked** here for three years."* ✅ Both are right. The *-ing* version puts a little more weight on the **ongoing** activity. ⭐

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (3:00 – 8:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ മൂന്ന് വർഷമായി ഇവിടെ ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു.

*(Break the Malayalam.)*
- ഞാൻ → I
- **മൂന്ന് വർഷമായി** → "three years have become" — a **length**! 📏
- ഇവിടെ → here
- ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു → am working *(present-looking verb!)*

*(Point out the trap.)* Notice: **ചെയ്യുന്നു** looks like a **present** verb. That's exactly why Malayalis say *"I am working."* Your language uses the present tense here. English does not.

Build it:

1. **Length or starting point?** → *three years* is a length → **FOR**
2. **Who** → I
3. **Still continuing? → have been** → have been
4. **Verb + ing** → working
5. **Where** → here
6. **Duration** → for three years

> ✅ **"I have been working here for three years."**

*(Short spoken form.)*
> ✅ **"I've been working here for three years."**

*(Now the "since" version.)* Same fact, different angle:
> ✅ **"I've been working here since 2023."**

*(Practise both — say them with me!)*
- *I've been working here **for** three years.*
- *I've been working here **since** 2023.*

*(Interview upgrade.)* Even more polished versions:
- ✅ *I've been with this company for three years.*
- ✅ *I have three years of experience in this field.*
- ✅ *I've been in this role since 2023.*

*(Write those down.)* Any of those three will impress an interviewer.

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ രാവിലെ മുതൽ നിന്നെ കാത്തിരിക്കുന്നു.

*(Find the clue.)* **രാവിലെ മുതൽ** — that **മുതൽ** means "from / starting at." It's a **starting point** 📍 → **SINCE** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **മുതൽ = since.** Whenever you see മുതൽ in your Malayalam sentence, use **since**. It's a perfect one-to-one match!

Build it:

1. **Starting point?** → morning → **since**
2. **Who** → I
3. **have been** → have been
4. **Verb + ing** → waiting
5. **Whom** → for you
6. **Since when** → since this morning

> ✅ **"I have been waiting for you since this morning."**

*(Careful — two "for"s!)* Notice this sentence has *for you* — but that's a **different "for."** That one belongs to the verb *wait* (wait **for** someone). Don't confuse it with duration-*for*.

- ✅ *I've been waiting **for** you **for** two hours.* ← both correct!

*(Say it out loud.)* Yes, English lets you use *for* twice. That's normal.

### Five more real-life examples

| Situation | ✅ Correct | since or for? |
|---|---|---|
| Explaining your illness to a doctor | **I've had this fever since Monday.** | starting point 📍 |
| Waiting at a hospital | **We've been waiting for two hours.** | length 📏 |
| Talking about a friendship | **We've known each other since school.** | starting point 📍 |
| Explaining a gap in your resume | **I've been looking for a job for six months.** | length 📏 |
| Someone asks about your English study | **I've been learning English since last year.** | starting point 📍 |

*(Highlight row 3.)* **"since school"** — you can use *since* with an **event**, not just a date!
- ✅ *since childhood*
- ✅ *since I moved here*
- ✅ *since the lockdown*
- ✅ *since we last met*

*(Note the grammar.)* When *since* is followed by a **whole clause**, that clause takes the **simple past**:
- ✅ *I haven't seen him **since he left**.* ← *left* = past ✅
- ✅ *She's been happy **since she got the job**.*

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (8:00 – 10:00)

### Trap 1: "Since" for durations — the #1 error

*(The big one.)*

| Malayalam | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| മൂന്ന് വർഷമായി | since three years | **for three years** |
| രണ്ട് ദിവസമായി | since two days | **for two days** |
| ഒരു മണിക്കൂറായി | since one hour | **for one hour** |
| കുറേ നാളായി | since long time | **for a long time** |

*(Explain why it happens.)* Because **-ആയി** feels like "it has been since..." in your head. It carries both meanings.

*(The fix — one question.)* Before you speak, ask:

> **"Am I saying a NUMBER, or a DATE?"**
> Number → **for**. Date → **since**.

*(Drill — I say Malayalam, you say the English word.)*
- അഞ്ച് വർഷമായി → **for** 🔊
- 2019 മുതൽ → **since** 🔊
- രണ്ടു മണിക്കൂറായി → **for** 🔊
- ഇന്ന് രാവിലെ മുതൽ → **since** 🔊
- കുറേ ദിവസമായി → **for** 🔊

### Trap 2: "I am working" instead of "I have been working"

*(This is the deeper structural trap.)*

Malayalam uses the **present** form for things that started in the past and continue:

- ഞാൻ ഇവിടെ 3 വർഷമായി **ജോലി ചെയ്യുന്നു** ← present verb
- അവൻ രാവിലെ മുതൽ **ഉറങ്ങുന്നു** ← present verb

So we produce:
- ⚠️ *I am working here for 3 years.*
- ❌ *He is sleeping since morning.*

*(Explain the English logic.)* In English, **"am working" means RIGHT NOW ONLY** — this minute, this moment. It cannot stretch back three years.

To stretch back into the past, English **must** add **have been**:

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I am working here for 3 years | **I have been working here for 3 years.** |
| He is sleeping since morning | **He has been sleeping since this morning.** |
| I am waiting since 2 hours | **I have been waiting for 2 hours.** |
| She is studying English since last year | **She has been studying English since last year.** |

*(Slogan.)* **Duration + still happening = HAVE BEEN + ING.** Say it three times.

### Trap 3: "How long" questions

Malayalis often ask ❌ *"How much time you are working here?"*

The natural questions:
- ✅ **How long have you been working here?**
- ✅ **How long have you lived in Kochi?**
- ✅ **How long have you known him?**

*(Note.)* **"How long"** is the question word for duration. Not "how much time," not "how many years."

*(And the answers.)*
- *How long have you been waiting?* → **For two hours.** / **Since ten o'clock.**
- *How long have you been learning English?* → **For six months.**

### Trap 4: Verbs that don't like "-ing"

*(Useful nuance.)* Some verbs describe **states**, not actions, so in that meaning they skip the *-ing*:

**know, have (own), believe, love, like, understand, be**

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I have been knowing him for 5 years | **I have known him for 5 years.** |
| I am having this bike since 2020 | **I have had this bike since 2020.** |
| She is being sick for 3 days | **She has been sick for 3 days.** |

*(Rule.)* For **states** → *have/has + V3*. For **actions** → *have/has been + -ing*.

- ✅ *I've **known** him for years.* ← state
- ✅ *I've **been working** here for years.* ← action

*(Connect back.)* Remember Day 01? *"I am having a car"* was wrong for the same reason. **In their state meaning, these verbs don't take -ing.** Everything connects. 🔗

*(And the flip side.)* ⭐ When the same word describes an **activity**, *-ing* returns: ✅ *I'm **having** lunch.* · *You're **being** rude.* ✅ It's the **meaning** that decides, not the word. ⭐
