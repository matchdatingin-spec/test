## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 5:00)

*(Explain how resumes are actually read.)*

### ⚡ The six-second reality

*(Set the constraint.)*

*(Explain.)* In that first scan, a recruiter looks for exactly **four** things: ✅

| Order | What they check |
|---|---|
| 1️⃣ | Your **current/last job title** 💼 |
| 2️⃣ | Your **years of experience** ⏳ |
| 3️⃣ | **Key skills** — do they match the job? 🎯 |
| 4️⃣ | **One** thing that stands out ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Everything else is read **only** if those four pass. ✅

*(The consequence.)* So your resume's job is **not** to describe you completely. ✅ It's to **survive the scan** and get you the call. 📞

### 🔑 THE BULLET FORMULA: V-W-N-R

*(The core skill of the video.)*

> ### **V** = **Verb** — a strong action verb, first word 💪
> ### **W** = **What** — what you did ✅
> ### **N** = **Number** — the scale 📊
> ### **R** = **Result** — what changed ⭐

*(Show it.)*

> ✅ **Reduced** *(V)* **90-day outstanding receivables** *(W)* **across 150 accounts** *(N)* **from ₹18L to ₹6L in eight months** *(R)* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Every bullet should have a number.** ✅ Every single one. ✅

*(The honest note.)* ⚠️ Not every bullet will have a clean **result** ✅ — but every bullet can have a **number**. ✅

### 💪 Strong verbs vs weak openings

*(Practical and immediately usable.)*

| ⚠️ Weak opening | ✅ Strong verb |
|---|---|
| *Responsible for handling* | **Managed** · **Handled** ✅ |
| *Was involved in* | **Led** · **Coordinated** ⭐ |
| *Duties included* | **Processed** · **Prepared** ✅ |
| *Helped with* | **Supported** · **Assisted** ✅ |
| *Worked on* | **Built** · **Developed** · **Implemented** ⭐ |
| *Was in charge of* | **Owned** · **Oversaw** ✅ |

*(The verb menu by function.)*

| Function | ✅ Verbs |
|---|---|
| **Improving** ⭐ | reduced, improved, increased, streamlined, automated |
| **Managing** | managed, led, coordinated, oversaw, supervised |
| **Creating** | built, developed, designed, implemented, launched |
| **Handling volume** | processed, handled, maintained, reconciled |
| **People** | trained, mentored, onboarded, supported |
| **Solving** ⭐ | resolved, identified, troubleshot, diagnosed |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Start every bullet with a verb.** ✅ Never with *"Responsible for."* ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Verb first. Number always. Result if you have one.** 📊
> **ക്രിയ ആദ്യം. എണ്ണം എപ്പോഴും.** ⭐

### 📊 Finding your numbers

*(The preparation step — connect to Day 79.)* 🔗

*(Explain.)* Most people say *"my work doesn't have numbers."* ✅ It always does. ✅

*(The prompts.)*

| Ask yourself | Example answer |
|---|---|
| **How many** things do I handle? | 150 accounts, 800 users, 40 calls/day ✅ |
| **How much** money passes through? | ₹2 crore monthly billing ⭐ |
| **How many** people? | team of 6, trained 12 joiners ✅ |
| **How long** does something take? | 5 days → 2 days ⭐ |
| **What percentage** changed? | errors down 40% ✅ |
| **How often**? | monthly reports, weekly reconciliation ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Honest approximations are fine.** ✅ *"Around 150,"* *"about ₹2 crore"* ✅ — as long as you can **explain how you got there** if an interviewer asks. ⚠️ Never invent a number: résumé claims do get probed in interviews and reference checks. ✅

### 📄 Part 1 — The bullet makeover

*(Ten real before/after pairs. This is the core deliverable.)*

| ❌ Before | ✅ After |
|---|---|
| *Responsible for accounting work* | **Managed end-to-end accounting for 150 client accounts** ⭐ |
| *Handled billing* | **Processed ~₹2 crore in monthly billing with zero audit queries in 2 years** ⭐ |
| *Was involved in team activities* | **Trained 12 new joiners on Tally and internal reconciliation processes** ⭐ |
| *Did reconciliation work* | **Reduced month-end closing from 5 days to 2 by automating 3 manual reconciliations** ⭐ |
| *Worked on customer support* | **Resolved ~40 Level-2 tickets daily for a base of 800 users across 3 locations** ⭐ |
| *Helped in report preparation* | **Prepared monthly MIS reports used by the finance head for board reporting** ⭐ |
| *Coordinated with clients* | **Managed 20+ client relationships, including quarterly review calls** ⭐ |
| *Used Excel for data work* | **Built Excel models with pivot tables and VLOOKUP, cutting report time by 60%** ⭐ |
| *Followed up on payments* | **Recovered ₹12L in overdue payments through a weekly follow-up system I designed** ⭐ |
| *Was part of a migration project* | **Supported migration of 4 years of records from manual books to Tally** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Read the two columns aloud. ✅ Same jobs. Completely different **candidates**. ⭐

---

### 📋 Part 2 — The resume layout

*(Practical structure, in order.)*

#### ✅ The one-page structure *(for under 10 years' experience)*

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **1. Name + contact** | name, phone, email, city, LinkedIn ✅ |
| **2. Summary** *(3 lines)* ⭐ | who you are + years + core strength |
| **3. Experience** | reverse chronological, 3–5 bullets each ⭐ |
| **4. Skills** | tools, software, languages ✅ |
| **5. Education** | degree, university, year ✅ |
| **6. Certifications** *(if any)* | ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Experience comes BEFORE education** ✅ — unless you're a fresher. ✅

#### ⭐ The summary — three lines that matter most

*(This is what gets read after the job title.)*

**Model *(experienced)*:**
> ✅ *"Accounts professional with 8 years' experience in receivables and month-end closing. Currently managing 150 client accounts in a logistics environment. Strongest in process improvement — reduced closing cycle from 5 days to 2."* ⭐

**Model *(fresher)*:**
> ✅ *"B.Com graduate (2024, Calicut University) with hands-on Excel and Tally skills built through coursework and a 3-month internship. Looking for an entry-level accounts role with structured training."* ⭐

*(The formula.)* ⭐ **Who you are + years/qualification + one specific strength** ✅

*(⚠️ Avoid.)*
- ❌ *"Seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation where I can utilise my skills for mutual growth"* 📜 ⚠️
- ❌ *"Hard-working and dedicated individual with good communication skills"* ⚠️ *(zero information)*

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ That first one appears on **thousands** of Indian resumes. ✅ It says nothing and it dates you immediately. ⚠️

### 📝 Part 3 — The cover letter

*(Many candidates skip it or write filler. Here's the useful version.)*

#### 🔑 The 4-paragraph structure

> ### **1. Why I'm writing** *(2 lines)* ✅
> ### **2. Why I fit** — match 2 requirements with evidence ⭐
> ### **3. Why this company** — one specific reason ✅
> ### **4. Close** — clear next step ✅

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Under 250 words.** ✅ One page, half full. ✅

#### 📄 Full model — experienced

> ✅ *Dear Ms. Nair,*
>
> *I'm applying for the Senior Accounts Executive position advertised on Naukri. I've spent eight years in accounts, currently handling receivables and month-end closing for 150 client accounts at a logistics firm in Ernakulam.*
>
> *Two things in your job description stood out. First, you're looking for someone with reconciliation depth — that's been my core work for six years, and I've handled it without an audit query in the last two. Second, you mentioned process improvement. When I joined my current role, month-end closing took five working days; I automated three manual reconciliations and brought it down to two, which has held for three years.*
>
> *I was also interested that this role sits within a shared services setup. I've only worked in single-entity accounting so far, and moving into a multi-entity environment is exactly the direction I'd like my career to take.*
>
> *I've attached my CV. I'd welcome the chance to discuss how I could contribute, and I'm available for a call at your convenience.*
>
> *Best regards,*
> *Vinod Kumar* ⭐

*(Break it down.)*
| Paragraph | What it does |
|---|---|
| 1 | states the role + positions him in one line ✅ |
| 2 | **matches two job requirements with evidence and numbers** ⭐ |
| 3 | shows he **read** the job description ⭐ |
| 4 | clear, warm close ✅ |

*(Emphasise paragraph 3!)* ⭐ **"I was also interested that this role sits within a shared services setup."** ✅ That single sentence proves he actually read the posting — which puts him ahead of most applicants. ⭐

#### 📄 Full model — fresher

> ✅ *Dear Sir/Madam,*
>
> *I'm writing to apply for the Junior Accounts Assistant role at Anand & Associates. I completed my B.Com at Calicut University this year and I'm looking to start my career in accounting.*
>
> *I know I'm applying without work experience, so I'd point to two things instead. I'm already comfortable with the tools — advanced Excel including pivot tables and VLOOKUP, plus basic Tally, which I learned independently in three weeks for my final-year project. And I've done real work with them: during my project I built a fee-tracking system for around 400 student records, which reduced the college office's monthly checking from two days to about an hour. They still use it.*
>
> *I applied to your firm specifically because you work with small and mid-sized businesses. I'd rather learn the full accounting cycle in that kind of environment than handle one narrow function in a large company.*
>
> *My CV is attached. I'd be grateful for the opportunity to discuss the role.*
>
> *Thank you for your time,*
> *Meera Suresh* ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Notice paragraph 2 — she **acknowledged** the gap in one clause and then spent the rest on **evidence**. ✅ No apology, no begging. ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (14:30 – 17:30)

### Trap 1: The Indian resume conventions ⚠️

*(A genuinely useful clear-out.)*

*(Explain warmly.)* Indian resume templates — especially older ones taught in colleges — include things that international recruiters find unusual or that actively hurt you. ✅

| ❌ Remove | Why |
|---|---|
| **Photograph** 📷 | not standard outside India; can invite bias ⚠️ |
| **Date of birth / age** | not asked for; can invite age bias ⚠️ |
| **Father's name** | not relevant to your work ✅ |
| **Marital status** | not relevant ✅ |
| **Gender** | not relevant ✅ |
| **Religion / caste / community** | never include ⚠️ |
| **Full home address** | city is enough ✅ |
| **Passport number** | only if specifically requested ✅ |
| **"Declaration: I hereby declare that..."** 📜 | outdated, uses a whole line ⚠️ |
| **Signature and date at the bottom** 📜 | not needed on a resume ✅ |
| **10th and 12th marks** *(if you're a graduate)* | takes space; degree is what matters ✅ |
| **Hobbies** *(unless relevant)* | usually filler ⚠️ |
| **"References available on request"** | assumed; wastes a line ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Removing these frees up **a third of a page** ✅ — space you can fill with **numbers and achievements**. ⭐

*(Firmly, warmly.)* And note — if a **local Kerala employer** specifically asks for a photo or DOB, provide it. ✅ This is about the **default** resume you send abroad or to large companies. ⭐

### Trap 2: "Responsible for" everywhere 📜

*(The single biggest resume-language problem.)*

*(Explain.)* Why do we write it? ✅ Because job descriptions are written that way, and we copy the format. ✅

*(But.)* *"Responsible for"* describes **the job**, not **your performance**. ✅ Two people can be "responsible for" the same thing and one may have done it badly. ⚠️

*(The fix.)*

> ### 👉 **Replace "Responsible for X" with a verb that shows you DID X.** ✅

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| Responsible for reconciliation | **Reconciled 150 accounts monthly** ⭐ |
| Responsible for the team | **Led a team of 6** ⭐ |
| Responsible for reports | **Prepared 4 monthly MIS reports** ✅ |
| Responsible for client handling | **Managed 20+ client relationships** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ **Ctrl+F your resume for "Responsible for."** ✅ Delete every instance. ⭐

### Trap 3: The objective statement 📜

*(A very persistent Indian convention.)*

| ⚠️ Old objective | ✅ Modern summary |
|---|---|
| *"To secure a challenging position in a reputed organisation where I can utilise my knowledge and skills for the mutual growth of the organisation and myself."* ⚠️ | *"Accounts professional with 8 years in receivables. Strongest in process improvement — cut month-end closing from 5 days to 2."* ⭐ |

*(Explain the difference.)* ⭐ The objective talks about **what you want**. ✅ The summary talks about **what you offer**. ✅

*(Firmly.)* Recruiters care about the **second**. ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* And the phrase *"reputed organisation"* / *"esteemed organisation"* ⚠️ appears on so many Indian resumes that it's become a signal of a **template** rather than a person. ✅ Delete it. ✅

### Trap 4: Under-selling — in writing 🙏 🔗 *(Days 78, 79, 82)*

*(The same reflex, now on paper.)*

| 🙏 Under-sold | ⭐ Accurate |
|---|---|
| *Assisted in a small project* | **Built the fee-tracking system used by the college office** ⭐ |
| *Basic knowledge of Excel* | **Advanced Excel: pivot tables, VLOOKUP, conditional formatting** ⭐ |
| *Have some experience in Tally* | **6 years in Tally, including chart-of-accounts setup during migration** ⭐ |
| *Was part of the team that...* | **Contributed to X by doing Y** ⭐ |
| *Helped reduce errors* | **Reduced entry errors by around 40%** ⭐ |

*(Highlight row 2!)* ⚠️ **"Basic knowledge of Excel"** ✅ is one of the most self-damaging phrases on Indian resumes. ✅

*(Explain.)* If you use pivot tables, that is **not** basic. ✅ Describe **what you can actually do**, and let the recruiter judge the level. ⭐

*(Firmly.)* ⭐ **Never write "basic knowledge."** ✅ List the **specific functions** you can use. ✅

### Trap 5: Grammar slips and format habits that get resumes rejected ⚠️

*(A practical checklist.)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| *I am having 5 years experience* 🔗 | **5 years of experience** ✅ |
| *Experiences* 🔗 *(Day 56)* | **Experience** ✅ |
| *Passed out in 2020* 🔗 | **Graduated in 2020** ⭐ |
| *Worked as a Accounts Executive* 🔗 | **an Accounts Executive** ✅ |
| Mixed tenses in one job | **past tense** for old jobs, **present** for current ✅ |
| Mixed 🇬🇧/🇺🇸 spelling 🔗 *(Day 73)* | pick **one** — India uses British ✅ |
| *Curriculum Vitae* as a heading 📜 | just your **name** as the heading ✅ |
| Inconsistent date formats | pick one: *Mar 2020 – Present* ✅ |
| Font changes mid-document | **one** font throughout ✅ |
| File named *resume.pdf* | **Vinod_Kumar_Accounts_CV.pdf** ⭐ |

*(Highlight the last one!)* ⭐ A recruiter downloading 200 files named *"resume.pdf"* will lose yours. ✅ **Name it properly.** ✅

*(And the tense rule.)*
- ✅ **Current job** → present tense: *"Manage 150 accounts"* ✅
- ✅ **Past jobs** → past tense: *"Managed 80 accounts"* ✅

*(Emphasise.)* ⭐ Be **consistent**. ✅ Mixed tenses are the most common resume error. ⚠️

### 💙 One honest thing about resumes

*(Close sincerely.)*

*(Warmly.)* If you've been sending out resumes and hearing nothing back, I want to say something. ✅

*(Explain.)* It is **very often** not about your ability. ✅ It's that your resume describes a **job** rather than a **person who achieved things**. ⚠️

*(Firmly.)* And that's fixable in **one evening**. ✅ Find your numbers. Rewrite your bullets with verbs. Delete the declaration and the photo. ⭐

*(Sincere.)* The same experience, described accurately, gets a completely different response. ✅ 💪
