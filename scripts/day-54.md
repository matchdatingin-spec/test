# Day 54 — Have, Has, and Had: 30 Real-Life Examples for Malayalis

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** A2 / B1
**One-line promise:** These three words live a **double life** — real verb *and* helper. Learn the two lives and the confusion ends.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Energetic, revealing.)*

Look at these two sentences:

> ✅ *"I **have** a car."* 🚗
> ✅ *"I **have** finished the work."* ✅

*(Point out.)* **Same word.** But it's doing **two completely different jobs**!

In the first one, *have* means **own** — it's the **main verb**. 🚗
In the second one, *have* means **nothing** — it's just a **helper** for *finished*. 🔧

*(Lean in.)*

And that double life is exactly why Malayalis produce sentences like:

> ❌ *"I am having a car."*
> ❌ *"I have went there."*
> ❌ *"He have two brothers."*
> ⚠️ *"I have a bath every morning."* 😄 *(correct British English — but is it what you mean?)*

*(Point at camera.)*

Today we separate the two lives — and I'll give you **30 real-life examples** so it sticks. Let's go. 🚀

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. The two-lives idea is the whole lesson.)*

### The table first 🔤

| Subject | Present | Past |
|---|---|---|
| **I / you / we / they** | **have** | **had** |
| **he / she / it** | **has** | **had** |

*(Emphasise.)* **Had** works for everyone in the past. ✅ Only the present splits into *have/has*. 😌

### 🎭 LIFE 1: "Have" as a REAL VERB = own / possess

*(Explain.)* Here, *have* carries **meaning**. It means **own, possess, experience**.

- ✅ *I **have** a car.* → I own it 🚗
- ✅ *She **has** two children.* → family 👨‍👩‍👧
- ✅ *I **have** a headache.* → experiencing it 🤕
- ✅ *We **have** a meeting at 3.* → scheduled 📅
- ✅ *He **has** long hair.* → appearance ✅

**Malayalam:** **ഉണ്ട്** ✅

*(THE CRITICAL RULE.)* 🔗 *(Day 01!)*

> ### 👉 "Have" for OWNING never takes -ing.

- ❌ *I am having a car* ❌
- ✅ **I have a car** ✅

*(Explain the logic again.)* Because owning is not an **action** — it's a **state**. It's true while you sleep, while you eat, while you do nothing. And **-ing is only for things happening**. ✅

### 🔧 LIFE 2: "Have" as a HELPER = builds perfect tenses

*(Explain.)* Here, *have* has **no meaning** at all. It's just grammar glue — it connects the past to now. 🌉

- ✅ *I **have** finished.* → helper + V3 ✅
- ✅ *She **has** gone.* → helper + V3 ✅
- ✅ *I **had** eaten before he came.* → helper + V3 ⛔

**Malayalam:** **-ഇട്ടുണ്ട് / -ഇരുന്നു** ✅

*(THE RULE.)*

> ### 👉 Helper "have" is ALWAYS followed by the THIRD form (V3).

- ✅ have **finished** · has **gone** · had **eaten** · have **seen** ✅
- ❌ have **went** ❌ · ❌ have **saw** ❌ *(those are second forms!)* 🔗 *(Day 17)*

### 🍽️ LIFE 3 *(the bonus)*: "Have" as an ACTION = eat / drink / experience

*(This is where -ing IS allowed!)*

*(Explain.)* Sometimes *have* replaces a real action verb — usually **eating, drinking, or doing**:

- ✅ *I'm **having** lunch.* = eating 🍛
- ✅ *We're **having** a great time.* = experiencing ✅
- ✅ *They're **having** a meeting.* = conducting ✅
- ✅ *I'll **have** a coffee.* = ordering ☕

*(Emphasise.)* **Here, -ing is fine!** ✅ Because eating **is** an action. ✅

*(The test — from Day 01.)* 🔗

> **Can someone SEE you doing it?** 👁️
> **Yes → -ing allowed.** ✅ *(having lunch)*
> **No → no -ing.** ❌ *(having a car)*

*(Slogan.)*
> **Own it → have. Eat it → having. Finished it → have + V3.** ✅

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എനിക്ക് ഒരു കാറും രണ്ട് സഹോദരന്മാരും ഉണ്ട്.

*(Notice.)* **ഉണ്ട്** = "exists / is there" ✅ It feels **present and immediate** — which is exactly why your brain reaches for *"am having."* ✅

*(Explain the trap once more.)* **ഉണ്ട്** feels like "right now," and school taught you "right now = -ing." Logical. Wrong. ✅

Build it:

1. **Which life?** → **owning** → real verb, **no -ing** ✅
2. **Who** → I
3. **have** *(I → have)* → have
4. **What** → a car and two brothers

> ✅ **"I have a car and two brothers."**

*(Note the Malayalam structure!)* **എനിക്ക്** = "to me." So Malayalam says *"To me, a car exists."* ✅ But English says *"I have a car."* — **you** are the subject, not the receiver. 🔄

*(Emphasise.)* **എനിക്ക് ... ഉണ്ട് → "I have ..."** ✅ Not *"To me is..."* ✅

*(Practise the possession family.)*
- ✅ *I have a bike.* ✅
- ✅ *She has three sisters.* ✅
- ✅ *They have a big house.* ✅
- ✅ *He has a good job.* ✅
- ✅ *We have enough time.* ✅

*(The negative and question forms!)*
| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **Negative** | *I **don't have** a car.* ✅ |
| **Question** | ***Do** you **have** a car?* ✅ |
| **British alt.** | *I **haven't got** a car.* / ***Have** you **got** a car?* ✅ |

*(Point out — this uses "do"!)* 🔗 *(Day 53)* Because here *have* is a **real verb**, so it needs the **do** helper for questions and negatives. ✅

- ⚠️ *Have you a car?* ← correct but old-fashioned/formal British ✅
- ✅ **Do you have a car?** ⭐ *(modern, standard)*

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ജോലി തീർത്തിട്ടുണ്ട്.

*(Find the clue.)* **-ഇട്ടുണ്ട്** ← that's the **helper** marker! ✅ *(Not ഉണ്ട് alone — this is attached to a verb)*

Build it:

1. **Which life?** → **helper** ✅
2. **Who** → I
3. **have** → have
4. **THIRD form** → finished
5. **What** → the work

> ✅ **"I have finished the work."**
> ✅ **"I've finished the work."** ⭐ *(natural contraction)*

*(Note — this uses no "do"!)* 🔗
| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **Negative** | *I **haven't** finished.* ✅ *(not "don't have finished")* |
| **Question** | ***Have** you finished?* ✅ *(not "Do you have finished")* |

*(Emphasise the two paths — this is the key insight!)*

> ### 👉 Real verb "have" → uses DO for questions/negatives.
> ### 👉 Helper "have" → works alone, no DO needed.

*(Show them side by side.)*

| | Real verb 🚗 | Helper 🔧 |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | I have a car | I have finished |
| **Negative** | I **don't have** a car | I **haven't** finished |
| **Question** | **Do** you have a car? | **Have** you finished? |

*(Point at camera.)* That table is the **whole lesson**. Screenshot it. 📸

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: "I am having" for possession

*(The #1 error of this entire series — and worth its own drill.)* 🔗

*(Explain the root once more.)* **ഉണ്ട്** feels present-tense and immediate. And English *-ing* also feels immediate. So the connection is natural — and wrong. ✅

*(The permanent fix.)* Ask:

> **Am I DOING something, or just OWNING/BEING something?** ⚙️
> **Owning or being → plain "have."** ✅

- Owning a car → not an activity → **I have a car** ✅
- Eating lunch → an activity → **I'm having lunch** ✅
- Going through a hard time → also an activity → **I'm having a difficult week** ✅

*(Drill — say these ten, fast.)*
1. I **have** a car. ✅
2. I **have** two brothers. ✅
3. I **have** a headache. ✅
4. She **has** a new job. ✅
5. They **have** a big house. ✅
6. I'm **having** lunch. ✅
7. We're **having** a meeting. ✅
8. I **don't have** time. ✅
9. **Do** you **have** a pen? ✅
10. He **has** long hair. ✅

### Trap 2: "have + second form"

*(Connects to Day 17.)* 🔗

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| I have went | **I have gone** |
| I have saw | **I have seen** |
| She has came | **She has come** |
| We have did it | **We have done it** |
| He has took it | **He has taken it** |

*(Rule.)* **When have/has/had is the perfect helper → the THIRD form follows.** ✅ *(Not when "have" means own or "have to" — those are different: ✅ "I have lunch at one," "I have to go.")*

*(The 15 V3 forms Malayalis need most.)* Say them with me:
**been · gone · done · come · seen · taken · given · made · said · told · got · known · written · eaten · forgotten** ✅

### Trap 3: "He have" instead of "He has"

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| He have two cars | **He has two cars** |
| She have finished | **She has finished** |
| My brother have a bike | **My brother has a bike** |
| Everyone have a phone | **Everyone has a phone** ✅ 🔗 |

*(Rule.)* **He, she, it, and any single person/thing → HAS.** ✅

*(Memory.)* **He, she, it — add the S!** 🔗 *(Day 28)* Same rule as *works, likes, knows.* ✅

### Trap 4: "Do have" vs "Have" in questions

*(A subtle but important one.)*

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Have you a pen? | **Do you have a pen?** | real verb → needs *do* |
| Do you have finished? | **Have you finished?** | helper → works alone |
| Don't you have seen it? | **Haven't you seen it?** | helper → *haven't* |
| Are you having a car? | **Do you have a car?** | possession → plain + *do* |

*(The self-check.)*

> **Is "have" followed by a THIRD FORM?** *(finished, gone, seen)*
> **Yes → it's a helper → no "do."** ✅
> **No, it's followed by a NOUN → it's a real verb → use "do."** ✅

*(Practise the pair.)*
- ✅ *Do you **have** a car?* ← noun follows ✅
- ✅ *Have you **seen** my car?* ← V3 follows ✅

### The contractions 🔊

*(Practical pronunciation.)*

| Full | Spoken ⭐ |
|---|---|
| I have | **I've** |
| he has | **he's** |
| they have | **they've** |
| I had | **I'd** |
| have not | **haven't** |
| has not | **hasn't** |
| had not | **hadn't** |

*(⚠️ Careful with "he's"!)*
- *He'**s** finished.* → *has* ✅ *(V3 follows)*
- *He'**s** tired.* → *is* ✅ *(description follows)*

*(And "I'd"!)* 🔗 *(Day 50)*
- *I'**d** gone.* → *had* ✅ *(V3 follows)*
- *I'**d** go.* → *would* ✅ *(plain verb follows)*
