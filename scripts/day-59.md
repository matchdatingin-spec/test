# Day 59 — Active vs Passive Voice with Real Office and Exam Examples

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes
**Level:** B1 / B2
**One-line promise:** Passive isn't "more formal." It's for when the **doer doesn't matter** — and using it wrongly makes you sound evasive.

---

## 🎬 1. THE HOOK (0:00 – 1:00)

*(Slightly amused, then serious.)*

Your manager asks: *"Who sent the wrong file to the client?"*

And you say:

> ⚠️ *"Sir, the wrong file was sent."*

*(Pause. Raised eyebrow.)*

*(Explain.)* You **think** that sounds respectful and professional. ✅

But what your manager **hears** is: *"Somebody, somewhere, at some point... a file went out. Nobody knows who. Certainly not me."* 😅

*(Point at camera.)*

**That's the passive voice hiding a person.** And in an office, that reads as **avoiding responsibility** — even when you didn't mean it that way.

*(Lean in.)*

The honest, professional answer is:

> ✅ **"I sent it, sir. My mistake — I'll correct it right away."** ⭐

*(Emphasise.)* Shorter. Clearer. And it makes you look **stronger**, not weaker. ✅

Today: what passive actually **does**, when it's right, when it's wrong. Let's go. 🚀

---

## 🧠 2. THE CORE LOGIC (1:00 – 4:00)

*(Slow. The "who matters" idea is the key.)*

### First — the mechanics 🔧

**ACTIVE** = the **doer** comes first ✅
> ✅ ***I*** **sent** the email. ← *I* is the doer, at the front 👤

**PASSIVE** = the **receiver** comes first ✅
> ✅ ***The email*** **was sent** *(by me)*. ← the email is at the front 📧

*(The formula.)*

> ### 👉 PASSIVE = **be** + **V3** *(third form)*

- ✅ *is sent · was sent · has been sent · will be sent · is being sent* ✅

*(Note.)* Every passive needs a **be** verb ✅ 🔗 *(Day 52 — and this is *be*'s Job 3!)*

*(Practise the transformation.)*

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| *I **wrote** the report.* | *The report **was written** (by me).* |
| *They **cancelled** the meeting.* | *The meeting **was cancelled**.* |
| *Someone **stole** my bike.* | *My bike **was stolen**.* |
| *We **will send** it tomorrow.* | *It **will be sent** tomorrow.* |
| *They **are repairing** the road.* | *The road **is being repaired**.* |

### 🔑 THE REAL QUESTION: does the DOER matter?

*(This is the whole lesson.)*

> ### 👉 Doer matters → **ACTIVE** ✅
> ### 👉 Doer doesn't matter, or is unknown, or is obvious → **PASSIVE** ✅

*(Test it.)*

| Sentence | Doer matters? | Choice |
|---|---|---|
| *I sent the email.* | Yes — it was **me** ✅ | **active** ✅ |
| *My bike was stolen.* | No — I don't know who! 🤷 | **passive** ✅ |
| *English is spoken in Canada.* | No — people in general 🤷 | **passive** ✅ |
| *The road is being repaired.* | No — workers, obviously ✅ | **passive** ✅ |
| *Gandhi was born in 1869.* | No — the fact matters ✅ | **passive** ✅ |

*(Emphasise.)* **Passive is not about politeness. It's about focus.** ✅

*(Slogan.)*
> **Passive pushes the doer into the background. Reach for it when the doer is unknown, obvious, or simply not your focus.** ✅
> **ആര് ചെയ്തു എന്നത് പ്രധാനമാണോ? → active. അല്ലെങ്കിൽ → passive.** ✅

### The 4 legitimate uses of passive ✅

**Use 1: The doer is unknown** 🤷
- ✅ *My phone **was stolen** yesterday.* ✅

**Use 2: The doer is obvious or unimportant** ✅
- ✅ *The bridge **was built** in 1985.* ✅ *(by engineers, obviously)*
- ✅ *The meeting **has been postponed**.* ✅

**Use 3: Scientific / academic / formal writing** 🔬
- ✅ *The samples **were tested** at three temperatures.* ✅
- ✅ *It **is believed** that...* ✅

**Use 4: When the receiver is the real topic** 🎯
- ✅ *Rice **is grown** in Kerala.* ✅ *(the topic is rice, not farmers)*

*(Emphasise.)* Four legitimate uses. ✅ **"Sounding formal" is not one of them.** ⚠️

## 🔍 3. SENTENCE BREAKDOWN (4:00 – 11:00)

### Sentence 1 — built piece by piece

**Malayalam:** എന്റെ ബൈക്ക് ഇന്നലെ മോഷണം പോയി.

*(Notice.)* **മോഷണം പോയി** — Malayalam doesn't name the thief either! ✅ Because nobody knows. ✅

*(Point out.)* So this is a **legitimate** passive. The doer is genuinely unknown. 🤷

Build it:

1. **Does the doer matter?** → No, unknown → **passive** ✅
2. **Receiver first** → My bike
3. **be + V3** *(past)* → was stolen
4. **When** → yesterday

> ✅ **"My bike was stolen yesterday."** ✅

*(Note the V3!)* **steal – stole – stolen** ✅
- ❌ *was stole* ❌ *(second form!)*
- ✅ **was stolen** ✅ *(third form)*

*(The V3 forms you need most for passive.)* 🔗 *(Day 17)*
**sent · written · done · made · given · taken · seen · told · found · built · stolen · broken · cancelled · completed · approved** ✅

*(Practise the "unknown doer" passives — say these five.)*
1. *My wallet **was stolen** at the bus stand.* ✅
2. *The window **was broken** last night.* ✅
3. *This temple **was built** 400 years ago.* ✅
4. *The meeting **has been cancelled**.* ⭐
5. *Your application **has been approved**.* ⭐

*(Highlight #4 and #5!)* ⭐ These are genuinely useful — and the passive is **correct** here, because who cancelled or approved doesn't matter. ✅

### Sentence 2 — built piece by piece *(the office correction)*

**Malayalam:** ഞാൻ ആ file അയച്ചു. എന്റെ തെറ്റാണ്.

*(Notice.)* Malayalam **names you**! **ഞാൻ** = I ✅

*(Point out.)* So the Malayalam is **active** and **honest**. ✅ The passive appears only when we translate and try to sound "formal." ⚠️

Build it:

1. **Does the doer matter?** → **Yes! It's me.** → **active** ✅
2. **Who** → I
3. **Verb** → sent
4. **What** → the file
5. **Take ownership** 🔗 *(Day 41)* → My mistake

> ✅ **"I sent it. My mistake — I'll fix it right away."** ⭐

*(Compare the impressions.)*

| Sentence | How it lands |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ *The file was sent wrongly.* | evasive 😬 — who did it? |
| ⚠️ *A mistake has been made.* | classic blame-dodging 😅 |
| ✅ **I sent the wrong file. I'll correct it now.** | ⭐ honest, confident, professional |

*(Emphasise strongly.)* In modern professional English — especially in international companies — **owning a mistake in active voice is a strength**. ✅ Managers respect it. Passive-voice dodging is noticed immediately. ⚠️

*(The professional formula.)* 🔗 *(Day 42)*
> ### ⭐ **Own it (active) + State the fix (active). Two sentences.**

- ✅ *"That was my mistake. I'll have it corrected by 5."* ⭐
- ✅ *"I missed that detail. I'm updating it now."* ⭐

### 📋 Where passive is genuinely CORRECT

#### 💼 In office emails ✅

*(These are all appropriate passives.)*

| ✅ Correct passive | Why |
|---|---|
| *The meeting **has been rescheduled** to Friday.* | who did it doesn't matter ✅ |
| *Your request **has been approved**.* | the approval is the point ✅ |
| *The report **will be shared** by tomorrow.* | focus on the report ✅ |
| *All documents **must be submitted** by Monday.* | a rule, no specific doer ✅ |
| *You **are requested** to attend.* ⚠️ | *(very formal Indian style — see below)* |

*(Note the last one.)* ⚠️ ❌ *"You are requested to attend"* is heavy, old-fashioned English. ✅ Say **"Please attend"** or **"Could you attend?"** ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 41)*

#### 🔬 In exam and academic writing ✅ *(IELTS!)*

*(This matters for Day 88–89.)* 🔗

| ✅ Correct passive |
|---|
| *It **is often argued** that technology harms communication.* ⭐ |
| *This problem **can be solved** in two ways.* ✅ |
| *More research **is needed** in this area.* ✅ |
| *Steps **should be taken** to reduce pollution.* ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* In **IELTS Writing Task 2**, passive is genuinely useful — it lets you discuss ideas without saying "I" repeatedly. ✅ Examiners like the variety. ⭐

*(But balance!)* Don't write **everything** in passive. Mix active and passive. ✅

#### 📰 In news and reports ✅

- ✅ *Three people **were injured** in the accident.* ✅
- ✅ *The new bridge **will be opened** next month.* ✅

#### 🔧 In processes and instructions ✅

- ✅ *First, the rice **is washed**. Then it **is soaked** for an hour.* ✅
- ✅ *The form **is filled in** online and **submitted** with two photos.* ✅ *(*fill* needs its particle: *fill **in** a form* (BrE) / *fill **out** a form* (AmE).)*

*(Note.)* **Describing a process** is a classic **IELTS Writing Task 1** task ✅ — and passive is exactly right there. ⭐ 🔗 *(Day 89)*

---

### 🔄 The "by" phrase — include it or not?

*(A useful detail.)*

*(Explain.)* You can add **"by + doer"** — but only when the doer is **interesting**:

- ✅ *The novel **was written by** Arundhati Roy.* ✅ *(interesting!)* ⭐
- ✅ *This temple **was built by** the Chera kings.* ✅ *(interesting)*
- ⚠️ *My bike **was stolen by** someone.* ← pointless! ✅ Just say *"was stolen."* ✅
- ⚠️ *The email **was sent by** me.* ← say *"I sent it."* ⭐

*(Rule of thumb.)* **If naming the doer is the point, active is usually clearer.** ✅

- ⚠️ *The report was written by me.* ← technically fine, but heavy
- ✅ **I wrote the report.** ⭐ *(shorter, stronger!)*

*(Emphasise.)* **If you're going to name the doer anyway — usually put them in front.** That's active voice. ✅

*(One honest exception.)* ⭐ Keep the passive when the **thing** is what you're talking about: ✅ *"The novel was written by Arundhati Roy."* ✅ Here the novel is the topic, so the passive is the better sentence. ⭐

## ⚠️ 4. THE MALAYALI TRAP (11:00 – 13:00)

### Trap 1: "Passive = formal = respectful"

*(The core cultural trap — explain with care.)*

*(Observation.)* In Kerala English education, passive voice was taught as **the formal register**. Government letters, school notices, and exam answers all use it heavily:

- *"You are hereby informed that..."* 📜
- *"It is requested that the needful be done..."* 📜
- *"The same is being forwarded for your kind perusal..."* 📜

*(Explain.)* This style is **real** — it's Indian administrative English, and it has its place in government correspondence. ✅

*(But.)* In **modern international business English**, this reads as:
- unclear 🤔
- outdated 📜
- possibly evasive 😬

*(The upgrade table.)*

| ⚠️ Heavy passive | ✅ Modern active |
|---|---|
| You are requested to submit the form | **Please submit the form** ⭐ |
| It is informed that the office will be closed | **The office will be closed** ✅ |
| The same is being sent herewith | **I've attached it** ⭐ |
| Kindly be informed that the meeting is postponed | **The meeting is postponed** ✅ |
| It is to be noted that | **Please note** ⭐ |
| Your kind cooperation is solicited | **Thanks for your help** ⭐ |

*(Emphasise.)* **Shorter. Warmer. Clearer.** 🔗 *(Day 41's principle!)* ✅

### Trap 2: Overusing passive to avoid blame

*(The professional risk.)*

| ⚠️ Evasive | ✅ Professional |
|---|---|
| A mistake has been made | **I made a mistake** ⭐ |
| The deadline was missed | **I missed the deadline — here's my plan** ⭐ |
| The file was not sent | **I didn't send it. Sending it now.** ⭐ |
| It was not informed to me | **Nobody told me** ✅ / **I wasn't informed** ✅ |

*(Note the last one.)* ⚠️ ❌ *"It was not informed to me"* is grammatically broken. ✅ Say **"I wasn't informed"** or **"Nobody told me."** ✅

*(Emphasise.)* **Active voice + ownership = leadership.** ✅ It's counter-intuitive, but taking direct responsibility makes you look **more** capable, not less. 💼

### Trap 3: Wrong verb form in passive

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| The file was send | **The file was sent** |
| It was wrote by him | **It was written by him** |
| The work is complete — *used as a passive* ⚠️ | **The work is completed** ✅ *(passive)* / **The work is complete** ✅ *(adjective — also correct, different structure)* |
| The meeting was cancel | **The meeting was cancelled** |
| He was gave a warning | **He was given a warning** |

*(Rule.)* **Passive = be + THIRD form.** ✅ Always the third form. 🔗

### Trap 4: Missing the "be" verb 🔗 *(Day 52)*

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| The report sent yesterday | **The report was sent yesterday** |
| My bike stolen | **My bike was stolen** |
| The meeting cancelled | **The meeting was cancelled** / **has been cancelled** ✅ |

*(Note.)* ⚠️ Careful — in **headlines**, English **does** drop the *be*! ✅
- ✅ *"Three injured in accident"* ✅ *(newspaper headline style)*

*(But in normal sentences, keep the be.)* ✅

### The passive across tenses 📋

*(Practical reference.)*

| Tense | ✅ Passive form |
|---|---|
| Present simple | *The work **is done** daily.* |
| Present continuous | *The road **is being repaired**.* |
| Past simple | *The file **was sent**.* |
| Present perfect | *The report **has been submitted**.* ⭐ |
| Future | *It **will be completed** tomorrow.* ⭐ |
| Modal | *It **can be done** / **should be checked**.* ⭐ |

*(Highlight the last three.)* ⭐ These are the ones you'll use in **work emails** constantly. ✅

*(Practise them.)*
- ✅ *The report **has been submitted**.* ✅
- ✅ *It **will be completed** by Friday.* ✅
- ✅ *This **can be done** in two ways.* ✅
- ✅ *The data **should be checked** first.* ✅

### The 3-second self-check 🎯

Before choosing:

1. **Do I know who did it, and does it matter?** → **active** ✅
2. **Is the doer unknown/obvious/irrelevant?** → **passive** ✅
3. **Am I using passive just to sound formal?** → **switch to active!** ⚠️
4. **Am I adding "by me"?** → **switch to active!** ⚠️

*(Drill — I say passive, you make it active.)*
- *"The report was written by me."* → **I wrote the report.** ✅
- *"A mistake was made by us."* → **We made a mistake.** ✅
- *"The email was sent by her."* → **She sent the email.** ✅
- *"You are requested to attend."* → **Please attend.** ✅
