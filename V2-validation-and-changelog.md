# AUDIT VALIDATION → REVISION V2

**Inputs:** `90-day-english-syllabus-malayalam.md` (V1, baseline) + `AUDIT-90-day-syllabus.md` (external critique)
**Output:** `90-day-english-syllabus-v2.md`
**Stance:** V1 is the baseline. The audit is a critique to be tested, not a work order. 10 of its recommendations were rejected or substantially modified.

---

## PART 1 — VALIDATION OF CRITICAL (🔴) FINDINGS

### C1 — Grammar density in Days 70–77

**Decision: ACCEPT FINDING / MODIFY FIX**

**Reason:** The localized finding is valid and serious — Days 71–76 are six consecutive abstract grammar systems at the exact point the brief required fluency emphasis, and they follow two skills weeks, so the course appears to regress. But the audit's supporting statistic ("~61% grammar days") is misused. A beginner course *must* be structure-dense in Weeks 1–7; grammar density in the foundation phase is correct design, not a defect. The audit's Course Balance score of 4/10 penalised appropriate early density along with inappropriate late density. Accepting the global framing would have led to de-grammaring Weeks 1–7, which would damage the course.

**Best correction:** Do not reduce grammar; relocate and reframe it. Phrasal verbs and Manglish correction leave Week 11 for Day 59 (they are lexical/prosodic, not grammatical). Gerunds/infinitives leave Week 11 for Day 41, reframed as preferences and plans. Passive voice moves into Week 12 as a real-world function (processes, instructions, news). Week 10 gains a listening day and an applied-explanation day. Week 11 gains an applied discussion day. The result: the longest uninterrupted grammar run in the back half falls from 6 days to 5, and every remaining advanced-grammar day carries a communicative outcome in its title.

---

### C2 — Missing V1/V2/V3 / past-participle foundation

**Decision: ACCEPT FINDING / MODIFY FIX** — independently verified as the only true dependency violation in V1.

**Verification:** V1 Day 32 is "Past Simple — the most useful irregular verbs," which by its own title owns V2 only. V1 Day 46 requires "have/has + past participle" and V1 Day 74 requires passive participles. No V1 day owns V3. Confirmed valid, and confirmed as the highest-severity finding in the audit.

**Reason for modifying the fix:** The audit proposed a standalone V1/V2/V3 day at Day 34. A dedicated form-drill day with no immediate use is inert — learners memorise three columns and forget the third, because nothing in Days 34–45 requires it. That reproduces the problem in a different form.

**Best correction:** Own the three-form *concept* where it is free, then the difficult forms where they are meaningful.
- **Day 30** (regular past) introduces the three-form idea at zero cost, because for regular verbs V2 and V3 are identical: `work – worked – worked`. The learner learns the framework without learning any new form.
- **Day 31** (irregular verbs) then teaches all three columns together — `go – went – gone`, `see – saw – seen` — rather than V2 alone, and states explicitly that column 3 is not used yet and will be needed from Day 45.
- **Day 45** (present perfect) opens by activating Day 31's third column.
- **Day 73** (past perfect) and **Day 78** (passive) both re-activate it.

This is cheaper than a new day, teaches V3 at the moment learning irregular verbs is already happening, and gives V3 three later uses instead of one. It also removes the audit's own concern that Day 32 was overloaded — learning three columns at once is less work than learning two and later relearning three.

---

### C3 — Insufficient listening

**Decision: ACCEPT** — with the ordering fault treated as the primary defect.

**Reason:** Three listening days is indefensible against a stated comprehension outcome. But the sharper fault is ordering, not volume: V1 asks for a three-minute conversation on Day 56 while the first conversational listening lesson arrives on Day 62. Adding days without fixing that order would leave the real problem in place.

**Best correction:** A six-rung ladder with each rung placed before the speaking milestone that depends on it — see Part 6 below.

---

### C4 — Article / preposition recycling

**Decision: ACCEPT FINDING / MODIFY FIX**

**Reason:** Valid — a no-L1-equivalent system given one lesson and never revisited will fossilise. But the audit's proposal of two generic "recycling days" risks producing two grammar-revision videos, which is exactly the format the brief warned against.

**Best correction:** Give the recycling a diagnostic, Malayalam-specific identity rather than a revision identity. **Day 48 — "The Little Things That Give You Away"** bundles the three highest-frequency invisible errors: missing/extra articles, wrong prepositions, and the disappearing third-person `-s`. This single day resolves three separate audit findings (C4, H11 agreement recycling, H-verb-dependent-prepositions) and reads as an error clinic rather than a grammar lesson. **Day 77** repeats the clinic inside a checkpoint. **Day 86** is the final pass. Articles are additionally applied at Days 14, 27, and 69.

---

### C5 — Hesitation / thinking-time ownership

**Decision: ACCEPT** — and I confirm the audit's account of how the gap arose. A "fillers and thinking time" day existed in the pre-final draft and was displaced by the opinion lesson without being re-homed.

**Reason:** This is the highest-leverage single intervention for a learner who has grammar and still freezes, and it is a prerequisite for the Day 56 conversation rather than a nice-to-have.

**Best correction:** **Day 55 — "Communication Strategies: thinking time, fast responses, and saying it another way."** Placed immediately before the Day 56 conversation checkpoint so it functions as preparation, not consolidation. Merging three related strategies (fillers, response speed, circumlocution) into one day is justified because they serve a single function: keeping the conversation alive while you assemble the sentence. This also converts Day 87 from remedial work into genuine consolidation.

---

### C6 — Reported speech overload

**Decision: ACCEPT FINDING / MODIFY FIX**

**Reason:** Five sub-systems in one day is indefensible, and one of them reverses the Day 17 do-support rule. But splitting into "reported statements" and "reported questions" produces two grammar days, worsening the Week 11 corridor the audit also complained about — the two recommendations were in tension and the audit did not notice.

**Best correction:** Split by *purpose*, not by structure. **Day 74** owns reported statements and backshift as grammar. **Day 75 — "Reporting Real Conversations"** owns reported questions, requests and instructions inside a communicative task. The load is split, and the second day counts toward communication rather than against it.

**Additional dependency surfaced during validation:** backshift requires past perfect (`he has finished` → `he had finished`). V1 had neither past perfect nor any awareness of this. **Day 73 (past perfect) is therefore placed immediately before Day 74**, which converts the audit's separate 🟠 "past perfect missing" finding from an add-on into a structural necessity.

---

### C7 — Whole-course balance (audit's additional critical claim)

**Decision: PARTIALLY REJECT** — see C1. The localized finding stands; the global statistic does not. V2 leaves the foundation phase's grammar density untouched.

## CHANGES REJECTED

These audit recommendations were deliberately **not** implemented.

**1. "Move Week 9 (pronunciation) before Week 8 (conversation)."** Rejected. Only the listening half of this finding is valid. Pronunciation refinement is learned far better under felt communicative need — minimal-pair work before a learner has ever attempted a conversation is decontextualised drilling, and it would also push the course's first real speaking experience to Day 63. V2 fixes the genuine defect narrowly by moving a listening day to D53.

**2. "Add a standalone V1/V2/V3 day."** Rejected in favour of owning the concept inside Days 30 and 31. A form-drill day with no immediate application is inert; learners would memorise the third column and lose it before Day 45.

**3. "Move comparatives to Week 2–3."** Rejected. Low dependency is not a reason to teach something early. Week 2 is already the densest noun-phrase week in the course, and comparatives are more useful once learners can build full sentences. The audit's real complaint was thematic tidiness in Week 10, which V2 fixes by retitling the week and merging comparatives with degree.

**4. "Split Day 12 into two days."** Rejected as stated. The audit was right that D12 held two systems but wrong that a bare split was the answer — that would have left prepositions with the same half-day they already had. V2 gives place prepositions a full day and keeps D12 for existentials plus the subject-obligatory rule, which addresses two additional unowned Malayalam transfers the audit listed separately.

**5. "Split Day 39 (should / must / have to)."** Deferred. These form one functional cluster — obligation and advice — that learners encounter together, and the `must` vs `have to` nuance is a lesson-level refinement, not a syllabus-level dependency. The day it would cost is better spent on listening.

**6. "Add 'used to'."** Rejected. Genuinely useful but 🟡, and past simple plus time expressions already delivers the narration outcome. Retained as lesson-level content inside Day 79 rather than as a syllabus topic.

**7. "Split reported speech into reported statements and reported questions."** Modified rather than accepted. A pure structural split would have produced two grammar days and worsened the Week 11 corridor the audit complained about elsewhere — the two recommendations were in tension. V2 splits by purpose instead.

**8. "~61% grammar days is a whole-course failure."** Rejected as a global judgement. A beginner course must be structure-dense early; the defect was localised to Days 64–77. Accepting the global framing would have led to weakening Weeks 1–7, which are among the strongest parts of V1.

**9. "Split passive voice across days."** Rejected; handled by scope restriction to present and past simple passive, and by reframing it as a real-world function.

**10. "Add per-day scope notes to the master syllabus."** Deferred. Necessary for safe lesson generation, but the brief restricts the master syllabus to topic titles only. This belongs in a separate companion document.

Not READY, for two honest reasons:

**1. Vocabulary ownership (6/10) — the one substantive gap remaining.** Only two days are explicitly lexical. Days 52 and 79–83 carry topical vocabulary implicitly, which means lesson generation will decide the actual vocabulary load rather than the syllabus. This is a real limitation, but fixing it properly requires either a 91st day or displacing a listening day, and listening is the higher-value use of a scarce slot. My recommendation is to solve it at the lesson-specification layer — a vocabulary spine assigning a topical field to each week — rather than by distorting the day structure.

These need a companion Topic Ownership Map fixing what each day may and may not cover. That document cannot live inside a master syllabus restricted to topic titles.
